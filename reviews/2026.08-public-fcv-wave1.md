# FY2027 Public FCV Climate-FCV Bank: Wave 1 Candidate Review

## Status and approval boundary

Burkina Faso, Haiti, Papua New Guinea, Ukraine, and Yemen are reviewed candidates. None is approved for production. This review does not promote a country package, update the approved release, or change the application submodule. South Sudan and `releases/current/runtime.json` remain unchanged.

The review compared the final schema 1.1 JSON ledgers and deterministic dossiers. It checked source and evidence-role balance, causal calibration, geographic qualification, pathway alternatives and uncertainty, and whether resilience factors are as specific as the risk mechanisms.

## Cross-country methodological consistency

- No package pads its evidence count with generic physical material. Physical-baseline records account for 12-20 percent of each ledger; vulnerability, institutional, direct-interaction, response, and resilience evidence forms the majority.
- Each package contains both climate-to-FCV and FCV-to-climate interaction directions. The balance varies by context: Ukraine emphasizes war damage to climate-relevant systems, while Burkina Faso and Haiti contain more climate-to-FCV hypotheses. Bidirectional pathways are present in all five.
- Co-occurrence is not treated as causal proof. Pathways use conditional language, identify mediators, list alternative explanations, and state uncertainty. Geological hazards in Papua New Guinea, the Kakhovka breach and seasonal cold in Ukraine, and structural aridity, groundwater governance, economic collapse, and food insecurity in Yemen remain distinct from anthropogenic climate attribution.
- Conflict, violence, governance, poverty, markets, displacement, asset inequality, service access, and programme design remain visible as independent or mediating drivers. Water scarcity is not presented as a single cause of conflict in Yemen or Burkina Faso.
- Analytical-inference pathways are explicitly conditional and carry evidence limitations. Direct classifications refer to observed links in the pathway, not proof that climate caused violence or that an intervention caused peace.
- Resilience factors identify pathway-specific institutions and actions, including secure access, representative water governance, inclusive targeting, maintenance finance, redundant services, municipal capacity, negotiated land arrangements, and locally legitimate mediation. They are not generic calls for more resilience.
- Candidate source counts remain within the planned range, every admitted source is cited by at least one atomic evidence record, and all nine evidence classes are represented in every country.

## Country-specific evidence gaps

### Burkina Faso

1. A complete, comparable national and subnational numeric baseline for heat, drought, rainfall, and flood hazards is absent.
2. Independent response evaluation is concentrated in the WFP portfolio; unintended, distributional, adaptation, and peace effects are rarely evaluated.
3. Conflict-related access and service constraints support the reverse pathway qualitatively, but their additional effect on climate losses and adaptation performance is not quantified.

### Haiti

1. Current high-resolution cyclone, flood, heat, drought, rainfall, and sea-level baselines are incomplete and not internally consistent across departments.
2. Independent evaluation is concentrated in the WFP portfolio through 2022; municipal DRM, adaptation, humanitarian, social-protection, and climate-security outcomes remain thin.
3. Displacement, access, and service disruption support the reverse pathway, but conflict's additional effect on losses, adaptation, and recovery is not quantified.

### Papua New Guinea

1. National and subnational projections remain incomplete for drought, landslide probability, coastal inundation, cyclone change, and settlement-level exposure.
2. Response evaluations are project-specific and provide little evidence on long-term maintenance, distributional performance, climate outcomes, or peace outcomes.
3. The reverse pathway rests mainly on displacement, insecurity, access, and service isolation without a national quantitative estimate of their additional recovery effect.

### Ukraine

1. No integrated current climate baseline reflects war-altered land, population, infrastructure, hydrology, monitoring coverage, and occupied-territory uncertainty.
2. Independent response evaluation is localized to Mykolaiv; climate-integrated reconstruction, municipal adaptation, displacement integration, and ecosystem recovery remain weakly evaluated.
3. Conflict damage to infrastructure, access, population distribution, and ecosystems is well documented, but its additional effect on future climate losses and adaptation performance is not quantified.

### Yemen

1. Localized climate, aquifer, groundwater-recharge, rainfall-intensity, coastal, and settlement exposure evidence remains incomplete amid degraded monitoring.
2. Current evidence from de facto authority areas, inaccessible districts, and underrepresented groups is uneven and often not comparable across territories.
3. Independent evaluations measure delivery and implementation constraints better than durable maintenance, welfare, aquifer, livelihood, inclusion, climate-resilience, or peace outcomes.

## Source and geographic limitations

Public-source access and territorial coverage are uneven across the wave. Burkina Faso, Haiti, Papua New Guinea, and Yemen have systematic evidence gaps in insecure or remote areas. Ukraine's occupied and frontline areas are incompletely observed. Local studies strengthen mechanism analysis but are not generalized to national populations.

Government and multilateral planning documents are used as evidence of stated priorities or intended capacity, not implementation. Modelled exposures are not treated as observed losses. Programme completion reports and evaluations are distinguished from causal impact evidence, and response outputs are not treated as proof of sustained resilience or peace.

Several packages depend on older structural studies because current public data are missing. The ledgers preserve review dates, refresh tiers, locators, limitations, and geographic qualifications so future updates can replace rather than silently extend stale evidence.

2. Review the country-specific causal calibration, especially Burkina Faso's and Haiti's climate-to-FCV inferences, Papua New Guinea's mobility and customary-land pathways, Ukraine's climate-war compounding language, and Yemen's water-conflict pathways.
3. Confirm whether the geographic caveats are prominent enough for intended users, particularly insecure, occupied, de facto authority, island, remote, displacement-site, and gang-controlled settings.

5. Prioritize the evidence gaps to address before any production approval. No approval is implied by this review or by the successful technical checks.
