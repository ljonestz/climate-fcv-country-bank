## Climate pressures and exposure

- Hazard maps place greatest river-flood risk along Nepal’s southern border and valleys, plus heat, drought, landslide, and air pollution. [NPL-E-002]
- Over 4.3 million people fall in the extreme heat-stress category for a modeled 20-year event, concentrated in southern municipalities. [NPL-E-003]
- Mid-century CMIP6 projections show larger extreme-precipitation changes in western Lumbini, Karnali, and Sudurpaschim than farther east. [NPL-E-004]
- The report projects persistent western drought and rising heat nationwide, with stronger warming under SSP5-8.5. [NPL-E-005]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- With 62% of workers in agriculture, Terai rainfed smallholders face floods; limited safety nets constrain coping. [NPL-E-001]
- Nepal's Second NDC describes the country as vulnerable because of fragile topography, climate-sensitive livelihoods, and limited adaptive capacity. [NPL-E-006]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC sets adaptation priorities across agriculture and food security, forests and watersheds, water and energy, settlements, infrastructure, tourism and heritage, health, and disaster risk reduction. [NPL-E-007]
- The government synopsis estimates US$18-20 billion for adaptation priorities through 2035 and identifies grants as a financing need for adaptation and loss-and-damage actions. [NPL-E-008]
- The synopsis identifies plans to strengthen data systems for slow- and rapid-onset events and to develop planning for climate-induced displacement and resettlement. [NPL-E-009]
