## Climate pressures and exposure

- Hazard maps place greatest river-flood risk along Nepal’s southern border and valleys, plus heat, drought, landslide, and air pollution. [NPL-E-002]
- Over 4.3 million people fall in the extreme heat-stress category for a modeled 20-year event, concentrated in southern municipalities. [NPL-E-003]
- Mid-century CMIP6 projections show larger extreme-precipitation changes in western Lumbini, Karnali, and Sudurpaschim than farther east. Scenario: CMIP6 SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5. [NPL-E-004]
- The report projects persistent western drought and rising heat nationwide, with stronger warming under SSP5-8.5. Scenario: CMIP6 SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5; stronger warming is reported under SSP5-8.5. [NPL-E-005]
- Source dated 2025-05-14 reports: NDC 3.0 reports September 2024 floods and landslides killed 249 and displaced 11,000 families; cited models estimate climate change raised event intensity 10% and likelihood about 70%. [NPL-E-103]
- The May 2025 NDC records flooding at Thame in August 2024 from two glacial-lake outbursts and separately identifies 47 potentially dangerous glacial lakes nationally; no event-specific attribution is claimed. [NPL-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- With 62% of workers in agriculture, Terai rainfed smallholders face floods; limited safety nets constrain coping. [NPL-E-001]
- Nepal's Second NDC describes the country as vulnerable because of fragile topography, climate-sensitive livelihoods, and limited adaptive capacity. [NPL-E-006]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP says climate-related floods, landslides and agricultural shifts threaten human security in Barekot and Nalgad, where over 90% rely on agriculture; gender inequalities heighten women's food, health and economic insecurity. [NPL-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-10-07 reports: The plan reports 21 affected districts and 246 deaths; 46,569 ha of Madhesh farmland were damaged, with crop losses expected to worsen food insecurity and negative coping. [NPL-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC sets adaptation priorities across agriculture and food security, forests and watersheds, water and energy, settlements, infrastructure, tourism and heritage, health, and disaster risk reduction. [NPL-E-007]
- The government synopsis estimates US$18-20 billion for adaptation priorities through 2035 and identifies grants as a financing need for adaptation and loss-and-damage actions. [NPL-E-008]
- The synopsis identifies plans to strengthen data systems for slow- and rapid-onset events and to develop planning for climate-induced displacement and resettlement. [NPL-E-009]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- UNDP's local baseline found no formal early-warning systems in Barekot/Nalgad, poor warning awareness/access, and funding/technical barriers to implementing local plans. [NPL-E-102]
- UNDP's 5 January 2025 update reports ten community volunteers deployed in Namobuddha, Roshi, Timal, Bethanchock, Rajpur and Phidim to support shelter-data collection and access to government assistance. [NPL-E-107]
- As of 5 January 2025, cash support in Roshi/Namobuddha for 260 households and debris clearance were ongoing; a separate 600-household cash-for-work activity in Kavre/Lalitpur remained planned. [NPL-E-108]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The 2024 Nepal flood plan identifies protection risks where displacement meets service gaps and unequal access, without measuring new violence. [NPL-P-101; NPL-E-105, NPL-E-106]
  - Documented impact: Housing disruption and displacement into temporary accommodation, with gaps in basic and protection services.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal access and pre-existing gender/disability vulnerability in displacement settings.
  - Possible consequence: The response plan identifies heightened protection risks and calls for violence-prevention and inclusive access; it does not estimate actual new violence.
