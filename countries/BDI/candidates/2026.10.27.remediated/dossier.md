## Climate pressures and exposure

- The report projects 0.5–1.0°C warming for 2040–2060 versus 1981–2000, with uncertain north–south rainfall differences. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify it. [BDI-E-001]
- Prior national analysis projected 1–3°C higher annual mean temperature and roughly 10% more rainfall by 2050, with a disrupted seasonal regime. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify it. [BDI-E-004]
- The INDC lists groundwater shortage, lowland flooding, soil-fertility loss, crop-season changes and unpredictable pest movements among projected risks. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify it. [BDI-E-006]
- Among 1,027 purposively selected climate-affected IDPs interviewed in Bujumbura, Cibitoke and Makamba in 2025, 63% reported flood impacts, 34% landslides and 34% unpredictable weather. [BDI-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The composite assessment scores 347 of 2,616 collines at very high combined climate, environmental and fragility risk, and none at low or very low risk. [BDI-E-002]
- The INDC identifies northern and northwestern vulnerability hotspots and flags the slope toward the Imbo Plain, northern terrain and central plateau as erosion-prone. [BDI-E-005]
- Among surveyed IDPs affected by floods, 96% said housing destruction or damage prompted movement; 22% also cited household food or water scarcity. [BDI-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- In the purposive sample, 91% reported no early-warning system. Of 92 respondents who received some warning, 37% could not act because of limited resources; crop and livestock adaptation were uncommon and often ineffective. [BDI-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The November 2024 appraisal proposed 71 locations covering 65 hotspot collines in seven provinces; six clearly readable names are Cibitoke, Gitega, Kirundo, Kayanza, Bujumbura and Muyinga. [BDI-E-102]
- The November 2024 appraisal proposed colline climate-action plans, early-warning and contingency systems, livelihood diversification and land certification; delivery and effects were not established. [BDI-E-104]
- WFP's June 2026 plan revision commits to climate-sensitive livelihoods and social protection, particularly for women smallholders, alongside government food-system and assistance capacity; these are intended actions, not evaluated results. [BDI-E-108]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The report describes land scarcity as an existing source of intercommunal conflict and warns climate and land degradation may reduce arable productivity and intensify this pressure. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify it. [BDI-E-003]
- The November 2024 appraisal describes a possible scarce-land mechanism: heavier rainfall and landslides may degrade land, heighten tensions, weaken cohesion and reopen post-conflict trauma. [BDI-E-103]

Limitations: 2015 assessment using earlier studies; the cited projection passage does not name a scenario. Adaptation measures describe plans, not evaluated results.
