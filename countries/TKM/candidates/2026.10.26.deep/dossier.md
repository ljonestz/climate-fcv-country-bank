# Turkmenistan Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Dashoguz irrigation dependence, heat and water-allocation pressures differ from Ashgabat's canal/reservoir and urban supply constraints. [TKM-E-101, TKM-E-102, TKM-E-103, TKM-E-104, TKM-E-114, TKM-P-101]
- Vulnerable-group participation and water-quality sensitivity require attention; women-led households and migrant families were among selected local training participants, not a representative population sample. [TKM-E-105, TKM-E-110, TKM-E-111, TKM-E-112, TKM-E-113]
- Independent evaluation records technical capacity gains and some water-saving finance, but also limited scaling and incomplete integration of local adaptation plans. [TKM-E-106, TKM-E-107, TKM-E-108, TKM-E-109, TKM-E-115]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No verified armed-conflict-to-adaptation pathway or climate-caused armed violence. Conditional water-user disputes are not an armed-conflict forecast. []
- Local evidence focuses on Dashoguz and Ashgabat, plus selected IOM communities in Akhal; other provinces have thinner coverage. []
- The policy brief derives from the same 2024 risk assessment and is not independent replication. Evaluation findings cover 2021–2024, not current implementation. []
- National WASH and older local-project pages were inaccessible on direct opening; indexed metadata was not used. []
- The assessment's speculative 'climate refugees' discussion and broad mortality claims were not extracted: no supported local incidence or migration forecast was established. []
- Water-user conflict is a conditional planning risk, not observed violence or a forecast of armed conflict. No reverse-FCV interruption of adaptation was verified. []
- Assessment evidence is selective and partly stakeholder-informed; no current nationwide local vulnerability ranking is available. []
- The policy brief derives from the same risk assessment, not an independent study. Evaluation contributions do not establish counterfactual impacts; training and project objectives are not demonstrated adoption. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-06-27 reports: Dashoguz heat days above 30°C increase by a median 18–22 days toward mid-century, from about 140 historically. [TKM-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-06-27 reports: Dashoguz irrigation has limited reuse, water-intensive crops and flood irrigation; farmer-managed channels face knowledge and management constraints. [TKM-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-06-27 reports: Ashgabat depends on Karakum Canal reservoirs; losses, irrigation withdrawals near Mary/Tejen and shared drinking/park supply constrain water management. [TKM-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: Water-saving credit schemes were used chiefly by greenhouse producers; currency conditions made them less attractive to wider producers. [TKM-E-107]
- Source dated 2024-06 reports: Women managing household water, children and people with chronic illness face differentiated risks from scarcity or reduced water quality. [TKM-E-113]
- Source dated 2024-06 reports: Dashoguz water supply depends on Tuyamuyun reservoirs; evaporation, sedimentation and irrigation-channel losses compound climate-related scarcity. [TKM-E-114]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025-06 reports: The evaluation finds stronger technical institutions and drought-prediction capacity, while local administrative support remained geographically limited. [TKM-E-108]
- Source dated 2025-05-20 reports: Over forty representatives joined seminars for Gorogly and Konegummez, including women-led households and families remaining behind when migrants leave. [TKM-E-110]
- Source dated 2025-05-20 reports: Participants practised water conservation and collaborative pasture/irrigated-land management; reported learning is not evidence of sustained adoption. [TKM-E-111]
- Source dated 2024-06 reports: Proposed measures include upstream coordination, efficient irrigation and women's participation in water decisions; effectiveness is not evaluated. [TKM-E-115]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-06-27 reports: The cited 2023 assessment scores vulnerable-group participation 10/100, unchanged since 2020, indicating no explicit inclusion in water policies. [TKM-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: Agricultural information centres were not scaled up and some local adaptation plans were not integrated into policy or legislation. [TKM-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-05-20 reports: The two-year IOM project partners with the environment ministry to support gender-sensitive local adaptation in Akhal and Dashoguz. [TKM-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-related water pressure and inefficient, competing irrigation uses can create water-user disputes in Dashoguz; violence is not established. [TKM-P-101; TKM-E-102, TKM-E-103]
  - Documented impact: Scarcity in the irrigation-dependent Dashoguz production system.
  - FCV mediator: Water-intensive crops, inefficient irrigation and competing demands concentrate pressure on limited water.
  - Possible consequence: The 2024 risk assessment warns of possible conflict among water users; it does not report violence or dispute incidence.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Upstream withdrawals, crop choice, infrastructure losses and allocation decisions affect scarcity independently of climate.']
  - Uncertainty: A stakeholder-informed conditional mechanism, not an identified causal effect, current dispute count or armed-conflict forecast.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities
