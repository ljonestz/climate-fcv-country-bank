## Climate pressures and exposure

- NDC names droughts, floods and heatwaves as major disasters, lists drought seasons in 2012-13, 2015-16, 2018-19 and 2023-24, and says drought exposure varies across Zimbabwe. [ZWE-E-001]
- NDC locates flood exposure in low-lying areas, dam overflows, river confluences, upstream backwater and cyclone paths; floods have spread to previously unaffected areas. [ZWE-E-002]
- Source dated 2024-05 reports: The appeal documents a February 2024 dry spell exceeding 30 days; its cover caption identifies wilting, prematurely maturing maize in Zvimba District. [ZWE-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: UNICEF reported 6,028 water points broken and 2,310 identified sources dry in 2024, primarily in Masvingo, Matabeleland South and Midlands provinces. [ZWE-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- World Bank says agriculture employs 70 percent of Zimbabwe's population; reliance on rainfed systems and maize leaves the sector vulnerable to climate events, especially ENSO-linked droughts. [ZWE-E-004]
- World Bank attributes the 2023-24 severe drought to El Nino and reports maize yield 60 percent below the five-year average, alongside widespread food insecurity and economic hardship. [ZWE-E-005]
- World Bank reports that the 2023-24 El Nino drought caused about US$363 million in damage and losses. [ZWE-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: The appeal reports child-wasting prevalence rose from 4.1% to 4.9% in rural areas and 3.0% to 5.6% in urban areas between 2023 and 2024. [ZWE-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: A UNICEF outreach in Sanyati identified a severely malnourished seven-year-old; her family lived 38 km from the nearest clinic, and the report says drought-related food insecurity and disrupted facility access left children vulnerable. [ZWE-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NDC reports that the National Adaptation Plan identifies 18 priority outputs across seven sectors, including agriculture, water, health, infrastructure and human settlements. [ZWE-E-003]
- Source dated 2025-03-19 reports: In drought-prone Mwenezi, Pikinini Jawanda lead farmer Chizivano Shava combines runoff trenches, infiltration pits, rainwater capture and two tanks totaling 130,000 litres; his wife reports less distant water collection and enough for household and garden use. [ZWE-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-09-17 reports: UNDP reports expansion of Vimbanayi from 23 to 35 hectares with improved water abstraction, storage and conveyance; a scheme leader said farmers now produce maize, chilli, sugar beans and vegetables with more income streams. [ZWE-E-109]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-03-19 reports: UNDP reported 230 Farmer Field Schools across 15 project districts and 54,284 rain-fed hectares using water-harvesting measures in 2024; seven irrigation schemes covering 527 hectares had been climate-proofed. [ZWE-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-09-17 reports: The September 2026 mission reported that Pikinini-Jawanda's 156-hectare scheme supports 300 farmers from 12 villages; farmers diversified crops and maintained production and financial records to manage the shared investment. [ZWE-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-09-17 reports: At Makoholi, 15 Farmer Field Schools and 56 demonstration plots directly reach 330 farmers and share learning with about 1,500 others; 188 extension officers have trained in fodder harvesting and feed formulation. [ZWE-E-110]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2024-05 reports: The appeal assessed drought-related food and water insecurity as heightening GBV, sexual-exploitation and child-marriage risks; it links long water-collection journeys for women and girls to greater sexual-violence exposure. [ZWE-E-102]
- Source dated 2024 reports: A 2024 stakeholder workshop framed water scarcity and artisanal mining as contributors to resource tensions, and identified climate-related livelihood shocks, displacement and harmful coping as pathways that may destabilize communities and strain host-area services.
