## Climate pressures and exposure


## Differentiated vulnerability

- The CCDR identifies Indigenous peoples, Afro-descendants and women as disproportionately vulnerable to climate impacts in Honduras. [HND-E-001]
- WFP identifies climate change, unequal growth, violence, displacement and limited basic services as interacting drivers of food insecurity. [HND-E-004]
- WFP reports that 2024 drought and erratic rain damaged agriculture and household incomes, especially among rural and marginalized communities. [HND-E-005]
- Source dated 2021 reports: In Pespire, San Lorenzo and El Corpus study communities, surveyed households reported drought-linked food-price and water/health burdens and livelihood interruptions; reduced food intake and savings use were common coping responses. [HND-E-104]
- Source dated 2021 reports: The survey’s “forced internal migration” category explicitly includes moves for work; its results must not be equated with standard conflict/disaster displacement counts. [HND-E-106]
- Source dated 2026 reports: The analysis reports restricted traditional fishing access at Amapala beaches and mangrove-loss concerns in San Lorenzo, alongside Cedeño coastal vulnerability. [HND-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The CCDR recommends tenure security, livelihood diversification, locally led investment and resilient rural roads to protect vulnerable groups. [HND-E-003]
- The World Bank country strategy identifies inclusion and rule-of-law support for Indigenous peoples, Afro-descendants and other vulnerable groups. [HND-E-007]
- Source dated 2021 reports: Among surveyed agricultural workers, roughly two-thirds worked third-party land; women aged 18–29 had the highest unemployment, indicating differentiated livelihood constraints. [HND-E-105]
- Source dated 2026 reports: The report identifies Goascorán’s binational water/adaptation cooperation as an existing capacity, while noting deforestation and intensive land-use pressures. [HND-E-109]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: WFP’s plan commits to gender-sensitive shock-responsive social protection, stronger national/local partners, healthier masculinities and GBV reduction. [HND-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The 2026 overview identifies limited implementation capacity, low coping capacity and crime as development constraints associated with emigration. [HND-E-006]
- Source dated 2026 reports: Leaders reported irrigation-related water-access tensions around Namasigüe and Choluteca and concern in Caridad/Alianza over proposed upstream Goascorán impoundment. [HND-E-107]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The plan describes crime, inequality and climate variability as joint constraints on humanitarian and longer-term initiatives, especially affecting women, Indigenous and Afro-descendant groups. [HND-E-111]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-related livelihood loss may compound existing exclusion and institutional constraints, increasing displacement or land-tension risks; the evidence does not establish climate-caused violence. [HND-P-001; HND-E-002, HND-E-004, HND-E-005, HND-E-006]

  - FCV mediator: Unequal access to basic services, violence, displacement and limited coping/implementation capacity can compound livelihood stress.
  - Possible consequence: Further food/water insecurity, migration, displacement and land tensions are conditional risks, not attributed observed conflict effects.

- Honduras evidence finds a stronger rainfall-deficit/migration association in higher-violence departments; the proposed safety constraint is interpretive, not causal proof. [HND-P-101; HND-E-101, HND-E-102]

  - Possible consequence: Cross-border movement may reflect intertwined livelihood and safety concerns; the study does not establish that drought causes violence or individual migration.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The CCDR warns that climate pressures may compound migration, internal displacement, land conflict and insecurity. Scenario: The inspected qualitative CCDR summary does not specify a scenario. [HND-E-002]
- Source dated 2021-03 reports: Rainfall deficits were associated with more subsequent US family-unit apprehensions from Honduran departments in 2012–2019; the relationship was stronger where homicide rates were higher. [HND-E-101]
- Source dated 2021-03 reports: The authors interpret intertwined weather/livelihood stress and violence as potentially limiting safe internal alternatives before cross-border movement. [HND-E-102]
- Source dated 2021-03 reports: The brief proposes local farmer participation in agricultural investment alongside responses to governance and violence, rather than assuming aid alone resolves migration pressures. [HND-E-103]
