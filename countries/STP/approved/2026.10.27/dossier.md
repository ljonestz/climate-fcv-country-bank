## Climate pressures and exposure

- SCD reports recurrent floods, landslides, erosion and storms threaten lives, livelihoods, the airport and primary roads concentrated along the coast. [STP-E-005]
- World Bank climate data show 1951-2010 warming of 1.5°C, mean rainfall falling 1.7 mm yearly, and March-May rainfall dropping 10.5 mm monthly. [STP-E-006]
- Source dated 2025-08-29 reports: UNICEF identifies rainy-season flood, landslide, erosion and drought exposure in mountainous and riverside areas, naming Ribeira Afonso, Malanza and Neves as disproportionately affected. [STP-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-05-02 reports: The World Bank authors summarize modeled annual flood losses rising from 1.9% of GDP in 2020 to 2.8% in 2050 and 4.1% in 2080 under SSP3-7.0 without additional climate policy. Scenario: SSP3-7.0, assuming no additional climate policy. [STP-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-05-02 reports: The assessment identifies Malanza and Santa Catarina among the most affected subdistricts; about 18,500 people are flood-affected annually on average under present-climate conditions. [STP-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- NDC reports December 2021-February 2022 floods and landslides damaged infrastructure, agriculture and WASH; recovery cost $37.5m, about 7% of 2022 GDP. [STP-E-001]
- NDC cites annual flood losses rising from 1.9% of GDP in 2020 to 2.8% by 2050 and 4.1% by 2080. Scenario: Cited assessment incorporates RCP4.5 and RCP8.5; NDC does not assign the loss figures to one pathway. [STP-E-002]
- NDC states fisheries provide 70-85% of animal protein and support 15% of active workers; extreme weather and erosion damage boats and isolate communities. [STP-E-003]
- Source dated 2025-08-29 reports: After December 2021 floods, Lembá District recorded 2,104 people affected and two child deaths; damage included bridges, a water plant, crops, fishing boats and over half of latrines. [STP-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-08-29 reports: UNICEF says irregular rainfall and prolonged dry spells threaten fragile food systems, particularly for vulnerable households, while food insecurity persisted. [STP-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-05-02 reports: World Bank feature interviews portray recurrent flood and wave damage as a livelihood concern for a fisherwoman in Praia Cruz and a fish trader in Praia Abade. [STP-E-107]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP’s project page describes climate-driven urban flooding and water-system turbidity affecting São Tomé and Santo António, with interruptions for about one-third of the population and added water-collection burdens for women and girls. [STP-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

## Institutions and delivery systems
