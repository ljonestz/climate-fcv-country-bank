# Senegal Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Studies in the Groundnut Basin, Ferlo/Louga and Casamance add local livelihood and institutional detail. An experiment documents group-biased cooperation under simulated risk; it is not evidence that a climate event caused violence. [SEN-E-101, SEN-E-102, SEN-E-108]
- Casamance interviews describe conflict-related restrictions on land and food production under environmental stress. The assembled reverse-FCV mechanism is explicitly conditional and not a national causal estimate. [SEN-E-108, SEN-E-109, SEN-P-101]
- The R4 evaluation reports reach and resilience-related gains but also transfer, monitoring and inclusion weaknesses. The accessible publisher summary cannot establish effect sizes or independent attribution. [SEN-E-103, SEN-E-104, SEN-E-105]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Half of Senegal's population and two-thirds of GDP face sea-level exposure; Dakar's population at coastal-flood risk is projected to rise 20-30% by 2050. Scenario: Scenario not specified here. [SEN-E-003]
- The diagnostic says northern Senegal is projected to experience more frequent extreme droughts, particularly in Saint-Louis; its cited CCDR scenarios estimate annual GDP losses of 3.6–4% by 2030 and 7–9.4% by 2050 without adaptation. Scenario: Climate projections for northern drought; cited CCDR GDP range varies by warming assumption and assumes no adaptation. [SEN-E-005]
- Senegal’s NDC reports declining rainfall, rising mean temperature, sea-level rise, and disruptions to cultivable land, water, and fisheries; it identifies northern regions, especially Saint-Louis, as facing a 20–30% increase in extreme-drought frequency, and identifies more wet extremes in Matam, Tambacounda, and Louga under its low-warming scenario. Scenario: NDC low-warming scenario for wet extremes; drought-frequency scenario is not further labeled in the cited summary. [SEN-E-006]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Summary models no-adaptation annual losses at 3-4% GDP by 2030 and 9.4% by 2050; over two million people could fall into poverty by mid-century. Scenario: No adaptation; SSP/RCP unstated here. [SEN-E-001]
- The diagnostic reports that the 2002 drought reduced agricultural production by about 35% and the 2011 drought by 20%; estimated production losses across 12 major crops during 1980–2020 totaled FCFA 852 billion, averaging 3.9% of agricultural GDP annually. [SEN-E-004]
- The study compared Groundnut Basin farmers with Ferlo/Louga agro-pastoralists, linked by seasonal southward herd migration; Ferlo has a long dry season and contested pasture. [SEN-E-101]
- In 13 Basse-Casamance communities, 234 agricultural interviewees reported environmental and rainfall change alongside widespread food-access difficulties; these are selected respondents' experiences, not national prevalence or climate attribution. [SEN-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- FAO lists a community-led garden in Mbam, Oudalaye commune, Matam Region, intended to strengthen food security, climate resilience and livelihoods for women and youth; the page lists over 200 participants and local-authority involvement. [SEN-E-106]
- Listed activities include three hectares for horticulture and agroforestry, water supply and farm inputs, climate-adaptation training, and an incubator for ten agri-entrepreneurs with an income platform for women and youth. [SEN-E-107]
- Casamance participants reported conflict and displacement affecting food security; qualitative findings describe landmines, fear and displacement restricting cultivated land while environmental pressures affect food systems. [SEN-E-109]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Selected-sector adaptation could add about 2% GDP by 2030 and reduce climate-induced poverty by 45% versus no action. Scenario: Selected adaptation versus no action; assumptions unstated. [SEN-E-002]
- The R4 initiative targeted 405,000 people across Kolda, Tambacounda, Kaffrine, Fatick and Kaolack through adaptation assets, agricultural insurance, savings/income support and government risk management; reported reach was 438,291. [SEN-E-103]
- The evaluation summary reports gains in food security, savings, insurance uptake, social cohesion, economic resilience and natural-resource management, while transfer delays, weak monitoring and staff turnover hindered delivery. [SEN-E-104]
- Women were active in savings groups but had limited access to unsubsidized insurance; youth and people with disabilities were under-targeted, and community structures had weak government anchoring and insurance literacy. [SEN-E-105]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- In selected Casamance communities, mines and displacement restrict farming under environmental stress; a conditional reverse-FCV vulnerability mechanism, not climate-caused violence. [SEN-P-101; SEN-E-108, SEN-E-109, SEN-E-110]
  - Documented impact: The same participatory study reports food-access difficulties and disrupted agricultural livelihoods.
  - FCV mediator: Landmines, fear and displacement restrict access to farmland and production, narrowing livelihood options under environmental stress.
  - Possible consequence: These constraints can leave climate-sensitive food systems less able to absorb further shocks; the interaction is conditional, not a measured joint causal effect.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Poverty, markets, salinization, deforestation and labor constraints can affect food access independently of conflict-climate interaction.']
  - Uncertainty: One purposive cross-sectional study combines respondent perceptions with historical context. It does not estimate a climate-conflict interaction, current mine prevalence or a national effect. ## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Out-group priming reduced cooperation, especially among farmers; focus groups linked distrust to stereotypes of mobile herders. The collective-risk treatment widened the in-group/out-group cooperation gap. [SEN-E-102]
- Participants proposed reforestation, sustainable farming and stronger environmental agencies; authors highlight women's roles in horticulture, seed management and local food-system resilience. [SEN-E-110]
