## Climate pressures and exposure

- Half of Senegal's population and two-thirds of GDP face sea-level exposure; Dakar's population at coastal-flood risk is projected to rise 20-30% by 2050. [SEN-E-003]
- The diagnostic says northern Senegal is projected to experience more frequent extreme droughts, particularly in Saint-Louis; its cited CCDR scenarios estimate annual GDP losses of 3.6–4% by 2030 and 7–9.4% by 2050 without adaptation. [SEN-E-005]
- Senegal’s NDC reports declining rainfall, rising mean temperature, sea-level rise, and disruptions to cultivable land, water, and fisheries; it identifies northern regions, especially Saint-Louis, as facing a 20–30% increase in extreme-drought frequency, and identifies more wet extremes in Matam, Tambacounda, and Louga under its low-warming scenario. [SEN-E-006]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Summary models no-adaptation annual losses at 3-4% GDP by 2030 and 9.4% by 2050; over two million people could fall into poverty by mid-century. [SEN-E-001]
- The diagnostic reports that the 2002 drought reduced agricultural production by about 35% and the 2011 drought by 20%; estimated production losses across 12 major crops during 1980–2020 totaled FCFA 852 billion, averaging 3.9% of agricultural GDP annually. [SEN-E-004]
