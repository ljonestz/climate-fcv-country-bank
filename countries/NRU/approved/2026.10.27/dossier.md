## Executive assessment

- Low-lying named districts on Bottomside have differentiated physical coastal exposure; household water storage and source reliability are separate sensitivities. [NRU-E-101, NRU-E-102, NRU-E-103, NRU-E-111]

- Warning systems, evacuation access, coordination and inclusive participation need strengthening. Proposed coastal works and project outputs do not demonstrate completed adaptation outcomes. [NRU-E-106, NRU-E-107, NRU-E-108, NRU-E-110, NRU-E-112]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025 reports: Most settlement and infrastructure lie on narrow Bottomside; low lagoon areas in Meneng, Anibare, Ijuw and Yaren are most exposed, unlike higher west and northwest districts. [NRU-E-101]
- Source dated 2025 reports: CIVRA estimates 0.7% of buildings are currently exposed to inundation and 2.3% may be exposed at 0.35-m sea-level rise by 2070; it gives 0.21–0.25 m rise by 2050. Scenario: The report gives low- and high-emissions 2050 sea-level estimates; the 0.35-m 2070 building-exposure estimate has no scenario specified in the cited passage. [NRU-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025 reports: 2023 data show 36.5% use rainwater for drinking and 48.6% rely heavily on desalinated water; only 56.4% have working tank downpipes, while groundwater is brackish or polluted. [NRU-E-103]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The Government warns sea-level rise may reduce coastal land and prompt internal displacement in coming decades. Scenario: Government statement refers to coming decades; no emissions scenario is specified in the cited passage. [NRU-E-104]
- RONAdapt II reports that wartime explosives and jagged limestone pinnacles hamper land development because removal or rehabilitation is costly. It identifies land rehabilitation as necessary for housing, water catchment and infrastructure and describes the Higher Ground Initiative as a climate-resilient land-use program. Explosive extent and separate costs are unspecified. [NRU-E-105]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP describes Buada Lagoon as Nauru’s only surface freshwater source; most people rely on rainwater collection or desalination, while groundwater is vulnerable to contamination. [NRU-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025 reports: Government, community, women’s and youth groups and persons with disabilities joined consultation; the plan calls for their inclusion in adaptation decisions. [NRU-E-108]
- Source dated 2021 reports: The 2021 NDC commits to a master land-use plan for moving coastal homes and critical infrastructure to Topside; construction of pilot Smart Village housing is conditional on support. [NRU-E-109]
- Source dated 2021 reports: The NDC proposes a technical coastal-erosion assessment and hard or nature-based protections; it lists the assessment as conditional on finance and sets a 2021 target. [NRU-E-110]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP lists the completed PACC project’s outputs as strengthened decision-maker capacity, institutional coordination, adaptation assessment tools and prepared policy directives. [NRU-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025 reports: In 2024, warnings were moderately developed and needed expansion; alerts covered tides, sea levels and drought, but evacuation routes were outdated. [NRU-E-106]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The framework finds gaps in cross-government coordination, technical expertise, adaptation data and independent climate-finance access. [NRU-E-107]

Limitations: Commitments and targets do not establish that projects were completed or outcomes achieved.
