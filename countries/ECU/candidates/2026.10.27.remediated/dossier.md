## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-09 reports: Twenty of 24 recorded heavy-rain flood disasters since 1992 affected a coastal province; erosion was highest in El Oro, Esmeraldas and Guayas. [ECU-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-05-29 reports: El Niño may reduce coastal fish income; episodic heavy rain and floods may disrupt livelihoods, markets and services in Amazon river areas. Scenario: FEWS NET outlook for May–December 2026, including expected El Niño conditions. [ECU-E-110]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-09 reports: Poor communities in Amazon and coastal regions face flood vulnerability; indigenous peoples are especially exposed. Dry/hot projections raise rural poverty more than urban poverty by 2050. Scenario: Dry/hot versus wet/warm scenarios; poverty projections to 2050. [ECU-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-05-18 reports: OCHA estimates 1.91 million people need assistance in ten priority provinces, concentrated in high-severity areas with simultaneous deprivations. [ECU-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-05-18 reports: Armed violence and criminal territorial control drive displacement, threats, extortion and service restrictions in coastal Guayas, Esmeraldas, Los Ríos, urban corridors and border zones. [ECU-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-05-18 reports: Climate disasters damage infrastructure, livelihoods and safe-water access in coastal and Amazon provinces with limited state presence; OCHA says shocks interact and reinforce overlapping deprivations. [ECU-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-05-29 reports: FEWS projects violence-driven food insecurity, compounded by weather, among poor informal workers, displaced people, migrants and coastal fishers. Scenario: Most-likely food-security outlook for June–December 2026; published 29 May 2026. [ECU-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-05-29 reports: Above-average heat may reduce irrigated second-season yields, while poor agricultural laborers face reduced work and income opportunities in coastal provinces. Scenario: 2026 second-season and June–December food-security outlook. [ECU-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-05-29 reports: National food supplies and most markets remain functional, but curfews, extortion and logistics costs restrict access and purchasing power, especially for poor and displaced households. [ECU-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Insecurity constrains multisector response where climate disasters add needs; climate-specific operational effects remain an inference. [ECU-P-101; ECU-E-107, ECU-E-108]

  - FCV mediator: Armed actors, insecurity and mobility restrictions constrain assessment, monitoring and sustained humanitarian operations.
  - Possible consequence: Climate-disaster assistance may be harder to sustain within the constrained multisector response.
