## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-11 reports: The CCDR records over 152 deaths and US$1.2 billion in losses from hazards over decades; landslides led to dozens of villages abandoned or displaced in 40 years. Shovi’s 2023 debris flow in Racha killed at least 32 and affected over 200. [GEO-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: By mid-century, heat risk covers Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and the coast under SSP1-2.6, expanding across Kolkheti Plain under SSP3-7.0; mountainous areas face more days above 25°C under SSP3-7.0. Scenario: Medium-term 2040-2059; SSP1-2.6 and SSP3-7.0. [GEO-E-107]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-11 reports: Among 100-year-flood-exposed people, 12% are poor and 77% have multiple vulnerabilities; among drought-exposed people, 12.1% are poor and 80% have multiple vulnerabilities. Kakheti is most drought-exposed; 91% there face other vulnerabilities. [GEO-E-102]
- The 2025 CCDR models higher food-price-related poverty under a dry/hot scenario than under stated policies, with particularly adverse consumption effects in Imereti, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti and Kakheti. These are scenario results, not observed poverty changes. Scenario: Dry/hot scenario versus SPS, 2030. [GEO-E-103]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The UN analysis places spatial, social, political (including unresolved conflict) and environmental (including climate) factors among intersecting sources of vulnerability. Conflict disproportionately affects forcibly displaced people, residents outside government control and communities near Abkhazia/South Ossetia ABLs. [GEO-E-104]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Isolated mountain communities have limited development and inadequate infrastructure; older women in small-scale agriculture are particularly climate-vulnerable. [GEO-E-105]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: The 2020 assessment says historic wetland drainage worsens lowland flood risk and is linked to coastal erosion, particularly around Poti; dams and port works also disrupt sediment delivery, including from Khobistskali, contributing to erosion and sediment accumulation at Poti port. [GEO-E-114]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP reports 150/154 planned weather stations in place; GECSA serves over 1,000 users in Kakheti and Shida Kartli, and landslide monitors are installed at 11 sites. [GEO-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025 reports: Monitoring, mapping, climate forecasting and early warnings have improved, but agricultural warnings and insurance remain weak; emergency social protection roles are unclear, with limited capacity to add beneficiaries or use municipalities as first responders. [GEO-E-106]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP reports maps for seven hazards, vulnerability assessments and forecasting across 11 basins; the Emergency Management Service holds risk data, but public access awaits a protocol. [GEO-E-111]
- Source dated not stated reports: Emergency plans were adopted in six of 11 municipalities: Akhmeta, Gori, Kobuleti, Lagodekhi, Signagi and Telavi. Community plans cover 45 vulnerable western communities; measures are underway in 15 across Kintrishi, Natanebi and Supsa. [GEO-E-112]
- Source dated not stated reports: Flood protection was finalized at 12 of 15 sites; the largest GCF-funded site remains under construction. [GEO-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Conflict-related displacement and inadequate housing/services can magnify climate-hazard vulnerability in Georgia; no climate-caused conflict claim. [GEO-P-101; GEO-E-109]
  - Documented impact: Climate impacts threaten displaced households' safety and economic stability.
  - FCV mediator: Protracted conflict-related displacement, inadequate housing and limited services and livelihood opportunities increase vulnerability.
  - Possible consequence: Displaced households may sustain greater hazard-related harm and livelihood disruption; no new violent conflict is inferred.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2025-12 reports: SSP3-7.0 projects less summer rain in the Kura-Aras Basin by mid-century, raising water-shortage and hydrological-drought risks that could prompt water-use conflict. Scenario: SSP3-7.0, medium-term 2040-2059. [GEO-E-108]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: Climate impacts compound displacement linked to episodic conflict and violence. IDPs face inadequate housing, limited services and livelihoods, plus heightened flood, drought and resource risks; much of Georgia is listed among areas with many IDPs. [GEO-E-109]
