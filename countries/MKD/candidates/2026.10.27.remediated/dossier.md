Critical remaining gaps:
- Automated machine-screening is not independent source verification or human approval. 10 of 10 evidence records and 0 of 0 pathways lack claim-level verification in this remediation; review the linked records before relying on them. - The CCA and CCDR identify climate, disaster and social-cohesion risks but do not establish that climate hazards cause inter-ethnic tension, political violence or armed conflict. []
- The reviewed sources did not document violence or insecurity interrupting adaptation or disaster response; displacement appears as a function that disaster-risk and social-protection systems should address, not a measured climate displacement event. []
- The most detailed municipal rankings rely on 2020–2021 inputs; the 2020 CCA is pre-pandemic and provides few named southern municipalities. []
- UNDRR reports scorecard workshops and planning, but the article provides neither municipality-level scores nor verified investment or outcome changes. []
- The CCA's general disaster/GBV assertion lacks event, location and period disaggregation; no measured local violence increase or complete supported mediator chain is retained. []
- A qualitative climate/demographic-emigration projection without a stated period was excluded. No climate-to-interethnic-violence or reverse-FCV pathway is inferred. []
- Municipal indices use 2020–2021 inputs; hazard rankings are model-based, not observed losses or current household prevalence. []
- UNDRR scorecards and planned investments describe institutional processes, not published local scores or verified resilience gains. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-10 reports: High flood exposure: Aerodrom, Petrovec, Centar; wildfire: Makedonski Brod, Chashka; landslide: Centar Zhupa, Mavrovo i Rostushe, Debar and Tetovo. [MKD-E-102]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: CCA projects hotter, drier conditions and more extremes; southern areas face less water access and greater drought, wildfire and heat vulnerability. Scenario: To 2100; magnitude depends on greenhouse-gas concentration. [MKD-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Highest socioeconomic vulnerability ranks include Rankovce, Novaci, Staro Nagorichane, Pehchevo, Novo Selo, Plasnica, Debar, Gradsko, Berovo and Resen. [MKD-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Combined drought-to-crops, heat-to-labor and river-flood damages could reach 4% of GDP annually by 2050. Scenario: RCP 4.5; maize/wheat, labor-productivity and riverine-flood channels. [MKD-E-103]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: Small farmers face water stress, crop and fodder losses; women bear disproportionate burdens in resource management and family care. Scenario: Projections to 2100; yield impacts expected by 2050. [MKD-E-107]
- The 2020 CCA identifies women's resource, information and participation constraints and discusses disasters as potentially exacerbating gender-based violence. It supplies no local event, time series or disaster-disaggregated incidence, so this is protection-risk context, not a measured increase in North Macedonia. [MKD-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2024-07-30 reports: In 2024 Gazi Baba, Gjorce Petrov and Ohrid joined MCR 2030; scorecard workshops were used to establish resilience baselines and discuss local investment priorities. [MKD-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Assistance can scale after disasters, but climate response lacks warning triggers and contingency finance; DRM law does not recognize social-protection capacity for displaced households. [MKD-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Health-worker migration, weak climate-health data and limited staffing impede emergency response; out-of-pocket costs restrict poorer households’ access. [MKD-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-07-30 reports: UNDRR says the scorecard process identifies municipal resilience gaps and informs local strategies; it describes the national disaster-management system as established and expected to support municipal capacity. [MKD-E-112]
