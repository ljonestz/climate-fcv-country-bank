## Climate pressures and exposure

- Average annual mean temperature increased by about 1°C between 1960-1991 and 1991-2020. [PSE-E-001]
- Average rainfall declined slightly over roughly 50 years and became more volatile; the CCDR estimates about 65% of the West Bank at medium or high flood risk, especially in the northwest. [PSE-E-002]
- CCDR SSP projections indicate approximately +2°C by 2055 and +3°C by 2090, with more frequent/intense extremes and worsening drought, especially late-century and in southern areas. Scenario: 2055/2090; SSP scenarios [PSE-E-003]
- The 2023 CCDR reports scenario-dependent city rainfall changes: about +10 mm/year in Deir el Balah, Gaza City and Khan Yunis under SSP1 but about -10 mm under SSP2/SSP3; West Bank cities may lose more than 20 mm under higher scenarios. It also reports modelled recharge sensitivity of 14-24% loss for 10% lower rainfall and 28-50% for 15% lower rainfall, and identifies sea-level rise and saltwater intrusion as additional pressures on Gaza groundwater. Scenario: CCDR future SSP1/SSP2/SSP3 discussion in its 2055/2090 projection context; no separate date is stated for the city rainfall or recharge sensitivity figures. [PSE-E-004]
- The CCDR projects greater water-scarcity risk in Gaza and greater flood risk in eastern parts of the West Bank, demonstrating spatially different climate exposure. Scenario: CCDR section 1.2.2 future-risk discussion; Figure 3 does not state a separate projection date. [PSE-E-005]
- FAO's Jericho crop-mapping page describes warmer and more evaporative conditions affecting crop calendars and water management; internally inconsistent temperature ranges and survey years prevent retaining precise anomaly figures. [PSE-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Mid-century projections indicate very high warm-season maximum temperatures and growing hot-day exposure; the CCDR identifies outdoor workers, older people, young children and people with cardiovascular disease as especially vulnerable. Scenario: Mid-century; hot-day example under SSP3 [PSE-E-006]
- The May 2026 World Bank report estimates that more than 90% of Gaza electricity assets had been damaged and only one 4 MW feeder was operating, serving a desalination plant. [PSE-E-013]
- The April 2026 Gaza RDNA estimates US$35.2bn in physical damage, US$22.7bn in economic losses and US$71.4bn in recovery/reconstruction needs following October 2023–October 2025 conflict impacts. Its official executive-summary extract reports about US$1.7bn in water and sanitation damage. [PSE-E-022]
- UNICEF estimated in July 2026 that 82% of Gaza families were water insecure, up to 70% could not access six litres/person/day, and nearly 90% of WASH infrastructure had been damaged or destroyed. [PSE-E-023]
- In reporting through 30 April 2026, UNICEF estimated that more than 800,000 people in the West Bank required health and WASH assistance, with Area C/Jordan Valley particularly constrained by displacement, movement restrictions and settler violence; more than 150,000 children faced education barriers. [PSE-E-026]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Jericho mapping says Palestinian farmers had limited water access constraining crop choices, while Israeli settlers had greater water and technology access. [PSE-E-102]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The 2021 Updated NDC identifies 12 highly climate-vulnerable sectors and reports implementation action plans for six sectors, with further planning conditional on finance. [PSE-E-007]
- The NDC sets conditional water adaptation targets including wastewater reuse, rainwater capture, lower non-revenue water, rehabilitation of wells/springs and Gaza desalination capacity. [PSE-E-008]
- The same Gaza study characterized many coping practices as short-term and reactive and argued that resource, livelihood and institutional constraints impeded transition to longer-term adaptation. [PSE-E-016]
- IPC classified over 1.2m people, 59% of Gaza’s population, in Crisis or worse during mid-April-June 2026 and projected over 1.4m, 67%, for July-December; IPC states that large-scale assistance after the Oct 2025 ceasefire improved food-security conditions. Scenario: Apr-Dec 2026 [PSE-E-024]
- OCHA reported that West Bank WASH damage from demolitions and settler attacks was increasing reliance on emergency water trucking, especially among Bedouin and herding communities; about 160 WASH structures had been affected by settler attacks in 2026 by late July. [PSE-E-025]
- Source dated 2025-08-14 reports: After settlers repeatedly disconnected Umm al Kheir's main water line, 200 residents, including 108 children, lacked running water and relied on water trucking. [PSE-E-105]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The Updated NDC estimated total indicative implementation cost for 2021-2040 at about US$5.93bn and an identified funding gap of at least about US$2.74bn, while noting incomplete costing. [PSE-E-009]
- Before October 2023, the CCDR documented severe fiscal pressure, EQA staffing shortages, weak coordination and absence of systematic climate-fiscal risk identification/screening. [PSE-E-011]
- The May 2026 World Bank economic update reports that suspension of clearance revenues since May 2025, financing constraints, arrears and borrowing had left PA fiscal management focused on short-term crisis containment. [PSE-E-012]
- The 2025 PLOS scoping review identified interacting Gaza WASH political-economy constraints including restrictions/blockade, institutional fragmentation, internal political divisions, financing, planning, transparency and sustainability problems. [PSE-E-018]
- IEG rated the Gaza Water Supply & Sewage System Improvement project outcome Satisfactory; approximately 150,000 beneficiaries obtained more reliable water, but overall water quality improved only marginally, and the wastewater BOD-reduction target was not fully reached. [PSE-E-019]
- The IEG review recorded access-related supervision constraints and a misprocurement episode; it reported no significant unintended project impacts and rated ICR quality modest. [PSE-E-021]
- Source dated not stated reports: Movement and transport restrictions linked to the security situation constrained field access and sampling; ARIJ adapted sampling and used satellite analysis to validate and extend observations. [PSE-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-08-14 reports: The WASH Cluster and Palestinian Water Authority used water trucking, mobile tanks and maintenance kits, which partly alleviated shortages, while communities continued requesting water. [PSE-E-106]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO's 2020–2025 project proposed stronger climate-resilient agriculture coordination, real-time agro-weather information for smallholders and two test plots with farmer associations. [PSE-E-107]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The Updated NDC states that restrictions on land/resources, movement, trade, borders and infrastructure reduce Palestinian adaptive capacity, especially in Area C and Gaza. [PSE-E-010]
- West Bank electricity imports from Israel declined from 97% to 88% between 2019 and 2025 as solar capacity expanded, although the World Bank estimates substantial further generation/network investment is needed for scale-up. [PSE-E-014]
- Gaza stakeholders consulted in 2008-2009 identified rainfall variability, heat and sea-level-related water/food pathways, but regarded conflict-related restrictions and recurrent violence as overwhelming or compounding forecast climate risks. [PSE-E-015]
- Mason et al. (2012) argued from consultations and case evidence that movement/access restrictions and damage or threats to agricultural and water assets constrained some West Bank coping and adaptation options. [PSE-E-017]
- IEG found that monitoring systems and rapid damage assessment after the 2014 conflict supported project redesign, additional financing and rehabilitation, but rated risk to development outcome Very High. [PSE-E-020]
- Taken together, the pre-2023 climate plans and inspected 2026 reports on infrastructure destruction, energy and water-service disruption, access constraints and fiscal pressure imply reduced practical capacity to implement those plans and respond to future hazards. This is a conditional synthesis of dated records, not a measured October 2026 adaptation-capacity estimate. [PSE-E-027]
- Projected heat, drought, groundwater-recharge and flood pressures could compound livelihood, health, food and water insecurity where damaged infrastructure, market/access restrictions and weak fiscal/institutional capacity limit coping; the evidence reviewed does not demonstrate that these climate pressures cause violence. [PSE-E-028]
- Source dated 2025-08-14 reports: OCHA reports heatwaves and low rainfall alongside pipeline reductions, permit limits, infrastructure demolitions and settler violence in West Bank water shortages; 52 communities faced access obstacles and 73 relied on trucking without official networks. [PSE-E-104]
