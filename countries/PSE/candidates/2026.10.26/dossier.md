# West Bank and Gaza Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Physical climate risks differ across Gaza and the West Bank. The 2023 CCDR supports historical warming and projected heat, water, flood and coastal pressures, but its pre-October-2023 exposure baseline is not a current map of Gaza. These pressures do not establish deterministic FCV outcomes. [PSE-E-001, PSE-E-002, PSE-E-003, PSE-E-004, PSE-E-005, PSE-E-006, PSE-P-001, PSE-P-002, PSE-P-003]
- Inspected 2026 fiscal and humanitarian reporting supports a prospective FCV-to-climate mechanism: damage, power and water failures, access constraints and fiscal stress can reduce the systems available for adaptation. Gaza and particular West Bank rural/herding communities face distinct constraints. The incremental loss of climate resilience is inferred rather than quantified. [PSE-E-012, PSE-E-013, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-025, PSE-E-026, PSE-E-027, PSE-P-004, PSE-P-005, PSE-P-006]
- The 2021 NDC records planned measures rather than verified implementation. Historical Gaza WASH evaluation and 2019–2025 West Bank solar expansion show specific delivery experience, with important limits. Neither the continuation of pre-2023 capabilities nor evaluated present-day climate or peace outcomes is established by this candidate. [PSE-E-007, PSE-E-008, PSE-E-009, PSE-E-010, PSE-E-011, PSE-E-014, PSE-E-019, PSE-E-020, PSE-E-021, PSE-E-024, PSE-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Post-2023 exposure mapping: this candidate combines a pre-conflict climate baseline with later damage and service records. It does not supply a comprehensive climate-risk overlay for Gaza's current population, displacement sites, drainage and service network. [PSE-E-002, PSE-E-005, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-027]
- Observation systems: the inspected climate and damage records do not establish the post-2023 condition of meteorological/hydrological networks or quantify resulting observational gaps. [PSE-E-001, PSE-E-003, PSE-E-022]
- NDC implementation: the 2021 targets include milestones that have passed, but this candidate does not verify completion, cancellation or redesign across the portfolio. [PSE-E-007, PSE-E-008, PSE-E-009, PSE-E-027]
- Independent adaptation evaluation: the IEG project review is historical and concerns WASH recovery, not a contemporary NDC/NAP or climate-resilient reconstruction evaluation. [PSE-E-019, PSE-E-020, PSE-E-021]
- Maladaptation and unintended effects: the NDC discusses maladaptation and the historical IEG review reports no significant unintended project impacts identified. Neither is a current empirical distributional evaluation of adaptation measures. [PSE-E-008, PSE-E-021]
- Differentiated vulnerability: heat-sensitive groups and humanitarian service needs are identified, but current climate-specific risk is not consistently disaggregated by gender, disability, displacement status or occupation. [PSE-E-006, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-026, PSE-E-028]
- Climate-to-FCV causality: the direct qualitative studies date from 2011–2012. The later sources do not establish climate as a cause of current violence or displacement. [PSE-E-015, PSE-E-016, PSE-E-017, PSE-E-028, PSE-P-001, PSE-P-002, PSE-P-003, PSE-P-008]
- FCV-to-climate quantification: restrictions, damage and fiscal stress support a plausible loss-of-capacity mechanism, but its contribution to expected climate damages is not measured here. [PSE-E-010, PSE-E-011, PSE-E-012, PSE-E-013, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-025, PSE-E-027, PSE-P-004, PSE-P-005, PSE-P-006]
- Peace outcomes: historical resilience and recovery experience is not evidence that climate programming produced measurable peace, violence-reduction or trust outcomes. [PSE-E-019, PSE-E-020, PSE-E-021, PSE-E-024, PSE-P-008]
- Geographic representation: detailed humanitarian records for Gaza and parts of Area C/Jordan Valley do not amount to even coverage of local institutions across all West Bank communities. [PSE-E-023, PSE-E-025, PSE-E-026]
- RDNA access and verification: the full PDF could not be retrieved. The retained figures are supported by its official continuous executive-summary extract; the WASH-needs table figure is excluded. [PSE-E-022]
- Environmental versus climate harm: damaged WASH systems and unsafe water indicate service and environmental vulnerability. They must not be relabelled as climate impacts without attribution evidence. [PSE-E-013, PSE-E-018, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-028, PSE-P-003, PSE-P-004]
- Class coverage is uneven: the candidate contains historical resilience experience but no evaluated peace outcome. Contemporary direct climate–FCV mechanisms remain partly inferential. [PSE-E-015, PSE-E-017, PSE-E-019, PSE-E-020, PSE-E-027, PSE-E-028, PSE-P-001, PSE-P-002, PSE-P-003, PSE-P-004, PSE-P-005, PSE-P-006, PSE-P-008]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Historical warming of ~1°C. [PSE-E-001]
- The 2023 CCDR reports volatile rainfall and modelled West Bank flood exposure. [PSE-E-002]
- CCDR projects roughly +2°C by 2055 and +3°C by 2090, with scenario uncertainty. [PSE-E-003]
- Rainfall/recharge changes are scenario-sensitive. [PSE-E-004]
- Gaza water risk and eastern WB flood risk differ. [PSE-E-005]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Mid-century heat projections identify outdoor workers, older people, young children and cardiovascular patients as vulnerable. [PSE-E-006]
- In the May 2026 update, Gaza's damaged electricity system left one 4 MW desalination feeder operational. [PSE-E-013]
- The April 2026 RDNA estimates extensive conflict damage and future recovery needs, not climate damage. [PSE-E-022]
- UNICEF's July 2026 update estimated severe water insecurity and WASH damage in Gaza. [PSE-E-023]
- UNICEF's 30 April 2026 reporting highlights Area C/Jordan Valley service constraints. [PSE-E-026]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The 2021 NDC documented adaptation planning, not completed outcomes. [PSE-E-007]
- The 2021 NDC set conditional water targets; completion is unverified. [PSE-E-008]
- The 2011 study described short-term enforced coping, not verified present-day adaptation capacity. [PSE-E-016]
- IPC's 23 July 2026 analysis classified April–June outcomes and projected July–December food insecurity; assistance-related improvement is attributed by IPC. [PSE-E-024]
- OCHA's July 2026 report describes water-infrastructure damage and trucking dependence in West Bank herding communities. [PSE-E-025]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The 2021 NDC costed planned actions through 2040; this is not a current financing-gap measurement. [PSE-E-009]
- The pre-October-2023 CCDR documented staffing, coordination and climate-fiscal screening gaps. [PSE-E-011]
- The May 2026 economic update described fiscal crisis containment after revenue suspension. [PSE-E-012]
- A 2025 scoping review synthesizes political-economy constraints on Gaza WASH services. [PSE-E-018]
- The 2018 IEG review found historical service improvements but only marginal water-quality gains. [PSE-E-019]
- The 2018 IEG review records supervision limits, misprocurement and modest ICR quality. [PSE-E-021]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heat and drought may increase water/food stress, but FCV consequences depend on access, infrastructure, institutions and markets rather than following automatically from climate. [PSE-P-001; PSE-E-003, PSE-E-004, PSE-E-006, PSE-E-011, PSE-E-018, PSE-E-028]
  - Documented impact: Greater water stress, heat exposure and potential agricultural pressure.
  - FCV mediator: Water-access arrangements, infrastructure condition, fiscal constraints, movement restrictions, markets and institutional capacity.
  - Possible consequence: Could deepen food, livelihood or service insecurity where households and institutions cannot substitute water, imports, income or services; violence is not demonstrated as an outcome.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The 2021 NDC attributes reduced adaptive capacity to restrictions, especially in Area C and Gaza. [PSE-E-010]
- The May 2026 report links a 2019–2025 decline in West Bank import reliance to solar expansion. [PSE-E-014]
- Gaza City consultations in 2008–2009 mapped conflict-compounded water and food vulnerability. [PSE-E-015]
- The 2012 study documented locality-specific West Bank restrictions on coping and adaptation. [PSE-E-017]
- The 2018 IEG review documented adaptive project management after 2014 conflict, with very high sustainability risk. [PSE-E-020]
- Dated 2026 conflict/service reports imply constraints on climate-plan implementation; the effect is not quantified. [PSE-E-027]
- Projected climate pressures may compound fragility through service and livelihood pathways; no climate-caused violence is demonstrated. [PSE-E-028]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- Keep Gaza and the West Bank as separate dated vulnerability baselines. The 2026 Gaza records concern destroyed services and infrastructure; the West Bank records identify Area C/Jordan Valley and particular herding communities. They are not one nationally uniform climate-impact estimate. [PSE-E-013, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-025, PSE-E-026, PSE-P-004, PSE-P-005]
- Use CCDR physical projections with their scenarios and periods. Its pre-October-2023 exposure and socioeconomic baseline is not a current Gaza baseline. [PSE-E-001, PSE-E-002, PSE-E-003, PSE-E-004, PSE-E-005, PSE-E-006, PSE-E-011]
- The 2008–2009 Gaza City consultations and the 2012 West Bank locality discussion are foundational qualitative evidence. They do not represent contemporary national conditions or a causal estimate. [PSE-E-015, PSE-E-016, PSE-E-017]
- Dated West Bank humanitarian sources concentrate on Area C/Jordan Valley and rural/herding water-access constraints. The candidate does not provide equally detailed climate-risk evidence for every West Bank locality. [PSE-E-025, PSE-E-026, PSE-P-005]
- The RDNA is an April 2026 damage/recovery estimate with access and remote-assessment limits. Only its official continuous executive-summary extract, not the underlying PDF tables, was accessible in this review. [PSE-E-022]
- UNICEF's West Bank reporting includes East Jerusalem, but this candidate does not contain a separate East Jerusalem climate-risk assessment. [PSE-E-026, PSE-P-005]
- The records identify vulnerable workers, children and service-dependent populations, but do not quantify current climate risk at displacement-site, refugee-camp or disability-disaggregated level. [PSE-E-006, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-026, PSE-E-028]
- Water/WASH, energy, food security, climate planning, fiscal capacity and service delivery dominate this candidate. Heat-sensitive groups and agriculture are represented, but detailed climate-health functions and post-conflict urban exposure are not quantified. Coastal and groundwater pressures are distinguished from conflict-related damage. Evidence on ecosystem outcomes, fisheries, waste, transport and private-sector adaptation is thinner, and no evaluated peacebuilding outcome is supplied. [PSE-E-003, PSE-E-004, PSE-E-006, PSE-E-007, PSE-E-008, PSE-E-009, PSE-E-011, PSE-E-012, PSE-E-013, PSE-E-014, PSE-E-018, PSE-E-019, PSE-E-020, PSE-E-021, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-024, PSE-E-025, PSE-P-001, PSE-P-002, PSE-P-003, PSE-P-004, PSE-P-005, PSE-P-006, PSE-P-008]
