# West Bank and Gaza Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Physical climate risk is substantial but geographically differentiated, and it should not be translated into deterministic FCV claims. Warming, drought, recharge, flood and coastal pressures are supported by credible climate evidence, with different patterns in Gaza and the West Bank. [PSE-E-001, PSE-E-002, PSE-E-003, PSE-E-004, PSE-E-005, PSE-E-006]
- The most strongly supported current Climate-FCV direction is FCV reducing adaptation, resilience and service-delivery capacity. In Gaza this is associated with extraordinary infrastructure destruction, displacement, energy/WASH failure and access constraints; in parts of the West Bank, documented water-infrastructure damage and movement/access constraints affect particular rural and herding communities. [PSE-E-012, PSE-E-013, PSE-E-022, PSE-E-023, PSE-E-024, PSE-E-025, PSE-E-026, PSE-E-027]
- Climate-policy and resilience capacities exist, but current effectiveness is weakly evaluated. The NDC/NAP architecture, West Bank solar expansion and historical Gaza WASH recovery experience demonstrate real capacity, while finance, staffing, access and conflict damage materially limit implementation. [PSE-E-007, PSE-E-008, PSE-E-009, PSE-E-010, PSE-E-011, PSE-E-014, PSE-E-019, PSE-E-020, PSE-E-021]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Post-2023 climate-exposure mapping: No verified source located provides a comprehensive climate-risk overlay using Gaza’s current population distribution, displacement sites, destroyed infrastructure, debris, drainage and service network. []
- Current climate observations: The packet does not establish whether conflict damage has degraded meteorological/hydrological monitoring or how observational gaps affect post-2023 baselines. []
- NDC/NAP implementation status: The 2021 NDC contains time-bound targets - some now past their stated milestone - but current completion, cancellation, destruction or redesign could not be verified across the portfolio. []
- Independent adaptation evaluation: No recent independent evaluation comparable to the IEG WASH review was located for NAP/NDC climate adaptation, current resilience programming or climate-resilient reconstruction. []
- Unintended effects and maladaptation: The NDC recognizes maladaptation conceptually, but empirical evidence of who gained, lost or was excluded from specific adaptation measures is weak. []
- Differentiated vulnerability: Women, persons with disabilities, displaced populations, refugees, informal workers, farmers/herders and children appear in different sources, but current climate-specific differential risk is not consistently quantified. []
- Climate-to-FCV causality: The most direct studies located are from 2011-2012 and primarily qualitative. Current sources demonstrate severe fragility and climate hazards separately but do not establish climate as a cause of violence, displacement or political grievance. []
- FCV-to-climate quantification: Evidence that conflict, infrastructure destruction, access restrictions and fiscal crisis reduce adaptive capacity is stronger, but no source located quantifies the resulting loss of climate resilience or increase in expected climate damages. []
- Peace-supporting outcomes: Resilience capacities are documented, but no credible evaluation located shows that climate/environmental programming produced measurable peacebuilding, violence-reduction or trust-building outcomes. []
- Geographic representation: Area C/Jordan Valley and Gaza are relatively visible in humanitarian sources; other West Bank localities and many community-level institutional systems are thinly documented. []
- RDNA verification limits: Current Gaza damage estimates are necessarily constrained by physical access and require later sector-level ground verification. []
- Environmental contamination: Conflict-related debris, sewage, pollution and contamination are major current environmental problems but have deliberately not been relabelled as climate impacts. []
- *None is wholly unsupported at class level. However, the resilience-peace-capacity class is supported only on its resilience component; this packet found no credible evaluated peace-supporting outcome. Direct-climate-fcv coverage is also only partial, because the contemporary causal literature is insufficient. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Historical warming of ~1°C. [PSE-E-001]
- More variable rain and substantial WB flood exposure. [PSE-E-002]
- Strong additional warming and drought risk. [PSE-E-003]
- Rainfall/recharge changes are scenario-sensitive. [PSE-E-004]
- Gaza water risk and eastern WB flood risk differ. [PSE-E-005]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Heat exposure is differentiated by occupation and health. [PSE-E-006]
- Gaza energy-system destruction constrains WASH and recovery. [PSE-E-013]
- Conflict has radically altered Gaza’s asset/service baseline. [PSE-E-022]
- Severe current Gaza water vulnerability is conflict/access driven. [PSE-E-023]
- Vulnerability is geographically and demographically differentiated in the West Bank. [PSE-E-026]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Climate planning covers 12 sectors, unevenly operationalized. [PSE-E-007]
- Water adaptation measures were formally planned. [PSE-E-008]
- Short-term coping may not build adaptive capacity. [PSE-E-016]
- Assistance is an important current coping buffer, but food insecurity remains severe. [PSE-E-024]
- Conflict/access pressures reduce water-service resilience in parts of the West Bank. [PSE-E-025]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Pre-conflict climate-finance gap was already large. [PSE-E-009]
- Climate institutions had material implementation constraints pre-2023. [PSE-E-011]
- Fiscal crisis narrows capacity for longer-term investment. [PSE-E-012]
- Gaza WASH vulnerability has multiple institutional mediators. [PSE-E-018]
- Historical WASH project achieved mixed but positive results. [PSE-E-019]
- Evaluation found fiduciary and evidence-quality limitations. [PSE-E-021]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heat and drought may increase water/food stress, but FCV consequences depend on access, infrastructure, institutions and markets rather than following automatically from climate. [PSE-P-001; PSE-E-003, PSE-E-004, PSE-E-006, PSE-E-011, PSE-E-018, PSE-E-028]
  - Documented impact: Greater water stress, heat exposure and potential agricultural pressure.
  - FCV mediator: Water-access arrangements, infrastructure condition, fiscal constraints, movement restrictions, markets and institutional capacity.
  - Possible consequence: Could deepen food, livelihood or service insecurity where households and institutions cannot substitute water, imports, income or services; violence is not demonstrated as an outcome.

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- Gaza and the West Bank must not be combined into a single contemporary vulnerability baseline. Gaza’s population, housing, energy, WASH and economic geography has been transformed by conflict and displacement, while the West Bank evidence identifies distinct pressures related to Area C, the Jordan Valley, refugee camps, rural/herding communities and urban/service systems. (The United Nations in Palestine2) []
- The CCDR’s climate projections remain useful for physical hazard direction, but its pre-October-2023 exposure and socioeconomic baseline for Gaza should not be treated as current. []
- Mason et al.’s Gaza stakeholder findings and West Bank locality cases are explicitly local/foundational evidence and are not generalized nationally. []
- Current West Bank humanitarian evidence is strongest for Area C/Jordan Valley, Bedouin/herding communities and specific governorates affected by demolitions, violence and movement restrictions; other West Bank populations are less represented. []
- Current Gaza humanitarian monitoring is extensive but physical access and verification constraints remain important; the RDNA explicitly relied substantially on remote assessment. []
- East Jerusalem appears in humanitarian West Bank reporting but is thinly covered in the climate-specific evidence used here. []
- Refugee camps, informal/displacement sites, people with disabilities, women-headed households and occupation-specific heat exposure require finer current climate-risk analysis. []
- Coverage is strongest for water/WASH, energy, food security, agriculture, public finance and basic-service delivery. Health and urban infrastructure are covered sufficiently to identify vulnerability but not to quantify detailed climate-health or urban-risk functions. Coastal and groundwater issues in Gaza are documented in climate plans and projections, although current conflict-related environmental damage must remain analytically separate. Evidence on ecosystems, biodiversity, fisheries, waste, transport, private-sector adaptation and climate-sensitive education infrastructure is comparatively thin. The report register does not support a robust assessment of peacebuilding-sector outcomes. []
