# Central African Republic Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Climate pressure is significant, but CAR's strongest climate signal is warming and increasing heavy-rainfall risk rather than an unambiguous national trend toward worsening drought. [CAF-E-001, CAF-E-002, CAF-E-003, CAF-E-004, CAF-P-001, CAF-P-002, CAF-P-005]
- The most credible Climate-FCV pathways operate through food systems, pastoral mobility, markets, access and institutions; the evidence does not justify describing climate as a primary or automatic cause of CAR's violence. [CAF-E-010, CAF-E-011, CAF-E-012, CAF-E-013, CAF-E-014, CAF-E-015, CAF-E-016, CAF-E-017, CAF-E-018, CAF-P-001, CAF-P-002, CAF-P-003, CAF-P-004, CAF-P-005, CAF-P-008]
- FCV conditions materially constrain resilience and delivery, while recent policy ambition exceeds demonstrated implementation capacity; historical independent evaluation shows that programme design and weak systems can generate both underperformance and unintended exclusion. [CAF-E-006, CAF-E-007, CAF-E-008, CAF-E-019, CAF-E-020, CAF-E-021, CAF-E-022, CAF-P-006, CAF-P-007]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: CAR-specific, high-resolution projections by prefecture/ecological zone are limited in the verified packet. Future drought is particularly uncertain; regional evidence should not be converted into a national claim of progressively worsening drought. []
- Observation and climate-information systems: The BTR itself reports insufficient climate-risk research, data, equipment and human capacity, while NDC 3.0 describes water-information systems that were not yet operational at the 2025 baseline. (UNFCCC) []
- Geographic coverage: Birao, selected pastoral corridors and older IEG field sites provide useful local evidence, but substantial areas and populations remain poorly observed. No finding from those locations should be generalized nationally. (UNHCR) []
- Differentiated vulnerability: National policy recognizes women, youth, persons with disabilities, displaced people and Indigenous peoples, but verified outcome evidence disaggregating climate impacts and adaptation performance across these groups is limited. (UNFCCC) []
- Causal uncertainty: The reviewed climate-security specialist source explicitly states that CAR-specific evidence is insufficient. Pastoral mobility is a credible mechanism, but climate, regional insecurity, armed groups, land governance and historical relations remain intertwined. (NUPI) []
- FCV-to-climate evidence strength: There is good evidence that conflict weakens services, transport, institutions and local regulation, but fewer studies directly estimate how much those FCV conditions increase climate losses or reduce adaptation effectiveness. []
- Current evaluation: The strongest independent evaluation is from 2018 and concerns a 2014-2016 operation. Recent World Bank and IFAD material is largely project supervision, design or self-report, so current programme effectiveness and unintended effects remain incompletely evaluated. (World Bank) []
- Resilience/peace mechanisms: Local route regulation, compensation and reciprocal farmer-pastoralist relations are plausible and documented qualitatively, but no rigorous evaluation was found establishing their effect on violence or climate resilience. (ICRC) []
- Mining and natural-resource governance: Mining and armed-group political economy are important independent FCV drivers, but this packet did not identify sufficiently strong verified evidence to characterize mining impacts themselves as climate-related or to establish a climate -> mining -> violence pathway. []
- Access limitations: Current OCHA/ReliefWeb and Crisis Group material encountered during research could not be opened to the level required by the source-verification rule and was therefore excluded rather than cited from search snippets. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Rapid observed warming since 1970s. [CAF-E-001]
- Substantial further warming by ~2050. [CAF-E-002]
- Heavier rainfall likely raises flood risk. [CAF-E-003]
- Current drought hazard; future trend uncertain. [CAF-E-004]
- CAR combines internal and cross-border displacement. [CAF-E-013]
- Refugee inflow sharply increased Birao service demand. [CAF-E-014]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- National policy recognizes differentiated vulnerability. [CAF-E-009]
- Nearly one-third faces acute food insecurity. [CAF-E-010]
- Food insecurity has major non-climate drivers. [CAF-E-012]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NAP is multi-sectoral. [CAF-E-005]
- Rainfall and production can improve food outcomes. [CAF-E-011]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Climate-risk evidence base remains weak. [CAF-E-006]
- Core adaptation institutions face implementation constraints. [CAF-E-007]
- Water planning/monitoring starts from major gaps. [CAF-E-008]
- Large programme reach was reported. [CAF-E-019]
- Some output targets remained incomplete. [CAF-E-020]
- Earlier emergency agriculture response performed poorly. [CAF-E-021]
- Delivery design can exclude vulnerable households. [CAF-E-022]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate shocks can affect food production, but whether this produces severe food insecurity in CAR is strongly mediated by conflict, markets, roads, poverty and assistance. (IPCinfo) [CAF-P-001; CAF-E-001, CAF-E-003, CAF-E-004, CAF-E-010, CAF-E-011, CAF-E-012]
  - Documented impact: Agricultural production and food availability are sensitive to rainfall and climate shocks.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict, displacement, degraded roads, prices and weak purchasing power shape whether production changes translate into household food access.
  - Possible consequence: Climate-related production losses could worsen acute food insecurity where conflict and market-access constraints limit coping.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed conflict', 'displacement', 'food/fuel prices', 'poverty', 'road conditions', 'humanitarian assistance', 'crop-input availability']
  - Uncertainty: IPC does not quantify a separate climate causal effect; future drought direction is uncertain.
- Climate-related mobility pressure can matter, but governance of routes and resources - and armed insecurity - determines whether mobility becomes cooperative, disruptive or violent. (NUPI) [CAF-P-002; CAF-E-004, CAF-E-016, CAF-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Changes in timing or geography of pastoral movements.
  - FCV mediator: Weak cross-border regulation, land/water access rules, armed-group involvement and local dispute-management capacity.
  - Possible consequence: Altered livestock movement may increase the likelihood of localized farmer-herder tensions where regulation and negotiated access are weak.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed-group activity', 'weapons', 'illegal taxation', 'historical land disputes', 'route governance', 'livestock economics and regional insecurity']
  - Uncertainty: NUPI explicitly identifies a lack of robust CAR-specific climate-security monitoring.
- Conflict can reduce the institutional capacity needed to manage ordinary and climate-amplified pastoral resource pressures. (NUPI) [CAF-P-003; CAF-E-004, CAF-E-017, CAF-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Pastoralists require mobility and negotiated resource access.
  - FCV mediator: Armed violence and state withdrawal can remove route controls, compensation procedures and dispute-resolution mechanisms.
  - Possible consequence: Climate and seasonal resource stress may become harder to manage peacefully when conflict erodes institutions that previously organized mobility.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Historical farmer-herder relations', 'land tenure', 'armed-group economics', 'local political competition']
  - Uncertainty: Practitioner evidence is not a nationally representative causal study.
- Conflict-driven population shocks can consume scarce service capacity and plausibly reduce resilience to subsequent climate shocks, but the combined effect has not been directly evaluated. [CAF-P-004; CAF-E-013, CAF-E-014]
  - Documented impact: CAR has large displaced populations; Birao experienced a particularly sharp refugee influx from the Sudan conflict.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict-driven displacement increases demand for water, health, shelter, education and livelihoods in low-capacity locations.
  - Possible consequence: Host areas with large service deficits may have less capacity to absorb an additional flood, drought or food-production shock.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Chronic underinvestment', 'remoteness', 'poverty', 'logistics and humanitarian financing shortfalls']
  - Uncertainty: No reviewed study estimates the marginal effect of displacement on climate-disaster losses in Birao.
- Conflict-damaged access and weak purchasing power can magnify the household consequences of climate-sensitive food-production changes. (IPCinfo) [CAF-P-005; CAF-E-010, CAF-E-011, CAF-E-012]
  - Documented impact: Agricultural output and food availability vary with rainfall and other shocks.
  - FCV mediator: Persistent conflict, displacement and degraded roads restrict market and humanitarian access; high prices and low purchasing power further constrain households.
  - Possible consequence: A localized climate-related production shock could have larger food-security effects where conflict has already impaired transport and markets.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Macroeconomic conditions', 'fuel prices', 'poverty', 'assistance coverage and local harvest conditions']
  - Uncertainty: Relative contributions of climate, conflict and market variables are not causally decomposed.
- Fragile institutional capacity constrains the information systems required to anticipate and manage climate risks, although the specific contribution of conflict is not quantified. (UNFCCC) [CAF-P-006; CAF-E-001, CAF-E-004, CAF-E-006, CAF-E-007, CAF-E-008, CAF-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Effective adaptation requires observations, risk analysis and water/hydrometeorological information.
  - FCV mediator: Weak institutions, limited staff/equipment, fragmented planning and broader fragility constrain data generation and use.
  - Possible consequence: Poor climate information can reduce the timeliness and targeting of adaptation and disaster-management decisions.

- Independent evaluation demonstrates that delivery rules in a fragile environment can themselves create resilience gaps by excluding vulnerable non-members. [CAF-P-007; CAF-E-021, CAF-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Emergency agricultural assistance is intended to protect or restore livelihoods.
  - FCV mediator: Weak administrative systems and targeting through formal organizations can shape who receives assistance.
  - Possible consequence: Programme design can unintentionally leave highly vulnerable households with less support and therefore lower recovery capacity.

- CAR evidence contains not only risk pathways but also peace-supporting institutions that can mediate resource variability without violence. (ICRC) [CAF-P-008; CAF-E-004, CAF-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Pastoral mobility creates recurring needs for negotiated access to land and water.
  - FCV mediator: Local agreements, compensation, regulated routes and farmer-pastoralist exchange can reduce disputes.
  - Possible consequence: Where legitimate local governance functions, resource variability may be managed through negotiation rather than escalation.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- CAR-specific climate-security evidence is thin. [CAF-E-015]
- Climate and insecurity can reshape pastoral mobility. [CAF-E-016]
- Armed control can turn mobility into a conflict risk. [CAF-E-017]
- Governance institutions can reduce or amplify farmer-herder risk. [CAF-E-018]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National climate evidence should not be treated as spatially uniform. The reviewed climate synthesis identifies northern CAR as relatively more drought-prone, while riverine flood exposure occurs across multiple parts of the country and recent flood experience includes Bangui and southwestern areas. The physical projections themselves often draw on wider regional models, making local confidence lower than national trend confidence. []
- *Birao/Vakaga is a specific displacement/service-delivery case: the rapid influx of refugees from Sudan is documented there, but it cannot be treated as representative of Bangui, western CAR or southern forest areas. (UNHCR) []
- Pastoral/transhumance findings apply most directly to livestock corridors and rural zones where farmer-pastoralist interaction occurs. They should not be generalized to all rural communities or used to characterize all CAR violence. (NUPI) []
- The IEG evaluation included field observation in M’Baïki, Bossembélé and Boali. Its localized implementation findings are analytically useful but do not establish nationwide conditions. []
- A major unrepresented geography is territory where insecurity and limited state/research access impede direct observation. The BTR's acknowledgement of weak regionalized climate analysis reinforces this concern. (UNFCCC) []
- *Relatively well covered: agriculture and food security; pastoralism/transhumance; water planning; humanitarian/basic-service delivery; roads/market access; climate information; adaptation institutions. []
- *Partially covered: health, forests and ecosystems, urban drainage/flood resilience, gender and differentiated vulnerability. []
- *Thin or absent: detailed mining-climate-FCV pathways; fisheries; hydropower performance; quantified forest-conflict-climate interactions; education resilience beyond humanitarian service pressure; systematic household adaptation outcomes; private-sector adaptation. []
