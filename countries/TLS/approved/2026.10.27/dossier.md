## Climate pressures and exposure

- The July 2026 feature identifies southern and eastern areas as facing moderate-to-severe drought risk and associated water and livelihood pressure. [TLS-E-001]
- Source dated 2024-02 reports: In RAEOA, wet-season landslides in Bene-Ufe damaged roads, settlements and farms; flooding and landslides displaced 280 families around Nibin, Usitaqueno, in 2021. [TLS-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-08 reports: The WASH climate assessment says all municipalities face flood exposure; the largest numbers exposed to extreme floods are in Bobonaro and Dili, followed by Ainaro and Baucau. [TLS-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- UNDP's cited historical assessment reports crop and livestock losses and water scarcity during the 2023–2024 El Niño drought. [TLS-E-002]
- FAO identifies smallholders' dependence on rainfed production as a sensitivity to dry spells and changing rainfall. [TLS-E-003]
- Source dated 2024-02 reports: Participants reported rainy-season landslides and floods destroying farms in Saburai, Tapo-Memo, Gildapil and Lebos (Bobonaro); Gildapil participants described hunger after maize loss. [TLS-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-02 reports: The report describes disputes over water, land and livestock; at Tapo-Memo, unequal irrigation access to the shared Malibaka River raised concern about possible future water conflict. [TLS-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-08 reports: The assessment expects drought and extreme rainfall to worsen water insecurity, heighten competition and lengthen women’s trips to collect water, with unsafe sources increasing disease risk. Scenario: Qualitative drought and extreme-rainfall risk narrative; no emissions scenario specified. [TLS-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The ASIS portal and national drought tool were launched with user training to support hotspot detection and anticipatory decisions. [TLS-E-004]
- Source dated 2024-02 reports: Communities reportedly protected 80 water resources in RAEOA; Usi Taqueno residents fenced and cleaned springs, while Saburai protected water distribution through tara bandu. [TLS-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-07-04 reports: UNICEF reports community catchment-management expansion to 35 additional sub-villages in Ainaro and Viqueque, implemented by 70 trained residents and nearly 350 other community members, including women and youth. [TLS-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-03-27 reports: Sixteen early-warning devices were handed to DNMG; disaster committees were supported in Dili sucos. The article says 15 PREPARADU sucos but lists 16, including six Maloa sites: Villa Verde, Motael, Colmera, Campo Alor, Bairo Pite and Dare. [TLS-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The project integrates resilient rural infrastructure, catchment management and risk information into village and municipal planning. [TLS-E-005]
- UNDP reports roads, irrigation and water-supply works across six target municipalities, plus local-company employment and reforestation. [TLS-E-006]
- Source dated 2025-07-04 reports: The 532 built nature-based structures (45 ponds, 313 terraces and 174 check dams) were reported to retain at least 12,567 m³ in one rainfall event; dry-season replenishment is expected, not measured. [TLS-E-109]
- The June 2024 EU/Mercy Corps close-out account reports 537 households with improved shock-preparation capacity, 1,284 people with climate-resilience knowledge and 68 mitigation/adaptation works in the Maloa watershed. [TLS-E-111]
