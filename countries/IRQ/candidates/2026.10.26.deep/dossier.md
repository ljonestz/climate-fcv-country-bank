# Iraq Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- New February2026fieldwork adds a local mechanism in Nimrud and Hatra: water stress and farming losses interact with irrigation/land competition and exclusion. These are participants' reported dynamics, not a representative causal estimate. [IRQ-E-110, IRQ-E-111, IRQ-P-101]
- Evidence on return agriculture and displacement-site water access is dated2022and2024. It documents economic and conflict-recovery barriers alongside climate stress, with local mediation capacities that can exclude less-connected groups. [IRQ-E-102, IRQ-E-105, IRQ-E-106, IRQ-E-112]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate-projection gap. The official SNC/BUR file could not be opened beyond its UNFCCC catalogue record during this review, so its detailed temperature, precipitation and yield projections are deferred. Verified evidence remains insufficient to quantify future drought, flood, dust-storm and salinity risk consistently across all governorates. []
- Attribution gap. Water scarcity reflects climate, structural aridity, upstream management, demand growth, domestic allocation, pollution, infrastructure and operational choices. The evidence does not provide a defensible percentage attribution to each driver. []
- Climate-FCV causal gap. Direct violence examples exist, but most climate-security literature is qualitative, correlational or pathway-based. It cannot establish a national marginal causal effect of climate stress on violence. []
- Geographic/population gap. Local studies cover a small subset of districts and governorates. Conflict-affected or hard-to-access areas, informal settlements, PWDs and several minority populations remain weakly represented. []
- Differentiated-vulnerability gap. National policy clearly identifies women, youth, rural populations and persons with disabilities, but comparative quantitative evidence measuring their different climate losses, coping resources and adaptation outcomes is insufficient. []
- Evaluation and unintended-effect gap. Formal adaptation investment is expanding, but major programmes generally provide theories of change, beneficiary targets or planned outputs rather than independently evaluated climate-resilience outcomes. Existing independent evaluations concern broader UNDP/WFP portfolios. (The United Nations in Iraq) []
- FCV-to-climate evidence gap. Armed-actor influence, conflict legacies and weak governance are credibly linked to poorer resource management and service delivery, but the magnitude of their effect on adaptation outcomes is rarely measured. []
- Mobility-data gap. A current UN synthesis provides September 2025 climate-displacement totals, but classifications can combine climate and broader environmental factors, and consistent longitudinal evidence connecting hazard, livelihood change, movement, destination and subsequent conflict outcomes remains limited. (The United Nations in Iraq) []
- Access/verification gap. The official WFP evaluation publication page was accessible, but its full report was not retrievable through the browsing environment and the page's displayed publication date conflicts with its stated evaluation coverage period. Its conclusions are therefore used cautiously and the date is not asserted as verified. (World Food Programme) []
- *None completely absent, but coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, response-performance and resilience-peace-capacity are only partially covered because the available evidence is localized, plan-based, modeled or not climate-specific in evaluation design. []
- Recent local conflict-climate evidence is concentrated in Ninewa, selected return districts and displacement sites; findings are not nationally representative. []
- NRC detailed report methods were unavailable in the inspected excerpt, and the UNDP climate-vulnerability PDF could not be opened. []
- Evidence on tested climate-adaptation outcomes that reduce FCV risk remains limited in the inspected materials. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Demand growth, upstream withdrawals, domestic management, pollution and climate pressures jointly drive Iraq’s water deficit; the projected 2035 gap is 10.9 billion m3. [IRQ-E-006]
- As of September 2025, 186,006 people were reported displaced by climatic factors in central and southern Iraq; 63% had moved from rural to urban areas. [IRQ-E-007]
- Water-service weakness compounds climate exposure. [IRQ-E-008]
- The2022 returnee survey describes stronger weather sensitivity in Sinjar, Shirqat, Hamdaniya and Telafar. Across the sample, about a quarter of farming abandonments cited water scarcity and about a tenth unpredictable weather; costs and conflict losses also mattered. [IRQ-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Outdoor and manual workers face substantial heat exposure. [IRQ-E-009]
- Climate-water-agriculture shocks can transmit through prices and poverty. [IRQ-E-010]
- National policy recognizes differentiated vulnerability. [IRQ-E-011]
- In the March–April2022 returnee survey, nearly 75% of surveyed farming households reported lower harvests than in 2014; over 80% of livestock owners reported smaller herds. [IRQ-E-101]
- Participants reported that women, youth, people with disabilities, low-income households and displaced or returnee groups face unequal access to land, capital, irrigation and employment in parts of Ninewa. [IRQ-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Water efficiency has modeled adaptation benefits. [IRQ-E-014]
- A major targeted adaptation investment is planned in three governorates. [IRQ-E-015]
- Local institutions can contain water-related disputes. [IRQ-E-023]
- In the March–April2022 returnee survey, iSIL-era conflict destroyed farm assets and infrastructure; limited replacement capacity and incentives hindered agricultural recovery. [IRQ-E-102]
- NRC's2024 publisher summary reports: The report says four in five displaced respondents linked return decisions to the ability to farm, and 93% based stay, move or return decisions on information about local resources. [IRQ-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Iraq has a defined national climate-governance architecture. [IRQ-E-012]
- KRI has localized adaptation planning and proposed MRL capacity. [IRQ-E-013]
- WFP assistance buffered shocks but sustainability/results evidence was limited. [IRQ-E-016]
- Stabilization delivered infrastructure, but climate integration was weakly systematic. [IRQ-E-017]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Modeled heat exposure and macroeconomic/food-price shocks could worsen labour income and poverty; any instability consequence remains an analytical inference conditioned by markets, protection and governance. [IRQ-P-001; IRQ-E-009, IRQ-E-010]
  - Documented impact: Modelled labour-productivity and earnings exposure, plus scenario-modelled GDP, food-price and poverty effects.
  - FCV mediator: Livelihood insecurity, food prices, poverty and unequal exposure.
  - Possible consequence: Where social protection, jobs and services are inadequate, these pressures could increase grievances or susceptibility to instability; violence is not an automatic consequence.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-009'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-009'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-010'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-010']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['International food prices', 'exchange rates', 'domestic economic policy', 'conflict legacies', 'unemployment and market structure']
  - Uncertainty: The World Bank modeling quantifies exposure and macroeconomic/poverty implications, but the pathway does not establish that heat or water stress independently causes instability.
- Water-linked rural displacement can transfer stress to urban services and jobs, potentially increasing tensions where local capacity is inadequate. (The United Nations in Iraq) [IRQ-P-002; IRQ-E-006, IRQ-E-007, IRQ-E-008, IRQ-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Rural livelihood loss and climate-associated displacement.
  - FCV mediator: Rapid rural-urban mobility, inadequate urban services, limited employment, exclusion and harassment.
  - Possible consequence: Host-community/displaced-community tensions may increase where service capacity and economic opportunities are already weak.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-007'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-008', 'IRQ-E-019'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-019']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Conflict displacement', 'poverty', 'demographic change', 'urban labour-market weakness and pre-existing service deficits']
  - Uncertainty: Current displacement figures classify “climatic factors”; they do not isolate anthropogenic climate attribution.
- Scarcer water can increase opportunities for violent competition, but escalation depends strongly on governance, allocation systems, weapons and local political relations. [IRQ-P-003; IRQ-E-006, IRQ-E-020, IRQ-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Tighter irrigation and groundwater access.
  - FCV mediator: Competition among user groups interacting with customary rights, centralized allocation, political affiliation and widespread weapons.
  - Possible consequence: Water disputes may escalate to violence in particular settings.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-020']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Water allocation decisions', 'upstream withdrawals', 'weapons', 'tribal/political affiliations', 'land rights and weak enforcement']
  - Uncertainty: No basis for a national claim that drought causes communal violence.
- In some localities, armed-actor political economy can undermine resource regulation and public services, reducing climate-adaptation capacity. [IRQ-P-004; IRQ-E-006, IRQ-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Armed actors or affiliates have reportedly bypassed regulations, controlled water-intensive activities or obstructed improvements to public systems in selected localities.
  - FCV mediator: Coercion, political influence, regulatory capture and illicit/semi-licit resource markets.
  - Possible consequence: Adaptation and equitable resource management can become less effective, increasing vulnerability to future climate stress.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Weak administrative capacity', 'aging infrastructure', 'market incentives and broader corruption independent of organized armed actors']
  - Uncertainty: Representativeness and prevalence are not established nationally.
- Water-governance choices mediate whether physical scarcity translates into cooperation or grievance. [IRQ-P-005; IRQ-E-006, IRQ-E-020, IRQ-E-024]
  - Documented impact: Local and inter-governorate disputes over centrally allocated water.
  - FCV mediator: Perceived inequity, limited participation, unclear responsibility and political mobilization.
  - Possible consequence: Allocation grievances may fuel protests, inter-community disputes or political confrontation.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-006'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-020']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Upstream international management', 'domestic infrastructure', 'historical allocation rules and political competition']
  - Uncertainty: Climate change is not separable from the broader water-governance system.
- Displacement becomes a larger FCV risk when support systems are unclear, unequal or politically manipulated. [IRQ-P-006; IRQ-E-007, IRQ-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Climate-displaced populations may fall outside consistent registration and assistance systems.
  - FCV mediator: Perceived neglect, unequal assistance, political patronage and exploitation.
  - Possible consequence: Distrust, protest risk and erosion of social cohesion may increase.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-007'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-007'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-007'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-022']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Conflict-related displacement', 'poverty and broader politicization of social assistance']
  - Uncertainty: Institutional treatment varies geographically; local examples cannot establish a uniform national pattern.
- FCV legacies can magnify climate vulnerability by weakening infrastructure, regulation and implementation capacity. [IRQ-P-007; IRQ-E-008, IRQ-E-017, IRQ-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Conflict-damaged or weak infrastructure, limited O&M and fragmented institutional capacity constrain reliable services.
  - FCV mediator: Reconstruction burden, weak governance, corruption and prioritization of security.
  - Possible consequence: Climate shocks may generate larger welfare and livelihood losses because preventive and adaptive systems are weaker.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-008'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-008'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-008'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-008']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Chronic underinvestment', 'tariff policy', 'demographic growth and technical inefficiency independent of armed conflict']
  - Uncertainty: Relative contribution of past conflict versus other institutional drivers is rarely quantified.
- Climate action can support peace when it expands inclusive participation and legitimate resource-sharing mechanisms. [IRQ-P-008; IRQ-E-023, IRQ-E-024]
  - Documented impact: Climate and natural-resource issues create common policy problems across communities and institutions.
  - FCV mediator: Participation, dialogue, trust and locally accepted conflict-resolution mechanisms.
  - Possible consequence: Inclusive climate action may reduce escalation risks and strengthen social cohesion, although an effect on violence has not been quantitatively demonstrated.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-023'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-023'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-023'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-023']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Existing social relations and political incentives may be more important than the climate forum/action itself']
  - Uncertainty: Evidence demonstrates a plausible entry point, not a measured peace impact.
- In Nimrud/Hatra, participants link water stress and farming losses with irrigation/land competition and existing grievances; local peace capacities are unevenly accessible. [IRQ-P-101; IRQ-E-110, IRQ-E-111, IRQ-E-112]
  - Documented impact: The qualitative assessment describes weakened farming livelihoods and agricultural productivity.
  - FCV mediator: Competition for irrigation and land interacts with unequal access, exclusion and weak services.
  - Possible consequence: Participants describe farmer-herder tensions in Nimrud and resource/service pressures in Hatra; further stress could intensify existing grievances, not necessarily produce violence.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRQ-E-110'], 'impact': ['IRQ-E-110'], 'mediator': ['IRQ-E-110', 'IRQ-E-111'], 'consequence': ['IRQ-E-110']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Post-conflict recovery, unemployment, patronage, service inequality and political influence also shape grievances independently of climate.']
  - Uncertainty: Source-stated qualitative mechanism from purposive February2026fieldwork; no causal estimate, representative prevalence or attribution to anthropogenic climate change.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Climate is a risk multiplier, not a deterministic conflict cause. [IRQ-E-018]
- Displacement can transmit climate stress into urban service and cohesion pressures. [IRQ-E-019]
- Water competition has produced localized violence, but climate is only one contributor. [IRQ-E-020]
- Armed-actor political economy can obstruct adaptation. [IRQ-E-021]
- Assistance gaps can turn displacement into a governance/social-cohesion risk. [IRQ-E-022]
- Inclusive climate governance can create peace-supporting dialogue opportunities. [IRQ-E-024]
- The2024 portal-published IOM brief recommends early warning, technology and broader agricultural investment to support durable returns; these are proposals, not evaluated adaptation results. [IRQ-E-104]
- NRC's2024 publisher summary reports: In the 2024 farming season, 57% of surveyed returnee households used less land or water; 5% reported being forced to move because of drought conditions. [IRQ-E-105]
- NRC's2024 publisher summary reports: Among displaced households in informal sites, one in five lacked clean water, three quarters reported water-sharing tensions, and 44% said authorities barred some groups from water. [IRQ-E-106]
- Across 294 subdistricts from 2020-2022, the study reports a direct association between skin temperature and battle events, with soil moisture and energy operating indirectly. [IRQ-E-108]
- Population density positively mediated modeled conflict activity, while wheat production did not reduce conflict as hypothesized. [IRQ-E-109]
- Participants described drought, reduced rainfall and water scarcity as undermining farming and increasing irrigation and land competition, including farmer-herder tension in rural Nimrud and Hatra. [IRQ-E-110]
- The assessment identifies tribal mediation, kinship networks, shared economic activity and community initiatives as local peace capacities, while noting exclusion of less-connected groups. [IRQ-E-112]
