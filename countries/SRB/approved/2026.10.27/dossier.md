## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-01-16 reports: South of Sava and Danube, apricot and almond face the highest growing-season frost risk; listed fruits cover ~10% of fruit area. Risk is expected high across most Serbia by mid-century. Scenario: Mid-century; no scenario specified in the BTR summary. [SRB-E-102]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The programme reports high/very-high extreme-precipitation risk on 7% of Serbia in 2001–2020, including central/western Serbia, parts of Vojvodina and eastern Serbia; it projects 56% in 2041–2060 under RCP8.5, citing Životić and Vuković-Vimić (2022). Scenario: The map compares 2001–2020 with 2041–2060 under RCP8.5. [SRB-E-104]
- Source dated 2023 reports: One strong-drought year occurred in 2011–2020; the programme projects 2–3 per decade in 2021–2040, 3–4 in 2041–2060, and 7–8 per decade under RCP8.5 in 2081–2100. Scenario: RCP8.5 for 2081–2100; no scenario specified for nearer periods. [SRB-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-01-16 reports: Maize covers ~45% of arable land, mainly in water-short areas; family farms manage nearly 90% of it and generally have low adaptive capacity. Driest-year yields fell up to 40% against 2011–2020. [SRB-E-101]
- Source dated 2015-01-16 reports: The study identifies older and disabled people, single mothers, women living alone and illiterate residents (mostly women and Roma) as vulnerable; it also notes language barriers for evacuated Roma women. [SRB-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP identifies fragmented institutions and procedures plus insufficient central/local prevention, preparedness and response capacity; its project aimed to improve coordination and technical, operational and human capacity and ended in January 2026. [SRB-E-111]
- Source dated 2025 reports: FY 2025 began ten green-infrastructure, mobility and climate-resilience space designs across Leskovac, Kragujevac, Užice, Niš and Novi Sad; consultations/gender analysis covered safety and access. [SRB-E-112]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Two draft analyses supported climate integration in a five-year Action Plan for Serbia’s Sustainable Urban Development Strategy; an urban cycling/greenway study launched in Užice. [SRB-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-01-16 reports: For 2024–2026, RSD 998m was allocated (89.3% national, 10.7% donor); BTR calls funding insufficient and says major monitoring-capacity investment was absent. [SRB-E-103]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Flood, landslide and erosion risks depend on local terrain and protection works; the programme recommends assessment at local-government level. [SRB-E-106]
- Source dated 2015-01-16 reports: In the four municipalities studied, the report found no local protection-and-rescue plans, untrained communities, inaudible sirens, no local radio or television guidance, and warnings reaching few people informally. [SRB-E-107]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Niš analysis recommended narrowing General Urban Plan boundaries to the continuous urban area and reducing construction zones against population/land-use projections; Leskovac planning addressed decline and urban-rural links. [SRB-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Flood evacuation into isolated, poorly lit or unsupported centres can heighten women's protection risks; the 2014 study does not measure violence incidence. [SRB-P-101; SRB-E-108, SRB-E-109]
  - Documented impact: Evacuated people depended on collective/reception centres during displacement.
  - FCV mediator: Isolated locations, inadequate lighting and a lack of continuous organized support could weaken women's protection.
  - Possible consequence: Potential trafficking, sexual-assault and harassment risks identified by the author; no observed incidence increase is established.
