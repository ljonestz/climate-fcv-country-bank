## Executive assessment

- Evidence distinguishes Danube/Siret flood exposure, coastal planning weaknesses in Constanța County and southern/southeastern agricultural drought sensitivity. [ROU-E-102, ROU-E-104, ROU-E-106]
- Historical flood-plan delivery and coordination gaps, and lower indexed adaptive capacity in eastern regions, identify institutional constraints rather than violent conflict. [ROU-E-105, ROU-E-108, ROU-E-112, ROU-E-113]
- The UCPM profile's conflict-risk projection lacks a specified mediator and is not expanded into a pathway. Municipal and meadow-restoration initiatives remain reported capacities, not proven outcomes. [ROU-E-109, ROU-E-111, ROU-E-117, ROU-E-118]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2026-03-16 reports: During 2020–2024 Romania averaged 18 additional heat-stress days versus 1981–2010, reaching 48 annually and about 13 tropical nights. [ROU-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-03-16 reports: In Romania's Danube Basin, about 25% of the population and 8% of territory are flood-exposed; Danube and Siret floods peak in spring and early summer. [ROU-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2026-03-16 reports: OECD reports droughts in 2020, 2022 and 2024 harmed agricultural output, agrifood prices and hydropower generation. [ROU-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-03-16 reports: OECD describes urban expansion in Constanța County coastal towns without sufficient elevation or drainage upgrades despite known pluvial and coastal flood risks. [ROU-E-104]
- Source dated 2023-12-18 reports: The Commission says drought is the key agricultural hazard and crop-failure risk is greatest in southern and southeastern Romania; PESETA IV estimates losses 16% higher at +2°C. Scenario: PESETA IV estimate for a world 2.0°C warmer than present; projected agricultural losses by value. [ROU-E-106]
- Source dated 2023-10-24 reports: World Bank describes Romania as particularly vulnerable to climate-related floods and drought; rising temperatures and heatwaves threaten people, the economy and infrastructure. [ROU-E-114]
- Source dated 2023-10-24 reports: CCDR calls water deficits a current concern and priority for strategic management, with implications for power generation, agriculture and consumers. [ROU-E-115]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2023-12-18 reports: Romania reported 166 weather stations, including 68 agrometeorological stations, and seven Doppler radars; 2023 regional modelling aimed at high-resolution subregional projections. [ROU-E-107]
- Source dated 2023-12-18 reports: Romania cited Alba Iulia's municipal plan as good practice and reported two regions joining the EU Adaptation Mission. [ROU-E-109]
- Source dated 2023-12-18 reports: A Danube-macroregion project linked shared risk-assessment methods and drought-emergency response among 14 partners from nine countries. [ROU-E-110]
- Source dated 2023-10-24 reports: CCDR recommends skills development and active labour-market policies to help vulnerable groups adapt to Romania's green-economy transition. [ROU-E-116]
- Source dated 2026-06-26 reports: As of June 2026, 25 Romanian local/regional authorities participated in Mission projects, with nine Charter signatories and €11.66 million EU funding allocated. [ROU-E-117]
- The EU Adaptation Mission portal describes meadow-restoration projects covering 40 hectares in Râu Sadului and Cristian, using drones to address drought, biodiversity loss and erosion. The short project account does not establish completed restoration or measured resilience gains. [ROU-E-118]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-03-16 reports: Of 2,627 second-cycle flood-plan actions (2016–2021), 67% had not started; local authorities lacked finance and expertise, while Romanian Waters lacked scale-up capacity. [ROU-E-105]
- Source dated 2023-12-18 reports: The Commission identified weak coordination between climate-adaptation and disaster-risk authorities, limited finance and no demonstrated national monitoring of adaptation goals. [ROU-E-108]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The profile places eastern Romania at lower adaptive capacity than central and western regions, citing weaker institutional capacity and knowledge/technology factors. [ROU-E-112]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Reviewers reported risk assessment was irregular after RO-RISK ended in 2018; risk and disaster-loss data were scattered across national, county and local entities and inconsistent. [ROU-E-113]
