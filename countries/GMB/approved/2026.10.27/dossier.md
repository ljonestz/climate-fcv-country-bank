## Climate pressures and exposure

- Coastal zones with over 60% of the population face sea-level rise, erosion, and salt intrusion; yields declined an estimated 1.2% annually since 2010 amid erratic rain and drought. [GMB-E-002]
- SSP5-8.5 projects more severe or prolonged rainy-season dry spells in the second half of the century; rainfall projections are highly variable. Scenario: SSP5-8.5; later-century. Report also compares SSP2-4.5. [GMB-E-003]
- The rapid assessment estimated that the 2022 floods affected 50,378 people and displaced 7,404 across Greater Banjul, North Bank, Central River, and Upper River regions. [GMB-E-005]
- Source dated 2023-11 reports: For the 2022 extreme-rain event, parts of West Coast Region received 276 mm in 20 hours; policy cites a separate World Bank estimate of 80,000 affected in Greater Banjul. [GMB-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-03-15 reports: By 2050 under moderate emissions, inland historical 25-year extreme-heat events are projected every two years; northeast days above 40°C more than double. Scenario: 2050 RCP4.5; historical baseline. [GMB-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-03-15 reports: Middle-river floodplains around Mansa Konko, Kuntaur and Janjanbureh have the assessment's highest fluvial hazard. [GMB-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-03-15 reports: Under future extreme coastal-flood scenarios, Banjul and Brikama show substantial increases in modeled flood depth compared with current conditions. Scenario: 2050, SSP5-8.5, 100-year coastal-flood event. [GMB-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- By 2050, modelled poverty rises 1.2 points (dry/hot) or 0.6 (wet/warm) without adaptation, versus 0.4 or 0.2 points with the stated adaptation pathway. Scenario: Dry/hot and wet/warm cases, with/without adaptation; SSP label unstated here. [GMB-E-004]
- The assessment reports that floods breached water and sewage systems and contaminated water bodies. Greater Banjul had the largest number affected, while rural communities were described as more vulnerable. [GMB-E-006]
- The NDC describes the economy as vulnerable to drought and changes in annual rainfall, and notes that rising temperatures and saltwater intrusion threaten mangroves and increase flood susceptibility. Scenario: The cited qualitative passage does not specify a climate scenario or dated projection. [GMB-E-007]
- Source dated 2023-11 reports: The policy says peripheral drought and desertification drive displacement toward low-lying urban areas. [GMB-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-07 reports: IMF states that water insecurity increases pressure for internal migration, which in turn increases deforestation through agricultural expansion and charcoal demand. [GMB-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2023-11 reports: NDMA cites funding, regional logistics/storage, supply pre-positioning, and staffing constraints, often only two decentralized staff. [GMB-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-03-15 reports: Current modeled coastal flooding inundates low-lying Tanbi Wetland west of Banjul; the report says its mangroves attenuate flooding inland. [GMB-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2023-11 reports: All eight regions had disaster committees; committees in 56 districts were partly functional, and community committees remained difficult to establish. [GMB-E-102]
- Source dated 2023-11 reports: Unequal agency cash-transfer amounts in one community can create tension and suspicion. [GMB-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-07 reports: IMF reports that coastal resilience is constrained by an unclear institutional framework, absent land-use policy, and limited technical and institutional capacity. [GMB-E-110]

Limitations: Affected and displaced counts are assessment estimates; the inspected report does not attribute the floods to climate change.
