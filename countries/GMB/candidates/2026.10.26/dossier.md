## Climate pressures and exposure

- Coastal zones with over 60% of the population face sea-level rise, erosion, and salt intrusion; yields declined an estimated 1.2% annually since 2010 amid erratic rain and drought. [GMB-E-002]
- SSP5-8.5 projects more severe or prolonged rainy-season dry spells in the second half of the century; rainfall projections are highly variable. [GMB-E-003]
- The rapid assessment estimated that the 2022 floods affected 50,378 people and displaced 7,404 across Greater Banjul, North Bank, Central River, and Upper River regions. [GMB-E-005]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- By 2050, modelled poverty rises 1.2 points (dry/hot) or 0.6 (wet/warm) without adaptation, versus 0.4 or 0.2 points with the stated adaptation pathway. [GMB-E-004]
- The assessment reports that floods breached water and sewage systems and contaminated water bodies. Greater Banjul had the largest number affected, while rural communities were described as more vulnerable. [GMB-E-006]
- The NDC describes the economy as vulnerable to drought and changes in annual rainfall, and notes that rising temperatures and saltwater intrusion threaten mangroves and increase flood susceptibility. [GMB-E-007]

## Bibliography and review decision

Limitations: Affected and displaced counts are assessment estimates; the inspected report does not attribute the floods to climate change.

The Gambia Second Nationally Determined Contribution. 2021 (date basis: document-version). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/NDC/2022-06/Second%20NDC%20of%20The%20Republic%20of%20The%20Gambia-16-12-2021.pdf [GMB-SRC-003] Methodology: Government NDC describing national climate vulnerabilities and commitments. Limitations: The cited passage is a qualitative national assessment and does not quantify the exposed population or attribute specific impacts.
