- Named vulnerability evidence distinguishes Costa Chica Afro-Mexican communities, drought-exposed northern/central states, coastal flood exposure and poverty/service-access constraints in the southern sierras. [MEX-E-102, MEX-E-109, MEX-E-110, MEX-E-111, MEX-E-112]

Critical remaining gaps:
- The official CONAPO/UNHCR 2026 diagnosis on climate- and disaster-related internal displacement could not be opened; its search-result description was not used as evidence. []
- The UNHCR Sin escape report PDF exceeded the web reader limit and was not used. []
- The UNFCCC registry listed a 2026-08 NDC attachment that timed out; claims cite only the directly inspected 2025-11 NDC 3.0 PDF. []
- The NDC's climate-security statements are a national policy diagnosis; the inspected sources do not measure local conflict incidence caused by climate change. []
- No directly read source established that armed insecurity interrupted delivery of a named Mexican climate-adaptation project; the NDC identifies environmental offenses as a risk to climate action and proposes prevention and mediation. []
- Berkeley/IMUMI's survey covers 87 Central American migrants in transit in Mexico and is non-representative; findings on climate and violence are not generalized to Mexico-origin communities. []
- IOM records relocations but does not assess their long-term livelihood, safety or social-cohesion outcomes; the 1998 and 2005 Chiapas cases are historical. []
- No complete country-specific pathway is inferred from a national climate-security policy diagnosis or by combining separate disaster, migration and violence statistics. []
- Observed response or relocation counts do not establish consent, livelihood restoration or social-cohesion outcomes. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-11 reports: NDC 3.0 reports intensifying extreme hydrometeorological events, altered rainfall, rising temperatures and seas, and pressure on water availability. [MEX-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: Mostly in Guerrero and Oaxaca’s Costa Chica, 16 municipalities have over 40% Afro-Mexican residents; coastal zones face floods, heat, salinity, sea rise and erosion. [MEX-E-102]
- Source dated 2024 reports: WB maps place high drought exposure in Chihuahua, Durango, Zacatecas and San Luis Potosí; flood exposure clusters in Tabasco and Guerrero-Michoacán coasts. [MEX-E-110]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025 reports: IOM identifies inequality, poverty, weak disaster assistance, limited credit and inadequate services as barriers to local resilience, especially for women and marginalized groups. [MEX-E-105]
- Source dated 2025 reports: IOM cites over 600 megaprojects since 2017; at least half involved social or ecological conflict, and some land- or water-use projects may displace people. [MEX-E-108]
- The 2024 Poverty and Equity Assessment cites a study estimating that more than 60% of people in Chiapas, Oaxaca and Guerrero were classified as vulnerable to climate-event-related poverty in 2022, compared with under 15% in named northern states. [MEX-E-109]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Poor residents are more exposed than non-poor to drought and landslides; the assessment relates drought exposure to rural and agricultural livelihoods. [MEX-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-04 reports: Women respondents more often reported work, food and water disruption after climate events; 22% of female respondents said they fled gender-based violence. [MEX-E-114]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The 2024 assessment reports that low paved-road and transport access concentrates in Sierra Madre Occidental municipalities and the sierras of Guerrero, Oaxaca and Chiapas. This identifies service-access constraints, not measured disaster-response performance. [MEX-E-112]
- Source dated 2024-02-14 reports: Municipal guidelines call for neighborhood and locality networks with authorities and civil protection for prevention, response and resilient recovery. [MEX-E-117]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025 reports: IOM documents post-1998 relocations in Motozintla, Frontera-Comalapa, Huixtla and Comitán, and post-Hurricane Stan relocations in Chiapas. [MEX-E-106]
- Source dated 2025 reports: IOM reports 51 families from El Bosque, Centla, were relocated to Frontera in November 2024 after reporting sea-level effects since 2019. [MEX-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-04 reports: Report identifies detention-focused migration enforcement, under-resourced COMAR and rarely granted disaster visas as barriers in Mexico's protection system. [MEX-E-115]
- Source dated 2024-02-14 reports: NOM-003 requires regional, state, metropolitan and municipal plans to integrate risk instruments and climate measures into territorial planning. [MEX-E-116]

## Bibliography and review decision

- Secretaría de Medio Ambiente y Recursos Naturales (SEMARNAT), Government of Mexico; submitted to UNFCCC. Actualización de la Contribución Determinada a nivel Nacional 3.0 de México. 2025-11 (date basis: document-version). https://www.unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-11/NDC%203.0%20Me%CC%81xico_spanish.pdf [MEX-SRC-101] Methodology: Government NDC synthesizes national diagnostics and proposed climate measures. Limitations: Costa Chica annex is regional; proposed measures are not evaluated outcomes.
- International Organization for Migration (IOM), Mexico. Panorama nacional de datos, tendencias y respuestas políticas al nexo entre migración, medio ambiente y cambio climático (MMACC) en México. 2025 (date basis: document-version). https://mexico.un.org/es/download/194966/304290 [MEX-SRC-102] Methodology: IOM synthesis of scientific literature, policy, programmes, laws and documented mobility cases. Limitations: Evidence is stronger in central-southern Mexico; the report notes research gaps in the north.
- World Bank. Mexico Poverty and Equity Assessment. 2024 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099021925181518988/pdf/P179488-c49ff360-e38a-4961-85e2-1ef4e6cc5294.pdf [MEX-SRC-103] Methodology: World Bank analysis combines census, household-survey, hazard-exposure and poverty-risk measures. Limitations: State vulnerability rates are estimates cited from a separate ENIGH-based model, not conflict measures.
- University of California, Berkeley School of Law Human Rights Clinic, in partnership with the Institute for Women in Migration (IMUMI). Climate Change and Migration from Central America: Insights from Migrants in Mexico. 2026-04 (date basis: publication). https://www.law.berkeley.edu/260325_human-rights-clinic_climate-migration-report/ [MEX-SRC-104] Methodology: Report combines a 2024 survey of 87 migrants in transit, key-informant interviews and desk research. Limitations: Small, non-representative sample; security findings principally concern migrants from four Central American countries.
- Secretaría de Desarrollo Agrario, Territorial y Urbano (SEDATU), Government of Mexico; Diario Oficial de la Federación. Norma Oficial Mexicana NOM-003-SEDATU-2023: Lineamientos para el fortalecimiento del sistema territorial ante amenazas naturales y cambio climático. 2024-02-14 (date basis: publication). https://dof.gob.mx/normasOficiales/9411/sedatu/sedatu.html [MEX-SRC-105] Methodology: Official standard sets national multiscale territorial-planning guidelines. Limitations: Normative requirements; the standard does not report implementation or compliance results.
