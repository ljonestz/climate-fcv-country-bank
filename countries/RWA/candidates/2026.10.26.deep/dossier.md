## Executive assessment

- The new material differentiates Huye and Gisagara's reported vulnerability and adoption constraints from flood-exposed Volcanoes Region districts; these are selected examples, not a nationwide local ranking. [RWA-E-104, RWA-E-105, RWA-E-108]
- Gicumbi's adaptation fund reports training and funding reach, while the completion review could not establish use or effectiveness of the weather-information system. Outputs and intentions are not demonstrated resilience gains. [RWA-E-109, RWA-E-110]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Opened sources report climate-related disaster losses and mobility but do not establish a country-specific climate-to-violence causal pathway. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []

- The reverse-FCV search did not locate evidence that conflict, insecurity, or displacement has constrained Rwanda's climate adaptation or resilience delivery. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- NDC reports about 1.4°C warming since 1970; rainy seasons shortened and intensified, especially in north and west. [RWA-E-001]
- By around 2050, mean annual temperature is projected 1.5-2.5 degrees C above recent levels under RCP4.5/RCP8.5. [RWA-E-002]
- Heavy rain triggered May 2023 floods and landslides, causing 131 deaths and over 110 injuries along the Nile-Congo divide. [RWA-E-003]
- A cited study says 2024 East African rains/floods were twice as likely and 5% more intense due to human climate influence. [RWA-E-004]
- NDC reports 58,760 homes damaged/destroyed by extreme weather in 2016-24; 18,000 people moved to temporary shelter in 2023. [RWA-E-005]
- NDC identifies Nyagatare, Kayonza, Kirehe and Bugesera as dry lowlands with frequent rainfall deficits. [RWA-E-006]
- The framework cites 74 percent of district roads as landslide-exposed and lists flood- and landslide-damaged road and bridge sites in 20 districts across all four provinces after the May 2023 rains. [RWA-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The modeled yield functions show crop-specific responses; although wetter conditions often yield net gains, the appendix identifies drier conditions as Rwanda's greatest crop risk. [RWA-E-007]
- A 904-household survey across 50 northwestern cells found vulnerability varied across landslide, flood, and combined-hazard settings; location and living conditions, demographic traits, education and economic status, household structure, and prior disaster experience were contributing dimensions. [RWA-E-101]
- The contextualized index identified 25 higher-vulnerability cells, mainly in landslide-prone areas and concentrated in the western study area; the authors call for localized, hazard-specific mitigation. [RWA-E-102]
- Tearfund reports that Huye's vulnerability index declined to 0.556 over the previous five years as adaptive capacity strengthened, while Gisagara's index rose to 0.626. [RWA-E-104]
- The completion report identifies Burera, Nyabihu, Rubavu and Musanze in the Volcanoes Region as highly climate-vulnerable; it associates major floods with landslides, erosion, crop losses and infrastructure damage. [RWA-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Land and property ownership reduced vulnerability in the study's component analysis, while the other four identified components were associated with increased vulnerability. [RWA-E-103]
- Smallholder farmers were willing to adopt agroforestry and terracing, but limited training, quality inputs, and finance impeded uptake. [RWA-E-105]
- Tearfund described capacity-building for locally led restoration, especially with women, to strengthen community participation in environmental decisions and local monitoring and management skills. [RWA-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The review found national climate strategies relevant to local communities and associated with positive changes, but identified coordination and targeted-support gaps affecting the most vulnerable communities. [RWA-E-106]
- The 2022-2023 Gicumbi Community Adaptation Fund pilot supported 2,324 people, including 47.7 percent women, in 25 cooperatives with training and funding; 192 more people in 64 cooperatives received business-planning training. [RWA-E-109]
- A crop-insurance and weather-information database was piloted in a few locations, but the completion report's author could not verify its use, effectiveness, pilot count, or scaling timeline. [RWA-E-110]
- In September 2025 the World Bank approved a $141 million disaster-risk financing operation organized around risk information and coordination, built-environment resilience, and natural-resource management. [RWA-E-113]

## Bibliography and review decision

- Government of Rwanda. Rwanda's 2035 Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0). 2025-12-08 (date basis: submission). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-12/Rwanda%20NDC3.0%20Final.pdf [RWA-SRC-001] Methodology: NDC compiles national climate records, cited studies and adaptation priorities. Limitations: Cited disaster estimates do not establish climate effects on conflict.
- World Bank Group. Country Climate and Development Report: Rwanda - Technical Appendix. 2022-09 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099645409262212286/pdf/P1769550e6109e0d50a4250e1f168d1ae38.pdf [RWA-SRC-002] Methodology: Technical appendix uses Rwanda-specific crop, labor, health and flood damage functions in a computable general-equilibrium model. Limitations: Scenario-dependent model using CMIP5 projections and a 2017 social accounting matrix; results are not observed impacts.
- International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction; UCLouvain repository. Assessing Community Vulnerability to Landslide and Flood in Northwestern Rwanda. 2025 (date basis: publication). https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/929370cc-6270-49a8-9894-c62eb3cee3ef [RWA-SRC-101] Methodology: Repository abstract reports a 904-household survey across 50 cells and principal component analysis. Limitations: Only the repository abstract was inspected; its linked full-text PDF returned HTTP 502, and the abstract does not name the higher-risk cells.
- Tearfund. Climate vulnerability in Rwanda. 2026 (date basis: publication). https://learn.tearfund.org/en/resources/research-report/climate-vulnerability-in-rwanda [RWA-SRC-102] Methodology: Publisher summary describes mixed quantitative and qualitative research on district climate vulnerability and grassroots policy implementation. Limitations: The publisher page's detailed summary was inspected, but its linked full report download failed; the summary omits the sample design and index formula.
- World Bank. Rwanda NDC Deep Dive: Advancing Financial Innovation to Scale up Climate Action (P172153), Implementation Completion and Results Report. 2024-06-24 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062524171044559/pdf/P172153193b87a0e180eb19403ed3fc9a0.pdf [RWA-SRC-103] Methodology: World Bank completion report reviews the project's outputs, implementation, outcome indicators, and lessons. Limitations: Project-specific results do not establish national adaptation effects; the report notes that the weather-information system's use and effectiveness could not be verified.
- Rwanda Transport Development Agency. Rwanda Emergency Connectivity Restoration Project (RECOR): Environmental and Social Management Framework (Draft). 2024-12 (date basis: document-version). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099050625121596001/pdf/P504023-1ae9b4f9-d7d6-426f-a7d7-efab803d014d.pdf [RWA-SRC-104] Methodology: Draft project environmental and social management framework with national and project-area baseline information and planned risk-management measures. Limitations: Draft and project-specific; some subproject locations were not identified. Its border-conflict statement is contextual, not causal analysis.

World Bank Steps Up Support to Increase Rwanda's Resilience to Natural Disasters. 2025-09-30 (date basis: publication). https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/09/30/world-bank-steps-up-support-to-increase-rwanda-s-resilience-to-natural-disasters [RWA-SRC-105] Methodology: World Bank announcement of approved disaster-risk financing and its intended policy pillars.
