## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2023-11 reports: The plan reports that its climate-threatened coastal zone holds 70% of residents and 81% of economic activity. [TTO-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-03-13 reports: The same Trinidad communities repeatedly scored high or very high susceptibility across 38 five-year February and November maps. Scenario: Maps span 2010-2100 using CORDEX rainfall; pathway unspecified. [TTO-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-03-13 reports: Port of Spain, East-West Corridor, Valencia and low South Oropouche repeatedly rank high or very high. Scenario: Same five-year study maps through 2100. [TTO-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2023-11 reports: The health-risk assessment names Tunapuna-Piarco, Penal-Debe, Port of Spain and Tobago among localities especially vulnerable to flash-flood health impacts. Scenario: The assessment gives a next-ten-year risk window; no emissions pathway is stated. [TTO-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-03-13 reports: Authors attribute chronic susceptibility to low topography, land-use choices and inadequate drainage; projected rainfall may compound these conditions. Scenario: Projected CORDEX rainfall through 2100; pathway unspecified. [TTO-E-107]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-03-13 reports: Authors recommend targeted drainage redesign, enforceable zoning, resilient building design and community-led flood warnings in flood-prone urban areas. [TTO-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-10-09 reports: All 14 corporations have Disaster Management Units; the article says they liaise with Meteorological Services but rely on ODPM GIS flood maps. [TTO-E-112]
- Source dated 2023-03-17 reports: Ministry's 2023 response says Disaster Management Units in 14 corporations ran public-awareness, Community Emergency Response Team (CERT), shelter and hydrological-observer training. [TTO-E-113]
- Source dated 2023-03-17 reports: Ministry reported 3,000 CERT volunteers trained by 2021 and 170 hydrological observers trained in 2022. [TTO-E-114]
- Source dated 2023-03-17 reports: The Community Flood Early Warning System (CFEWS) includes hydrological monitoring, a portal, SMS alerts, mapping, interagency coordination and community participation. [TTO-E-115]
- Source dated not stated reports: Government reports a September 2023 launch of a US$10 million project for South Oropouche flood, sea-level and water-deficit resilience. Scenario: Four-year project launched September 2023; completion status unverified. [TTO-E-117]
- Source dated not stated reports: Project targets 9,758 direct beneficiaries and 175 hectares of coastal and wetland ecosystems. Scenario: Target for the four-year project launched September 2023, approximately 2023–2027; no achieved beneficiary count. [TTO-E-118]
- Source dated not stated reports: Government cites flood vulnerability, population density and economic/cultural activity in selecting the basin; CAF says resident engagement is critical. [TTO-E-119]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-10-09 reports: Most spatial strategies for 14 Trinidad municipalities lacked climate-adaptation measures; plans were outdated and local planners unassigned. [TTO-E-109]
- Source dated 2024-10-09 reports: Trinidad interviewees cited staff climate-risk knowledge gaps and technical/non-technical disconnects weakening collective adaptation work. [TTO-E-110]
- Two participants in the local-government study describe funding constraints and a high recurrent-cost share in municipal budgets. The accounts are not a representative budget audit. [TTO-E-111]
- Source dated 2023-03-17 reports: Ministry acknowledged funding, equipment and manpower constraints, and minimal local climate-policy integration in 2023. [TTO-E-116]
