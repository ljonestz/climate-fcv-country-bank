# Burkina Faso Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Seasonal drought and food-access evidence adds Karo Peli and Dori detail, while the four-province monitoring framework has important station and historical-data gaps. [BFA-E-101, BFA-E-102, BFA-E-108, BFA-E-109]
- Four named community anticipatory-action plans add local capacity design. They document intended triggers and support, not delivered aid or measured effects; no climate-terrorism inference is accepted. [BFA-E-110, BFA-E-111]

Critical remaining gaps:
- The bank lacks a complete numeric climate baseline and scenario set for heat, drought, rainfall, and flood hazards at national and subnational scales. []
- Only the WFP portfolio provides independent response evaluation; unintended effects, distributional performance, and long-term adaptation or peace outcomes remain largely unevaluated. []
- The fcv-to-climate reverse pathway is supported mainly through access and service constraints; quantitative estimates of conflict's additional effect on climate losses and adaptation performance are absent. []
- OCHA reports that rainfall-station coverage is thinnest inside the Loroum–Oudalan–Séno–Yagha anticipatory-action area; Dori was the only listed station inside it. []
- The public Oxfam page summarizes four community plans, but the linked detailed plan PDFs could not be inspected, so local targeting, triggers and delivery performance remain unverified. []
- The accessed IMF climate-security statement is national-level; no new localized causal study linking specific rainfall or flood events to farmer-herder violence was accessed. []
- The searched sources document insecurity-related access and displacement, but do not quantify how insecurity changed climate adaptation delivery or climate-resilience outcomes in named places. []
- The national IMF climate-terrorism assertion was excluded: no new localized causal study was established. []
- September 2026 Karo Peli Phase 3! retains the assistance qualifier; early-2026 UNICEF need estimates are not treated as current October prevalence. []
- FEWS NET's named-region bird losses were not misattributed to rainfall; Dori station data were not extrapolated to other framework provinces. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Drought and heat pressures compound water scarcity and agricultural risk. [BFA-E-001]
- High agricultural employment and widespread rain-fed land create broad livelihood exposure. [BFA-E-002]
- Conflict-displaced households faced concentrated flood exposure in unsafe urban sites. [BFA-E-006]
- FEWS NET reports that late and uneven rains and prolonged August–September dry spells reduced maize yields, pasture growth and water-catchment replenishment, especially in northern Burkina Faso. [BFA-E-101]
- OCHA's comparison reports late onset, rainfall 10–50% below normal, 7–10-day dry spells and low dam levels across the four framework provinces; at Dori in Séno, rainfall was 321 mm by 31 August versus a 414 mm 1991–2020 normal. [BFA-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Multiple shocks create differentiated food and protection sensitivity. [BFA-E-008]
- FEWS NET assessed Karo Peli at Crisis (IPC Phase 3!) in September 2026, with assistance preventing worse gaps, and projected Phase 3 through January 2027 as poor rainfall, dry spells and low incomes constrain food access. [BFA-E-102]
- FEWS NET identifies insecurity and armed-group attacks as the main constraint on household food and income access in the north and east; attacks disrupt market supply and displaced 11,316 people in August 2026. [BFA-E-103]
- UNICEF's early-2026 appeal estimates 4.5 million people, including 2.9 million children, needing assistance, with climate shocks, displacement and food insecurity jointly shaping needs. [BFA-E-106]
- UNICEF's early-2026 appeal identifies highest nutrition severity in Liptako, Soum, Koulsé, Goulmou, Tapoa, Sirba, Yaadga, Bankui and Sourou; displacement strains health systems and water collection exposes women and girls to protection risks. [BFA-E-107]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Conflict and access constraints erode household and institutional coping options. [BFA-E-009]
- Land, water, and livelihood assets provide potential adaptive and cohesion capacity. [BFA-E-010]
- Farmer rain gauges and institutional links expand localized climate-information capacity. [BFA-E-015]
- Oxfam and partners report four 2025–2027 drought anticipatory-action plans for Santéna (Boussouma), Damesma (Kaya), Somiaga (Ouahigouya) and Zougoungou (Gomponsom), developed with community vulnerability-and-capacity assessments and triggers using ANAM forecasts, PRESASS analysis and community early-warning observations. [BFA-E-110]
- The four plans envisage adapted agricultural inputs, food assistance, hygiene and dignity kits, heat-protection kits and income-generating support for vulnerable households; Oxfam says several thousand people are targeted. [BFA-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- An updated national plan establishes adaptation priorities and institutional responsibilities. [BFA-E-003]
- Independent evaluation found response strengths alongside major effectiveness and sustainability limits. [BFA-E-011]
- Nationwide municipal systems provide an institutional platform for crisis-sensitive delivery. [BFA-E-012]
- Municipal crisis delivery reached displaced households but remained exposed to fiscal shocks. [BFA-E-013]
- The January 2026 IMF staff report proposes 2026-2027 RSF reforms covering disaster-risk finance, resilient infrastructure and utilities, and climate-finance mobilization; these are reform commitments, not demonstrated resilience effects. [BFA-E-105]
- OCHA reports that the CERF drought framework covers Loroum, Oudalan, Séno and Yagha; its observational trigger was not activated in July–August, while national bulletins lacked province-level values and historical records needed for a formal alternate trigger. [BFA-E-108]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Drought, heat, and erratic rainfall can deepen livelihood and food-security stress where rainfed dependence, poverty, insecurity, and weak access limit alternatives. [BFA-P-001; BFA-E-001, BFA-E-002, BFA-E-004, BFA-E-008, BFA-E-009]
  - Documented impact: Reduced crop and livestock productivity, water stress, food insecurity, and livelihood loss
  - FCV mediator: High rainfed-agriculture dependence, poverty, weak market and input access, displacement, and insecurity
  - Possible consequence: Household distress, mobility, protection risks, and localized grievances may intensify where alternative livelihoods and assistance are inaccessible

  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed violence, inflation, market disruption, poverty, and policy choices can produce food insecurity and distress without a climate shock.']
  - Uncertainty: The interaction is well supported at national scale, but the relative contribution of climate pressures is not estimated.
- Climate-related resource stress may aggravate farmer-herder disputes when land governance and dispute resolution are weak or grievances are exploited. [BFA-P-002; BFA-E-004, BFA-E-007, BFA-E-014]
  - Documented impact: Changed resource availability and livelihood mobility among farmers and pastoralists
  - FCV mediator: Overlapping land claims, weak resource governance, armed-group exploitation, and disrupted customary dispute resolution
  - Possible consequence: Farmer-herder disputes or political exploitation of local grievances may become more likely, but are not an automatic result of climate pressure
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['BFA-E-004', 'BFA-E-007'], 'impact': ['BFA-E-004', 'BFA-E-014'], 'mediator': ['BFA-E-004', 'BFA-E-007', 'BFA-E-014'], 'consequence': ['BFA-E-007', 'BFA-E-014']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Agricultural expansion, herd growth, political exclusion, weapons, insurgency, and unresolved tenure disputes independently shape violence.']
  - Uncertainty: Available evidence supports conditional mechanisms rather than a Burkina Faso-wide causal effect.
- Conflict displacement can place households on flood-prone land, where heavy rain compounds shelter loss and housing insecurity. [BFA-P-003; BFA-E-006, BFA-E-012, BFA-E-013]
  - Documented impact: Damage to temporary shelters, asset loss, and repeated housing insecurity
  - FCV mediator: Conflict displacement, scarce safer urban land, poverty, and limited municipal shelter capacity
  - Possible consequence: Repeated shelter loss and constrained access to safer land and services may deepen household insecurity and prolong displacement.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['BFA-E-006'], 'impact': ['BFA-E-006'], 'mediator': ['BFA-E-006', 'BFA-E-012'], 'consequence': ['BFA-E-006', 'BFA-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Housing exclusion, poverty, conflict displacement, and poor drainage can create the same shelter risks without climate change.']
  - Uncertainty: Direct flood exposure and shelter loss are documented at selected sites; repeated displacement is plausible, while local tension was not measured.
- Conflict and access constraints can magnify climate losses by restricting farming, markets, early warning, assistance, and recovery. [BFA-P-004; BFA-E-001, BFA-E-008, BFA-E-009, BFA-E-011]
  - Documented impact: Higher need for food, water, livelihood, and climate-risk services
  - FCV mediator: Armed insecurity, blocked transport, disrupted markets, weak services, and constrained humanitarian access
  - Possible consequence: Climate shocks may produce larger and more unequal losses because conflict limits early warning, farming, markets, assistance, and recovery
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['BFA-E-001'], 'impact': ['BFA-E-008', 'BFA-E-009'], 'mediator': ['BFA-E-009', 'BFA-E-011'], 'consequence': ['BFA-E-008', 'BFA-E-011']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Chronic poverty, remoteness, underinvestment, and macroeconomic shocks can independently reduce response reach and worsen losses.']
  - Uncertainty: The sources establish access and performance constraints but do not quantify the additional climate loss attributable to conflict.
- Last-mile hydrometeorological services may reduce livelihood losses when information is trusted, timely, and accessible in insecure areas. [BFA-P-006; BFA-E-001, BFA-E-002, BFA-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Risk of mistimed sowing, seed loss, and weak local climate information
  - FCV mediator: Uneven last-mile services, institutional coordination, community liaison coverage, and insecurity-related access gaps
  - Possible consequence: Accessible climate information may protect livelihoods and reduce distress, while unequal coverage could leave high-risk communities behind

  - Alternative explanations: ['Input quality, soil conditions, market access, extension services, and insecurity also determine crop and livelihood outcomes.']
  - Uncertainty: Outputs and testimonials do not establish forecast accuracy, equitable uptake, yields, or conflict-sensitive coverage.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Regional evidence supports mediated, not deterministic, climate-insecurity pathways. [BFA-E-004]
- Climate-security analysis entered a multi-actor national strategy process. [BFA-E-005]
- Resource stress can be exploited within wider armed-conflict dynamics, but attribution is limited. [BFA-E-007]
- Conflict-sensitive transhumance governance is a documented regional resilience practice. [BFA-E-014]
