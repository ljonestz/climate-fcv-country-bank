## Executive assessment

- The NDC adds Yap/western Chuuk water-risk and tenure/governance detail, while the 2010 report supplies explicitly historical Pohnpei outer-atoll, Chuuk and Yap examples. [FSM-E-102, FSM-E-103, FSM-E-105, FSM-E-106, FSM-E-107]
- Adaptation financing, technical skills and coordination constrain implementation; these institutional findings are not a claim that climate causes violence. [FSM-E-104, FSM-E-109]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Coastal secondary roads face storm, flood and rising-sea risks that threaten access to schools, health facilities and markets. [FSM-E-001]
- Kolonia's coastal transport and economic assets face flood, sea-level and typhoon exposure. [FSM-E-005]
- Source dated 2010 reports: In Yap, 2007–08 surges damaged coastal infrastructure, food and housing; the report says main-island water remained nonpotable and harbor protections were only 30–60 cm above high tide. [FSM-E-107]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Isolated communities' reliance on secondary roads makes service and market access particularly sensitive to road disruption. [FSM-E-002]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: NDC says coastal and atoll communities 1–2 m above sea level face sea-level rise, drought, erosion and stronger storms; shocks are eroding assets and farm/fishing livelihoods. [FSM-E-101]
- FSM's 2025 NDC identifies drought risk in Yap/western Chuuk and reports limited Chuuk utility water coverage, with Weno the only supplied island, versus much broader Pohnpei utility reach. [FSM-E-102]
- Source dated 2010 reports: The field report says Pohnpei’s outer atoll islets Pingelap, Mokil, Ant, Pakin, Ngatik, Nukuoro and Kapingamarangi suffered extreme hardship during 2007–08 marine inundation. [FSM-E-105]
- Source dated 2010 reports: In Chuuk, Tropical Storm Chataan’s 2002 heavy rain caused over 30 landslides and 47 deaths. The report describes poor roads and unpotable drinking water; high tides and runoff filled potholes on the coastal business-district road. [FSM-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- SCORE proposes road upgrades across all four states alongside asset management, emergency-response capacity and road safety. [FSM-E-003]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The design includes disability access and women's representation; these commitments are not measured distributional outcomes. [FSM-E-004]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: State governments manage land, coast, health, education, water and roads. Yap includes traditional leaders in government; the NDC says tenure matters for adaptation/relocation and can hinder infrastructure. [FSM-E-103]
- The 2025 NDC reports financing for only a quarter of identified ecosystem-based adaptation needs and insufficient technical capacity, limiting implementation. [FSM-E-104]
- The IMF's 2026 risk matrix identifies potential infrastructure, food-security and reconstruction burdens from climate shocks; these are staff-assessed risks, not measured local effects. [FSM-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-05-05 reports: IMF says adaptation progress is constrained by limited institutional capacity, fragmented interagency coordination, and weak climate-risk integration in fiscal and infrastructure planning; it also identifies political fragmentation and administrative capacity as fragility drivers. [FSM-E-109]
