## Climate pressures and exposure

- After Beryl, Southern Grenadines communities lost water and electricity services; residents were evacuated from Union Island. [VCT-E-001]
- Source dated 2024-07-26 reports: Beryl brought Category 3–4 winds to the Southern Grenadines; building damage was extreme on Union Island, extremely severe on Mayreau, severe on Canouan and much lower on Bequia and Mustique. [VCT-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Storms, rainfall variability and drought pressure infrastructure and livelihoods; steep terrain and unregulated development amplify flash-flood and landslide risk. [VCT-E-003]
- Source dated 2024-07-26 reports: On Union Island, Beryl damaged the hospital, water network, power plant, airport and nearly all berthing structures; on Palm Island it damaged a desalination plant, while Mayreau suffered major structural and communications damage. [VCT-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-07-26 reports: The assessment estimated US$230.6 million in direct physical damage, about 22% of 2023 GDP; the Southern Grenadines accounted for US$186.8 million, or 81% of the national total. [VCT-E-103]
- Source dated 2019-08-02 reports: The 2019 policy identifies low-lying Grenadines coralline islets as exposed to sea-level rise and storm surge, with coral-bleaching risk to Tobago Cays; lower rainfall also contributes to drought and saltwater-intrusion pressure. [VCT-E-105]
- Source dated 2019-08-02 reports: The policy describes small farms on slopes often above 30 degrees and reports that cultivation practices on these slopes contribute to landslides during heavy rain; local farmers also noted longer dry periods and more intense rainfall. [VCT-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- UNDP worked with Union Island's environmental alliance and local emergency committee on community recovery needs. [VCT-E-005]
- Source dated 2019-08-02 reports: The policy sets adaptation priorities for vulnerable populations and proposes stronger participatory planning, climate information and monitoring, and Grenadines microgrids using solar, wind and battery storage. [VCT-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-03-27 reports: UNDP reports a two-day disaster-management and school-safety workshop at the Tobago Cays Marine Park office for teachers and disaster-committee members from Union Island and Mayreau. [VCT-E-108]
- Source dated 2025-12-16 reports: UNDP reports that 20 Union Island students, educators, community members and entrepreneurs took part in hands-on design and fabrication training; a 3D printer was handed to Union Island Secondary School. [VCT-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-12-16 reports: UNDP says Beryl-related isolation and disrupted supply chains can delay imported parts; the workshop presented local fabrication of water-pipe connectors and fishing-gear parts as possible emergency uses. [VCT-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The BERRy project reported temporary income support, cleanup employment and grants to affected small businesses, many women-led. [VCT-E-002]
- The2025 disaster-financing operation supported land-use rules, school safety, community preparedness and climate-finance tracking. [VCT-E-004]
- Public building staff received hazard-resilient residential construction-code training after Hurricane Beryl. [VCT-E-006]
- Source dated 2026-03-27 reports: The programme reports EC$385,000 of equipment supplied to Paget Farm Primary, Mayreau Primary, Union Island Secondary, Stephanie Browne Primary and Mary Hutchinson Primary. [VCT-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-03-27 reports: UNDP reports that 32 government and statutory-body staff were trained in post-disaster damage and needs assessment, with housing as the focus because it was the most damaged sector in Beryl. [VCT-E-110]
