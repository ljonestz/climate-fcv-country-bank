## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2001-04 reports: The report records crop damage and livestock deaths during Antigua's 1983 drought; some households lacked piped water for months and hotels trucked water. [ATG-E-101]
- Source dated 2001-05 reports: The OAS summary identifies housing on the Cook Creek floodplain as exposed; during Hurricane Lenny, floodwater floated houses whose ground floors were about one metre above grade. [ATG-E-104]
- The 2017 rapid assessment reports damage to about 95% of Barbuda's housing stock, with 45% described as uninhabitable. These are immediate post-Irma estimates, not present housing conditions. [ATG-E-106]
- Source dated 2017 reports: The assessment reports that all Barbuda water sources were affected: the desalination plant flooded, pipes were damaged, cisterns were polluted, and groundwater faced surface contamination and saltwater intrusion. [ATG-E-107]
- Source dated 2017 reports: The assessment says damaged schools and childcare facilities on Barbuda left students displaced to Antigua while education services were restored. [ATG-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2001-04 reports: OAS ranked watershed 27–46, southeast between English Harbour and St. James's Club, at very high drought risk, citing low rainfall, shallow soils, slopes, wind exposure and degraded slopes with many goats near Nelson's Dockyard National Park. [ATG-E-102]
- Source dated 2001-04 reports: The report identifies flat lands around and south of Codrington as Barbuda's highest drought-risk area and notes groundwater dependence alongside aquifer concerns from sand mining. [ATG-E-103]
- Source dated 2001-05 reports: The report says shallow, low-permeability soils and rapid drainage combine with sealed urban surfaces and grazing-related soil compaction to increase runoff and flood vulnerability. [ATG-E-105]
- Source dated 2019 reports: CEDAW states that Irma led to evacuation of Barbuda's entire population to Antigua and that women and girls continued to face education, health and livelihood challenges. [ATG-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-07-08 reports: The government says Local Area Adaptation Plans were prepared for the City of St. John's, Airport/Fitches Creek, and the Jolly Harbour–Johnsons Point area. [ATG-E-114]
- Source dated 2026-07-08 reports: The Department reports that stakeholders helped shape the sectoral and local plans and select their strategies and interventions. [ATG-E-115]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The UNEP terminal review rates outcome 1 largely achieved: the Environment Registry was developed, launched and operational, and stakeholders reported its use for greenhouse-gas inventory and broader climate MRV work. [ATG-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The review finds financial sustainability moderately unlikely because recurring Registry maintenance funding is unsecured, technical support remains external, and integration with the Ministry of Finance is limited. [ATG-E-112]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The review says Registry tools are used within the Environment Department but adoption across government remains incomplete; it also identifies dependence on individual champions and risks from staff turnover and shifting priorities. [ATG-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- After Irma's Barbuda evacuation, CEDAW warned that recovery land decisions could undermine women's communal tenure without inclusive participation; the bank retains this as a historical governance risk, not a verified dispossession outcome. [ATG-P-101; ATG-E-109, ATG-E-110]
  - Documented impact: Evacuation to Antigua and disruption of education, health and livelihoods for affected women and girls.
  - FCV mediator: Post-disaster land redistribution decisions and women's participation in recovery governance can affect communal tenure and access to recovery resources.
  - Possible consequence: CEDAW warned in 2019 of possible loss of women's communal land rights; this is a concern/recommendation, not an adjudicated or verified later outcome.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Pre-existing tenure systems, gender inequality and independent land-policy decisions also shape recovery rights.']
  - Uncertainty: Historical treaty-body risk assessment, not proof of dispossession, a causal violence estimate or present legal advice; no attribution of Irma itself to anthropogenic change.
