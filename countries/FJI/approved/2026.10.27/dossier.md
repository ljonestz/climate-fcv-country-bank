## Climate pressures and exposure

- The CCDR summary identifies stronger cyclones, heat, drought, floods and rising seas as threats to Fiji's infrastructure, agriculture and tourism. [FJI-E-001]
- UNDP identifies rural, maritime and coastal communities with greater exposure and less access to timely warnings. [FJI-E-004]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: IMF staff’s map estimates that half of Lomaloma village on Vanua Balavu would be permanently flooded under a 1 m sea-level-rise scenario; mapped nearby retreat areas are hypothetical. Scenario: 1 m above present sea level; IMF map shows possible retreat areas as hypotheses. [FJI-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The report recommends upgrading vulnerable informal settlements and targeting shock-responsive social protection, rather than treating household exposure as uniform. [FJI-E-002]
- The June 2024 practice paper reports 42 relocation requests and prioritization of Nabavatu/Cogea, with some cyclone-displaced families still in tents; this is publication-era status, not current relocation progress. [FJI-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-09-04 reports: Floods, cyclones and erosion damaged homes and land across the seven sites; flooding cut road access in Vunivau, Nacekoro and Dratabu, salt intrusion affected Vunidogoloa, and river erosion limited Matawalu farmland. [FJI-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: IMF summarizes post-flood household surveys in Votua, Nawaqarua and Etatoko showing mixed relocation views; land ties, livelihoods, limited productive land and amenities, and tenure constrained resettlement; an Etatoko plantation lease was shortened for relocating households. [FJI-E-109]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: Residents of Vunidogoloa, Cakaudrove Province, recalled coastal and river flooding, erosion, rising tides and waves, salt intrusion and food insecurity; 26 households, about 140 people, relocated roughly 1 km inland in 2014 after seawalls failed. [FJI-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Warning barriers include connectivity, language, mobility and accessibility constraints affecting women, older people, persons with disabilities and marginalized communities. [FJI-E-005]
- The initiative proposes inclusive warning design and coordinated institutions; its launch does not demonstrate reliable last-mile delivery. [FJI-E-006]
- Source dated 2025-09-04 reports: Nawaqarua used early-warning alarms and drainage; Nacekoro planted mangroves and vetiver. Walls were sought at Matawalu, Nacekoro and Nawaqarua, but some walls elsewhere blocked drainage or prolonged floods. [FJI-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Government coordination, implementation monitoring and budgeting need strengthening; the summary describes existing sovereign disaster financing as covering relatively modest events. [FJI-E-003]
- Source dated 2024-06 reports: Fiji’s 1987, 2000 and 2006 coups affected community and civil-society trust and government engagement. [FJI-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-09-04 reports: In March 2024, Matawalu, Nawaqarua and Nacekoro lacked relocation consensus; Vunivau and Nabavatu awaited government support, with procedural delay and tent shelters noted at Nabavatu. [FJI-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: After moving, residents reported less coastal-flood exposure and better water, sanitation, schools and health access, but new cyclone and heat exposure, incomplete paths and drainage, and reduced fishing access, especially for women and older people. [FJI-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: The relocation was community-initiated and led through village governance; later, residents reported no ongoing government or donor monitoring or formal channel for unfinished construction concerns, while the women’s committee continued village development work. [FJI-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-related relocation may interact with tenure, consent and trust to create disputes; Fiji evidence supports a conditional risk mechanism, not climate-caused violence. [FJI-P-101; FJI-E-102, FJI-E-103, FJI-E-109, FJI-E-111]
  - Documented impact: Relocation decisions can separate households from valued land, livelihoods and community arrangements.
  - FCV mediator: Incomplete consensus, insecure tenure and pre-existing mistrust in government or policing can shape the process.
  - Possible consequence: The practice paper warns of possible cohesion loss or disputes if relocation proceeds without adequate consensus; this is not measured conflict incidence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Conciliation Resources' 2024 practice paper warns that relocation proceeding with minimum post-scoping consent could strain cohesion and escalate disputes; it describes consent thresholds then in place, not current legal advice. Scenario: Only 60% consent after scoping. [FJI-E-102]
- A seven-day listening exercise in Qauia, Nanuku and Maravu found no support for climate-driven settlement migration; tenure/service concerns and mistrust shaped conflict risks, with relocation a conditional future flashpoint. Scenario: Government relocates these settlements. [FJI-E-103]
