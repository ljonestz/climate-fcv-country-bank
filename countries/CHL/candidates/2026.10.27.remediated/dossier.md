# Chile Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Named Mapuche coastal and riverine livelihoods provide local context, alongside historical land and water disputes. Those disputes are not attributed to climate change. [CHL-E-101, CHL-E-103, CHL-E-104, CHL-E-112, CHL-E-113]
- Viña del Mar and Valparaíso fire findings distinguish physical losses, protection needs and response constraints; small-sample perceptions are not prevalence. [CHL-E-105, CHL-E-106, CHL-E-107, CHL-E-108]
- National urban/rural water-access differences and the 2026 sector plan identify service and institutional priorities. Basin pilots and training reach are outputs, not measured adaptation effects. [CHL-E-109, CHL-E-110, CHL-E-111]

- No inspected source establishes a climate-change-to-armed/political-violence pathway in Chile; this is a search gap, not evidence of absence. []
- The Mapuche-specific climate chain is workshop-based and conceptual; public commune-level indicators disaggregated for Indigenous communities were not found. []
- No inspected source showed territorial insecurity disrupting ongoing climate adaptation or routine water services; UNICEF's reported safety concerns are tied to the February 2024 wildfire emergency. []
- The 2024 UNFCCC BTR PDF timed out and the official repository link for the water-plan PDF returned a cache miss; the accessible DGA plan summary was used for water-plan claims. []
- Mapuche territorial disputes, dispossession and water-related vulnerability are documented, but their co-occurrence does not establish climate-caused political violence or insecurity interrupting adaptation. []
- The post-fire protection assessment is a small rapid-interview exercise. Perceived safety and violence concerns do not establish a fire-caused increase or representative prevalence. []
- A conceptual coastal-water projection without a stated period was excluded rather than assigning a medium-term horizon. Indigenous-disaggregated local exposure information remains incomplete. []
- The September 2026 water-plan account reports formal measures, pilot indicators and training, not evaluated service improvements or hydrological effects. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-03-07 reports: February 2024 fires affected six regions; Valparaíso had 14,954 ha damaged and 6,587 homes destroyed; FIBE recorded 4,163 severely affected children. [CHL-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-04-10 reports: The study places Lafkenche communities in coastal Carahue, Puerto Saavedra, Toltén and Teodoro Schmidt; Wenteche farming communities lie along the Cautín and Quepe rivers. [CHL-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-04-10 reports: The study reports Mapuche–forestry-company land disputes and conflicts over culturally significant sites, biodiversity and water sources. [CHL-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-03-07 reports: UNICEF interviewed 44 people in 14 Viña del Mar locations; leading reported needs were protection, waste collection and drinking water. [CHL-E-106]
- UNICEF's rapid interviews after the February 2024 fires in Viña del Mar identify displacement, family separation and protection concerns. Reported perceptions of local insecurity and physical/sexual violence are not population prevalence, a measured increase caused by fire, or conflict data. [CHL-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-09-28 reports: DGA reports 2022 public-network drinking-water coverage of 99.9% in urban areas versus 57% of the rural population; irrigated agriculture and livestock account for about 73% of consumptive demand. [CHL-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2024-04-10 reports: Participants identify community organization, territorial knowledge, medicinal plants and seed-saving as adaptation capacities; culturally appropriate rural water systems and government coordination are proposed. [CHL-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-09-28 reports: The plan's implementation developed 38 basin-security indicators piloted in Camiña, Maipo, Valdivia and Seno Andrew with 195 participants; a municipal water-action kit reached representatives of more than 170 municipalities. [CHL-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-03-07 reports: Fire damage caused water cuts; emergency taps/tanks were installed, but UNICEF noted coordination problems and water shortages for cooking and washing. [CHL-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-09-28 reports: Chile's formal water-sector adaptation plan has 10 measures and 35 actions for urban/rural water and sanitation, ecosystems, productive water use, territorial planning, knowledge and preparedness for droughts and floods. [CHL-E-110]
- Source dated 2025-07-03 reports: The resolution says 99.59% of Indigenous land-fund purchases through 2024 occurred in the four regions and were for Mapuche applicants; 1,131 applications remained pending when recommendations were delivered. [CHL-E-113]
