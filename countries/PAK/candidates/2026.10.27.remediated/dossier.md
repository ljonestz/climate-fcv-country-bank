## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC describes GLOF risk in the upper Indus; storms and seawater intrusion on the Balochistan and Sindh coasts; river floods and agricultural heat in Punjab and Sindh; and aridity across parts of Punjab, Sindh, and Balochistan. [PAK-E-002]
- The PDNA reports 2022 floods affected 33 million people, displaced nearly 8 million, and killed over 1,700; 94 districts were calamity-hit by October 11. [PAK-E-005]
- The PDNA estimates 780,000 houses destroyed and 1.27 million partly damaged; Sindh accounted for 83% of housing damage. [PAK-E-007]
- Source dated 2025-07-25 reports: As of 25 July 2025, NDMA reported 266 deaths and 628 injuries from heavy rain and flash floods since 26 June; reported deaths included 144 in Punjab, 63 in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, 25 in Sindh and 16 in Balochistan. [PAK-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The NDC reports Pakistan contributes about 0.9% of global GHG emissions yet is highly climate-vulnerable, citing floods, rainfall shifts, glacier melt, and vector-borne disease. [PAK-E-001]
- The NDC cites moderate or severe food insecurity among 40% of households in FY2020 as limiting capacity to absorb further climate shocks. [PAK-E-003]
- The NDC identifies agriculture as highly affected by climate variability and reports that it accounts for 95% of national freshwater withdrawals. [PAK-E-004]
- The PDNA estimates US$14.9 billion damage, US$15.2 billion losses, and US$16.3 billion recovery needs; housing, agriculture, and transport were hardest hit. [PAK-E-006]
- The PDNA estimates flood-related poverty rose 3.7–4.0 percentage points (8.4–9.1 million people) and multidimensional poverty 5.9 points. Scenario: Preliminary short-term flood-impact estimate; relief and recovery design may change outcomes. [PAK-E-008]
- Source dated 2024-11-19 reports: The policy says declining groundwater, recurring drought and heatwaves erode agriculture and displace vulnerable, resource-dependent rural communities, increasing poverty in Balochistan. [PAK-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-11-19 reports: The policy reports that rain-induced floods displaced over 1.3 million people in Balochistan in 2022; women, youth and vulnerable communities face disproportionate climate-related food and health risks. [PAK-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-11-19 reports: The policy says climate disasters combined with social norms obstruct women's access to healthcare, including transport, supplies, skilled staff and information. [PAK-E-103]
- IPC's February 2026 analysis classified food insecurity for December 2025–March 2026 across 45 rural districts, citing weather shocks and localized insecurity among several drivers. [PAK-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-02-18 reports: The household assessment associated drought with income declines of 30% in Balochistan, 14% in Sindh and 9% in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; respondents also reported water scarcity and reduced crop or livestock production. [PAK-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-02-18 reports: IPC identifies localized insecurity as a main food-insecurity driver in Kachi and Tank, disrupting livelihoods, markets, services and income. [PAK-E-107]
