# Paraguay Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Chaco water constraints differ from eastern-region conditions; Indigenous community accounts around Irala Fernández identify storage and emergency-delivery gaps. Bañados adds a distinct urban flood-livelihood case. [PRY-E-101, PRY-E-102, PRY-E-103, PRY-E-104, PRY-E-106, PRY-E-108, PRY-E-113, PRY-E-114]
- Water-service competition and weak local grievance/institutional capacity are relevant mediators, but retrieved sources do not establish climate-caused violent conflict. Proposed community water and fire measures are not verified implementation. [PRY-E-107, PRY-E-110, PRY-E-111, PRY-E-112, PRY-E-115, PRY-E-116, PRY-E-117]

Critical remaining gaps:
- The reviewed accessible sources provide no verified source-stated pathway from named climate stress to organized violence or conflict escalation. UNICEF/DAPSAN reports generalized WASH service-use/access conflicts but supplies no case location, parties, timing or climate attribution. []
- Evidence is strongest for drought, WASH vulnerability, Indigenous Chaco accounts and some Asunción flooding; direct recent subnational conflict-security documentation remains a gap. []
- UN Human Security project material and GCF readiness material were not directly accessible; a MADES 2026 page returned 404. Search-index snippets were not used as findings. []
- The generalized WASH competition and service-access conflicts cannot support a specific climate→violence pathway without place, parties, chronology and mediator evidence. []
- FAPI plan proposals are distinguished from historical community accounts and are not represented as delivered or evaluated interventions. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated not stated reports: FAO says severe droughts have occurred about each decade since the 1960 s; 2020–22 affected western Central Chaco, the Pilcomayo area, Irala Fernández and Mariscal Estigarribia, especially December–February. [PRY-E-101]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO contrasts Central Chaco’s scarce, temporary, salty surface water and non-recharging salty groundwater with generally higher rainfall and more usable, recharging water resources in the east. [PRY-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-11 reports: Report projects +2°C mean monthly temperature by 2050 and uncertain rainfall. [PRY-E-109]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: FAPI says drought and heat raise fire threat; fires from adjacent lands/ranches damaged forests, crops and homes in Indigenous territories, including Itapúa and Caazapá. [PRY-E-116]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated not stated reports: FAO links drought to lower subsistence production for small farmers and Indigenous communities, hydropower generation, waterway transport and drinking-water availability; it identifies Indigenous, rural and agricultural communities as most affected. [PRY-E-102]
- Source dated 2024 reports: In the Western Region (Chaco, Alto Paraguay, Boquerón, Presidente Hayes), the CCDR identifies high Indigenous concentrations and drought-related land degradation/water-access problems; riverbank bursts are associated with illness. [PRY-E-104]
- Source dated 2024 reports: A 2019–23 drought set the Paraguay River’s record-low at Asunción; in records since 1904, about 18% of observations would impede normal navigation near the city. [PRY-E-105]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Bañados near central Asunción is an informal settlement with rural migrants; floods average once in five years, causing relocation and job losses. [PRY-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-11 reports: The report names Chaco, Pilcomayo, Irala Fernández and Mariscal Estigarribia as drought-affected; poor rural/peri-urban, dispersed and Indigenous communities have limited WASH infrastructure. [PRY-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-11 reports: It says prolonged drought leaves water demand unmet, heightens competition among uses and pressures WASH supply, especially in Asunción metro. [PRY-E-110]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: Chaco member accounts say water shortage and rainfall shifts alter crop calendars, reducing food crops, pasture and livestock feed. [PRY-E-113]
- FAPI's 2020 Chaco community accounts describe reliance on rainwater storage, scarce or saline water sources, insufficient storage through prolonged drought and water trucks not reaching Indigenous communities near Irala Fernández. [PRY-E-114]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2020-11 reports: FAPI proposes rainwater storage, feasible solar wells, emergency delivery, water governance and community system-maintenance training. [PRY-E-115]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: FAPI proposes local fire brigades, joint monitoring, agency complaint follow-up and territorial planning. [PRY-E-117]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024 reports: The CCDR reports weak local environmental data; limited, centralized agency and court capacity constrains complaints and local participation, especially in remote areas. [PRY-E-107]
- Source dated 2024-11 reports: Annex reports conflicts around service use/access but gives no place, parties, timing or climate attribution. [PRY-E-111]
- Source dated 2024-11 reports: Annex notes poor Indigenous WASH access, non-climate-adapted infrastructure, municipal storage gaps, unstable financing and weak implementation. [PRY-E-112]
