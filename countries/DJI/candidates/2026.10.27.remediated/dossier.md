## Climate pressures and exposure

- NDC reports 134.7 mm annual rainfall, 21 heatwave days yearly, and heatwaves lengthening 8 days per decade. [DJI-E-001]
- NDC projects 1.2°C warming by 2030 and 2.1°C by 2050 against available reference data. Scenario: The inspected NDC passage does not identify the emissions scenario. [DJI-E-002]
- The CCDR summary reports single-event urban flood damages in Djibouti City up to US$43 million and projects annual flood damages +US$10 million and sea-level losses +US$38 million by mid-century. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify the climate scenario for these mid-century estimates. [DJI-E-008]
- Source dated 2025-07-09 reports: In inland pastoral areas of Dikhil, Obock and Tadjourah, March–May rainfall was about 50% below average; the brief says deficits impaired rangeland regeneration and pasture and water availability, worsening livestock body condition. [DJI-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Recent heatwaves affected health of children and older people, livestock, crops, and infrastructure. [DJI-E-003]
- The NDC says rural women’s work burden rises as water, firewood, and pasture journeys lengthen. [DJI-E-004]
- The NDC warns climate effects may worsen land degradation and poverty for pastoralists and small agro-pastoralists; livelihood losses may prompt movement to city outskirts. Scenario: The inspected passage does not specify a scenario. [DJI-E-005]
- Extreme heat, drought, and floods threaten livelihoods; climate-related losses could reach 6% of GDP annually by 2050. Scenario: The press release does not specify the climate or economic scenario behind the estimate. [DJI-E-007]
- CCDR projects heat stress could reduce rural livestock revenue 24% and human productivity up to 3.5% by mid-century. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify scenarios for the heat-related projections. [DJI-E-009]
- The CCDR summary reports malaria incidence rose 37-fold over the preceding two decades following the arrival of a new mosquito species. [DJI-E-010]
- The May–June 2025 IPC analysis estimates Phase 3+ food insecurity among 30% of analyzed populations in Dikhil Rural and Obock Rural and 25% in Tadjourah Rural. [DJI-E-101]
- The same May–June 2025 IPC table estimates Phase 3+ rates of 50% in Markazi and 30% in Ali Addeh and Holl-Holl camps; it lists livelihood, purchasing-power and assistance constraints alongside climate shocks in some zones. [DJI-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-07-09 reports: The FAO brief reports 33,800 refugees and asylum seekers, most without livelihoods and reliant entirely on humanitarian assistance, highlighting limited buffers when food access deteriorates. [DJI-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-02-21 reports: The February 2025 PAD lists refugees/asylum seekers in Obock, Holl-Holl, Ali Addeh and Djibouti-Ville; conflict and disasters are separate movement contexts. [DJI-E-107]
- Source dated 2025-02-21 reports: The PAD describes Dikhil's weak services, unemployment and aridity, with migration and climate vulnerability adding pressures. [DJI-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NDC’s 2030 adaptation priorities include agriculture/livestock, water, coastal zones, gender, and regional coverage. [DJI-E-006]
- The February 2025 appraisal proposes basic services, safety nets and resilient drainage/water investments in high-hazard, vulnerable locations, including refugee-hosting areas. Scenario: Proposed project investments, not evaluated delivery. [DJI-E-109]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP lists a six-year project under implementation for 100,000 people, half women, in Ali Sabieh, Dikhil, Tadjourah and Obock. Planned activities include local early warning, water and rangeland management, and climate-resilient livelihoods. Scenario: UNDP project targets and planned outcomes during implementation; not an evaluation of achieved coverage or impact. [DJI-E-110]
