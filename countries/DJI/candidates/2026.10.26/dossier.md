Critical remaining gaps:
- The revised 2025 NDC is available in French; paraphrases were made from the French text, and local climate projections are not disaggregated by region in the cited passages. []
- The World Bank CCDR full report and model assumptions were not retrieved; its summary does not provide uncertainty ranges for the stated urban and livelihood estimates. []
- The NDC reports a risk of rural livelihood-driven movement toward urban peripheries, but does not establish conflict, violence, or measured migration outcomes. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []
- The bank contains no completed mediated climate-FCV pathway; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- NDC reports 134.7 mm annual rainfall, 21 heatwave days yearly, and heatwaves lengthening 8 days per decade. [DJI-E-001]
- NDC projects 1.2°C warming by 2030 and 2.1°C by 2050 against available reference data. [DJI-E-002]
- The CCDR summary reports single-event urban flood damages in Djibouti City up to US$43 million and projects annual flood damages +US$10 million and sea-level losses +US$38 million by mid-century. [DJI-E-008]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Recent heatwaves affected health of children and older people, livestock, crops, and infrastructure. [DJI-E-003]
- The NDC says rural women’s work burden rises as water, firewood, and pasture journeys lengthen. [DJI-E-004]
- The NDC warns climate effects may worsen land degradation and poverty for pastoralists and small agro-pastoralists; livelihood losses may prompt movement to city outskirts. [DJI-E-005]
- Extreme heat, drought, and floods threaten livelihoods; climate-related losses could reach 6% of GDP annually by 2050. [DJI-E-007]
- CCDR projects heat stress could reduce rural livestock revenue 24% and human productivity up to 3.5% by mid-century. [DJI-E-009]
- The CCDR summary reports malaria incidence rose 37-fold over the preceding two decades following the arrival of a new mosquito species. [DJI-E-010]
