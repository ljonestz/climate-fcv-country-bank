## Climate pressures and exposure

- The project ESMF describes northwest urban clusters 1 and 2 as relatively drought-prone, with less rainfall and higher temperatures than other parts of Bangladesh. [BGD-E-001]
- Coastal districts in southwest clusters 3 and 5 are described as vulnerable to salinity intrusion, cyclones, and tidal water surges. [BGD-E-002]
- The note describes low-lying delta populations as exposed to cyclones and storm surges, with coastal erosion and seawater intrusion affecting land and groundwater. [BGD-E-005]
- UNDP identifies Cox's Bazar exposure to cyclones, heavy rain, landslides, flash floods, storm surges and extreme temperatures; poverty and weak coping capacity compound impacts. [BGD-E-101]
- The 12 July 2026 Gender Alert reports 286 weather incidents in Cox's Bazar camps on 4-9 July, affecting 26,119 refugees, damaging 2,809 shelters and displacing 4,307 people. [BGD-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The note reports that climate change, pollution, resource overuse, and rapid urbanization are degrading ecosystems and increasing livelihood threats, especially for the rural poor. [BGD-E-004]
- The paper says Bangladesh's Delta Plan 2100 divides 64 districts into six ecological zones and identifies 58 districts as extremely vulnerable to climate change. [BGD-E-007]
- The paper reports regional health differences: groundwater depletion in Barind and drought-prone areas, saline drinking water on the coast, and especially pronounced effects for pregnant women and children. [BGD-E-008]
- UNDP's project description links overcrowded temporary hillside shelters, environmental degradation and infrastructure pressure in Cox's Bazar camps and host areas with heightened weather exposure. [BGD-E-102]
- The 2024 SAFE+2 report describes intense monsoon landslide risk in refugee camps and LPG reducing fuelwood pressure on slope-stabilizing forests. [BGD-E-104]
- Gender inequality shapes warning access and evacuation; women and girls face delayed movement, livelihood disruption and greater protection and GBV risks. [BGD-E-109]
- In the Chattogram Hill Tracts, road disruption constrains obstetric care; floods and landslides may delay referrals and raise maternal/newborn risks. [BGD-E-110]
- The 2025-2026 JRP describes infrastructure, service and livelihood pressure in Cox's Bazar and links labor competition, environmental degradation and prices with tension risks; climate is not identified as the cause. [BGD-E-112]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- RUTDP plans climate-resilient urban services and stronger city management across 87 municipalities and city corporations in 36 districts, targeting about 21 million people, half of them women. [BGD-E-003]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Studies cited by the note estimate mangroves protect 1.1-3.5 million people from cyclone flooding; villages with mangroves had about half the monetary losses of other villages during Cyclone Sidr. [BGD-E-006]
- UNDP's project page reports forecasting, landslide warning and 10 gauges in camp/host areas; its 2,000-person training figure is treated as a target, not verified completion. [BGD-E-103]
- The 2024 SAFE+2 report records 1,777,331 LPG refills across UNHCR/IOM operations, including 989,011 funded by SAFE+2; it reports protection, forest and refugee-host benefits without detailed causal methods. [BGD-E-105]
- SAFE+2 reports restoring 578 hectares in 2024 and describes landslide and human-elephant-conflict benefits; neither avoided losses nor human social-conflict effects are established. [BGD-E-106]
- SAFE+2 reports support to 5,000 host farmers and 600 Rohingya and a resilience-index increase from 31 to 42; the inspected summary does not establish scale, comparison or attribution. [BGD-E-107]
- The 2025-2026 JRP plans restoration, cleaner energy, inclusive warnings and climate/disaster training for refugees and hosts; these remain planned measures, not verified results. [BGD-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Displacement-related settlement and mobility constraints in Cox's Bazar magnify vulnerability to heavy rain, landslides and flooding, without implying climate-driven violence. [BGD-P-101; BGD-E-101, BGD-E-102, BGD-E-108, BGD-E-109]
  - Documented impact: Reported shelter and infrastructure damage and repeated displacement in refugee camps.
  - FCV mediator: Forced-displacement settings combine crowded temporary hillside shelters, restricted mobility, degraded slopes and limited services.
  - Possible consequence: These conditions narrow safe evacuation and coping options and magnify weather-related losses; they do not establish climate-caused refugee-host violence.
