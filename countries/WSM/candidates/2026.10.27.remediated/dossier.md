## Climate pressures and exposure

- The announcement identifies low-lying coastal settlement as a major source of climate exposure in Samoa. [WSM-E-001]
- Flooding in 2018 and 2020 disrupted Faleolo International Airport; coastal transport assets face storm-surge, flood and rising-sea risk. [WSM-E-004]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- A West Coast Road business account recalls cyclone and wave damage to the road serving coastal businesses. [WSM-E-005]
- Source dated 2023-09-18 reports: Rural coastal Salani had 93% of 88 households on customary land; inland suburban Vaitele had 80% of about 1,300 households on freehold land. [WSM-E-101]
- Source dated 2023-09-18 reports: At Salani, 10/20 participants partly or fully linked inland moves to climate factors (four entirely); storm surge and cyclone rainfall were recurring triggers. [WSM-E-102]
- Source dated 2023-09-18 reports: At Vaitele, 6/20 households linked movement partly or fully to climate factors; seven participants cited social reasons and seven freehold access, with overlap in environmental changes. [WSM-E-103]
- Source dated 2017 reports: At Matafa'a, runoff, land management, variable rain, floods and drought were linked to poor water quality and livelihood and health effects; elderly people, women and children bore water-collection burdens. [WSM-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-06-30 reports: Women at a Lalomanu workshop said inland relocation reduced sea access and coastal-road customers for craft and seafood businesses. [WSM-E-109]
- The Pacific synthesis records Samoa participants' reports of domestic-violence concerns, but explicitly warns that sample/cultural differences may explain the Samoa–Tonga contrast. [WSM-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Sālelologa on Savai'i is targeted for resilient infrastructure and urban planning to manage settlement growth and exposure. [WSM-E-002]
- Source dated 2023-09-18 reports: Seventeen of 20 Salani households near water moved inland; short moves were easier where customary access avoided land-purchase costs. [WSM-E-104]
- Source dated 2017 reports: The report says a mountain gravity-fed system and native reforestation were installed; Matafa'a had water during the three-month September 2011 El Niño drought, with clean water in all homes. [WSM-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Training and female representation in urban-planning committees are planned alongside multi-hazard warnings, not demonstrated delivery results. [WSM-E-003]
- The feature reports a completed road upgrade and improved business confidence; testimony does not establish the size or durability of resilience gains. [WSM-E-006]
- Source dated 2019-10-28 reports: UNDP described Salei'a as exposed to sea-level rise, flooding, erosion, stronger tropical storms, rainfall and heat; by 2019 a revetment and raised bridge provided daily access and a disaster escape route. [WSM-E-105]
- Source dated 2017 reports: Matafa'a's community-managed scheme set a household maintenance fee; the Independent Water Scheme Association supported filter maintenance and water quality. [WSM-E-108]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: In June 2025, PREPARE planned six years of hazard monitoring, early warning, response and urban resilience work, with township planning focused on Sālelologa. [WSM-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: The ESMF identifies Sālelologa land disputes as a potential planning conflict risk; proposed controls map disputes, exclude disputed areas and use consultation and grievance handling. Scenario: June 2025 preparation-stage project risk scenario. [WSM-E-112]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: The ESMF flags possible exclusion of women, youth, people with disabilities and other marginalized groups from Sālelologa planning. Scenario: June 2025 preparation-stage risk scenario. [WSM-E-113]
