Critical remaining gaps:
- No directly read public source in this extraction established climate change causing or worsening violent conflict in Panama; reported water-user conflicts are contextual to adaptation programming. []
- The official 2026 climate-mobility report exceeded fetch limits and the 2024 NDC2 PDF open timed out; indexed snippets were not used. []
- MiAmbiente's January 2024 Gardi Sugdub account records impending relocation and service needs but not subsequent completion, consent, tenure outcomes or service quality. []
- FAO rural-service and poverty indicators use different vintages from 2021–2023 and do not provide consistent district/comarca-level drought impact estimates. []
- Gardi Sugdub mobility is not equated with violent conflict. The January 2024 relocation account needs later independent follow-up on consent, tenure and services. []
- No full causal or reverse-insecurity FCV chain is assembled. Climate-informed water-right governance addresses pre-existing conflicts without establishing that climate caused them. []
- The July–December 2026 forecast remains date-bounded; no forecast is treated as an observed result. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated not stated reports: FAO names Arco Seco, Veraguense savanna, Cerro Punta and Ngäbe-Buglé as drought-affected; 2019 was severe and 2023–24 rainfall was 43% below average. [PAN-E-108]
- Source dated not stated reports: SINAPROC reports forecast predominant rainfall deficits nationwide from July through December 2026, potentially reaching 55% in some zones, while noting contrasting rainfall and flood risks on the Caribbean slope. [PAN-E-119]
- The SINAPROC 2026 page reports 11 unnamed basins under water stress and contrasting heavy rainfall in Bocas del Toro, warning of possible flood/landslide conditions. [PAN-E-120]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2023-06-27 reports: MiAmbiente reports over 116,000 direct and indirect beneficiaries across the climate-vulnerable Chiriquí Viejo and Santa María basins; consultations raised crop-loss, extreme-event and drinking-water quantity and quality concerns. [PAN-E-104]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO says drought threatens subsistence farmers’ food security and drinking/sanitation water, while low Canal levels disrupt maritime operations and revenue. [PAN-E-109]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO reports rural poverty at 29.3% (2022) and 32.3% (2023); 76% of Indigenous people live rurally, and three Indigenous comarcas had the highest 2021 poverty indicators. [PAN-E-110]
- Source dated not stated reports: In 2022, basic water access was 86.1% rural versus 94.6% nationally; sanitation was 66.4% versus 85.9%. [PAN-E-111]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Canal Authority says 2023 was the third-driest year in the Canal basin, with about 30% rainfall deficit; it cut daily transits from July because it could not store over half the water needed for the 2024 dry season. [PAN-E-113]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Authority says Canal reservoirs supply water to over half of Panama’s population, including Panama City, San Miguelito, Arraiján, La Chorrera and Colón, and support Canal operations and other economic uses. [PAN-E-114]
- Source dated 2024-01-24 reports: MiAmbiente linked sea-level rise at Gardi Sugdub, Guna Yala, to an impending mainland relocation to Isberyala and called it climate displacement; its January 2024 report does not document completion. [PAN-E-116]
- Source dated 2024-01-24 reports: At a January 2024 Guna Congress meeting, Sáhilas described basic needs for new Isberyala, including water, health services and household furniture. [PAN-E-117]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated not stated reports: MiAmbiente reports a vulnerability-index method and two capacity workshops completed with the initially selected municipalities, including Las Palmas (Veraguas) and Chame (Panamá Oeste); maps are intended to distinguish corregimientos and inform local planning. [PAN-E-101]
- Source dated not stated reports: The ministry describes a Kusapín, Ngäbe-Buglé pilot involving local, government and traditional authorities and proposing gender-aware vulnerability mapping, participatory land-use planning, climate-risk management and local planner training. [PAN-E-102]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated not stated reports: The programme says climate-informed water-right decisions aim to strengthen governance in two basins selected partly due to existing water-user conflicts. [PAN-E-103]
- Source dated 2023-06-27 reports: The ministry reports adaptation practices on 120 farms covering 600 hectares of silvopastoral systems, 226 hectares of agroforestry and 50 rainwater-harvesting systems, including in Santa María basin. [PAN-E-105]
- Source dated 2023-06-27 reports: The programme reports eight climate-resilient water-management instruments, including vulnerability studies, adaptation plans, hydrological balances and environmental flows, plus soil-climate zoning for 12 priority crops. [PAN-E-106]
- Source dated 2023-06-27 reports: The programme reports over 40 agrometeorological and hydrological stations, two flood early-warning systems in priority basins and advice for over 6,000 producers across five regions. [PAN-E-107]
- The Canal Authority page describes December 2025 water-saving arrangements for the 2026 dry season and a planned Río Indio lake requiring community participation and respect for residents' rights. [PAN-E-115]
- Source dated not stated reports: Government declared a national emergency on 26 August 2026 for El Niño-related conditions after a May alert; SINAPROC describes monitoring, interagency coordination and public-preparedness functions. [PAN-E-118]
