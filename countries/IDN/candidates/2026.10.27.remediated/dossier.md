## Climate pressures and exposure


## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2023-11 reports: The study places weather-related displacement among the human-security concerns of conflict-afflicted Papuans: extreme rain and flash floods near the Cyclops Mountains killed at least 50 people and displaced thousands in 2019; prolonged drought and extreme cold displaced 6,000 people from Agandugume and Lambewi in 2023. [IDN-E-101]
- Source dated 2023-11-27 reports: The World Bank expects climate risks to weigh disproportionately on poor and economically insecure people reliant on agriculture and natural resources; women and marginalized groups may face greater livelihood, food- and water-security, migration, and cultural-identity impacts. Scenario: Not specified by source. [IDN-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-10-17 reports: The UNDP-led mixed-method study focuses on West and East Nusa Tenggara and identifies climate change as a direct threat to local livelihoods, water resources, food security, and community resilience. [IDN-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2023-11-27 reports: The report identifies local knowledge and governance as resilience assets, but says village budgets are not yet optimized for climate-smart development. [IDN-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-04-27 reports: A World Bank brief describes restored West Java slopes and springs managed through a village-owned water enterprise; in Margagiri, village micro-dams, drainage, and agroforestry reportedly reduced heavy-rain damage and improved farm output. [IDN-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-04-27 reports: The brief reports that over 75,000 Indonesian villages receive direct fiscal transfers for climate-resilience investment, averaging USD 3.8 billion annually, and that a village climate-risk index steers added allocations to the most vulnerable communities. [IDN-E-108]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- In coal-dependent East Kalimantan and South Sumatra, a poorly managed mitigation transition could transmit job/service losses through unequal adjustment into mistrust and instability; the World Bank frames this as prospective risk, not observed conflict. [IDN-P-101; IDN-E-103, IDN-E-104]
  - Documented impact: The World Bank anticipates job losses and worker/family dislocation in coal-dependent regions if transition is poorly managed.
  - FCV mediator: Dependence on coal, unequal adjustment opportunities and reduced access to social/economic services can distribute transition costs unevenly.
  - Possible consequence: The source warns of possible mistrust, insecurity and social instability without mitigation; it does not document realized conflict.
