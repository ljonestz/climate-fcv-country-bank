## Climate pressures and exposure

- Most areas of Syria have warmed at nearly 0.5°C per decade since the early 1980s during the spring cereal-growing season. [SYR-E-001]
- Under SSP5-8.5, increases in Syrian daily maximum temperatures could reach about 2.5-3.2°C by the 2050s. Scenario: 2050s, SSP5-8.5 [SYR-E-002]
- For the Euphrates-Tigris basin including large parts of Syria, WWA estimated that the likelihood of a 2020-2023-type agricultural drought increased by a factor of about 25 in the present 1.2°C-warmer climate, primarily because of increased temperatures. [SYR-E-003]
- As of Mar 2026, more than 1.5 million refugees and 1.8 million IDPs had returned since Dec 2024, while 5.5 million remained internally displaced and 15.6 million required assistance. [SYR-E-006]
- The World Bank estimates that conflict during 2011-2024 damaged about one-third of Syria's pre-conflict gross capital stock and caused about US$108.2 billion in physical building/infrastructure damage; Aleppo, Rif Dimashq and Homs were hardest hit. [SYR-E-009]
- Source dated 2026-09-01 reports: For 2025, FAO estimated that over 65% of both the human and livestock populations were potentially exposed to severe or extreme drought conditions. [SYR-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- FAO's 28 August 2026 brief reported above-average 2026 cereal production, a significant rebound from drought-affected 2025, and cereal import requirements of about 2.8 million tonnes, close to the five-year average. [SYR-E-004]
- Kelley et al. identified agricultural policy, water scarcity, unsustainable practices and groundwater depletion as factors that increased Syria's vulnerability to the pre-2011 drought. [SYR-E-013]
- Source dated 2026-09-01 reports: FAO's retrospective geospatial assessment identifies rainfed croplands and grazing areas as especially drought-vulnerable, with many locations experiencing repeated one-to-two-month drought periods during 2021–2025. [SYR-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-08 reports: UNICEF reports that 10 million people cannot meet basic water needs and that drought-like conditions, erratic rainfall and flooding have exacerbated vulnerabilities across northern governorates, along the Euphrates and in coastal regions. [SYR-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-08 reports: UNICEF reports that renewed violence in Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh displaced more than 170,000 people in early 2026; it also reports 187,000 displaced after the July 2025 As-Sweida escalation and continuing pressure from returns. [SYR-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-12-31 reports: FEWS NET's December 2025 context report lists Aleppo, Daraa, Deir ez-Zor, Al-Hasakah, Homs, Idlib, Ar-Raqqa and Rural Damascus as food-security areas of concern, and identifies recurrent drought, low Euphrates levels, displacement, insecurity and economic shocks among drivers of poor households' acute food insecurity. [SYR-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- UNICEF reported that 4.1 million people obtained safe water through 60 rehabilitation projects in 2025 and that safe-water access was maintained for 1.5 million people during March violence. [SYR-E-008]
- The UN CCA reports that irrigated land declined from 25% in 2011 to 17% in 2021 because of damaged irrigation/drainage infrastructure and reduced maintenance, increasing reliance on private groundwater wells. [SYR-E-010]
- The UN CCA reports that solar-powered pumps reduced farmers' fuel dependence but further accelerated groundwater depletion. [SYR-E-011]
- The Red Cross review identifies instability, lack of data/evidence and practical/legal/institutional barriers for displaced populations as recurring obstacles to adaptation in armed-conflict settings, including the regional context in which Syria is examined. [SYR-E-018]
- The Eastern Ghouta Adaptation Fund project is designed to reduce water-scarcity vulnerability through integrated natural-resource management, wastewater treatment, efficient water supply and water-efficient irrigation. [SYR-E-022]
- Source dated 2025-04-29 reports: FAO reports rehabilitating the West Maskanah–Farm No. 9 irrigation scheme in Aleppo Governorate, with the stated aim of ensuring irrigation access for about 3,970 smallholder households across 3,645 hectares; works included canals, field distribution and drainage systems. [SYR-E-107]
- Source dated 2025-03-19 reports: FAO's 2025–2027 plan proposes irrigation and drainage rehabilitation, climate-smart practices and resource management, livelihood support for smallholders including IDPs and host communities, and clearance of mines and unexploded ordnance from farming land. [SYR-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- UNICEF reported 13.6 million people needing WASH assistance in 2025, with water systems operating at less than half capacity and more than 70% of sewage discharged untreated. [SYR-E-007]
- ICRC/Norwegian Red Cross reported significant Syria/Iraq gaps in UNFCCC-mandated documentation, particularly National Adaptation Plans, and linked conflict-affected state capacity constraints to lagging adaptation. [SYR-E-017]
- WFP's evaluation found important contributions to addressing food insecurity through large-scale food assistance, but declining financial resources hindered performance and WFP had not fully demonstrated effective prioritization of the most vulnerable, including women and girls. [SYR-E-019]
- The Eastern Ghouta adaptation project received a five-month no-cost extension partly because security conditions caused works to be suspended in late 2024/early 2025. [SYR-E-023]
- The World Bank's current Syria overview states that official economic data remain limited and uneven in reliability even as high-frequency indicators suggest recovery; it also reports agriculture still recovering from the sharp 2025 drought. [SYR-E-024]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Kelley et al. proposed a pathway from anthropogenic climate influence through severe drought, agricultural collapse and migration to increased conflict risk, while acknowledging poor governance, poverty and other sociopolitical factors. [SYR-E-014]
- Selby et al. found no clear and reliable evidence that drought-related migration contributed to the onset of Syria's civil war, while explicitly noting that this does not prove climate or drought had no effect. [SYR-E-015]
- Eklund et al.'s satellite analysis found that claims of a broad drought-driven agricultural collapse could not be substantiated because cropland recovered quickly after the 2007-2009 drought. [SYR-E-016]
- In selected Aleppo programme locations, FAO introduced climate-smart practices including drip irrigation and heat-related livestock measures, while village committees incorporated adaptation into community action plans. [SYR-E-020]
- JP Syria 2.0 reports as a lesson that participatory recovery planning can improve targeting, reduce conflict risks and strengthen community acceptance, while investments can also mitigate or exacerbate tensions. [SYR-E-021]
- Source dated 2025-03-19 reports: FAO's ERPA announcement presents stronger agriculture as a proposed route to community resilience and peace by reducing competition over scarce resources. [SYR-E-109]
