# Syria Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Syria's most robust climate signal is rising heat and temperature-amplified drought rather than a demonstrated climate-to-conflict effect. Supporting evidence: SYR-E-001-E-005, E-014-E-016. Pathways: SYR-P-001, P-003, P-007. [SYR-E-001, SYR-E-002, SYR-E-003, SYR-E-004, SYR-E-005, SYR-E-014, SYR-E-015, SYR-E-016, SYR-P-001, SYR-P-003, SYR-P-007]
- FCV conditions clearly reduce climate resilience through damaged water, irrigation, energy and service systems and through implementation/access constraints. Supporting evidence: SYR-E-007-E-011, E-017-E-018, E-023. Pathways: SYR-P-002, P-005, P-008. [SYR-E-007, SYR-E-008, SYR-E-009, SYR-E-010, SYR-E-011, SYR-E-017, SYR-E-018, SYR-E-023, SYR-P-002, SYR-P-005, SYR-P-008]
- Local adaptation and humanitarian capacities exist, but evaluated resilience and peace outcomes remain much weaker than evidence of activities, reach and programme intent. Supporting evidence: SYR-E-008, E-019-E-023. Pathways: SYR-P-005, P-006, P-008. [SYR-E-008, SYR-E-019, SYR-E-020, SYR-E-021, SYR-E-022, SYR-E-023, SYR-P-005, SYR-P-006, SYR-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate-projection gap: Heat and drought projections are comparatively strong, but this packet lacks equivalently verified Syria-specific projection/attribution evidence for future flood patterns, wildfire risk, coastal hazards and compound extremes. []
- Geographic and population gap: Comparable current evidence is lacking across the northeast, northwest, coast, southern governorates, urban peripheries, pastoral areas and heterogeneous return settings. Findings from Eastern Ghouta, Aleppo or historical Jazira studies should not be generalized nationally. []
- Differentiated-vulnerability gap: Women and girls, children, people with disabilities, IDPs and returnees are identified as vulnerable, but robust disaggregated estimates linking climate exposure, access restrictions, poverty, gender, disability and livelihood type are scarce. WFP's evaluation itself found that effective prioritization of the most vulnerable, including women and girls, had not been fully demonstrated. (World Food Programme13) []
- Evaluation and unintended-effect gap: A formal WFP evaluation exists, but it evaluates a broad food-assistance portfolio rather than climate adaptation. No fully verified independent impact evaluation of major Syria climate-adaptation projects was located. Potential unintended effects such as groundwater depletion from solar pumping are documented descriptively but not rigorously quantified. (UN Syria10) []
- Causal Climate-FCV gap: The best-known drought-conflict case remains contested. Current evidence supports climate effects on water, agriculture and food-security stress more strongly than climate effects on violence, recruitment, grievance or political instability. (Columbia OCP5) []
- FCV-to-climate evidence gap: FCV impacts on infrastructure and specific adaptation delivery are comparatively well supported, but nationwide evidence quantifying how insecurity, territorial governance, access rules and institutional fragmentation alter adaptation outcomes remains limited. []
- National policy verification gap: Syria's First NDC is confirmed by official registry metadata and an accessible mirror, but the primary UNFCCC PDF could not be opened in this browsing environment; detailed policy text therefore remains LOCATOR NOT VERIFIED. No current primary-text-verified National Adaptation Plan was located. (Climate Laws8) []
- Current-data gap: The World Bank explicitly characterizes official Syrian economic data as limited and uneven in reliability, making post-transition comparisons and projections particularly uncertain. (World Bank17) []
- *None completely absent, but four classes remain only partially covered at decision-grade quality: coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, response-performance, and resilience-peace-capacity. Direct-climate-fcv evidence exists but is substantially contested and weak for present-day climate -> violence outcomes. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Rapid observed spring warming. [SYR-E-001]
- Substantial projected warming. [SYR-E-002]
- Warming strongly intensified basin agricultural drought. [SYR-E-003]
- Large returns coexist with persistent displacement and need. [SYR-E-006]
- Conflict caused very large, geographically uneven capital damage. [SYR-E-009]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- 2025 cereal collapse was drought-dominated but multi-causal. [SYR-E-004]
- Dry onset and input costs jointly constrained 2026 planting. [SYR-E-005]
- Governance and water policy mediated historical drought vulnerability. [SYR-E-013]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Emergency and rehabilitation systems sustained water access at scale. [SYR-E-008]
- Conflict damage reduced irrigation capacity and shifted pressure to groundwater. [SYR-E-010]
- A coping measure may worsen groundwater depletion. [SYR-E-011]
- First NDC exists, but detailed policy locator remains unverified. [SYR-E-012]
- Conflict obstructs adaptation through instability, data gaps and displacement barriers. [SYR-E-018]
- Eastern Ghouta has a concrete water-adaptation programme. [SYR-E-022]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- WASH systems remain severely degraded. [SYR-E-007]
- Conflict-related institutional weakness constrains national adaptation processes. [SYR-E-017]
- Food assistance contributed, but resource and targeting limitations remained. [SYR-E-019]
- Insecurity directly delayed a climate-adaptation project. [SYR-E-023]
- Current recovery assessments carry major data uncertainty. [SYR-E-024]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Warming and drought can worsen agricultural and food stress through livelihoods and markets, but current evidence does not show that this automatically causes violence. (ODI: Think change1) [SYR-P-001; SYR-E-001, SYR-E-003, SYR-E-004, SYR-E-005]

  - FCV mediator: Agricultural livelihood dependence, input prices, market access, household purchasing power and existing conflict-related vulnerability.
  - Possible consequence: Could increase food insecurity, harmful coping and livelihood-related mobility or humanitarian dependence; evidence here does not demonstrate increased violence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- One influential study links drought to conflict through agriculture and migration. [SYR-E-014]
- Evidence for the historical drought-migration-war chain is disputed. [SYR-E-015]
- Satellite cropland evidence weakens the “agricultural collapse” premise. [SYR-E-016]
- Local programme outputs show adaptation practices and community planning. [SYR-E-020]
- Programme design explicitly considers both peace-supporting and tension risks. [SYR-E-021]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National statistics should not obscure large geographic differences. Conflict-damage estimates are especially high in Aleppo, Rif Dimashq and Homs, while other governorates experienced substantially lower assessed physical damage; this alone implies very different recovery and adaptation baselines. (World Bank4) []
- The historical drought-conflict literature focuses heavily on northeast Syria/Jazira and rural-to-urban migration. Its findings cannot be generalized to Damascus, the coast, southern governorates, the northwest or contemporary return areas. The basin-level 2020-2023 WWA attribution likewise covers a large Euphrates-Tigris region and should not be converted into a Syria-wide locality-specific attribution. (Cambridge Repository11) []
- *Eastern Ghouta evidence in this packet concerns a specific Adaptation Fund water-resilience project. Its security-related delay is directly observed, but neither its implementation experience nor any eventual outcome should be treated as nationally representative. (Adaptation Fund16) []
- *Aleppo programme evidence on climate-smart agriculture, participatory recovery and local committees is also locality/programme-specific. The same source records security and organizational-access difficulties in eastern rural Aleppo during 2025. (MPTF Gateway14) []
- Important underrepresented geographies/populations include parts of the northwest and northeast under changing institutional arrangements, coastal and wildfire-prone communities, southern areas affected by recent hostilities, informal urban peripheries, pastoral populations, and return communities for which comparable climate-livelihood-service datasets are not publicly available. []
- Evidence is strongest for agriculture/cereals, water and groundwater, irrigation, WASH, displacement/return, food security and physical infrastructure. Electricity is covered in the ODI reconstruction analysis but less deeply in the atomic ledger than water. (ODI: Think change1) []
- Evidence is thinner for wildfire attribution, ecosystem services, coastal climate risk, public health attribution, urban heat, pastoral systems, land-tenure interactions and climate-sensitive private-sector recovery. Environmental degradation, pollution, conflict debris and earthquakes have therefore not been relabelled as climate impacts without explicit evidence. []

- World Weather Attribution. *Human-induced climate change compounded by socio-economic water stressors increased severity of drought in Syria, Iraq and Iran* (World Weather Attribution7). 2023 (date basis: publication). https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/human-induced-climate-change-compounded-by-socio-economic-water-stressors-increased-severity-of-drought-in-syria-iraq-and-iran/ [SYR-SRC-002] Methodology: 36-month SPEI; observations and climate models using published peer-reviewed attribution methods Limitations: Regional basin result cannot be generalized to every Syrian locality; rapid attribution study, not a conflict-causality study.

- UNHCR. *UNHCR Syria Operational Update - March 2026* (UNHCR Data Portal3). 2026 (date basis: publication). https://data.unhcr.org/en/documents/details/122121 [SYR-SRC-005] Methodology: Operational displacement, return and assistance data Limitations: Rapidly changing population movements; estimates depend on operational data systems.

- PNAS / academic authors.
