## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2026-08-10 reports: Floods displaced 129,000 people in 2021 and 156,000 in 2022, concentrated in five states; poorer Klang Valley households can be trapped in repeated evacuations. [MYS-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2026 reports: Flood and poverty exposure overlap in Sarawak’s Kanowit, Kapit, Sibu and Song districts and coastal Sandakan, Semporna, Sabak Bernam and Batu Pahat; poorer households have weaker housing and fewer savings or insurance. [MYS-E-101]
- Source dated 2026 reports: During the first half of 2026, dry spells lowered paddy yields by over 60% in areas of Kedah, Perlis and Perak. [MYS-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-03 reports: Sabah uses iForSABAH, GeoForest and forest-fire danger maps, while both Borneo states report technical and budget gaps in climate monitoring. [MYS-E-109]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026 reports: Climate planning faces weak participation, low climate knowledge and public-trust concerns. [MYS-E-102]
- Source dated 2026 reports: Indigenous land-tenure insecurity in Sabah and Sarawak leaves customary rights contested and undermines forest governance and conservation. [MYS-E-103]
- Source dated 2026 reports: The CCDR proposes targeted housing, livelihood and financial support for poorer flood-prone communities and climate planning with women, youth, Orang Asli and other Indigenous peoples. [MYS-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-03 reports: Sabah’s climate governance is centralized in the Chief Minister’s Department; Sabah and Sarawak have overlapping mandates and silos that impede coordination. [MYS-E-108]
- Source dated 2026 reports: The late-2024 National Disaster Risk Reduction Policy 2030 decentralizes risk management, but local human and financial shortages hinder implementation. [MYS-E-111]
- Source dated 2026 reports: Adaptation policies remain fragmented across ministries and sectors; Malaysia’s National Adaptation Plan is still under development. [MYS-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The 2026 ISIS note warns that climate-related displacement can weaken protective systems and, where recognition/services are inadequate, aggravate resource tensions; this is conditional policy analysis, not observed climate-caused violence. [MYS-P-101; MYS-E-105, MYS-E-106, MYS-E-107]
  - Documented impact: Loss of housing/livelihood stability and disruption of social/economic/legal protections.
  - FCV mediator: Recognition, service access and protection gaps can limit safe coping and fair allocation during displacement.
  - Possible consequence: The ISIS authors warn of escalating resource competition and tensions with host communities or the state; no measured violence outcome.
