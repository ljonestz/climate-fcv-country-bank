# Ghana Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Kintampo assessment examples distinguish selected-community exposure and adaptive capacity; rankings from nine purposively selected communities cannot be generalized nationwide. [GHA-E-104, GHA-E-105, GHA-E-106]
- Gushegu adds a conditional heat/resource-pressure mechanism mediated by tenure and dispute institutions; political and identity dynamics remain central. [GHA-E-107, GHA-E-108, GHA-P-101]
- Regional profiles and warning-system arrangements add capacity detail, without proving effective warning reach or reduced losses. [GHA-E-102, GHA-E-103, GHA-E-109, GHA-E-110, GHA-E-111]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The CCDR reports about 45,000 people affected by floods annually and identifies roughly half of Ghana’s 550-km coastline as vulnerable to erosion and flooding associated with sea-level rise. [GHA-E-002]
- The brief says a three-month 2024 drought cut yields by about half in affected locations; recurring Bagre Dam floods have displaced communities and damaged farms, homes, and roads. [GHA-E-004]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: The assessment identifies Cheranda, Babato, Dawadawa and New Longoro among Kintampo communities most affected by bushfires, which damage vegetation and crops including cashew. [GHA-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-12-23 reports: The strategy assigns coastal flood/sea-level-rise risk to Greater Accra, Volta and Central; river flooding to Northern, Upper East and Eastern; drought and heat to Northern, Upper East, Upper West and Savannah. [GHA-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- CCDR modelling estimates climate change could raise Ghana’s poverty rate by 1–2 percentage points by 2050, or leave about one million more people poor than a no-climate-change case. Scenario: CCDR comparison with a no-climate-change case; the cited summary passage does not specify an emissions scenario. [GHA-E-001]
- Across the study area, respondents most often identified drought (39%), bushfires (22%), and floods (16%) as climate-related concerns; more than 97% of surveyed households engaged in crop farming. [GHA-E-003]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: The BTR reports vulnerability rising from coastal zones through the transition belt to northern savannas; Upper West is highest, while Greater Accra, Ashanti and Central are lowest across 216 districts and six ecological zones. [GHA-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: The assessment ranks Busuama, Dawadawa and Alhassan Akura as Kintampo’s most vulnerable communities and Kintampo least; Busuama combines low capacity, rainfed livelihoods, weak service/market access and flood-prone homes. [GHA-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-12-23 reports: The strategy identifies women and girls, people with disabilities and older people in rural and low-income areas as facing higher disaster risk amid limited resources, decision power and protective services. [GHA-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Ghana’s updated NDC sets out 19 policy actions, including 13 adaptation actions, and estimates planned coverage of 38 million people by 2030, mainly women and youth; EPA and NDPC have coordination and planning roles. Scenario: Government implementation plan through 2030; intended coverage, not realized reach. [GHA-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: Kintampo and Cheranda scored high in adaptive capacity, linked to credit, remittances, markets, irrigation, crop diversity and extension; Busuama, Dawadawa, Alhassan Akura and New Longoro scored very low. [GHA-E-105]
- The December 2025 strategy's regional composite rates coping capacity very low in Savannah, North East, Oti and Northern and very high in Greater Accra. [GHA-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-12 reports: The BTR reports local-government shortages of finance and staff, weak rural/remote data integration, limited technical capacity and coordination, and early-warning and monitoring gaps that restrict preparedness. [GHA-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: The BTR reports warning communications at NADMO headquarters and 20 subnational offices, plus community disaster-risk committees trained in local assessment, response planning and preparedness. [GHA-E-103]
- Source dated 2022-12-09 reports: A Gushegu chief-led committee hears farmer-herder disputes and may decide compensation, but is informal and cannot enforce decisions; some parties distrust its decisions or allege favoritism. [GHA-E-108]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The brief warns that chronic unreliability in northern food systems could increase migration and conflict risks related to competition over land and water through 2080. Scenario: Projection to 2080; the cited passage does not identify the climate or socioeconomic scenario. [GHA-E-005]
- Source dated 2022-12-09 reports: In Gushegu, the author links heat stress and resource scarcity to herders’ mobility and feeding adaptations that damage crops or breach tree tenure and can trigger farmer-herder disputes. [GHA-E-107]

Ghana Country Climate and Development Report. 2022-10 (date basis: document-version). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099945110272227913/pdf/P1772610ec78640c40be7e027e5c6ea517f.pdf [GHA-SRC-001] Methodology: World Bank CCDR with climate-risk, economic, and poverty analysis. Ghana’s Updated Nationally Determined Contribution under the Paris Agreement (2020–2030). 2021-11 (date basis: document-version). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/NDC/2022-06/Ghana%27s%20Updated%20Nationally%20Determined%20Contribution%20to%20the%20UNFCCC_2021.pdf [GHA-SRC-003] Methodology: Government NDC setting out mitigation and adaptation actions, institutional responsibilities, and intended coverage.
