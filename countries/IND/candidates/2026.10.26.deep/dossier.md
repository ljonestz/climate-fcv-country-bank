# India Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Comparative district flood/drought and heat indices identify named differences; outdoor workers, people with limited shelter and other vulnerable groups face different coping constraints. [IND-E-101, IND-E-102, IND-E-103, IND-E-104, IND-E-105, IND-E-106, IND-E-109]
- A national study finds drought–IPV associations, with different samples for precipitation and socioeconomic drought, but cannot establish causality or income mediation. Heat-plan counts describe planning coverage, not effectiveness. [IND-E-107, IND-E-108, IND-E-110]

Critical remaining gaps:
- A peer-reviewed national study provides explicit drought–intimate-partner-violence associations but cannot establish causality or test the proposed household-income pathway; this is not evidence of an armed-conflict pathway. []
- The reviewed material does not directly establish that conflict or insecurity changes climate exposure or blocks adaptation access. []
- District heat classifications use 1982–2022 hazard data with older Census and NFHS indicators; they should not be read as a refreshed 2026 risk inventory. []
- No complete causal income-mediated drought→IPV chain is assembled: the study explicitly could not test the proposed mediator. The two drought definitions have different analytical samples. []
- District hazard/risk examples are not assigned the national study's violence associations as local effect estimates. No armed-conflict pathway or reverse insecurity constraint was verified. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-05-20 reports: Comparing 2012–2022 with 1982–2011, CEEW found extra warm nights per summer in Mumbai (15), Bengaluru (11), Bhopal and Jaipur (7 each), Delhi (6) and Chennai (4); warm nights rose faster than hot days. [IND-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-07-20 reports: Delhi's SAPCC 2.0 assessment identifies South Delhi as most and New Delhi as least climate-vulnerable; it projects higher summer maximum temperatures and more very hot days by mid-century, with cool roofs, cooling corridors, green infrastructure, drainage and wetland measures envisaged. [IND-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-12-17 reports: The district assessment placed 51 districts in very high and 118 in high flood-risk bands; about 85% of those districts are in Assam, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Gujarat, Odisha and Jammu and Kashmir. [IND-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-12-17 reports: The assessment placed 91 districts in very high and 188 in high drought-risk bands; more than 85% of these districts are in Bihar, Assam, Jharkhand, Odisha, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Chhattisgarh, Kerala, Uttarakhand and Haryana. [IND-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-12-17 reports: Among the 50 districts with highest flood risk and 50 with highest drought risk, 11 were very high risk for both, including Patna, Alappuzha, Charaideo, Dibrugarh, Sibsagar, South Salmara-Mankachar, Golaghat, Kendrapara, Murshidabad, Nadia and Uttar Dinajpur. [IND-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-05-20 reports: CEEW classed 417 of 734 districts (57%), home to 76% of residents, at high or very high heat risk; Delhi, Maharashtra, Goa, Kerala and Gujarat ranked among the highest-risk states. [IND-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-05-20 reports: CEEW found high heat vulnerability in districts of seven named states, where heat combined with chronic illness, older people and under-fives; Odisha districts with green cover and blue infrastructure had lower vulnerability. [IND-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-05-01 reports: NHRC cited 3,798 NCRB-recorded heat and sun-stroke deaths in 2018–2022 and identified economically weaker people, outdoor workers, older people, children and homeless people as at risk from inadequate shelter and resources; it urged 11 states to provide protections. [IND-E-109]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The 2023 study reports drought–IPV associations, not causation. Precipitation analysis used 122,696 married women across NFHS 2015–16 and 2019–21; government-declared socioeconomic drought analysis used 62,464 women in NFHS 2015–16 only. Adjusted odds ratios vary by drought definition and violence type. [IND-E-110]

## Bibliography and review decision

- Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Science and Technology, Government of India. District-level Climate Risk Assessment for India: Mapping Flood and Drought Risks released. 2024-12-17 (date basis: publication). https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2085134&lang=2&reg=48 [IND-SRC-101] Methodology: Government release summarizing a district assessment by IIT Mandi, IIT Guwahati and CSTEP. Limitations: Risk bands are comparative; the release summarizes rather than reproduces the underlying report.
- Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW). How Extreme Heat is Impacting India: Assessing District-level Heat Risk. 2025-05-20 (date basis: publication). https://www.ceew.in/publications/mapping-climate-risks-and-impacts-of-extreme-heatwave-disaster-in-indian-districts [IND-SRC-102] Methodology: CEEW district heat-risk index using 35 hazard, exposure and vulnerability indicators. Limitations: Relative bands, dated indicators; state findings need validation.
- Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, Government of India. Mitigating the Urban Heat Island Effect in Indian Cities. 2026-07-20 (date basis: publication). https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2286772&lang=1&reg=48 [IND-SRC-103] Methodology: Ministry press release summarizing heat-action-plan status and the Delhi SAPCC 2.0 assessment. Limitations: Ministry-reported plan counts and planned measures do not establish effectiveness or completed outcomes.
- National Human Rights Commission of India. NHRC calls for preventive measures by 11 States to protect vulnerable people from heat waves. 2025-05-01 (date basis: publication). https://nhrc.nic.in/media/press-release/nhrc-india-calls-for-preventive-measures-by-11-states-to-protect-the-lives-vulnerable-people-from-heat-waves-in-this-summer [IND-SRC-104] Methodology: NHRC press release describing its communication to state governments and cited NCRB figures. Limitations: The reported death count is attributed to NCRB; NHRC's requested measures are not evidence of implementation.
- American Journal of Epidemiology / National Library of Medicine PubMed Central. Climate and Gender: Association Between Droughts and Intimate Partner Violence in India. 2023-11-15 (date basis: publication). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10999644/ [IND-SRC-105] Methodology: Cross-sectional analysis of 122,696 married women, combining survey IPV reports with precipitation-based and government socioeconomic drought classifications. Limitations: Self-reported and cross-sectional data limit generalizability and causal inference; household income pathway was not tested.
