## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2020-04-15 reports: RCP4.5 projects 5–10% summer/autumn precipitation reductions in southern Lika and southern Dalmatia for 2011–2040, with more drought periods. Scenario: RCP4.5 relative to 1971–2000; projection period 2011–2040. [HRV-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: Registered 2010–2021 disaster damages totaled EUR 3.318 billion; drought accounted for EUR 1.095 billion, floods EUR 539 million and fires EUR 249 million. [HRV-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: Reported disaster losses were highest in Primorje-Gorski Kotar (EUR 471 million), Osijek-Baranja (EUR 458 million), Virovitica-Podravina (EUR 448 million) and Vukovar-Srijem (EUR 324 million). [HRV-E-107]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The 2024 screening identifies 2,951 potentially significant flood-risk areas in the Danube basin (68% of area; 2.37 million residents/91%) and 1,107 in the Adriatic basin (59%; 1.09 million/86%); mapped area grew about 5% nationally since 2018. [HRV-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-01-30 reports: OECD reports 910 fatalities and EUR 4.1 billion losses from climate-related extreme events (1980–2023); flood risk covers 15% of non-coastal territory, including Zagreb and Rijeka. [HRV-E-110]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2020-04-15 reports: The Strategy frames climate change as a Croatian security threat and says climate-related relocation may indirectly destabilize society, while noting migration is multi-causal. [HRV-E-101]
- Source dated 2020-04-15 reports: The Strategy reports earlier, shorter growing seasons and lower yields in Slavonia and Dalmatia; Dalmatian fruit, olive and grape growers face heat and drought stress. [HRV-E-103]
- Source dated 2020-04-15 reports: Summer drought was the largest single cause of agricultural climate-variability damage in 1980–2014; reported 2013–2016 loss totaled HRK 3 billion. [HRV-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: County social-vulnerability indicators include age, income inequality, disability, household size and literacy; economic indicators include poverty risk, unemployment, fiscal balance and county GDP. [HRV-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-01-30 reports: OECD identifies low-lying islands and river deltas as vulnerable to sea-level rise, coastal floods and salinisation; 2024 brought heavy-rain floods, record March rain, storms and widespread summer heatwaves. [HRV-E-111]
