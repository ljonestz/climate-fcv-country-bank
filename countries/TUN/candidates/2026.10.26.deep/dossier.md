# Tunisia Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Governorate detail differentiates northern drought and flood-service exposure from inland/southern heat; the health brief's 2050 projections are kept distinct from current rural and peri-urban service gaps. [TUN-E-101, TUN-E-102, TUN-E-103, TUN-E-104, TUN-E-105]
- Water scarcity and regional disparities support a bounded national social-conflict pathway. Separate local evidence from Kesra, Oued El Barka and Zaouiet Ben Ammar describes governance and water-reliability gains, not climate-attributed conflict reduction. [TUN-E-108, TUN-E-109, TUN-E-110, TUN-E-111, TUN-P-101]
- Recent national water-scarcity and reform summaries coexist with older meteorological, coastal and groundwater evidence; planned investments and recommendations are not delivered results. [TUN-E-112, TUN-E-115, TUN-E-116, TUN-E-117, TUN-E-118, TUN-E-119, TUN-E-120]

Critical remaining gaps:
- UNDP explicitly links climate-aggravated water scarcity and regional disparities with social conflict, but its 2020 document gives no named governorate conflict episode and does not establish violent conflict. []
- ILO reports reduced water-related conflicts alongside project-supported climate resilience, but does not state the conflicts were climate-induced or quantify a before/after trend. []
- No inspected primary source directly documents insecurity blocking climate adaptation or climate-service access. The ILO military-restriction detail is contextual and not an assessed causal pathway. []
- The full 92-page World Bank CHVA was not retrieved; claims from that assessment are limited to its five-page French policy brief. The 2021 World Bank Climate Risk Country Profile PDF returned 403. []
- UNDP water and coastal baselines are mostly from 2015-2019; FAO indicators have mixed vintages; INM's observed temperature period ends in 2012. These are not current trend estimates. []
- The 2026 World Bank press release summarizes the Tunisia Economic Monitor but does not expose its model assumptions or subnational job and water impacts. []
- The 2026 health policy brief supplies a 2050 period but does not state the model scenario for population flood, dry-day and heat projections; these are low-confidence summary-level claims, not independently replicated estimates. []
- Kesra's reported reduction in water disputes is retained as local resilience evidence, not proof that climate caused the disputes or that the intervention's causal impact was identified. []
- No governorate-specific climate-driven violent incident or reverse insecurity-to-adaptation mechanism was established. Military restrictions around one project village are contextual only. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2026-06 reports: By 2050, 1.1m people face ≥15-cm flood exposure; highest in Ben Arous, Tunis, Sfax and Nabeul. [TUN-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: By 2050, annual consecutive dry days may exceed 114; increases largest in Béja, Bizerte and Manouba. [TUN-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Heat exposure highest inland and south; temperatures rise 1.9-5.3°C by 2050. [TUN-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: By 2050, flooding may disrupt up to one-third of emergency services in Bizerte and Manouba, disable Jendouba facilities, and expose primary-care centers in Tunis, Manouba and Ariana. [TUN-E-104]
- Source dated 2020-12-03 reports: UNDP cited 2015 data showing Tunisia's coastal zone held two-thirds of the population, 79% of economic activities and 76% of tourism investment, and was highly climate-vulnerable. [TUN-E-112]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO reports about 600 mm rain in the north, 300 mm in the center, 150 mm in the south and under 100 mm in the extreme southwest; 80% of surface water is in the north and 5% in the south. [TUN-E-114]
- Source dated not stated reports: INM reports a significant rise of about 2.1°C in annual maximum, mean and minimum temperatures over 1978-2012, with regional differences. [TUN-E-116]
- Source dated not stated reports: INM projects less rainfall and higher temperatures under RCP4.5 and RCP8.5; under RCP8.5, rainfall falls 1-14% by 2050 and 18-27% by 2100. [TUN-E-117]
- Source dated 2026-09-22 reports: Tunisia has about 380 m³ of renewable freshwater per person yearly, below the absolute-scarcity threshold. [TUN-E-118]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated not stated reports: FAO notes drought affects food, health and sanitation; rural women bear added burdens collecting alternative water sources. [TUN-E-113]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Sisseb-El Alem aquifer in Kairouan shows a falling piezometric level, which FAO identifies as evidence of high overexploitation. [TUN-E-115]
- Source dated 2026-09-22 reports: World Bank estimates water scarcity could cut GDP 6.4% by 2050 and eliminate at least 30% of agricultural jobs by mid-century; agriculture employs up to half of rural workers. [TUN-E-119]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-03-19 reports: A rehabilitated spring serves 342 Zaouiet Ben Ammar households; the project site was in an area long restricted for military reasons. [TUN-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Rural/peri-urban primary-care sites lack staff, diagnostics and supplies; high-risk areas have emergency-access gaps. [TUN-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: CHVA recommends near-term emergency readiness and five-year upgrades to health facilities, WASH, supply chains and climate finance. [TUN-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-03-19 reports: Kesra water works supported about 100 farmers/20 ha, raised fig-tree density from 40 to 70/ha and created 4,000 workdays. [TUN-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-03-19 reports: Oued El Barka restored water reliability: 120 farmers returned, including 100 smallholders who left land from scarcity; water did not run out during 2021-2023 droughts. [TUN-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-09-22 reports: Plan Eau 2050 envisions about US$900 million yearly through mid-century; World Bank identifies water governance, service performance and protections for vulnerable households as priorities. [TUN-E-120]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-aggravated water scarcity interacts with regional disparities and possible social conflict; no violent local incident is established. [TUN-P-101; TUN-E-111]
  - Documented impact: Uneven resource availability and pressure on water users.
  - FCV mediator: Regional disparities in water resources and access.
  - Possible consequence: UNDP describes these disparities as frequent drivers of social conflicts, without identifying violent incidents.
