# South Africa Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Provincial climate impacts and household vulnerability differ, with water, sanitation and food poverty shaping sensitivity in named provinces. [ZAF-E-101, ZAF-E-102]
- Cape Winelands evidence identifies De Doorns, Touwsrivier and Witzenberg vulnerabilities, but the indicators are dated and future risks are modeled. [ZAF-E-109, ZAF-E-110, ZAF-E-111, ZAF-E-112]
- National mandates, municipal prioritization and community organizations provide an adaptation-readiness picture, not proof of delivered outcomes. Historical weather-crime associations remain causally and geographically bounded. [ZAF-E-103, ZAF-E-104, ZAF-E-105, ZAF-E-106, ZAF-E-107, ZAF-E-108, ZAF-E-113]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No country-specific source-stated climate-change-to-organized-conflict or conflict-to-adaptation-constraint pathway was verified. The accessible crime study concerns weather and crime, not anthropogenic climate attribution. []
- A 2024 South Africa Biennial Transparency Report and two relevant World Bank PDFs were discoverable, but direct PDF reading failed at the web reader's 10 MiB limit; indexed passages were not used as evidence. []
- The most granular local vulnerability profile found was for Cape Winelands; it relies on dated 1996/2011 social indicators, 2017 water data and modeled future risk, so it does not provide a current nationwide municipal comparison. []
- No source-stated climate-change-to-organized-conflict or conflict-to-adaptation mechanism was verified. Historical weather-crime associations do not identify a mediated pathway or anthropogenic climate effect. []
- The Cape Winelands local profile uses 1996/2011 social indicators, 2017 water data and modeled mid-century risks. It is not a current nationwide municipal comparison. []
- NDC legal and planning commitments are not evidence of compliance or delivered services. Workshop statements are not representative municipal or farmer surveys. []
- Some relevant BTR and World Bank PDFs exceeded the web reader's size limit; their indexed snippets were not used as evidence. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-10 reports: The NDC reports recent floods in KwaZulu-Natal, Eastern and Western Cape; Northern Cape/interior heatwaves; droughts in Eastern Cape, Western Cape and Free State; Gauteng storms and Western Cape wildfires. Poor communities suffered most; businesses and municipalities struggled to recover. [ZAF-E-101]
- Source dated 2023-07 reports: Under RCP 8.5, increased future flood risk is projected for Paarl, Stellenbosch, De Doorns, Touwsrivier and Montagu. [ZAF-E-110]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-10 reports: The NDC identifies high household climate vulnerability in KwaZulu-Natal, North West, Limpopo and Eastern Cape; eastern Eastern Cape municipalities are more vulnerable. Unsafe water/toilet access and food poverty are prominent. [ZAF-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-03-31 reports: A sector report presented to participants identified water and land stress as compounding climate impacts for smallholder and household producers reliant on rainfall, marginal land, inputs and finance. [ZAF-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-03-31 reports: The built-environment presentation says informal settlements and systems fall outside formal controls intended to protect people from hazards such as floodlines; poorer, more vulnerable communities live in these areas. [ZAF-E-107]
- Source dated 2023-07 reports: De Doorns and Touwsrivier score very high socioeconomic vulnerability; Touwsrivier also ranks worst for Breede Valley connectivity. [ZAF-E-109]
- Source dated 2023-07 reports: The profile's 2017-based table gives Witzenberg demand of 146.71 litres per capita daily against 144.23 supply, with a vulnerability score of 1.02. [ZAF-E-111]
- Source dated 2023-07 reports: Witzenberg's water vulnerability score is projected to rise to 1.7 by 2050 with population pressure, declining rainfall and runoff, and increased evaporation. [ZAF-E-112]
- A study of monthly weather and police records for 1,158 South African police wards in 2001-2012 reports warmer-month associations with violent crime and modest rural property-taking responses to growing-season drought, not urban crime. Effects are subtle, mediation is unidentified and weather findings do not establish anthropogenic climate attribution. [ZAF-E-113]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The 2025 NDC sets proposed 2031-2035 priorities for disaster, health, sanitation, emergency accommodation and care support, including informal settlements and marginalized flood-displaced groups. These are policy commitments, not delivered services. [ZAF-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-03-31 reports: Participants highlighted community-based organizations for their direct local interface and knowledge of realities facing poorer and more vulnerable people as potential adaptation-readiness partners. [ZAF-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-10 reports: The 2024 Climate Change Act establishes recurring climate-risk and vulnerability assessments, adaptation planning and reporting for national, provincial and municipal government and identified sectors. [ZAF-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-03-31 reports: Workshop synthesis says climate risks were not perceived among municipalities' top ten priorities, making climate response difficult to prioritize amid pressing governance issues. [ZAF-E-105]
