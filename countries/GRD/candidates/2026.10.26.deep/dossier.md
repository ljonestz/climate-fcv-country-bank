# Grenada Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Named parish poverty and sister-island water dependence help distinguish vulnerability within Grenada; the underlying indicators are historical or undated. [GRD-E-101, GRD-E-105, GRD-E-108]
- The draft highlights shelter protection risks. NAWASA's October 2026 report provides named-site water investments, with operational work and outcome verification still outstanding. [GRD-E-102, GRD-E-110, GRD-E-111, GRD-E-112]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No countrywide causal estimate of climate impacts on conflict or violence is established by these sources. []
- Full subnational coverage and independently evaluated long-term response effectiveness remain gaps. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []
- The bank contains no completed mediated climate-FCV pathway; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- The official IOM climate-mobility assessment returned HTTP 403 and the Government-led Beryl PDNA PDF exceeded the browser fetch limit; no claims use indexed summaries. []
- A publicly linked IFRC rapid assessment was marked Internal on its cover and excluded; the WFP BRISP report PDF presented a bot-verification page. []
- No accessible country-specific source connected armed conflict or insecurity with climate impacts or adaptation delivery; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- The Government climate policy file is a December draft; its risk statements and proposed actions are not treated as adopted or measured outcomes. []
- These sources do not establish a countrywide causal estimate of climate-related conflict or violence. []
- The draft's policy period is internally inconsistent (2025–2030 versus 2025–2035); adoption and numerical source dates need human confirmation. []
- Shelter GBV is a stated protection risk, not observed incidence or evidence of armed conflict. Administrative procurement and coordination barriers are not attributed to insecurity. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Hurricane Beryl damaged Grenada's economy and infrastructure in 2024; recovery increased fiscal pressures. [GRD-E-001]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Unsustainable agriculture, settlement expansion, forest loss and climate change jointly pressure Grenada's productive landscapes. [GRD-E-004]
- Beryl damaged housing and livelihoods in Carriacou and Petite Martinique inJuly2024. [GRD-E-005]
- The undated draft reports a 2020 national poverty-or-poverty-risk figure of 54.8%, with the highest rates in St. Mark and St. Andrew; weak savings and housing security increase household vulnerability. [GRD-E-101]
- Source dated not stated reports: The draft says nearly half of households are female-headed and women and children face higher post-disaster gender-based-violence risk, particularly in emergency shelters. [GRD-E-102]
- The draft policy identifies service disruption, community stability and climate-related mobility as security concerns; these are policy risk statements, not measured conflict or displacement effects. [GRD-E-103]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO identifies Carriacou, Petite Martinique, southern and southeastern mainland Grenada, and the drier north as drought-affected areas, with water scarcity straining tourism, agriculture and local supplies. [GRD-E-105]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO reports Carriacou livestock losses of 20% and 40% in the 1984 and 1992 droughts, and a 17% decline in national banana production during the 2009 drought. [GRD-E-106]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO says water shortages disproportionately affect women and girls through missed work or school, compromised hygiene and added care for sick relatives. [GRD-E-107]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry reports mainland dry-season daily yield of 31,800 m³ against demand of 54,600 m³; 91% of households in Carriacou and Petite Martinique use cisterns as main storage. [GRD-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Grenada's2026 financing programme combined contingent disaster liquidity with water, infrastructure and social-safety-net reforms. [GRD-E-002]
- NAWASA reported in October 2026 that two 100,000-imperial-gallon tanks had been constructed at Belair and Mt. Royal. The estimated three-to-six-day storage buffer still depended on remaining operational connections and commissioning. [GRD-E-110]
- Source dated 2026-10-11 reports: NAWASA says the Mt. Royal communal rainwater retrofit serves about 56 households and 169 residents; storage was also added at Princess Royal Hospital and two medical stations. [GRD-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-10-11 reports: NAWASA reports over 300 farmers benefited across the tri-island state; on Carriacou, 18 received drip or micro-sprinkler support and 22 gained over 60,000 gallons of harvesting storage; 19 shade houses remain in phased installation. [GRD-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Grenada's climate-smart farming faces institutional planning gaps, limited finance and technical services, and limited knowledge of sustainable practices. [GRD-E-003]
- UNDP and CDEMA supported residential construction-code training for Grenada's public technical officers after Beryl. [GRD-E-006]
- Source dated not stated reports: Stakeholder consultations identify limited staff, slow procurement, coordination difficulties and overlapping agency mandates as implementation barriers. [GRD-E-104]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry says water responsibilities are split among agencies without a single sector-wide oversight entity; the 2020 Water Policy proposed separate resource-management oversight. [GRD-E-109]
