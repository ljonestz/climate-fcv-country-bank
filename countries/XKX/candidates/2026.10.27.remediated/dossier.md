## Climate pressures and exposure

- The CCDR compendium reports frequent forest fires, floods, and landslides; 36.8 percent of Kosovo's area is at medium wildfire risk and 15.8 percent at high risk, with forests increasingly susceptible since 2000. [XKX-E-001]
- The compendium says mountainous areas face flash floods, including in the Drini basin where floods occur every two to three years; severe floods in 2013, 2014, and 2016 caused more than €4 million in estimated damage. [XKX-E-002]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Early-2023 floods affected about 22,000 people, including 3,000 children, mostly in Skenderaj and Mitrovica. Average-year floods affect about 10,000 people, damaging homes/schools and interrupting water and power. [XKX-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-08-05 reports: A June–July 2026 Podujeva survey of 101 respondents across 35 settlements found recurrent, materially damaging floods among participating households, with impacts concentrated on agricultural land and crops. [XKX-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The government strategy identifies poverty, limited health and social-welfare services, illegal construction in hazard zones, inadequate water and sewage infrastructure, and weak municipal land-use planning as factors that heighten climate-hazard impacts. [XKX-E-003]
- The NDC reports drought losses above €50 million in 2009-2017, mainly crop failures; the 2022 drought affected agriculture across more than 10 percent of Kosovo's territory. [XKX-E-004]
- The NDC identifies disproportionate climate risks for women, children, and marginalised groups; women own 4.9 percent of farm land despite 49 percent of agricultural labor, while floods can disrupt children's education and services. [XKX-E-005]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The brief names Ferizaj and Prizren as most vulnerable; children facing both hazard exposure and multidimensional poverty face greatest risks. [XKX-E-101]
- Source dated 2024 reports: After 2021 and 2023 were Kosovo's hottest years, family medicine centres in Pristina and Mitrovica reported more child admissions, especially above 30°C. [XKX-E-102]
- Source dated 2024 reports: UNICEF flags disabled/health-condition and Roma, Ashkali and Egyptian children as facing added challenges; fires increased since 2007 in Mitrovica and Prizren, while human-caused fires removed 13% of Kosovo tree cover in 2001–2023. [XKX-E-104]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The plan flags women in rural Prizren as potentially responsible for agricultural water management yet having limited water access; it calls for women's participation in planning, early warnings and drought-tolerant seed support. [XKX-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2023 reports: War ordnance contaminated wooded hills west of Peja, which faces flash floods, hotter summers and milder, less snowy winters. Clearance released 355,502 m², reopening park trails and resource access. [XKX-E-107]
- Source dated 2023 reports: In Mitrovica, war/industrial-closure migration and weakened services coexist with hotter summers, less snow and possible July–August drought. Clearance restored Barë grazing/firewood access and Bajgora woodland/pipeline access; wind power was exported. [XKX-E-108]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: Prizren's 2023–2028 rural plan sets out 14 measures and 74 proposed actions with estimated costs of €22.4955 million across agriculture, biodiversity, forestry and rural tourism. [XKX-E-109]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The plan proposed irrigation-system rehabilitation studies, water-storage studies, Manastrica Dam design/construction for drinking water and irrigation, drip-irrigation subsidies and ponds for irrigation/firefighting; dam construction was scheduled for 2025–2028. [XKX-E-110]
- The non-representative Podujeva survey summary reports drinking-water interruptions, drainage/maintenance gaps and uneven warning access among respondents. [XKX-E-113]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2023 reports: KMAC weighs risk, environment/development and poverty in site priorities; hazards contributing to climate impacts receive top clearance priority. Operators lacked climate-specific procedures at report time. [XKX-E-105]
