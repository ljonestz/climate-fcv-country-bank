## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-05 reports: Spring 2024 floods in northern and western Kazakhstan evacuated over 115,000 people and damaged farm assets and small plots. [KAZ-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-05 reports: The report expects stronger, longer drought affecting northern rain-fed grain and summer-autumn pastures. Scenario: The cited passage refers to global warming scenarios but specifies none or a projection period. [KAZ-E-102]
- Source dated 2025 reports: GIZ projects end-period national drought probabilities near 50%, 31% and 15% for low, medium and high hazards. Scenario: SSP5-8.5; modeled period through 2080 or later; national probabilities for low, medium and high hazard classes. [KAZ-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025 reports: The report says 2021 heat reached 46.5°C in Kyzylorda, Mangystau and Turkestan, drying soil and pasture feed. [KAZ-E-106]
- Source dated 2025 reports: GIZ reports cited findings of Mangystau livestock losses and full runoff use in Shu-Talas and Nura-Sarysu basins. [KAZ-E-107]
- Source dated 2018-04 reports: World Bank mapped Kyzylorda, Zhambyl, Almaty, East Kazakhstan and Kostanay as climate-susceptible; most farming was in at-risk areas. [KAZ-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- In the historical Chu–Talas account, climate-related stress can aggravate allocation, information and canal-management disputes; the Aspara prevention example highlights dialogue/accounting without implying climate determinism. [KAZ-P-101; KAZ-E-110, KAZ-E-111]
  - Documented impact: Pressure on seasonal irrigation-water availability and allocation.
  - FCV mediator: Disputed allocations, weak shared information and damaged/lossy canal infrastructure.
  - Possible consequence: Risk of cross-border disputes; the source's Aspara account describes diplomacy/accounting as helping avoid violence, not climate inevitably causing violence.
