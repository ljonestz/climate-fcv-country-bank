## Climate pressures and exposure


## Differentiated vulnerability

- The CCDR’s cited projection shows land in Côte d’Ivoire rated over 50% suitable for cocoa falling by 57% in the 2050s, while lower-suitability classes expand. [CIV-E-001]
- The CCDR reports severe flooding in Abidjan in June 2018, with more than 20 deaths, and identifies low-lying informal settlements and poorer residents among those exposed. [CIV-E-003]
- The NDC links heat with increased malaria risk and floods or polluted water with cholera and diarrhoea, noting particular risks for children, older people and those with chronic illness where health capacity and access are limited. [CIV-E-004]
- The NDC reports that women hold less than 20% of land and have substantially lower access to finance, while describing women, youth, children, older people and people with disabilities as especially vulnerable to climate impacts. [CIV-E-005]
- Bafing proposal consultations describe women accessing land mainly through husbands and dry-season distance barriers to water, with herders dependent on surface sources. [CIV-E-104]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NDC 3.0 plans northern pastoral measures, forest-zone agroforestry and coastal-erosion protection; these are not evaluated results. [CIV-E-006]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024 reports: Insecurity raises concerns about remote and cross-border travel; livestock-border restrictions made transhumant movements harder and weakened exchanges among herders, hosts and authorities. IOM proposes local adaptation capacity, community risk assessments, resilient infrastructure and flood early warning. [CIV-E-107]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Drought-related mobility may increase resource competition and tensions. [CIV-P-001; CIV-E-002, CIV-E-007]
  - Documented impact: Changed pastoral movement.
  - FCV mediator: Farmer-herder competition over land and water.
  - Possible consequence: Potential community tensions.

  - Uncertainty: Prospective qualitative mechanism; no measured recruitment or causal conflict effect.
- In Bafing, seasonal resource scarcity intersects with transhumance, overlapping land use and crop damage to compound farmer-herder tensions. [CIV-P-101; CIV-E-103, CIV-E-104, CIV-E-105]
  - Documented impact: Reduced pasture and water concentrate herds and farm plots near residual resources, increasing crop damage risk.
  - FCV mediator: Transhumance, overlapping land/water use, corridor encroachment and weak pastoral infrastructure shape resource competition.
  - Possible consequence: The proposal describes recurrent farmer-herder tensions and crop/livestock damage across Koro, Touba and Ouaninou, with social-cohesion consequences.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The CCDR says climate-related migration and more intense drought could add to north–south movement and herder–farmer competition over scarce land and water, with potential stability and recruitment implications in vulnerable rural areas. [CIV-E-002]
- The qualitative case study identifies possible risks from large-scale environmental migration and community tensions, insecurity in economic, health and food systems, and increased herder–farmer conflict; it says these risks can be averted. [CIV-E-007]
- Source dated 2026-04-01 reports: Government links rising temperature and falling rainfall to arable-land loss and greater land-conflict risk; its matrix also names farmer/herder shares, youth, population growth and migration as vulnerability factors. [CIV-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-04-01 reports: Lower rainfall reduced Bouaké's Loka reservoir (70% of city supply) in 2018; three weeks without piped water prompted emergency tankers and temporary migration. Northern shortages also constrained agriculture. [CIV-E-102]
- The Bafing proposal describes dry-season pasture/water scarcity concentrating herds and farms near residual resources, with crop damage and farmer-herder tensions across Koro, Touba and Ouaninou. [CIV-E-103]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Approved proposal plans water/fodder pastoral areas and community parks to reduce herd wandering and crop damage; it also supports local conflict-management mechanisms, climate-data use, and women/youth livelihood diversification. [CIV-E-105]
- Source dated 2024 reports: IOM describes northern farmer-herder competition over pasture and water; it says climate effects on agriculture push rural mobility and aggravate existing tensions and social-cohesion risks. [CIV-E-106]
