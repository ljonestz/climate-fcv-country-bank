# Iran Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- New evidence differentiates donor-basin water injustice and supply stress in Khuzestan and Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari from drought-related political responses in Isfahan and Hamedan. Water policy and climate effects are not disentangled. [IRN-E-105, IRN-E-106, IRN-E-108]
- Khuzestan aquifer observations add named local water-system vulnerabilities around Dezful and Andimeshk, but the measurements end in 2018. They must not be presented as a current household-access assessment. [IRN-E-110, IRN-E-111]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- WWA estimated the 2021-2025 five-year Iranian SPEI-60 drought at about a 1-in-5-year event in the present climate versus roughly 1-in-250 in a 1.3°C cooler climate. [IRN-E-001]
- WWA estimated five-year rainfall totals in Iran during 2021-2025 as approximately a 1-in-25-year low-rainfall event, but attribution uncertainty encompassed no change. [IRN-E-002]
- Iran's 2015 INDC projected mean temperature to rise by more than 1.5°C and surface runoff to fall 25% by 2030. Scenario: to 2030 [IRN-E-003]
- World Bank WDI reports annual freshwater withdrawals equal to 72% of internal renewable water resources in 2022. [IRN-E-004]
- A nationwide study estimated approximately 74 km³ of groundwater depletion during 2002-2015. [IRN-E-005]
- WWA describes Iran's current water crisis as jointly arising from repeated drought, increasing evaporative demand, water-intensive agriculture and unsustainable groundwater extraction. [IRN-E-011]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The same study found extreme groundwater overdraft over roughly 77% of national land area and concluded human withdrawals were the principal basin-scale cause, although meteorological/hydrological drought intensified depletion. [IRN-E-006]
- The World Bank expects high food inflation, import shortages and conflict-related shocks to increase poverty. Scenario: current outlook [IRN-E-022]
- The 2024 article reports tanker dependence in over200villages of Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari and salinity-related water and date-palm losses in Khuzestan, drawing on cited historical sources. [IRN-E-106]
- For 2008–2018, the Khuzestan study identifies groundwater decline in eastern/northeastern aquifers and drawdown up to 33 metres around Dezful and Andimeshk. [IRN-E-110]
- The study finds groundwater quality generally declines southward; 27% of sampled groundwater was unsuitable for agriculture, with better-quality aquifers concentrated north and east. [IRN-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- WWA reports farmers as particularly exposed because irrigation-dependent income, higher input costs and limited livelihood alternatives reduce adaptive capacity and may contribute to urban migration. [IRN-E-012]
- Iran's current FAO portfolio includes a US$3 million National Adaptation Plan project for priority sectors for 2025-2028. [IRN-E-014]
- FAO launched a 2025 climate-resilient agriculture initiative using drought-tolerant crops, good agricultural practices, participatory varietal selection and farmer field schools. [IRN-E-015]
- UNDP reports more than 700 rural women involved in green jobs in its Lake Urmia-related programme. [IRN-E-019]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Iran's INDC states that sanctions and financial/technological restrictions constrain capacity development and institutional structures for climate action. [IRN-E-013]
- UNDP's May 2025 wetland programme factsheet reports an average 27.7% reduction in irrigation water use and approximately 22.5% increase in crop yields among participating pilot-site farmers. [IRN-E-016]
- UNDP reports formal collaboration among environmental, agricultural, community and private-sector actors through integrated wetland-management structures. [IRN-E-017]
- A UNDP terminal evaluation consultancy for the 2021-2024 Lake Urmia and other wetlands component was publicly procured in 2023. [IRN-E-020]
- The World Bank reports that conflict, sanctions and social unrest have disrupted Iranian economic activity and that damaged infrastructure and water/energy shortages constrain non-oil growth. [IRN-E-021]
- The author argues that drought-related water problems can reduce political trust and that weak water-governance responses shape local contention. [IRN-E-109]
