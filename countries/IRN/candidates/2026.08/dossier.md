# Iran Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Iran's hydroclimatic risk is increasing, but the national water crisis is a compound climate-management problem rather than a climate-only outcome. Supporting evidence: IRN-E-001-E-007; pathways IRN-P-001 and P-003. (World Weather Attribution) [IRN-E-001, IRN-E-002, IRN-E-003, IRN-E-004, IRN-E-005, IRN-E-006, IRN-E-007, IRN-P-001, IRN-P-003]
- The best direct evidence for Climate-FCV interaction is local to the ZayandehRud Basin, where climate/water stress interacts with allocation, perceived injustice and competing water claims; national climate-conflict causality is not demonstrated. Supporting evidence: IRN-E-009-E-010; pathway IRN-P-002. (Directory of Open Access Journals) [IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010, IRN-P-002]
- FCV-related economic and external constraints plausibly weaken adaptation and service-delivery capacity, while Iran retains significant institutional and community resilience assets; independent evidence on net adaptation effectiveness remains weak. Supporting evidence: IRN-E-013-E-022; pathways IRN-P-004-P-008. (World Bank) [IRN-E-013, IRN-E-014, IRN-E-015, IRN-E-016, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-018, IRN-E-019, IRN-E-020, IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022, IRN-P-004, IRN-P-005, IRN-P-006, IRN-P-007, IRN-P-008]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Warming strongly increased multi-year drought likelihood. [IRN-E-001]
- Rainfall attribution is weaker than drought/evaporative attribution. [IRN-E-002]
- National policy projected hotter conditions and lower runoff. [IRN-E-003]
- National freshwater withdrawals are high relative to internal resources. [IRN-E-004]
- Groundwater depletion is large and long-running. [IRN-E-005]
- Climate and demand pressures compound one another. [IRN-E-011]
- Iran hosts a large protection-sensitive Afghan population. [IRN-E-023]
- Cross-border mobility changed rapidly in 2026. [IRN-E-024]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Human withdrawals dominate groundwater depletion. [IRN-E-006]
- Local scarcity was primarily governance-related. [IRN-E-007]
- Rural vulnerability differs markedly within one basin. [IRN-E-008]
- Current FCV/economic shocks heighten household sensitivity. [IRN-E-022]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Irrigation-dependent livelihoods have limited coping options. [IRN-E-012]
- A national adaptation planning process is funded and under implementation. [IRN-E-014]
- Climate-smart agricultural capacity is being developed. [IRN-E-015]
- Some adaptation programming explicitly includes women's livelihoods. [IRN-E-019]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- External restrictions can constrain adaptation capacity. [IRN-E-013]
- Pilot sites report water savings with higher yields. [IRN-E-016]
- Cross-sector wetland institutions exist in project areas. [IRN-E-017]
- An independent-style terminal evaluation was commissioned. [IRN-E-020]
- FCV/economic conditions coincide with infrastructure and utility constraints. [IRN-E-021]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Warming-enhanced drought can worsen irrigation stress, but household and FCV consequences depend heavily on water governance and economic alternatives. (World Weather Attribution) [IRN-P-001; IRN-E-001, IRN-E-011, IRN-E-012]
  - Documented impact: More severe agricultural drought and water stress.
  - FCV mediator: Irrigation dependence, livelihood losses, high input costs and limited alternatives.
  - Possible consequence: Where household buffers are weak, these pressures could increase distress mobility or socioeconomic grievances; violence is not demonstrated.

- In ZayandehRud, climate-related water stress is documented as one factor interacting with allocation and perceived injustice in local disputes - not as a stand-alone conflict cause. (Directory of Open Access Journals) [IRN-P-002; IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Climate-water-conflict perceptions vary across basin users. [IRN-E-009]
- Allocation and injustice mediate local climate-water tensions. [IRN-E-010]
- Participatory environmental governance has been scaled geographically. [IRN-E-018]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National physical evidence is strongest for groundwater and drought. The groundwater analysis covers all 30 major basins, though depletion rates vary substantially. (Nature) []
- *ZayandehRud/Isfahan findings must remain local. Both the conflict-perception study and the rural vulnerability study show meaningful upstream/downstream or district heterogeneity; neither justifies a national claim about farmer conflict or water governance. (Directory of Open Access Journals) []
- Wetland programme evidence covers Lake Urmia and selected sites including Shadegan, Bakhtegan, Parishan and Anzali. UNDP reports broader replication across 25 provinces/38 wetlands, but reported scale should not be confused with nationally representative impact evidence. (UNDP) []
- Urban exposure is represented mainly by national/WWA discussion of major-city water stress and World Bank reporting of water and energy shortages. Detailed municipal evidence for Tehran and other cities remains limited in this packet. (World Weather Attribution) []
- Afghan displacement and return figures establish that Iran has a large and rapidly changing displaced/protection-sensitive population. They do not demonstrate that climate conditions caused either presence in Iran or 2026 returns/deportations. (UNHCR Data Portal) []
- *Well covered: water resources, groundwater, irrigated agriculture, wetlands and rural livelihoods. []
- *Partially covered: social protection, urbanobject Object,object Object systems, energy, food markets and refugee/service pressures. []
- *Thin or absent: coastal adaptation, fisheries, detailed flood risk, dust-source attribution, health impacts, industrial water adaptation, municipal finance and independently evaluated infrastructure resilience. []
