# Iran Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Iran's hydroclimatic risk is increasing, but the national water crisis is a compound climate-management problem rather than a climate-only outcome. [IRN-E-001, IRN-E-002, IRN-E-003, IRN-E-004, IRN-E-005, IRN-E-006, IRN-P-001, IRN-P-003]
- The best direct Climate-FCV evidence is local to the ZayandehRud Basin, where farmer perceptions link climate and water stress with allocation, perceived injustice and competing claims; national climate-conflict causality is not demonstrated. [IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010, IRN-P-002]
- FCV-related economic and external constraints plausibly weaken adaptation and service-delivery capacity, while Iran retains significant institutional and community resilience assets; independent evidence on net adaptation effectiveness remains weak. Supporting evidence: IRN-E-013-E-022; pathways IRN-P-004-P-008. (World Bank) [IRN-E-013, IRN-E-014, IRN-E-015, IRN-E-016, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-018, IRN-E-019, IRN-E-020, IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022, IRN-P-004, IRN-P-005, IRN-P-006, IRN-P-007, IRN-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: the official 2015 INDC contains 2030 temperature and runoff projections but does not identify an underlying model or scenario in the inspected passage. A modern source-accessible CMIP6 national and subnational set is needed. [IRN-E-003]
- Rainfall attribution: WWA's attribution is strong for warming-related SPEI severity, but its rainfall-only estimate includes no-change possibilities. [IRN-E-001, IRN-E-002, IRN-P-001]
- Geographic/population gaps: Nationally representative evidence on differentiated vulnerability by gender, income, ethnicity, disability, rural/urban status and migration status is incomplete. Current studies are often basin- or programme-specific. [IRN-E-009, IRN-E-012, IRN-E-016, IRN-E-019, IRN-P-002, IRN-P-006]
- Causal Climate-FCV evidence: the ZayandehRud study is subnational and perception-based. No verified national study in this set demonstrates a climate effect on conflict. [IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010, IRN-P-002]
- FCV-to-climate strength: policy and macroeconomic evidence supports mechanisms through sanctions, finance, technology, infrastructure and trade, but does not quantify adaptation delays or losses. [IRN-E-013, IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022, IRN-P-004, IRN-P-005]
- Evaluation: a terminal evaluation was publicly procured for a wetland component, but the final independent report was not located. Programme figures are therefore implementer-reported. [IRN-E-016, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-018, IRN-E-019, IRN-E-020, IRN-P-006, IRN-P-008]
- Unintended effects: Evidence is weak on rebound effects - for example whether water-efficiency gains lead to expanded irrigated acreage or total consumption - and on winners/losers from allocation or conservation programmes. [IRN-E-016, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-018, IRN-P-006]
- Water-use statistics: FAO and WWA use different agriculture-withdrawal/consumption figures and metrics. The discrepancy is unresolved and neither figure should be substituted for the other. [IRN-E-011, IRN-E-015, IRN-P-001]
- Dust and land degradation: The national policy and WWA discuss dust/desertification, but this packet does not establish the relative contributions of climate, land use, wetland loss, salinization and other anthropogenic drivers well enough for a strong attribution claim. [IRN-E-003, IRN-E-011, IRN-P-001]
- Current conflict and infrastructure: World Bank evidence describes disruption and damaged infrastructure, but granular sector evidence separating direct damage from chronic underinvestment, sanctions and water scarcity is needed. [IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022, IRN-P-005, IRN-P-007]
- Coastal risk: Coastal pressures were a country priority, but sufficiently detailed, accessible and locatable Iranian coastal/sea-level evidence was not secured in the final verified source set. [IRN-E-003]
- Refugee hosting and mobility: rolling UNHCR figures changed by October 2026, and the original end-2025 and 1 August 2026 values were not recoverable from an archived primary passage. They are excluded pending a dated source. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The 2021-2025 heat-and-rainfall drought was far more likely in the warmed climate. [IRN-E-001]
- Iran's 2021-2025 rainfall deficit was rare, but attribution included no-change possibilities. [IRN-E-002]
- National policy projected hotter conditions and lower runoff. [IRN-E-003]
- Freshwater withdrawals equalled 72% of internal renewable resources in 2022. [IRN-E-004]
- Iran lost about 74 km³ of groundwater during 2002-2015. [IRN-E-005]
- Climate and demand pressures compound one another. [IRN-E-011]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Human withdrawal dominated Iran's 2002-2015 groundwater depletion. [IRN-E-006]
- Current FCV/economic shocks heighten household sensitivity. [IRN-E-022]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Irrigation-dependent livelihoods have limited coping options. [IRN-E-012]
- A national adaptation planning process is funded and under implementation. [IRN-E-014]
- Climate-smart agricultural capacity is being developed. [IRN-E-015]
- Some adaptation programming explicitly includes women's livelihoods. [IRN-E-019]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- External restrictions can constrain adaptation capacity. [IRN-E-013]
- Pilot sites report water savings with higher yields. [IRN-E-016]
- Cross-sector wetland institutions exist in project areas. [IRN-E-017]
- A terminal evaluation was procured in 2023, but no final report was verified. [IRN-E-020]
- FCV/economic conditions coincide with infrastructure and utility constraints. [IRN-E-021]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Warming-enhanced drought can worsen irrigation stress, but household and FCV consequences depend heavily on water governance and economic alternatives. (World Weather Attribution) [IRN-P-001; IRN-E-001, IRN-E-011, IRN-E-012]
  - Documented impact: More severe agricultural drought and water stress.
  - FCV mediator: Irrigation dependence, livelihood losses, high input costs and limited alternatives.
  - Possible consequence: Where household buffers are weak, these pressures could increase distress mobility or socioeconomic grievances; violence is not demonstrated.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Water allocation', 'groundwater pumping', 'crop choice', 'prices', 'sanctions', 'inflation', 'land tenure']
  - Uncertainty: No national causal estimate links drought exposure to migration or grievances.
- In ZayandehRud, climate-related water stress is documented as one factor interacting with allocation and perceived injustice in local disputes - not as a stand-alone conflict cause. (Directory of Open Access Journals) [IRN-P-002; IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010]
  - Documented impact: Reduced water availability and perceived worsening of water insecurity.
  - FCV mediator: Allocation disputes, upstream/downstream differences, perceived injustice and competing sectoral claims.
  - Possible consequence: Local and regional water disputes may intensify when water insecurity and perception gaps are not managed.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRN-E-009'], 'impact': ['IRN-E-009'], 'mediator': ['IRN-E-010'], 'consequence': ['IRN-E-010']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Poor governance', 'allocation decisions', 'groundwater pumping', 'agricultural policy and historical claims to water']
  - Uncertainty: Survey-based perception evidence cannot establish a unique climate causal effect.
- Drought can accelerate groundwater depletion, but human withdrawal was the dominant documented 2002-2015 driver; downstream FCV effects remain unmeasured. [IRN-P-003; IRN-E-005, IRN-E-006]
  - Documented impact: Reduced recharge and increased groundwater depletion during dry conditions.
  - FCV mediator: Unsustainable withdrawals and governance of access to groundwater.
  - Possible consequence: Persistently depleted aquifers could deepen livelihood insecurity and competition among water users.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRN-E-006'], 'impact': ['IRN-E-005', 'IRN-E-006'], 'mediator': ['IRN-E-006'], 'consequence': ['IRN-E-005']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Agricultural subsidies/policies', 'illegal or poorly controlled wells', 'allocation rules', 'infrastructure and demographic demand']
  - Uncertainty: The groundwater study quantifies physical depletion drivers, not conflict or distributional outcomes.
- Iran's own climate policy identifies sanctions and external restrictions as adaptation-capacity constraints, but the scale of their effect remains unevaluated. (Asia Pacific Energy Policy) [IRN-P-004; IRN-E-013, IRN-E-014]
  - Documented impact: Need for finance, technology and institutional capacity.
  - FCV mediator: Sanctions and restrictions on finance, technology and international cooperation.
  - Possible consequence: Adaptation planning or implementation may be slower, more expensive or narrower than otherwise possible.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRN-E-013'], 'impact': ['IRN-E-013'], 'mediator': ['IRN-E-013'], 'consequence': ['IRN-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Domestic policy choices', 'fiscal priorities', 'institutional fragmentation and technical capacity']
  - Uncertainty: The INDC is a government position and does not quantify sanctions' marginal adaptation effect.
- Current FCV and economic disruption can plausibly narrow infrastructure-resilience capacity, although quantitative adaptation losses are unmeasured. (World Bank) [IRN-P-005; IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Stress on economic activity and infrastructure.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict-related damage, sanctions, trade disruption, weak investment and import constraints.
  - Possible consequence: These conditions may reduce capacity to maintain, finance or upgrade water, energy and other climate-sensitive infrastructure.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRN-E-021'], 'impact': ['IRN-E-021'], 'mediator': ['IRN-E-021'], 'consequence': ['IRN-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Structural economic weaknesses', 'domestic investment policy', 'resource pricing', 'aging infrastructure']
  - Uncertainty: No source in the verified set directly estimates lost adaptation investment.
- Participatory wetland management provides a plausible resilience mechanism, but independent evidence of durable conflict- or vulnerability-reduction effects is missing. (UNDP) [IRN-P-006; IRN-E-016, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Pressure on wetlands and dependent livelihoods.
  - FCV mediator: Competing water use, weak inter-organizational collaboration and livelihood dependence.
  - Possible consequence: Degradation could increase livelihood stress; participatory and integrated basin management may reduce such risk.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['IRN-E-016'], 'impact': ['IRN-E-016'], 'mediator': ['IRN-E-017'], 'consequence': ['IRN-E-016']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Urban development', 'pollution', 'agricultural runoff', 'enforcement and water allocation']
  - Uncertainty: Programme reporting cannot establish counterfactual effectiveness.
- Climate stress can add to household economic pressure, but sanctions, prices and conflict remain independent and potentially dominant explanations. (World Bank) [IRN-P-007; IRN-E-012, IRN-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Agricultural and economic stress.
  - FCV mediator: Inflation, income pressure, food-market conditions and unequal household buffers.
  - Possible consequence: Combined pressures could raise grievances or lower coping capacity, especially among poorer households.

- Iran has observable institutional and community adaptation assets, but their contribution to durable peace or FCV reduction has not been independently demonstrated. (FAOHome) [IRN-P-008; IRN-E-015, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Need for more efficient production and basin coordination.
  - FCV mediator: Institutional fragmentation and competing user interests.
  - Possible consequence: Multi-stakeholder institutions and livelihood alternatives may reduce vulnerability and provide channels for managing distributional tensions.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Climate-water-conflict perceptions vary across basin users. [IRN-E-009]
- Allocation and injustice mediate local climate-water tensions. [IRN-E-010]
- Participatory environmental governance has been scaled geographically. [IRN-E-018]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National physical evidence is strongest for groundwater and drought. The groundwater analysis covers all 30 major basins, though depletion rates vary substantially. (Nature) [IRN-E-001, IRN-E-002, IRN-E-005, IRN-E-006, IRN-P-001, IRN-P-003]
- ZayandehRud findings must remain local. The 2022 farmer-perception study documents upstream/downstream heterogeneity but does not justify a national claim about farmer conflict or water governance. [IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010, IRN-P-002]
- Wetland programme evidence covers Lake Urmia and selected sites including Shadegan, Bakhtegan, Parishan and Anzali. UNDP reports broader replication across 25 provinces/38 wetlands, but reported scale should not be confused with nationally representative impact evidence. (UNDP) [IRN-E-016, IRN-E-017, IRN-E-018, IRN-E-019, IRN-P-006, IRN-P-008]
- Urban exposure is represented mainly by national/WWA discussion of major-city water stress and World Bank reporting of water and energy shortages. Detailed municipal evidence for Tehran and other cities remains limited in this packet. (World Weather Attribution) [IRN-E-001, IRN-E-011, IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022, IRN-P-001, IRN-P-005, IRN-P-007]
- Well covered: water resources, groundwater, irrigated agriculture, wetlands and rural livelihoods. [IRN-E-004, IRN-E-005, IRN-E-006, IRN-E-009, IRN-E-010, IRN-E-012, IRN-E-015, IRN-E-016, IRN-E-018, IRN-P-001, IRN-P-002, IRN-P-003, IRN-P-006]
- Partially covered: social protection, urban systems, energy and food markets. [IRN-E-011, IRN-E-012, IRN-E-021, IRN-E-022, IRN-P-005, IRN-P-007]
- Thin or absent: coastal adaptation, fisheries, detailed flood risk, dust-source attribution, health impacts, industrial water adaptation, municipal finance and independently evaluated infrastructure resilience. [IRN-E-003, IRN-E-020, IRN-E-021, IRN-P-005]
