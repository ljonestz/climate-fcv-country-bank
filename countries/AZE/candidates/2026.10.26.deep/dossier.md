Critical remaining gaps:
- No existing AZE baseline was present in the assigned worktree. []
- The World Bank CCDR PDF was readable but marked on its cover as an embargoed confidential draft not for circulation; it was excluded and supplies no claims. []
- The 2024 National Adaptation Plan PDF was too large for the available reader, so the NDC’s reproduced index is used with its stated attribution to that plan and an index-method limitation. []
- The UNDRR Risk Spotlight publisher page exposed only metadata and a download prompt; its report body could not be read and no claims rely on it. []
- No reviewed primary report establishes that climate change caused violence in Azerbaijan. The collected evidence instead states separate climate vulnerabilities and links conflict legacies such as mines and displacement to return, land access and recovery. []
- The NDC’s occupation-era environmental damage statements are government assertions, not independently verified findings; NDC and official-speech figures are attributed and not independently audited. []
- No evaluated evidence was found on whether the listed adaptation, return, mine-action or kahriz interventions reduced climate vulnerability or improved peace outcomes. []
- Conflict-legacy mine contamination constrains safe land use and return, but the retrieved material does not verify a specific operational climate-adaptation blockage or an evaluated adaptation/peace effect. []
- Government terminology about conflict-affected territory is attributed rather than adopted as an independent political or environmental finding. []
- The embargoed/confidential-draft CCDR is excluded; only public permitted source findings enter the bank. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure


## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-11-05 reports: For 2041–2069, the NDC reports highest vulnerability scores for Eastern Zangazur and Shirvan-Salyan (0.73), followed by Mil-Mugan (0.72) and Central Aran (0.68). [AZE-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-11-05 reports: The NDC reports water resources fell about 15% over recent decades, irrigation uses up to 90% of water, and 200+ landslides disrupted rural communities and transport. [AZE-E-102]
- At the November2024 event, ANAMA's chair described potential soil/air contamination from explosive remnants and presented mine action as relevant to climate resilience. The event report does not supply environmental sampling or a verified adaptation-effect estimate. [AZE-E-107]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025-11-05 reports: Listed measures include seasonal reservoirs, canal rehabilitation, drought-resistant crops, flood forecasting and early warning for irrigation and water supply. [AZE-E-103]
- The November2025 NDC presents green-energy zones, smart agriculture and reforestation as government ambitions for post-conflict reintegration in Karabakh and East Zangezur; delivery and environmental/peace outcomes are not evaluated. [AZE-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-03-18 reports: IOM says kankans’ inherited kahriz skills risk disappearing without support and cites a capacity session in Ganja involving students from four universities. [AZE-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated not stated reports: The page lists 12 coordinating groups, including population return, mine/UXO clearance, environmental protection and efficient agricultural-land use. [AZE-E-105]
- Source dated not stated reports: The page says former IDP return to Aghdam began on 27 March 2025 and continues alongside demining; it lists the Khachinchay reservoir and restored roads and power infrastructure. [AZE-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-03-18 reports: IOM reports nearly 200 kahrizes rehabilitated since 2000, benefiting over 30,000 households in 20 districts; the systems provide decentralized water in arid regions. [AZE-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-03-18 reports: With the State Water Resources Agency, IOM produced a cost-benefit comparison and a transparent, data-driven method to prioritize kahriz rehabilitation. [AZE-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-09-02 reports: The speech reports 25.6% of estimated contaminated territory released for safe use, facilitating about 38,200 former IDP returns and restored activity in Karabakh and East Zangezur. [AZE-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-09-02 reports: The speech says UNDP, UNICEF and UNHCR supported ANAMA with institutional and technical assistance, procurement and explosive-ordnance risk education during 2020–2025. [AZE-E-112]
