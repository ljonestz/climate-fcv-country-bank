# Mozambique Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Mozambique has high and spatially differentiated climate exposure, with substantial projected warming, but disaster losses should not be equated automatically with anthropogenic climate-change effects. [MOZ-E-001, MOZ-E-002, MOZ-E-003, MOZ-E-011, MOZ-E-012, MOZ-P-001, MOZ-P-002]
- The most supportable Climate-FCV formulation is one of mediated and compound risk: climate pressures interact with food security, livelihoods, displacement, land access, public services and institutions, while armed conflict, poverty, markets, inequality and governance remain independent drivers. [MOZ-E-009, MOZ-E-010, MOZ-E-015, MOZ-E-016, MOZ-E-017, MOZ-E-018, MOZ-E-019, MOZ-P-001, MOZ-P-002, MOZ-P-003, MOZ-P-004, MOZ-P-005, MOZ-P-006, MOZ-P-007]
- Formal adaptation architecture and local resilience capacities exist, but implementation, financing, decentralised technical capacity and independently evaluated effectiveness remain uneven; conflict in Cabo Delgado creates a distinct obstacle to resilience and recovery. [MOZ-E-004, MOZ-E-005, MOZ-E-006, MOZ-E-014, MOZ-E-018, MOZ-E-020, MOZ-E-021, MOZ-E-022, MOZ-E-023, MOZ-E-024, MOZ-E-025, MOZ-E-026, MOZ-P-004, MOZ-P-005, MOZ-P-007, MOZ-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: The verified NDC projection uses seven GCMs and specifies horizons but does not identify the emissions scenario associated with the cited 1.5-3.0°C mid-century range. Newer, district- or basin-resolved projections for heat, rainfall extremes, drought, sea-level rise and cyclone-related hazards should be independently assembled. []
- Geographic representation: The strongest direct climate-security evidence is from Mecufi. There is no comparable verified causal evidence in this packet for much of southern Mozambique, major cities, most of Zambézia/Nampula, major river basins or many conflict-affected northern localities. []
- Population differentiation: The NAP explicitly identifies vulnerable demographic groups but also acknowledges limited sex-disaggregated data. Disability, age, gender, return status, livelihood and urban-informal-settlement differences need more systematic measurement. []
- Causal uncertainty: Qualitative evidence supports several plausible climate -> livelihood/food/land -> FCV pathways, but no retained study establishes a national causal effect of climate shocks on violence, insurgent recruitment or communal conflict. []
- FCV-to-climate evidence: Evidence that conflict undermines adaptation is credible but mostly indirect or local - displacement, service overload, damaged institutions and natural-resource pressure. Comparative measurement of adaptation/recovery performance between conflict-affected and more stable districts is lacking. []
- Evaluation: Independent evaluation is available for the 2019 Idai humanitarian response and an older climate-mainstreaming programme, but current programmes - including 2024/25 anticipatory action and recent cyclone/flood responses - lack equivalent independent outcome evidence in the verified source set. []
- Unintended effects: Evidence is particularly thin on whether resettlement, land allocation, adaptation infrastructure, cash programmes, early-warning systems or ecosystem interventions inadvertently redistribute risk or generate grievances. []
- Access and verification: The World Bank CCDR full PDF exceeded the browsing crawler's size limit, and the WFP IAHE linked full report was not directly retrievable. Claims from both were therefore limited to their verified official publication pages. No unverified PDF-page claims from those documents have been retained. []
- *None are wholly absent, but four are only partially covered: coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, direct-climate-fcv and resilience-peace-capacity. Response-performance is also partial because independent evaluations are dated or event-specific. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- National historical climate baseline uses 32 stations. [MOZ-E-001]
- Mid-century warming of 1.5-3.0°C in cited modelling. [MOZ-E-002]
- Repeated historical cyclone/flood/drought exposure. [MOZ-E-003]
- Acute food insecurity remained extensive in 2025/26. [MOZ-E-009]
- Displacement drivers differ sharply by province. [MOZ-E-011]
- 2026 floods/storms disrupted major social infrastructure. [MOZ-E-012]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Vulnerability is differentiated by gender, age, disability, poverty and health. [MOZ-E-007]
- Climate-development modelling projects higher poverty in a hot scenario. [MOZ-E-008]
- Food insecurity is explicitly multi-causal. [MOZ-E-010]
- Armed conflict directly constrains displacement recovery and services. [MOZ-E-013]
- Cabo Delgado insecurity has major non-climate drivers. [MOZ-E-019]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Local adaptation planning had broad formal reach by 2021. [MOZ-E-005]
- Anticipatory livelihood assistance was implemented before forecast flood risk. [MOZ-E-021]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Large financing requirement constrains implementation. [MOZ-E-004]
- Formal adaptation system retains coordination/data/capacity gaps. [MOZ-E-006]
- Dedicated local technical adaptation capacity was weak in Mecufi. [MOZ-E-018]
- Preparedness and coordination improved Idai emergency response. [MOZ-E-022]
- Idai response had significant recovery and monitoring weaknesses. [MOZ-E-023]
- Adaptation-mainstreaming intervention showed partial rather than complete performance. [MOZ-E-024]
- Evaluation could not cleanly attribute adaptation-mainstreaming outcomes. [MOZ-E-025]
- Gender integration in the evaluated adaptation project was thin. [MOZ-E-026]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Cyclone/flood losses can worsen food and service stress through livelihoods, markets and displacement, especially where non-climate fragilities are already severe. [MOZ-P-001; MOZ-E-003, MOZ-E-009, MOZ-E-010, MOZ-E-012]
  - Documented impact: Crop/livelihood losses and damage to schools, health facilities and water infrastructure.
  - FCV mediator: Food access, market access, displacement, service scarcity and coping-capacity erosion.
  - Possible consequence: Where conflict, poverty or displacement already constrain households, repeated disaster losses could deepen humanitarian dependence and local competition, but violence is not an automatic outcome.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-003'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-012'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-009', 'MOZ-E-010'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-003']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Food prices', 'poverty', 'armed conflict', 'infrastructure quality', 'market isolation and pre-existing service shortages']
  - Uncertainty: No identified national study estimates how much conflict or fragility risk changes because of cyclone exposure alone.
- In Mecufi, climate-related livelihood stress is plausibly linked to competition through food insecurity and socioeconomic inequality rather than directly to violence. [MOZ-P-002; MOZ-E-002, MOZ-E-015, MOZ-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Local pressure on arable land, fisheries and food availability.
  - FCV mediator: Food insecurity, poverty, youth unemployment and competition over resources.
  - Possible consequence: These conditions may increase incentives for contentious or illicit livelihood strategies and disputes, but the Mecufi evidence does not demonstrate that climate stress causes violent recruitment or violence.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-002', 'MOZ-E-015'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-015'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-015', 'MOZ-E-019'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-015']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Longstanding poverty', 'unemployment', 'political exclusion', 'criminal economies', 'insurgent mobilisation', 'land governance']
  - Uncertainty: No counterfactual and no national generalisability.
- In Mecufi, climate-related land pressure and conflict-driven displacement can interact through land access and governance. [MOZ-P-003; MOZ-E-017, MOZ-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Reduced or degraded cultivable land in the local assessment.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict displacement, rapid land occupation, unequal access and land governance.
  - Possible consequence: Disputes between hosts, displaced households or politically/economically stronger and weaker groups could intensify.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-017'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-017'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-017'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-017']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Pre-existing tenure problems', 'land acquisition', 'demographic change', 'elite capture', 'commercial projects']
  - Uncertainty: Future escalation is explicitly conditional.
- Conflict-related livelihood and displacement pressures can degrade natural assets that otherwise buffer climate hazards. [MOZ-P-004; MOZ-E-016]
  - Documented impact: Local mangroves serve as a protective ecological asset.
  - FCV mediator: Insurgency-driven displacement, livelihood loss and pressure on services/natural resources.
  - Possible consequence: Unsustainable extraction, including mangrove cutting for salt production, may weaken natural buffering capacity and increase future hazard vulnerability.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-016'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-016'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-016'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-016']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Commercial extraction', 'weak environmental enforcement', 'pre-existing mangrove degradation and poverty']
  - Uncertainty: Magnitude of mangrove loss attributable specifically to conflict displacement is not quantified.
- Conflict can make climate adaptation harder by disrupting the institutions, services and mobility on which preparedness and recovery depend. [MOZ-P-005; MOZ-E-011, MOZ-E-013, MOZ-E-018, MOZ-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Northern violence has caused displacement, infrastructure destruction and school closures.
  - FCV mediator: Insecurity, restricted mobility, damaged infrastructure, weakened services and overstretched humanitarian capacity.
  - Possible consequence: Hazard preparedness, recovery and long-term adaptation may be slower or less equitable in conflict-affected districts.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-011'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-011'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-011'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-011']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Poverty', 'national fiscal constraints', 'remoteness and pre-existing infrastructure deficits']
  - Uncertainty: Few evaluations directly measure adaptation outcomes in conflict versus non-conflict districts.
- Climate-related disaster disruption may become politically salient through public-health failures and mistrust rather than through climate exposure alone. [MOZ-P-006; MOZ-E-014]
  - Documented impact: Damage, poor sanitation and communicable-disease risks.
  - FCV mediator: Weak public-health/adaptation intervention, rumours and distrust of authorities.
  - Possible consequence: The Mecufi assessment reports a pathway toward insecurity, protest or social unrest where disease outbreaks are interpreted through mistrust.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-014'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-014'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-014'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-014']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Pre-existing institutional mistrust', 'misinformation', 'disease dynamics unrelated to climate and political grievances']
  - Uncertainty: No incidence/risk effect size is provided.
- Implementation capacity is a mediator of climate vulnerability, but its relationship to fragility requires further empirical testing. [MOZ-P-007; MOZ-E-004, MOZ-E-005, MOZ-E-006, MOZ-E-018, MOZ-E-024, MOZ-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Large adaptation requirements and recurring livelihood/infrastructure losses.
  - FCV mediator: Fiscal constraints, weak coordination, incomplete technical capacity and unequal service delivery.
  - Possible consequence: Uneven implementation could leave poorer or conflict-affected communities with lower resilience, potentially reinforcing existing inequalities and grievances.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MOZ-E-004'], 'impact': ['MOZ-E-004'], 'mediator': ['MOZ-E-004'], 'consequence': ['MOZ-E-004']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Broader public-finance constraints', 'governance choices', 'conflict', 'donor priorities and implementation quality']
  - Uncertainty: No evidence found demonstrating that adaptation funding gaps themselves cause instability.
- Mozambique has locally documented peace-supporting capacities, but evidence of their effect on climate-FCV outcomes remains limited. [MOZ-P-008; MOZ-E-014, MOZ-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Shared analysis of climate-security risks by authorities, communities and peacebuilders.
  - FCV mediator: Trust, participation, conflict mediation, early warning and locally legitimate decision-making.
  - Possible consequence: These capacities could reduce escalation and improve conflict-sensitive adaptation, although effectiveness has not yet been demonstrated.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Local multistakeholder climate-security analysis exists in Mecufi. [MOZ-E-014]
- Climate-linked livelihood stress may amplify competition through socioeconomic mediators. [MOZ-E-015]
- Conflict/displacement can weaken ecosystem-based resilience locally. [MOZ-E-016]
- Hazard impacts and conflict displacement can jointly tighten land access. [MOZ-E-017]
- Community dialogue practices provide local peace-supporting capacities. [MOZ-E-020]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National climate-policy evidence is relatively broad, but direct Climate-FCV evidence is not. The GPPAC pathways apply specifically to Mecufi; the Agency for Peacebuilding findings cover Ancuabe, Chiure, Mecufi and Pemba. Neither should be generalized across Cabo Delgado, much less Mozambique. []
- Cabo Delgado requires separate treatment because ongoing armed conflict, displacement, extractive-economy grievances, unemployment and strained state-community relations materially change the way climate and disaster pressures are experienced. The evidence identifies these factors independently of climate. []
- Sofala and Gaza in early 2026 are documented primarily as flood-affected settings, while UNICEF's displacement assessment differentiates Sofala's disaster-related displacement from conflict-driven displacement concentrated in Cabo Delgado. (UNICEF1) []
- Zambézia and Sofala provide evidence of anticipatory agricultural support; this evidence cannot establish performance in other provinces. (FAOHome12) []
- Evidence is thin for Maputo/Matola urban climate-FCV pathways, inland drought-prone southern districts, major river-basin governance beyond disaster exposure, and many northern areas inaccessible or insecure during field research. Coastal erosion and fisheries appear in local evidence, but there is no sufficiently broad causal study here to nationalize those findings. []
- Agriculture and food security have the strongest coverage, including hazard exposure, rain-fed livelihood sensitivity, food-market mediators and anticipatory responses. Fisheries and coastal ecosystems are represented mainly in the Mecufi study and national climate projections, so evidence is narrower geographically. []
- Health, WASH and education have strong current disaster/service-impact evidence from UNICEF. Infrastructure is covered at a broad level, though transport networks, electricity and municipal drainage have less detailed evidence in the retained set. []
- Natural-resource governance is relevant in Cabo Delgado, particularly through land and mangrove use, but mining, LNG/gas development and commercial land acquisition are primarily independent political-economic factors in the evidence bank assembled here and should not be labelled climate impacts without a demonstrated pathway. []
