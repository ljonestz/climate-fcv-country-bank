## Climate pressures and exposure

- Mozambique's NDC climate assessment used daily maximum/minimum temperature and rainfall observations since 1960 from 32 synoptic stations to analyse 1960-2005 climate changes. [MOZ-E-001]
- Seven GCMs in the NDC project an average atmospheric temperature increase of 1.5-3.0°C for 2046-2065, with more warm days and fewer cold days. Scenario: 2046-2065; 7 GCMs; emissions scenario not specified in cited passage [MOZ-E-002]
- The NDC's 1980-2019 event inventory records 21 cyclones, 20 floods and 12 droughts. [MOZ-E-003]
- The combined IPC analysis estimated about 3.5 million people in IPC Phase 3 or above during October 2025-March 2026, with 46 of 108 analysed districts in Crisis. Scenario: Oct 2025-Mar 2026 [MOZ-E-009]
- In February 2026 UNICEF reported 661,532 IDPs nationally; about 72% were in Cabo Delgado, largely due to ongoing conflict, while around 14% were in Sofala, primarily due to climate-related shocks. [MOZ-E-011]
- By 28 February 2026, UNICEF reported more than 778,000 people affected by floods and storms, with damage to 594 schools, 290 health facilities and other educational infrastructure. [MOZ-E-012]
- Source dated 2026-02-09 reports: In 2025, conflict drove major displacement from Memba District, Nampula: over 112,000 people fled, 65% children. Separately, about 15% of IDPs were in Sofala, mainly after climate shocks. [MOZ-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-02-09 reports: Dikeledi and Jude affected over 1.3 million people in Q1 2025, mostly in Nampula, damaged 131 health facilities and 3,128 classrooms, and disrupted learning for 365,000-plus students. [MOZ-E-104]
- Source dated not stated reports: Normal-to-above-normal northern and above-normal central/southern rains caused floods, road disruption and over 100,000 hectares inundated; Massingir Dam reduced impacts in Chókwè, Guijá, Chibuto and Xai-Xai. [MOZ-E-111]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The NAP identifies women, children/adolescents, older people, persons with disabilities, people in extreme poverty and people suffering illness as priority groups whose adaptive capacity requires strengthening. [MOZ-E-007]
- Under the World Bank CCDR's worst-case “hot” projection, Mozambique's poverty level is projected to be 5% higher by 2050, corresponding to about 1.6 million additional people in poverty. Scenario: 2050; World Bank “hot” scenario [MOZ-E-008]
- IPC identifies irregular rainfall, prolonged dry spells and cyclone effects alongside conflict-related displacement, insecurity and high food prices as key drivers of food insecurity. [MOZ-E-010]
- UNICEF's 2026 appeal reported 412,000 people displaced in 20 northern districts and school closures from attacks that kept about 50,000 children from school. [MOZ-E-013]
- UNDP's selected Chiúre–Mecúfi–Pemba sample reports Cyclone Kenneth impacts alongside weak housing/sanitation and displacement-related vulnerability. [MOZ-E-101]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC reported 123 districts with local adaptation plans formulated by October 2021. [MOZ-E-005]
- Ahead of anticipated 2024/25 flooding, FAO reported providing agricultural inputs and mobile cash transfers to nearly 4,000 farmers in Sofala and Zambézia. [MOZ-E-021]
- Source dated 2025-11-21 reports: The evaluation reports 268 local disaster-risk committees trained, early-warning systems integrated into district plans, and LoCAL adaptation planning embedded in government systems with community participation. [MOZ-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-08-26 reports: Community-approved plans propose fairer land access, dialogue with government, reforestation, resilient agriculture and small dams, with community-led implementation intended. Scenario: Near-term community plans as described in the August 2026 release; implementation and outcomes not yet evaluated. [MOZ-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Mozambique's NDC states that implementation of its adaptation and mitigation actions is conditional on international climate support and estimated total 2020-2025 investment demand at about US$7.586 billion. [MOZ-E-004]
- The NAP identifies updated vulnerability studies, clear coordination mechanisms, stronger climate-information systems and institutional capacity-building as still-needed measures. [MOZ-E-006]
- Mecufi's district government was reported to have no specific technical capacity for climate mitigation and adaptation at the time of the local assessment. [MOZ-E-018]
- The Idai IAHE found that Scale-Up activation, joint preparedness, government collaboration and timely aerial assessments strengthened the immediate collective response. [MOZ-E-022]
- The Idai IAHE identified delays in early recovery and opportunities for stronger anticipatory/early-action triggers, cash use, local civil-society/private-sector engagement and coordinated monitoring. [MOZ-E-023]
- UNDP's final evaluation concluded the AAP Mozambique project only partially achieved its outcomes, although it contributed to institutional coordination and technical capability. [MOZ-E-024]
- The same evaluation found monitoring weaknesses and parallel interventions made clear attribution of observed mainstreaming results impossible. [MOZ-E-025]
- The UNDP evaluation reported that the project implemented only one specifically gender-focused activity and that two cited pilot projects had no explicit gender component. [MOZ-E-026]
- Source dated 2026-02-09 reports: UNICEF deployed 3,924 mobile teams across 1,556 communities and resettlement sites; integrated brigades served hard-to-reach conflict- or climate-shock-affected areas in five named provinces. [MOZ-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-11-21 reports: Evaluation finds humanitarian access in insecure northern districts constrained by conflict and instability; activity and staff training were interrupted in Cabo Delgado, while mobile brigades and boat clinics adapted delivery. [MOZ-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-11-21 reports: Local consultative councils selected 86 climate-adaptive infrastructure projects; the evaluation also reports municipal upkeep of drainage machinery and Chókwè dike maintenance by local authorities. [MOZ-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-08-13 reports: INGD reports eight anticipatory-action activations across six basins and for a cyclone (Dec 2025–Mar 2026), evacuating 7,214 people and warning about 1.5 million up to five days ahead; volunteers helped first when formal capacity was exceeded. [MOZ-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

  - Documented impact: Crop/livelihood losses and damage to schools, health facilities and water infrastructure.
  - FCV mediator: Food access, market access, displacement, service scarcity and coping-capacity erosion.
  - Possible consequence: Where conflict, poverty or displacement already constrain households, repeated disaster losses could deepen humanitarian dependence and local competition, but violence is not an automatic outcome.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The Mecufi climate-security assessment involved local government, traditional leaders, communities, climate specialists and peacebuilders using focus groups, dialogues and surveys. [MOZ-E-014]
- In Mecufi, the assessment associates drought, rising temperature and irregular rainfall with pressure on arable land, fisheries and food access, then describes food insecurity and resource competition interacting with youth unemployment and poverty. [MOZ-E-015]
- The Mecufi assessment reports that conflict-driven IDP arrivals increased pressure on natural resources, services and livelihoods and were associated with mangrove cutting for salt production. [MOZ-E-016]
- The Mecufi assessment describes cyclone/flood/drought-related loss of productive soils combined with IDP arrival and land occupation as a possible source of land disputes. [MOZ-E-017]
- Source dated 2025-09-29 reports: UNDP's review cites earlier analysis that conflict-driven flight from homes and fields coincided with erratic-rainfall crop losses; displaced fishers also shifted to unfamiliar subsistence farming. [MOZ-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-08-26 reports: Cabo Delgado's conflict, displacement, climate exposure and resource pressure overlap in Nankumi (Ancuabe), Naminawe (Metuge) and Metula (Pemba); site selection noted host–IDP tension risk. [MOZ-E-109]
