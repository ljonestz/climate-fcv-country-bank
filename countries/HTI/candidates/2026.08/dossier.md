# Haiti Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Haiti faces material cyclone, flood, heat, drought, rainfall, and coastal pressures, but the oldest admitted projection source uses earlier model generations and the bank lacks a current high-resolution national hazard set. [HTI-E-001, HTI-E-002, HTI-E-006, HTI-P-001, HTI-P-002]
- The strongest Climate-FCV evidence supports mediated and bidirectional interactions through rainfed livelihoods, food and water systems, displacement, armed access constraints, and weakened services rather than climate determinism. [HTI-E-004, HTI-E-005, HTI-E-007, HTI-E-008, HTI-E-009, HTI-P-002, HTI-P-003, HTI-P-004]
- National planning, municipal civil protection, humanitarian delivery, and emerging climate-security coordination provide documented capacity, while implementation, distributional effects, and independent outcome evaluation remain limited. [HTI-E-003, HTI-E-010, HTI-E-011, HTI-E-012, HTI-E-013, HTI-E-014, HTI-P-005, HTI-P-006]

Critical remaining gaps:
- The bank lacks a current, high-resolution and internally consistent numeric baseline and scenario set for cyclone, flood, heat, drought, rainfall, and sea-level hazards across Haiti. [HTI-E-001, HTI-E-002, HTI-P-001]
- Independent evaluation is limited to the WFP portfolio through 2022; municipal DRM, adaptation, humanitarian, social-protection, and climate-security outcomes and unintended effects remain largely unevaluated. [HTI-E-010, HTI-E-011, HTI-E-012, HTI-E-013, HTI-E-014, HTI-P-005, HTI-P-006]
- The fcv-to-climate reverse pathway is supported through displacement, access, and service disruption, but quantitative estimates of conflict's additional effect on climate losses, adaptation, and recovery are absent. [HTI-E-007, HTI-E-008, HTI-E-009, HTI-E-015, HTI-P-003, HTI-P-004]
- Distributional evidence for disability, age, gender, livelihood, host-community status, and different urban and rural settings is incomplete and not consistently linked to hazard exposure. [HTI-E-005, HTI-E-007, HTI-E-009, HTI-E-013, HTI-P-002, HTI-P-004]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Haiti faces rising heat, uncertain seasonal rainfall, and sea-level pressures. [HTI-E-001]
- Cyclone, flood, drought, and coastal exposure is nationally material but spatially uneven. [HTI-E-002]
- Armed violence drives displacement and sharply restricts services and humanitarian access. [HTI-E-007]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Rainfed dependence creates food and livelihood sensitivity to interacting climate and security shocks. [HTI-E-005]
- Flood and rainfall health risks rise where WASH and health systems are weak. [HTI-E-006]
- Conflict displacement concentrates people in sites and communities with high hazard sensitivity. [HTI-E-009]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Violence erodes the service and access systems needed to absorb climate and health shocks. [HTI-E-008]
- Planned cross-sector actions target watershed, food, water, health, and municipal resilience. [HTI-E-012]
- The current WFP plan provides intended adaptive and delivery capacity across food systems and safety nets. [HTI-E-013]
- Chronic institutional weakness limits equitable shock response and recovery. [HTI-E-015]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The national plan defines sector and municipal adaptation responsibilities but not delivered outcomes. [HTI-E-003]
- Independent evaluation found adaptive delivery alongside constrained resilience transition. [HTI-E-010]
- Municipal civil-protection structures provide a nationwide disaster-response platform. [HTI-E-011]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Cyclone and flood losses may deepen displacement and exclusion where poverty, unsafe settlement, and weak local delivery constrain recovery. [HTI-P-001; HTI-E-001, HTI-E-002, HTI-E-004, HTI-E-011, HTI-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Damage to homes, roads, health facilities, crops, water systems, and local livelihoods
  - FCV mediator: Poverty, deforestation, unsafe urbanization, weak services, limited municipal resources, and low institutional legitimacy
  - Possible consequence: Displacement, unequal recovery, livelihood loss, and perceived exclusion may deepen social tension, but violence is not an automatic climate outcome

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Climate-security risks are mediated and bidirectional, not deterministic. [HTI-E-004]
- Emerging climate-security coordination offers a potential resilience and peace platform. [HTI-E-014]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National syntheses are complemented by evidence from Port-au-Prince, displacement sites, coastal plains, watersheds, and municipal systems; urban and site findings must not be generalized across rural or remote Haiti. [HTI-E-002, HTI-E-007, HTI-E-008, HTI-E-009, HTI-E-011, HTI-P-003, HTI-P-004, HTI-P-005]
- The admitted sources provide little comparable evidence for current hazard, access, and capacity conditions across all departments and municipalities, especially gang-controlled and hard-to-reach areas. [HTI-E-007, HTI-E-008, HTI-E-015, HTI-P-003]
- Agriculture, food security, water, health, shelter, transport, local governance, humanitarian access, and social protection are represented; energy, fisheries, private-sector exposure, and ecosystem outcomes remain thin. [HTI-E-005, HTI-E-006, HTI-E-008, HTI-E-011, HTI-E-013, HTI-P-002, HTI-P-003, HTI-P-005]
