## Climate pressures and exposure

- An older World Bank-GFDRR profile reports observed warming since 1960 and projects further warming, more hot days and nights, uncertain rainfall outside a drier June-August signal, and sea-level rise exposure for Haiti. Scenario: Older AR4-era national and Caribbean projections to 2060 and 2090 [HTI-E-001]
- The World Bank climate-health assessment identifies Atlantic hurricane exposure, low-lying coastal plains, heavy rainfall, periodic drought, and repeated flood and storm events as major hazards affecting Haiti. [HTI-E-002]
- UN reporting documents gang violence as a primary driver of mass displacement, restricted movement, complicated humanitarian access, school closure, and loss of basic services, with women and children disproportionately affected. [HTI-E-007]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: Melissa damaged Grand’Anse roads and isolated communal sections; Les Cayes hospital flooding and minor Jérémie/Port-de-Paix damage were reported separately from insecurity restrictions. [HTI-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- SIPRI reports that most Haitian agriculture is rainfed and that drought, irregular rainfall, soil and water stress interact with insecurity and economic crisis to threaten production and food security. [HTI-E-005]
- The climate-health assessment and SIPRI synthesis link heavy rainfall and flooding with water- and vector-borne disease risks where waste management, safe water, sanitation, and health systems are inadequate. [HTI-E-006]
- UN reporting finds that displaced households often live with overstretched host families or in crowded, high-risk sites lacking adequate food, water, sanitation, shelter, and medical care. [HTI-E-009]
- Source dated 2026-02-24 reports: UNICEF reports May 2025 heavy rains inundated about 3,900 homes in Nord-Est; Melissa later damaged schools, latrines and water systems especially in southern departments. [HTI-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-02-24 reports: Following Melissa, UNICEF reports cholera outbreaks in Bainet, Marigot, Belle-Anse, Grand-Gosier and Thiotte, exposing vulnerabilities where climate shocks damaged services. [HTI-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-02-24 reports: UNICEF reports violence-driven site growth after Mirebalais/Saut d’Eau attacks and displacement in Petite-Rivière; most displaced people lived outside formal sites or with hosts. [HTI-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-10 reports: Melissa road damage disrupted Jacmel–Port-au-Prince connections and Belle-Anse/Marigot supply and emergency-access routes. [HTI-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-09 reports: UNFPA’s August 2026 snapshot reports violence displacement in Kenscoff, Dessalines and Petite-Rivière and constrained access to prompt care for sexual-violence survivors. [HTI-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- UNICEF and ICRC report that armed violence interrupts clinics, supply chains, water, health, and transport services and forces humanitarian operations to adapt or suspend delivery in affected areas. [HTI-E-008]
- Haiti's adaptation plan prioritizes climate-smart agriculture, irrigation, integrated water management, reforestation, watershed management, capacity building, and disease prevention among its highest-priority actions. [HTI-E-012]
- WFP's 2024-2028 plan intends to link emergency assistance with safety nets, livelihood opportunities, home-grown school feeding, climate resilience, national systems, and humanitarian logistics. [HTI-E-013]
- The World Bank's 2022 diagnostic identifies chronic fragility, weak and uneven state capacity, recurrent crises, and limited shock-responsive social protection and service systems as structural constraints on inclusive recovery. [HTI-E-015]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: UNFPA maintained GBV hotlines/referrals and mobile services despite access barriers; reproductive-health kits supplied facilities, while projected coverage was an intended benefit. [HTI-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-09 reports: In July–August 2026, UNFPA reports delivering SRH and GBV services where access was possible, but financing remained well below its appeal. [HTI-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Haiti's National Adaptation Plan sets a 2022-2030 framework covering agriculture, health, infrastructure, and water, with municipal capacity, governance, costing, resource mobilization, and monitoring as stated priorities. [HTI-E-003]
- WFP's independent country-plan evaluation found that the portfolio adapted to a difficult context and reached many people, but contextual and funding constraints kept it focused on emergency assistance and impeded intended integration and resilience shifts. [HTI-E-010]
- A World Bank results brief reports expansion of certified Municipal Civil Protection Committees to all 140 municipalities, training of volunteers, national DRM reforms, and use of strengthened systems during the 2021 earthquake response. [HTI-E-011]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: UNFPA reports mobile clinics reached 2,453 Melissa-affected people in Petit-Goâve, alongside separate internally displaced populations in metropolitan and Artibonite sites. [HTI-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-10 reports: FEWS NET reports funding-related inability to preposition cyclone-season food, compounded by violence-related access interruptions and logistics costs near displacement sites. [HTI-E-110]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- SIPRI and NUPI assess that climate change is rarely a main conflict driver in Haiti but can interact with livelihoods, mobility, armed actors, and political-economic grievances, while violence and instability weaken community resilience. [HTI-E-004]
- SIPRI documents a Haiti Climate Security Group and recommends climate-resilient livelihood options, integrated dialogue, and a national climate-security policy, while not demonstrating that these mechanisms have produced peace outcomes. [HTI-E-014]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: UNFPA reports November 2025 insecurity postponed planned distributions and mobile clinics, including support for hurricane-affected women/girls around Petit-Goâve and southern areas. [HTI-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-10 reports: The October 2025 assessment reports gang passage fees and restricted agricultural-input delivery along RN1/RN2/RN3, while Melissa separately flooded displacement sites in metropolitan Port-au-Prince. [HTI-E-108]
