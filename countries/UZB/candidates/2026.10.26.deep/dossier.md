Critical remaining gaps:
- The 2021 NDC records government projections and planned adaptation; implementation status and updated subnational risk data were not checked. []
- The World Bank CCDR release does not provide the underlying model assumptions for the 2030 population-risk or 2050 economy estimates. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []
- The bank contains no completed mediated climate-FCV pathway; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- No country-specific primary evidence was found that climate change caused violent conflict in Uzbekistan; NUPI's source-stated tension pathway is conditional and regional. []
- No evidence was located linking FCV events to blocked adaptation access, interruption of a named project, or reduced resilience delivery; the June 2022 protest trigger was constitutional, not climate-related. []
- Detailed methods and district-level figures for GGGI's four-district risk profile remain unavailable because the full report PDF could not be read; the Atlas was excluded because it is marked for internal use only. []
- The 2021 UNDP project counts are closeout-reported reach and practices, not independently measured climate-resilience impacts; the FAO 2026 account describes consultations and proposed restoration rather than completed outcomes. []
- The public-hosted Atlas marked internal-use-only was excluded; its substantive extracted claims were removed from the uncommitted intake before publication. []
- GGGI's target-area poverty/training percentages are not adopted as population estimates without methods, thresholds or sample vintage. []
- No new complete local climate-violence or reverse-FCV chain is inferred; the transboundary tension warning remains regional and conditional. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC reports Uzbekistan’s average temperature rose about 0.29°C per decade from the early 1950s, roughly twice the global rate. [UZB-E-001]
- The NDC expects increasing aridity, especially in western Uzbekistan, as declining water resources and changing precipitation contribute to longer droughts and extreme events. [UZB-E-003]
- The CCDR release projects at least 8 million people will live in very high climate-risk areas by 2030. [UZB-E-005]
- GGGI's March2025 publisher summary identifies restricted water supply in Bozataw, Chimbay, Karauzyak and Kegeyli, associated with upstream diversion and climate change; their separate contributions are not measured. [UZB-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- For 2039 with 90% water availability, the NDC projects 10–13% cotton and winter-wheat yield losses and 10–12% for alfalfa under RCP4.5/8.5. [UZB-E-002]
- Without adaptation, the CCDR estimates the economy could be 10% smaller by 2050, with lower employment and household incomes. [UZB-E-006]
- The release identifies Aral Sea degradation, water scarcity, drought, heat, rainfall volatility, and dust storms as societal and economic risks. [UZB-E-007]
- Across six metrics, NUPI ranks Uzbekistan as most water-insufficient in Central Asia; 9 of 13 provinces have high water stress, including four extremely high. [UZB-E-107]
- Participants near the Lower Amu Darya State Biosphere Reserve and Baday-Tugay connected land degradation with insufficient irrigation and drinking water, limited employment, and high agricultural costs; they identified reliable water as essential for restoration. [UZB-E-112]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Planned social-sector adaptation includes climate-risk information, hydrometeorological early warnings, and greater participation by women and local communities in planning and management. [UZB-E-004]
- The CCDR recommends social protection for groups vulnerable to climate risks and skills training to prepare workers for green-economy jobs. [UZB-E-008]
- GGGI's March2025 summary reports poverty-related adaptation constraints, particularly among young women and dehkan farmers in its four-district target area. [UZB-E-105]
- GGGI's March2025 summary describes expensive formal finance, limited formal management training and missing climate strategies among target-area farmer groups. [UZB-E-106]
- Three project advisory centers in Nukus and Kegeyli district served 21,200 farmers and household-land owners, including more than 6,000 women, according to UNDP's 2021 closeout account. [UZB-E-110]
- UNDP reports that 41,200 people applied resource-saving climate-resilient agriculture and 43,750 farmers used water-saving practices such as laser leveling and drip irrigation in Karakalpakstan. [UZB-E-111]
- Karakalpakstan participants supported restoring degraded Tugay forests near Baday-Tugay with native riparian species; FAO reports that water access and livelihood pressures will affect sustained community participation. [UZB-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- At project closeout, UNDP reported 10 automated meteorological stations and two equipped hydrological posts in Karakalpakstan, supporting drought early warnings with six-to-eight months' lead time for the lower Amu Darya. [UZB-E-109]
- Under FOLUR-Uzbekistan, farmers, pastoralists, women, protected-area staff, communities, and authorities reviewed restoration proposals, identified priority sites, and discussed implementation and maintenance roles across three regions, including Karakalpakstan. [UZB-E-113]
