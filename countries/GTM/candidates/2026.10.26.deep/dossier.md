Critical remaining gaps:
- Searches did not find a directly inspected Guatemala primary passage explicitly linking the climate and food-security pressures documented here to violence, armed conflict or displacement. []
- The July–September 2026 food-security projections in the government/IPC sources had elapsed by 27 October 2026; realized outcomes were not verified. []
- The Guatemala 2025 Biennial Transparency Report could not be opened because the official PDF redirect looped; its search listing was not used as evidence. []
- Expired July–September forecasts are excluded; the October 2026–February 2027 outlook is not treated as an observed 2026 outcome. []
- The named grain-bank example and joint-programme geography add local context, but no supported climate→violence or reverse insecurity-to-adaptation mechanism was retrieved. []

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2026-07-16 reports: WFP reports that climate variability compounded by El Niño is reducing Dry Corridor harvests and food access, with women bearing a disproportionate household food-allocation burden. [GTM-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-07-16 reports: In Uriles Pinalón, Jalapa, a woman farmer described late, insufficient rain and poor milpa yields; WFP says women in rural Indigenous communities, including Ch’orti’-speaking areas, often reduce their own food portions for household members. [GTM-E-102]
- Source dated not stated reports: The project profile identifies irregular rainfall, drought, floods, landslides, environmental degradation and low crop yields in its Dry Corridor context; it reports that Indigenous people, especially women and children, are disproportionately affected by poverty and social exclusion. [GTM-E-107]
- The August 2026 IPC publisher page reports an estimated 2.5 million people in Phase 3+ acute food insecurity for April–June 2026. This is a historical national estimate; later projections are not represented as observed outcomes. [GTM-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-07-16 reports: WFP reports installing a CERF-supported grain bank and silo in Uriles Pinalón; local women organize and manage the grain, reducing the need for households to travel to town to buy maize and beans. [GTM-E-103]
- Source dated not stated reports: The UN joint programme targets more than 7,000 households, especially children under five and pregnant or lactating women, in seven Dry Corridor municipalities across Chiquimula and Jalapa: Jocotán, Camotán, Olopa, San Juan Ermita, Chiquimula, San Luis Jilotepeque and San Pedro Pinula. [GTM-E-106]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated not stated reports: Planned outputs include drought-adapted food systems, community water infrastructure and watershed planning with women's participation, plus stronger central and local capacity for food and water security and gender-responsive resilience. [GTM-E-108]
