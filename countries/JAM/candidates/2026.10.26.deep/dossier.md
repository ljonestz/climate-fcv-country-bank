# Jamaica Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Parish CRVAs provide named Trelawny and Westmoreland examples, distinguishing composite vulnerability from hazard exposure. Recommended local measures remain proposals. [JAM-E-101, JAM-E-102, JAM-E-103, JAM-E-104, JAM-E-105]
- The IEG approach paper explicitly links violence/economic constraints to greater climate-shock vulnerability, at a generic settlement level. The national adaptation audit documents dated implementation/coordination gaps without attributing them to insecurity. [JAM-E-106, JAM-E-107, JAM-E-108, JAM-E-109, JAM-E-110, JAM-E-111, JAM-E-112, JAM-E-113, JAM-P-101]

Critical remaining gaps:
- The reviewed material supports co-location of climate/disaster vulnerability with crime, violence and exclusion, and an explicit reverse link from violence to climate vulnerability; it does not establish that climate shocks cause or escalate violence in Jamaica. []
- No directly inspected source established that insecurity prevents climate-adaptation access in a named community; the IEG approach paper's reverse link is national or generic-zone and not quantified. []
- The parish CRVAs provide named local assessments, but projections and composites carry stated data and scenario uncertainties; recommended actions are not proof of delivery. []
- The Auditor General's LoCAL status is dated through January 2026. The undated LG-ACT webpage does not establish present implementation or outcomes. []
- The 2025 IEG approach paper is a contextual diagnosis for an intended evaluation, not a completed programme-impact evaluation. []
- No climate-caused violence or named local insecurity blockage to adaptation is established. Generic national reverse vulnerability must not be assigned to Trelawny/Westmoreland hotspot communities without evidence. []
- Citation correction: overlapping-risk and reverse-link claims use the December 2025 IEG approach paper, while the separate recovery-constraint claim uses the 2022 SCD. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-08-10 reports: Westmoreland's assessment projects warming, more erratic rainfall, drought, flooding and coastal surge risks; low-lying Negril and Savanna-la-Mar are named, while interior farming areas face water stress. [JAM-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-08-10 reports: Salt Marsh, Falmouth, Duncans, Troy, Wakefield, Bunkers Hill, Stettin, Wait-a-Bit, Ulster Spring and St. Vincent fall in Trelawny's highest composite-risk tier (moderate): very high sensitivity and low adaptive capacity elevate risk despite generally low hazard exposure. [JAM-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-08-10 reports: Darliston, Enfield, Petersville and Belmont are identified as interior vulnerability hotspots with older populations, low incomes and poor service access; the report rates adaptive capacity as generally low, including in Savanna-la-Mar. [JAM-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-12-19 reports: The diagnostic reports overlapping climate/disaster risk, service and built-environment deficits, crime/violence, and social/economic exclusion in low-income often informal coastal settlements; women, youth, people with disabilities, fisherfolk and farmers are disproportionately affected. [JAM-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025-08-10 reports: The assessment identifies poor roads, limited health/education access, climate-sensitive farming and weak rural institutional presence as vulnerabilities; it recommends road, drainage, water, crop, mobile-health and preparedness measures for hotspot communities. [JAM-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-08-10 reports: The CRVA proposes water harvesting and irrigation support in Darliston, Petersville and Broughton; gender-sensitive community training in Enfield and Whithorn; and drainage, resilient housing and mangrove protection in coastal towns. [JAM-E-105]
- Source dated 2022 reports: Female-headed households and children face disproportionate climate-shock impacts; rural poverty is more exposed where infrastructure is weaker and livelihoods depend on nature-based agriculture, while costly insurance and poor social-protection targeting constrain recovery. [JAM-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-02 reports: The audit's January 2026 status table still lists the Local Climate Adaptive Living Facility pilot as not started; its narrative says implementation had not begun by October 2025 while UNCDF–GCF grant-agreement clearance was pending. [JAM-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-02 reports: For 2017–July 2024, the Climate Change Focal Point Network had no documented stand-alone meetings and one joint session; the Advisory Board met 14 of 32 expected times, weakening information exchange and coordination. [JAM-E-110]
- Source dated 2026-02 reports: The audit says engagement with youth and vulnerable groups was largely event-based; it also records a 2024 youth workshop with about 100 participants and six-location adaptation workshops with 137 participants. [JAM-E-111]
- Source dated not stated reports: The government brief proposes Westmoreland water governance: reliable rural potable water and urban drainage, rainwater storage and treatment, drought-period monitoring, community operator training, and participatory parish/community development committees. [JAM-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The IEG Jamaica context diagnosis links crime/violence and constrained economic activity to weaker climate-shock resilience in excluded settlements; this reverse mechanism is generic and unquantified, not a claim that climate causes crime. [JAM-P-101; JAM-E-106, JAM-E-107, JAM-E-108]
  - Documented impact: Household and community vulnerability is heightened where basic services, housing and economic opportunities are weak.
  - FCV mediator: The approach paper links spatial inequality and inner-city decay to crime/violence that hampers economic activity and undermines coping.
  - Possible consequence: Greater vulnerability to climate shocks, as stated by the IEG context diagnosis; no measured increase or named-community adaptation-access incident.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['JAM-E-106'], 'impact': ['JAM-E-106', 'JAM-E-108'], 'mediator': ['JAM-E-107'], 'consequence': ['JAM-E-107']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Poverty, land-use planning, public services, employment and broader economic shocks independently affect vulnerability and crime.']
  - Uncertainty: Source-stated reverse mechanism in a December 2025 evaluation approach paper. Not a causal estimate, completed evaluation result, current crime map or evidence that climate shocks cause crime.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2025-12-19 reports: It reports that weak land-use planning and limited services/jobs deepen spatial inequality and inner-city decay, raise crime and violence, and hamper economic activity; this increases vulnerability to climate shocks. [JAM-E-107]
- Source dated not stated reports: The ministry describes mayor-chaired Parish Safety and Security Committees as local coordination and citizen-participation channels; the mandate lists natural disasters, exclusion of vulnerable groups and environmental risks among security and sustainability concerns. [JAM-E-113]
