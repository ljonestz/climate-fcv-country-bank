## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2020-12-18 reports: OECD reports earlier spring runoff nationwide since the 1980 s; cited records show maximum spring runoff fell 43% on average between 1877–1965 and 1966–2005, with Grodno more affected than Brest oblast. [BLR-E-104]
- Source dated 2020-12-18 reports: OECD reports that Pripyat runoff during Gomel's growing season could decline by up to 25% by 2035, partly due to climate-linked precipitation decline, intensifying seasonal water stress for agriculture. Scenario: The OECD report gives a 2035 projection but does not specify the emissions pathway. [BLR-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2020-12-18 reports: In Kopyl rayon, OECD reports centralized water access for 98% of Kopyl town residents but 27% across rural settlements; many small communities rely on shallow wells with limited routine water-quality checks. [BLR-E-106]
- Source dated 2017-11-09 reports: The regional assessment identifies southern Belarusian Polesie, including the Polesie State Radioecological Reserve, as a climate-security hotspot where drought and flood risks threaten livelihoods, health and local economic security. Scenario: The hotspot assessment describes risk pathways; no emissions scenario is specified. [BLR-E-107]
- Source dated 2020 reports: The UNCCD article reports 7.9 mm of rain in Homiel during the April 2020 planting season, 19% of normal, and says roughly 45% of the region lies in the Chornobyl-contaminated zone, making peat fires a public-health concern. [BLR-E-109]
- Source dated 2024-09-10 reports: Farmers interviewed in Brahin district report near-annual heatwaves causing drought, water shortages and crop failures; late May or June frosts require protective crop covering and add costs. [BLR-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2020 reports: UNCCD reports about 1,000 ha of degraded peatland rewetted in Mogilev region in 2018–2019 with local-authority, forestry and community partnership; its 2020 Homiel phase targeted at least 2,000 ha and improved fire monitoring and early warning. [BLR-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-09-10 reports: Three sisters running a Brahin farm diversified and rotated crops, shifted grains toward drought-resilient winter cereals, and used an EU–UNDP-supported greenhouse with ventilation, heating and watering systems. [BLR-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-02-07 reports: NC8 reported adopted agriculture, forestry and water adaptation strategies and health-preparedness measures; it said a national adaptation plan was planned for 2024. [BLR-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-02-07 reports: During review, Belarus said its hydrometeorological network is updated annually but sanctions pressure had ended some bilateral agreements with neighbours for exchanging hydrometeorological information. [BLR-E-103]
- Source dated 2017-11-09 reports: The assessment says irregular monitoring in the most contaminated areas undermines timely warnings and prevention as floods, drought, dust and fires could spread Chornobyl radionuclides across Polesie. Scenario: The assessment identifies conditional spread pathways without a quantified emissions scenario. [BLR-E-108]
