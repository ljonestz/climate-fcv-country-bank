# Myanmar Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Climate risk is high and geographically differentiated, but the strongest current vulnerability signal is the intersection of hazards with conflict displacement, food insecurity, poverty and constrained services - not climate pressure alone. [MMR-E-001, MMR-E-002, MMR-E-003, MMR-E-004, MMR-E-005, MMR-E-008, MMR-E-009, MMR-E-010, MMR-E-011, MMR-E-012]
- FCV-to-climate mechanisms are better evidenced than climate-to-violence mechanisms. Conflict, access restrictions, territorial fragmentation and repeat displacement demonstrably impede disaster response and adaptation, whereas climate effects on armed conflict remain highly mediated and causally uncertain. [MMR-E-017, MMR-E-018, MMR-E-019, MMR-E-020, MMR-E-021, MMR-E-024, MMR-E-025]
- Myanmar retains important policy, local, humanitarian and community resilience capacities, but independently evaluated post-2021 effectiveness evidence is scarce. [MMR-E-006, MMR-E-007, MMR-E-013, MMR-E-014, MMR-E-015, MMR-E-016, MMR-E-022, MMR-E-023, MMR-E-024, MMR-E-025, MMR-E-026, MMR-E-027]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: A current, publicly accessible Myanmar-specific downscaled projection set with transparent model ensembles, modern SSPs and subnational hazard detail was not identified. The NAPA's useful regional detail relies on a legacy A2/PRECIS framework. (UNFCCC1) []
- Current national adaptation planning: The UNFCCC registry still identifies the July 2021 updated NDC as Myanmar's active submission; that NDC said a NAP process would be initiated. Searches conducted for this packet did not identify a subsequently submitted Myanmar NAP. This should be independently rechecked before database publication. (UNFCCC7) []
- Geographic/access gaps: Current field evidence is weakest where conflict, access restrictions or changing territorial control impede observation. Lack of evidence cannot be interpreted as low vulnerability. []
- Evaluation gap: The strongest independent adaptation-performance evidence located is the 2018 Dry Zone mid-term evaluation and a 2019 terminal-evaluation registry record. Comparable independent evaluations under post-2021 conflict conditions are lacking. []
- Unintended effects: There is qualitative evidence that humanitarian and environmental action can be politically instrumentalized, but little systematic evaluation of whether climate/resilience programmes alter legitimacy, exclusion, resource control or conflict incentives. (SIPRI6) []
- Differentiated vulnerability: Evidence is insufficiently systematic across gender, disability, age, ethnicity, statelessness, livelihood, urban informality and territorial-control conditions. Rohingya vulnerability is documented particularly in Rakhine, but should not be reduced to climate exposure. (Doctors Without Borders4) []
- Causal uncertainty: The climate -> FCV direction lacks credible identification of independent climate effects on recruitment, political support, communal violence or armed-conflict intensity. Most evidence is mediated synthesis rather than causal estimation. []
- FCV -> climate/adaptation measurement: There is strong qualitative/event evidence that insecurity and access restrictions obstruct response, but little longitudinal quantification of how much adaptation coverage, recovery speed or resilience is lost because of conflict. []
- Sector gaps: Fisheries, urban systems, energy, health adaptation and coastal/sea-level adaptation have thinner current evidence than agriculture, food, displacement and humanitarian access. []
- Inaccessible/full-text limits: The 2026 peer-reviewed article's public abstract was accessible but the full text was restricted; two large UNDP reports were usable only through verified publication-page summaries in this research session. (Sage Journals8) []
- *None are completely unsupported, but coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, institutional-capacity, response-performance and resilience-peace-capacity remain only partially covered. In particular, response-performance evidence is disproportionately pre-2021 and resilience-peace evidence is highly localized. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Observed warming ≈0.2°C/decade. [MMR-E-001]
- Mid-century warming increases under both SSPs. [MMR-E-002]
- Wetter mean conditions do not remove flood variability. [MMR-E-003]
- Vulnerability varies sharply by ecological zone. [MMR-E-005]
- Nearly four million IDPs. [MMR-E-008]
- Displacement is heavily concentrated in Sagaing. [MMR-E-009]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Exposure and poverty amplify climatic risk. [MMR-E-004]
- Stateless displacement creates distinct Rakhine vulnerability. [MMR-E-010]
- Acute food insecurity is nationwide and severe. [MMR-E-011]
- Hunger is explicitly multi-causal. [MMR-E-012]
- FCV erodes underlying adaptive/service capacity. [MMR-E-026]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- National policy recognizes conflict displacement in adaptation design. [MMR-E-007]
- Security conditions condition resilience delivery. [MMR-E-013]
- Localized resilience is proposed for a multi-shock food system. [MMR-E-027]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Adaptation policy recognizes coordination and capacity deficits. [MMR-E-006]
- Water infrastructure gains were limited at mid-term. [MMR-E-014]
- Positive project performance had scale/sustainability limits. [MMR-E-015]
- Early-warning information had not yet achieved broad village effects. [MMR-E-016]
- Conflict politics shape climate action itself. [MMR-E-024]
- Third-party delivery is an FCV adaptation of service architecture. [MMR-E-025]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Dry Zone climate stress can worsen farm hardship, but any effect on conflict dynamics is mediated through finance, livelihoods and pre-existing political violence. [MMR-P-001; MMR-E-001, MMR-E-002, MMR-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Reduced rain-fed agricultural performance and livelihood security.
  - FCV mediator: Debt, landlessness risk, rising input costs, declining credit, poverty and an existing armed-conflict environment.
  - Possible consequence: These combined pressures may influence grievances and household/community relationships with armed resistance actors; the evidence does not show climate as an independent cause of recruitment or violence.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-001', 'MMR-E-002'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-021'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-021'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed violence', 'scorched-earth tactics', 'credit contraction after 2021', 'inflation/input prices', 'land tenure', 'poverty and political mobilization']
  - Uncertainty: SIPRI/NUPI directly presents the chain but does not estimate the independent effect of climate stress. (SIPRI6)
- Conflict and access restrictions have demonstrably reduced the reach of climate-disaster response in specific Myanmar settings. [MMR-P-002; MMR-E-017, MMR-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Damage to shelter, WASH, infrastructure, livelihoods and services.
  - FCV mediator: Humanitarian-access restrictions, insecurity, territorial contestation and blocked transport.
  - Possible consequence: Slower relief and recovery can prolong unmet needs and deepen vulnerability.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-017', 'MMR-E-019'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-019'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-017', 'MMR-E-019'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-017', 'MMR-E-019']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Physical remoteness', 'road destruction', 'terrain and the scale of the disaster']
  - Uncertainty: Evidence is strong for specific events but cannot be assumed uniform across Myanmar. (Doctors Without Borders4)
- Conflict displacement can turn a climatic shock into repeated displacement and additional protection risk. [MMR-P-003; MMR-E-008, MMR-E-010, MMR-E-018, MMR-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Temporary relocation, camp damage, WASH shortages and mine/ERW uncertainty.
  - FCV mediator: Prior conflict displacement and settlement in vulnerable locations.
  - Possible consequence: People displaced by conflict may face repeated displacement and accumulating asset/service losses during climatic disasters.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-018'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-018'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-008', 'MMR-E-010', 'MMR-E-020'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-018']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Poverty', 'poor settlement siting and inadequate infrastructure can generate vulnerability independently of conflict']
  - Uncertainty: National IDP figures do not indicate how many IDPs are directly climate-exposed. (World Bank5)
- Conflict-related mobility constraints can reduce people's ability to use migration or evacuation as climate coping strategies. [MMR-P-004; MMR-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Need to evacuate, migrate or relocate.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict, mobility restrictions, insecurity and contested territory.
  - Possible consequence: Households unable to move safely may remain in highly exposed areas or lose access to livelihoods and assistance.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-020'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-020'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-020'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-020']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Household income', 'social networks', 'documentation status and transport availability']
  - Uncertainty: Limited quantitative evidence identifies the relative contribution of each mobility constraint. (SIPRI6)
- In post-coup Myanmar, disaster and climate action can become part of struggles over authority rather than merely technical adaptation. [MMR-P-005; MMR-E-019, MMR-E-023, MMR-E-024]
  - Documented impact: Large relief and governance needs.
  - FCV mediator: Competing authorities' attempts to demonstrate governing capacity and legitimacy.
  - Possible consequence: Disaster response may reshape grievances and perceived authority without the hazard itself causing renewed conflict.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-019'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-019'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-023', 'MMR-E-024'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-024']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Long-standing ethnic politics', 'military strategy', 'territorial contestation and pre-existing legitimacy']
  - Uncertainty: Political effects vary strongly by actor and locality. (SIPRI6)
- Locally mediated harvest access can support food security and temporary de-escalation, but it is not evidence that adaptation causes peace. [MMR-P-006; MMR-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Threats to harvest and food security.
  - FCV mediator: Armed confrontation preventing normal agricultural activity.
  - Possible consequence: Negotiated agricultural access may create a temporary peace-supporting window.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-022'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-022'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-022'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-022']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Ceasefire incentives may be humanitarian', 'military or political rather than climate-specific']
  - Uncertainty: No evidence of durable peace effects. (SIPRI6)
- Conflict and divided authority appear to make adaptation capacity territorially uneven and politically contested. [MMR-P-007; MMR-E-006, MMR-E-016, MMR-E-024, MMR-E-025]
  - Documented impact: High demand for climate information, agricultural adaptation and disaster preparedness.
  - FCV mediator: Fragmented territorial control, contested institutions and politicization of environmental action.
  - Possible consequence: National adaptation plans and services may have uneven territorial reach and may be interpreted through political competition.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-006'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-016'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-024', 'MMR-E-025'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-006']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Long-standing administrative capacity and finance constraints predate the coup']
  - Uncertainty: No national post-2021 implementation audit was located.
- Alternative delivery institutions can bridge some resilience gaps, but effectiveness and equity require independent evaluation. [MMR-P-008; MMR-E-013, MMR-E-023, MMR-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Local infrastructure, food and service needs.
  - FCV mediator: Weak/contested state reach and security conditions.
  - Possible consequence: Community assets, grassroots institutions and third-party implementation may sustain limited resilience where conventional public delivery is constrained.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MMR-E-013'], 'impact': ['MMR-E-013'], 'mediator': ['MMR-E-023', 'MMR-E-025'], 'consequence': ['MMR-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Selection toward more stable areas may itself explain better delivery']
  - Uncertainty: Programme outputs and institutional activity are not evidence of durable resilience or peace outcomes. (World Food Programme3)

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Conflict reduced flood-response reach. [MMR-E-017]
- Conflict displacement compounded flood and protection exposure. [MMR-E-018]
- Political/access restrictions delayed cyclone relief. [MMR-E-019]
- Conflict drives mobility; climate hazards compound it. [MMR-E-020]
- Livelihood stress is a possible mediator into conflict dynamics. [MMR-E-021]
- Harvest access generated a localized peace-supporting window. [MMR-E-022]
- Non-state/local institutions can supply resilience functions. [MMR-E-023]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National evidence is strongest for climate trends/projections, displacement totals, food insecurity and policy frameworks. The most detailed Climate-FCV evidence is instead subnational: the Dry Zone for agriculture and livelihood stress; Rakhine for Cyclone Mocha, aid restrictions, Rohingya/stateless vulnerability and competing disaster governance; Karenni for a localized harvest ceasefire; and Karen/KNU areas for environmental governance. These findings must not be generalized to all Myanmar. (SIPRI6) []
- The September 2024 Yagi/monsoon assessment provides broader multiregional evidence across central, eastern and southeastern Myanmar, but the World Bank explicitly warns that satellite analysis cannot cleanly separate Yagi flooding from contemporaneous monsoon flooding. (World Bank5) []
- Sagaing's exceptionally high current displacement burden, Rakhine's large displaced/stateless population and Magway's displacement require separate treatment from Ayeyarwady/delta, Yangon, Shan, Kachin and coastal contexts. (UNHCR Data Portal2) Areas under intense or changing territorial control are also likely to be less observable, making “no data” different from “no impact.” []
- Agriculture, food security, water, humanitarian response, displacement, transport, shelter and climate-information systems are comparatively well represented. Forest/environmental governance has some direct post-coup evidence through local/competing institutions. Fisheries are present in national policy and vulnerability framing but have much thinner current empirical evidence. Urban heat, urban drainage, energy-system climate resilience, coastal fisheries and health-system adaptation are also underdeveloped in this packet. []
