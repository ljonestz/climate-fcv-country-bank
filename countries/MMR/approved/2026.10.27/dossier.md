- Climate projections: A current, publicly accessible Myanmar-specific downscaled projection set with transparent model ensembles, modern SSPs and subnational hazard detail was not identified. The NAPA's useful regional detail relies on a legacy A2/PRECIS framework. (UNFCCC1) []
- Current national adaptation planning: The UNFCCC registry still identifies the July 2021 updated NDC as Myanmar's active submission; that NDC said a NAP process would be initiated. Searches conducted for this packet did not identify a subsequently submitted Myanmar NAP. This should be independently rechecked before database publication. (UNFCCC7) []
- Geographic/access gaps: Current field evidence is weakest where conflict, access restrictions or changing territorial control impede observation. Lack of evidence cannot be interpreted as low vulnerability. []
- Evaluation gap: The strongest independent adaptation-performance evidence located is the 2018 Dry Zone mid-term evaluation and a 2019 terminal-evaluation registry record. Comparable independent evaluations under post-2021 conflict conditions are lacking. []
- Unintended effects: There is qualitative evidence that humanitarian and environmental action can be politically instrumentalized, but little systematic evaluation of whether climate/resilience programmes alter legitimacy, exclusion, resource control or conflict incentives. (SIPRI6) []
- Differentiated vulnerability: Evidence is insufficiently systematic across gender, disability, age, ethnicity, statelessness, livelihood, urban informality and territorial-control conditions. Rohingya vulnerability is documented particularly in Rakhine, but should not be reduced to climate exposure. (Doctors Without Borders4) []
- Causal uncertainty: The climate -> FCV direction lacks credible identification of independent climate effects on recruitment, political support, communal violence or armed-conflict intensity. Most evidence is mediated synthesis rather than causal estimation. []
- FCV -> climate/adaptation measurement: There is strong qualitative/event evidence that insecurity and access restrictions obstruct response, but little longitudinal quantification of how much adaptation coverage, recovery speed or resilience is lost because of conflict. []
- Sector gaps: Fisheries, urban systems, energy, health adaptation and coastal/sea-level adaptation have thinner current evidence than agriculture, food, displacement and humanitarian access. []
- Inaccessible/full-text limits: The 2026 peer-reviewed article's public abstract was accessible but the full text was restricted; two large UNDP reports were usable only through verified publication-page summaries in this research session. (Sage Journals8) []
- *None are completely unsupported, but coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, institutional-capacity, response-performance and resilience-peace-capacity remain only partially covered. In particular, response-performance evidence is disproportionately pre-2021 and resilience-peace evidence is highly localized. []
- No inspected public source estimated localized climate-related conflict incidence independently of conflict, economic and household vulnerability factors; available links concern food insecurity and coping, not climate-driven violence. []
- UNDP's 2025 phone survey under-represents remote, conflict-affected, stateless and displaced households; its Rakhine deprivation estimates may understate need. []
- The official OCHA July 2026 Humanitarian Access Snapshot could not be opened (HTTP 403); search-proxy text was excluded from evidence. []
- FAO's 2026–2028 resilience measures are commitments, not evaluated outcomes; its cited passages do not identify the 11 states/regions or report comparative local results. []
- OCHA's Rakhine flood update records physical and logistical access constraints but does not attribute them to armed conflict; no FCV linkage is inferred from this report. []
- Main checked the August Health Cluster and July Rakhine original reports. Conflict-driven access restrictions and flood-related logistics must remain distinct. []
- The MPI study's natural-hazard category is broader than climate; its model associations do not estimate climate-caused poverty or conflict. []
- Routine/default app-use approval granted by Lindsey Jones on 2026-10-27; independent source verification remains incomplete. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Mean annual temperature over Myanmar increased about 0.2°C per decade during 1961-2015. [MMR-E-001]
- Regional mean temperature is projected to rise around 1-1.5°C by 2050 under SSP2-4.5 and 1.5-2°C under SSP5-8.5. Scenario: ~2050; SSP2-4.5/SSP5-8.5 [MMR-E-002]
- Mid-century annual precipitation is projected to increase roughly 5-10% under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, with heavier precipitation becoming more frequent/intense. Scenario: 2040-2060; SSP2-4.5/SSP5-8.5 [MMR-E-003]
- The 2012 NAPA assessed Coastal, Central Dry and Northern Hilly areas as the country's most climate-vulnerable broad zones. [MMR-E-005]
- UNHCR/UN in Myanmar estimated 4,051,000 internally displaced people as of 12 October 2026. [MMR-E-008]
- Sagaing hosted an estimated 1,335,200 IDPs, about 33.0% of Myanmar's total, on 12 October 2026. [MMR-E-009]
- Source dated 2026-10-08 reports: PPS 2025 respondents reported natural-hazard exposure for 23% of rural versus 10% of urban households; rates were highest in Kayah (52%), Bago (32%), Kayin and Magway (30%), and lowest in Tanintharyi (4%) and Yangon (7%). [MMR-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-08-31 reports: The August bulletin reports monsoon flooding affecting over 470,000 people in Sagaing, Mandalay, Magway, Ayeyarwady and Mon; overflowed latrines contaminated water and food, with AWD cases in Shwebo, Lemyethna, Mandalay and Bago. [MMR-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-07-21 reports: Early-July heavy rain and rising rivers flooded 18 wards and 201 villages in Rakhine, affecting 98,282 people; Buthidaung, Kyauktaw, Minbya, Mrauk-U and Ponnagyun were among the hardest-hit townships. [MMR-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The NAPA identifies concentration of people/economic activity in coastal and low-lying areas, climate-sensitive livelihoods, poverty and limited technology as compounding vulnerability factors. [MMR-E-004]
- Of the national IDP total, 224,749 were recorded as stateless persons in Rakhine State on 12 October 2026. [MMR-E-010]
- WFP estimates 12.4 million people face acute hunger in Myanmar in 2026, including 1 million at emergency levels. [MMR-E-011]
- WFP identifies political crisis, conflict, economic downturn, pre-existing poverty and climate-related shocks jointly as drivers of the hunger emergency. [MMR-E-012]
- UNDP's 2025 polycrisis assessment describes severely strained public health systems and declining human capital amid political instability, conflict and economic disruption. [MMR-E-026]
- UNDP's survey-adjusted models associate natural-hazard and conflict exposure with higher predicted multidimensional poverty; the conflict association is larger. [MMR-E-102]
- Source dated 2026 reports: FAO reports that the September 2024 floods and March 2025 earthquake damaged crops, livestock, inputs and incomes; repeated extreme-weather impacts compound vulnerability and food insecurity in conflict-affected, economically isolated areas. [MMR-E-104]
- Source dated 2026 reports: FAO says many rural households depend on one income source, such as small-scale fishing or monocropping; coastal fishers have few alternatives, and limited diversification heightens shock exposure and asset-depleting coping. [MMR-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-08-31 reports: By 22 August, Sittwe had over 150 Cholera/AWD cases, almost half severe; all were reported in IDP sites, where overcrowding and poor water quality were identified as outbreak drivers. [MMR-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC explicitly proposes supporting people displaced by conflict to improve climate-resilient agricultural practices. [MMR-E-007]
- WFP reports resilience/community-asset projects such as roads, irrigation canals, flood dykes and home gardens in areas with relative stability. [MMR-E-013]
- UNDP's 2023 food-security roadmap treats climatic extremes together with COVID-19, the 2021 takeover and high food prices, and proposes localized conservation agriculture, market, social-protection and nutrition measures. [MMR-E-027]
- Source dated 2026-10-08 reports: UNDP reports that natural-hazard, economic and conflict shocks often co-occur; modeled poverty rises with cumulative shocks as assets, savings and household buffers are depleted. [MMR-E-103]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The updated NDC identifies institutional coordination, finance, capacity/technology, awareness and multi-stakeholder partnerships as enabling conditions for adaptation and states that a NAP process would be initiated. [MMR-E-006]
- The Dry Zone adaptation project's water outcome was rated Moderately Satisfactory at mid-term; evaluators found little increase in dry-season water supply at that stage. [MMR-E-014]
- The same evaluation rated agriculture/livestock adaptation Satisfactory but identified replication and sustainability as concerns. [MMR-E-015]
- Climate-information work was rated Satisfactory but evaluators reported limited presence and effect among villages/farmers because implementation was still early. [MMR-E-016]
- A 2026 peer-reviewed study finds that climate/environmental “greening” has become part of wartime competition over authority and legitimacy among the military, NUG and KNU. [MMR-E-024]
- World Bank support inside Myanmar is delivered through third parties such as UN agencies and ICRC rather than directly through state authorities. [MMR-E-025]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: OCHA found extreme access difficulties in 127 of 330 townships; nearly 95% clustered in conflict-affected northeast, northwest, Rakhine and southeast, where local organizations remain a response backbone. [MMR-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-08-31 reports: WHO reports MRCS water chlorination, awareness and early detection in Yangon alongside a sharp case decline; shared risk indexing, prepositioned supplies and EWARS supported faster alert verification and response. [MMR-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-07-21 reports: Flood-damaged roads and communications, transport disruption, limited stocks and incomplete assessments delayed aid; partners had started WASH, mobile health and cash assistance, but response remained below assessed needs. [MMR-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Dry Zone climate stress can worsen farm hardship, but any effect on conflict dynamics is mediated through finance, livelihoods and pre-existing political violence. [MMR-P-001; MMR-E-001, MMR-E-002, MMR-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Reduced rain-fed agricultural performance and livelihood security.
  - FCV mediator: Debt, landlessness risk, rising input costs, declining credit, poverty and an existing armed-conflict environment.
  - Possible consequence: These combined pressures may influence grievances and household/community relationships with armed resistance actors; the evidence does not show climate as an independent cause of recruitment or violence.

  - FCV mediator: Humanitarian-access restrictions, insecurity, territorial contestation and blocked transport.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- During the September 2024 floods, the World Bank found that ongoing insecurity hampered humanitarian response and recovery; conflict also constrained aid access and supply transport in affected regions. [MMR-E-017]
- Some people previously displaced by conflict had to relocate again because of the 2024 floods, while flooding increased uncertainty around landmine/ERW locations. [MMR-E-018]
- Following Cyclone Mocha, MSF reported military-authority restrictions that prohibited scale-up of a cyclone-specific humanitarian response and large relief distributions. [MMR-E-019]
- SIPRI/NUPI assesses violent conflict as the primary driver of displacement, while extreme weather additionally shapes mobility and restricted movement can increase disaster vulnerability. [MMR-E-020]
- SIPRI/NUPI links Dry Zone heat, extended dry spells and inadequate water to livelihood losses and financial hardship that, together with debt, high input costs and declining credit, can influence support for local armed resistance. [MMR-E-021]
- SIPRI/NUPI documents a localized temporary ceasefire brokered by religious leaders in Karenni to allow farmers to harvest crops. [MMR-E-022]
- SIPRI/NUPI reports that some EAOs provide disaster relief and local environmental governance; it specifically notes KNU cooperation with grassroots conservation actors and support for local land/forestry policies. [MMR-E-023]
- Source dated 2026 reports: FAO reports field projects in 11 states/regions and its 2026–2028 plan commits to climate-resilient production, flood/drought early warning, local finance and conflict-sensitive natural-resource management. [MMR-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: For its 2026 planning period, OCHA expects worsening climate disasters and epidemics to erode resilience; northern Rakhine is identified as a food-insecurity and malnutrition hotspot under combined monsoon, conflict and economic pressures. Scenario: 2026 humanitarian planning period; no emissions or climate-model scenario stated. [MMR-E-107]
