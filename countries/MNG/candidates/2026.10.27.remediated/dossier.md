## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-04-30 reports: The 2023-2024 dzud affected over 188,300 people, including 80,215 children; two of 20 reported deaths were children. [MNG-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-05-27 reports: December 2023 risk mapping rated 90% of territory high or very high risk; surveyed herders with under 200 animals lost 61% in Sukhbaatar versus 6% in Khovd. [MNG-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-02-19 reports: Plan prioritizes herder households with 100-400 livestock units, limited basic services, pregnant or lactating women, single parents, disabled members or widowed elders. [MNG-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-04-30 reports: Rural herder women and children faced malnutrition risks as families cut spending on check-ups, education, hygiene and warm clothes; UNICEF observed child mental distress. [MNG-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2024-02-19 reports: The plan aimed to assist 33,400 of 150,500 people identified in need and reported discounted hay and fodder for high-risk provinces. [MNG-E-107]
- Source dated 2025-05-27 reports: World Bank summary says social protection expanded during COVID-19 and recommends adaptive disaster plans, better household data systems and advance payments for herders facing dzud. [MNG-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-05-27 reports: Herders received dzud warnings, but community response remained limited by inaccessible pasture and shortages of trained staff and equipment. [MNG-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-02-19 reports: Government risk maps combined remote sensing and ground observations of snow, drought, pasture productivity, livestock numbers and carrying capacity to guide early action. [MNG-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-04-30 reports: By 25 April, mobile health services reached 141,873 people in high-risk areas, including young children, pregnant women and persons with disabilities; road support covered 78 soums in 11 provinces. [MNG-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-04-30 reports: UNICEF was organizing emergency child-protection training for 320 local responders and refresher support for rapid-response teams and community groups addressing GBV. [MNG-E-111]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Pasture stress and inter-aimag grazing mobility can interact with access and governance constraints to produce disputes; armed conflict is not established. [MNG-P-101; MNG-E-102]
  - Documented impact: Herders seek Otor pasture in other aimags as grazing access becomes constrained.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal access and limited systematic management of movement and shared pasture can mediate resource competition.
  - Possible consequence: The report describes disputes between incoming herders and local users, without event counts or armed-conflict evidence.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Herd size, grazing pressure and land-management arrangements also affect degradation and disputes.', 'Not all mobility or resource competition produces conflict.']
  - Uncertainty: Qualitative source-stated mechanism, not a measured climate-attributable conflict effect or a prediction for named receiving settlements. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
