Critical remaining gaps:
- No direct, country-specific primary source-stated climate-to-FCV or FCV-to-adaptation pathway was found in the completed searches; this is an evidence-search gap, not evidence that no such relationship exists. []
- The Statistics Poland 2024 flood report could not be directly opened; search-indexed claims about local-government losses and affected population were excluded. []
- The 2025 IMGW report PDF exceeded the available fetch limit; its claims are limited to the directly read publisher landing-page summary. []
- The World Bank urban case study summarizes selected municipal projects; source adaptation plans and independent project evaluations were not inspected. []
- The 21 September 2024 flood impact counts are explicitly preliminary government figures, not a final damage assessment. []
- National climatic/resource indicators are not treated as local vulnerability rankings. Eastern Poland institutional capacity is not an FCV classification. []
- No complete supported climate–FCV chain is assembled; historic flood impacts, municipal adaptation and implementation constraints remain distinct from violence/conflict claims. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated not stated reports: IMGW-PIB reports Poland's 2025 mean air temperature at 9.5°C, 0.8°C above the 1991–2020 normal, and annual precipitation at 89.3% of the long-term norm, classifying 2025 as dry. [POL-E-101]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry states that Poland's mean annual temperature rose about 2°C since 1951, at roughly twice the global-average rate. [POL-E-102]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry identifies March 2026 as Poland's third-warmest and third-driest March since 1951: 6.4°C mean temperature, 3.3°C above normal, and 29% of normal precipitation. [POL-E-103]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry says rising temperatures, long dry spells and snowless winters worsen Wielkopolska's water balance; open-pit mining and intensive farming further deepen drought problems. [POL-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry reports annual water availability of 1,600 m³ per person in Poland, versus 4,500 m³ for Europe. [POL-E-104]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry identifies Wielkopolska among Poland's most drought-affected regions, citing low rainfall, hydrology, intensive farming, low forest cover and a water balance worsening since the early 2000s. [POL-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-09-21 reports: At the 21 September briefing, the government reported 749 settlements under disaster state, 2.39 million residents in affected areas, 57,000 directly flood-affected residents and more than 6,544 evacuees. [POL-E-112]
- Source dated 2024-09-21 reports: Preliminary reports counted 11,502 damaged residential buildings, 6,033 farm buildings and 724 public buildings; Lower Silesia alone reported damage to 54 schools. [POL-E-113]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry lists lake protection in Powidz Landscape Park, Ślesiński Canal modernization, Gniezno Lakeland water-resource restoration and hydrological restoration in the Warcica basin as in implementation. [POL-E-108]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Bydgoszcz's project began in 2017: 14 km new and about 90 km renovated storm sewers, six reservoirs and 22 treatment plants; target capture was about 37,000 m³. [POL-E-109]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The report describes Katowice's post-industrial adaptation features, including a green roof and planted public spaces; Bogucki Park's revitalization included a planned underground rainwater reservoir. [POL-E-111]
- Source dated not stated reports: The project targets medium cities and spa municipalities in Podkarpackie, Lubelskie, Świętokrzyskie, Podlaskie, Warmian-Masurian and part of Mazowieckie. [POL-E-115]
- Source dated not stated reports: A PLN 6.27m project, including PLN 5.33m EU funds, plans municipal training, advice and peer exchange for adaptation plans. [POL-E-117]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated not stated reports: On 2 April 2026, the Ministry and regional partners signed a water-retention and water-security letter, joining administration, science and public bodies around catchment-scale planning. [POL-E-107]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Gdańsk invested PLN 380m in drainage and flood infrastructure during 2001–2015; storage rose over fivefold, while losses in two heavy-rain events fell from PLN 130m (2001) to PLN 10m (2016). [POL-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-09-21 reports: The Prime Minister said the 2024 disaster showed a need for new flood-control infrastructure. [POL-E-114]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry says 58 of 75 medium Eastern Polish cities (>15,000 residents) are losing functions and municipalities have limited planning staff. [POL-E-116]
- The Ministry webpage describes a statutory adaptation-plan deadline of 2 January 2028 for cities of at least 20,000 residents; it does not assess compliance. [POL-E-118]
