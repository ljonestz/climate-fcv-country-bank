# Papua New Guinea Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Papua New Guinea faces increasing heat, heavy-rainfall, flood, landslide, drought, and coastal pressures with material differences among island, coastal, Highlands, basin, urban, and remote settings. [PNG-E-001, PNG-E-002, PNG-E-003, PNG-E-004, PNG-E-005, PNG-P-001, PNG-P-002, PNG-P-003]
- Climate-FCV risks are mediated through subsistence livelihoods, customary land, displacement, remoteness, insecurity, uneven aid, and weak service reach; geological hazards and non-climate mobility drivers remain separate. [PNG-E-005, PNG-E-006, PNG-E-007, PNG-E-008, PNG-E-009, PNG-E-010, PNG-P-003, PNG-P-004, PNG-P-005]
- National targets, local networks, churches, customary institutions, and local governments provide adaptation and recovery capacity, while formal coordination, evaluation, sustainability, and equitable reach remain uneven. [PNG-E-011, PNG-E-012, PNG-E-013, PNG-E-014, PNG-E-015, PNG-P-005, PNG-P-006]

Critical remaining gaps:
- National and subnational projections remain incomplete for drought, landslide probability, coastal inundation, cyclone change, and settlement-level exposure, and complex terrain increases model uncertainty. [PNG-E-001, PNG-E-002, PNG-E-003, PNG-E-011, PNG-P-001, PNG-P-003]
- Independent response evidence is limited to selected projects and programmes; unintended effects, maintenance, distributional performance, and long-term climate and peace outcomes remain largely unevaluated. [PNG-E-009, PNG-E-014, PNG-P-005, PNG-P-006]
- The fcv-to-climate reverse pathway is supported mainly through protracted displacement, access, and service isolation; quantitative estimates of conflict's additional effect on climate losses and recovery are absent. [PNG-E-007, PNG-E-010, PNG-P-004]
- Geological hazards, environmental degradation, extractive impacts, demographic pressure, and development displacement are often entangled with climate risks and require stronger attribution and event-specific evidence. [PNG-E-005, PNG-E-006, PNG-E-008, PNG-P-003]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Average rainfall and hydrological loading are projected to rise unevenly. [PNG-E-001]
- Heat rises across lowland, island, and Highlands settings through different exposure metrics. [PNG-E-002]
- Flood exposure is concentrated in coastal lowlands and major basins. [PNG-E-003]
- Disaster displacement is multi-hazard and cannot be treated as wholly climate-related. [PNG-E-005]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Customary tenure and host consent are central to relocation feasibility. [PNG-E-006]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- El Nino drought and frost produced severe food stress and erosive coping in assessed areas. [PNG-E-004]
- Remoteness and insecurity can exclude communities from resilience support. [PNG-E-010]
- The NDC defines quantified national adaptation targets through 2030. [PNG-E-012]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Unequal or conflict-insensitive response can create grievances and service disruption. [PNG-E-009]
- National policy acknowledges mobility, water, and landslide-data gaps. [PNG-E-011]
- Formal governance is fragmented and local delivery capacity is highly uneven. [PNG-E-013]
- Independent evaluation shows service gains alongside weak attribution and sustainability evidence. [PNG-E-014]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heavy rainfall can deepen service isolation where rugged terrain, weak transport, insecurity, and uneven provincial capacity restrict recovery. [PNG-P-001; PNG-E-001, PNG-E-003, PNG-E-010, PNG-E-013]
  - Documented impact: Flooding, slope saturation, road disruption, settlement exposure, and service isolation
  - FCV mediator: Rugged terrain, weak transport, uneven provincial capacity, insecurity, and limited remote-service reach
  - Possible consequence: Repeated isolation and unequal recovery may deepen deprivation and grievance, but climate pressure does not automatically cause violence

  - Alternative explanations: ['Road underinvestment, remoteness, insecurity, maintenance failure, and extreme topography can isolate communities without climate change.']
  - Uncertainty: Physical and access links are documented, but grievance and conflict consequences are not causally estimated.
- Drought and frost can erode food security and coping where subsistence dependence, poor market access, and strained local support narrow alternatives. [PNG-P-002; PNG-E-002, PNG-E-004, PNG-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Crop loss, food and water shortages, reduced meals, and stress on household support networks
  - FCV mediator: Subsistence dependence, market and transport constraints, strained wantok support, poverty, and weak formal safety nets
  - Possible consequence: Distress migration, exclusion, or competition for assistance may increase when households exhaust coping options
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['PNG-E-002', 'PNG-E-004'], 'impact': ['PNG-E-004'], 'mediator': ['PNG-E-004', 'PNG-E-015'], 'consequence': ['PNG-E-015']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Market prices, crop disease, transport failure, poverty, and policy choices independently shape food insecurity and migration.']
  - Uncertainty: The pathway is supported by operational and qualitative evidence but not a causal estimate of migration or tension.
- Coastal mobility can create land and service disputes when customary tenure and host consent are not addressed through inclusive relocation. [PNG-P-003; PNG-E-005, PNG-E-006, PNG-E-008]
  - Documented impact: Loss of habitability, freshwater and livelihood pressure, and movement to receiving land
  - FCV mediator: Customary tenure, lineage rights, host consent, compensation, cultural attachment, population pressure, and weak relocation systems
  - Possible consequence: Relocation may stall or generate land and service disputes unless agreements and host-community support are inclusive
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['PNG-E-005', 'PNG-E-006'], 'impact': ['PNG-E-006', 'PNG-E-008'], 'mediator': ['PNG-E-006', 'PNG-E-008'], 'consequence': ['PNG-E-006']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Tectonic movement, volcanic hazards, population pressure, reef processes, development decisions, and pre-existing land disputes also shape mobility.']
  - Uncertainty: Carteret evidence is multicausal and local survey evidence cannot be generalized across island communities.
- Conflict and insecurity can prolong displacement and restrict assistance, magnifying climate losses and slowing recovery in remote areas. [PNG-P-004; PNG-E-001, PNG-E-002, PNG-E-003, PNG-E-004, PNG-E-007, PNG-E-010, PNG-E-013]
  - Documented impact: Displacement, service disruption, food stress, and demand for remote delivery
  - FCV mediator: Communal violence, protracted conflict displacement, insecurity, contested return, high transport costs, and implementer risk aversion
  - Possible consequence: Conflict-affected communities may experience slower, less complete, and more unequal climate recovery because insecurity restricts reach and return
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['PNG-E-001', 'PNG-E-002', 'PNG-E-003', 'PNG-E-004'], 'impact': ['PNG-E-007', 'PNG-E-010'], 'mediator': ['PNG-E-007', 'PNG-E-010', 'PNG-E-013'], 'consequence': ['PNG-E-007', 'PNG-E-010']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Remoteness, limited fiscal capacity, transport scarcity, and institutional fragmentation can delay recovery independently of conflict.']
  - Uncertainty: The access mechanism is directly reported, but no national quantitative estimate of conflict's additional recovery effect is available.
- Resilience interventions can generate local grievance when aid, water, land, or infrastructure decisions are perceived as unfair or poorly negotiated. [PNG-P-005; PNG-E-009, PNG-E-014]
  - Documented impact: Resource allocation, WASH sharing, and assistance decisions with uneven local benefits
  - FCV mediator: Perceived favouritism, patronage, weak consultation, unclear sharing rules, gender exclusion, and pre-existing intercommunity disputes
  - Possible consequence: Conflict-insensitive interventions may create grievance, mistrust, dispute, or temporary service interruption despite resilience objectives
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['PNG-E-009'], 'impact': ['PNG-E-009'], 'mediator': ['PNG-E-009'], 'consequence': ['PNG-E-009', 'PNG-E-014']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Political competition, historic disputes, project design failure, and institutional weakness can create grievance without a climate hazard.']
  - Uncertainty: Direct local examples demonstrate possible unintended effects but not their prevalence or a national climate-conflict effect.
- Local networks can bridge weak formal reach and support recovery, but their resilience and inclusiveness vary across communities and groups. [PNG-P-006; PNG-E-013, PNG-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Demand for information, mediation, shelter, material support, local service delivery, and recovery assistance
  - FCV mediator: Trust, reciprocity, customary authority, church reach, local inclusion, identity boundaries, and unequal power
  - Possible consequence: Inclusive local networks can support recovery and cohesion, while strained or captured networks may exclude marginalized households
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['PNG-E-015'], 'impact': ['PNG-E-013', 'PNG-E-015'], 'mediator': ['PNG-E-013', 'PNG-E-015'], 'consequence': ['PNG-E-015']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Formal public services, household wealth, remittances, geography, and external aid also determine coping and recovery.']
  - Uncertainty: Qualitative evidence does not establish uniform network strength, inclusiveness, or causal peace outcomes.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Conflict can prolong displacement and weaken hazard recovery in parts of the Highlands. [PNG-E-007]
- One urban-edge site documents mixed flood and insecurity displacement drivers. [PNG-E-008]
- Local networks are important but uneven and potentially exclusionary resilience institutions. [PNG-E-015]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National evidence is complemented by distinct examples from Hela, Southern Highlands, Bougainville and the Carteret Islands, Port Moresby, Goroka, major river basins, and selected remote communities; none should be generalized nationally. [PNG-E-001, PNG-E-003, PNG-E-006, PNG-E-007, PNG-E-008, PNG-E-009, PNG-P-001, PNG-P-003, PNG-P-004, PNG-P-005]
- The sources do not provide comparable current evidence for every province, local-level government, island, customary system, or conflict-affected area, and remote access creates systematic observation gaps. [PNG-E-010, PNG-E-011, PNG-E-013, PNG-P-004, PNG-P-006]
- Agriculture, food security, water, transport, land, displacement, local governance, humanitarian delivery, health, and education are represented; energy, fisheries, extractives, urban systems, and private-sector exposure remain thin. [PNG-E-003, PNG-E-004, PNG-E-006, PNG-E-009, PNG-E-014, PNG-P-001, PNG-P-002, PNG-P-003, PNG-P-005]

## Bibliography and review decision

Limitations: The synthesis does not causally identify most pathways; some findings are locality-specific and projection uncertainty remains.

Limitations: Targets are intended commitments rather than evaluated implementation; the document predates later policy and hazard developments.
