Critical remaining gaps:
- Automated machine-screening is not independent source verification or human approval. 12 of 23 evidence records and 6 of 6 pathways lack claim-level verification in this remediation; review the linked records before relying on them. [PNG-E-001, PNG-E-002, PNG-E-003, PNG-E-004, PNG-E-007, PNG-E-009, PNG-E-010, PNG-E-011, PNG-E-012, PNG-E-013, PNG-E-014, PNG-E-015, PNG-P-001, PNG-P-002, PNG-P-003, PNG-P-004, PNG-P-005, PNG-P-006]
- National and subnational projections remain incomplete for drought, landslide probability, coastal inundation, cyclone change, and settlement-level exposure, and complex terrain increases model uncertainty. []
- Independent response evidence is limited to selected projects and programmes; unintended effects, maintenance, distributional performance, and long-term climate and peace outcomes remain largely unevaluated. []
- The fcv-to-climate reverse pathway is supported mainly through protracted displacement, access, and service isolation; quantitative estimates of conflict's additional effect on climate losses and recovery are absent. []
- Geological hazards, environmental degradation, extractive impacts, demographic pressure, and development displacement are often entangled with climate risks and require stronger attribution and event-specific evidence. []
- No inspected source establishes that climate hazards caused armed conflict; the evidence supports climate risks in conflict-affected settings and joint programmes addressing both. []
- The Western Province flood assessment's original PDF could not be opened; its two claims are restricted to the directly opened IRP summary and lack village-level detail. []
- IOM's 2024 annual displacement page returned HTTP 403; displacement statistics from its search-result text were not used. []
- The UN annual results report is self-reported and includes expected future reach alongside completed 2024 activities; these are distinguished in claim wording. []
- Training, grants and community governance are plausible capacities, not independent proof of reduced climate losses or conflict. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- PNG-E-101 to PNG-E-108 remain legitimate standalone capacity, context or bounded impact records. No record from Idauwi/Paipali in Hela is attached to the Carteret relocation pathway. HJP2 design is not evidence of joint climate/peace outcomes. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The 2025 climate-security assessment projects rising national annual rainfall under both reported pathways, with larger mid-century increases in parts of the Highlands. Scenario: Low- and high-emissions pathways reported in the source [PNG-E-001]
- The assessment projects increasing heat exposure in lowland Southern and Momase areas and more warm nights in both Southern Highlands and island settings such as Milne Bay. Scenario: Scenario-dependent mid-century projections [PNG-E-002]
- High flood exposure is identified in lowlands, coasts, and major river basins, while the assessment projects expansion of severe-flood area by mid-century. Scenario: Middle-of-the-road emissions scenario reported in the source [PNG-E-003]
- Recorded disaster displacement between 2010 and 2021 involved floods and storms as well as earthquakes and volcanic activity, showing that PNG mobility evidence must keep climatic and geological hazards separate. [PNG-E-005]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The 2024 joint assessment covered prolonged flooding in 60 Western Province villages and one care centre, representing about 55,000 affected people. [PNG-E-107]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- IOM's 2015 assessment describes customary tenure, receiving-land agreements, host consent and compensation as constraints on relocation in source-specific PNG examples, including Carteret. [PNG-E-006]
- UNDP's HJP2 design describes inter-group violence, displacement and sorcery accusation-related violence (SARV) alongside climate vulnerability in Hela, Southern Highlands, Enga and Western Highlands. [PNG-E-105]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The assessment summary identifies contaminated water, food insecurity from damaged gardens, disease risks, displacement, damaged homes and schools, and heightened protection concerns in flood-affected communities. [PNG-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- During the 2015-2016 El Nino drought and frost, WFP assessments found serious food and livelihood disruption, reduced meals and famine-food coping, with six local-level governments classified as most severely affected and 48 as highly affected. [PNG-E-004]
- Remote and insecure communities are reported to receive less assistance because transport, security, cost, and institutional disconnection favour accessible locations. [PNG-E-010]
- The Enhanced NDC sets quantified 2030 adaptation targets for vulnerable people, early-warning coverage, and resilience standards for transport and built assets. [PNG-E-012]
- Source dated 2025-04-09 reports: UN-supported Agricultural Lab Centres opened in conflict-affected Idauwi and Paipali, Hela, for community learning on sustainable agriculture, disaster-risk reduction, food security and resilience. [PNG-E-102]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Qualitative evidence indicates that perceived favouritism, political capture, unequal aid, or poorly negotiated resource sharing can generate grievances and interrupt local services after response interventions. [PNG-E-009]
- PNG's Enhanced NDC records major information gaps for climate-related mobility, non-climate migration drivers, isolated-province water needs, and integration of rainfall and geophysical data for landslide analysis. [PNG-E-011]
- The IMF diagnostic and a four-community study find fragmented climate and disaster policy, weak coordination, unclear subnational roles, uneven provincial systems, and reliance on churches and local actors for remote reach. [PNG-E-013]
- Independent evaluations found that rural service investments often remained operational but lacked adequate comparative evidence and faced monitoring and sustainability risks, while some UNDP climate-policy products were incomplete or too recent for outcome assessment. [PNG-E-014]
- Source dated 2025-04-09 reports: In 2024, 202 participants across Bougainville, East Sepik, Morobe, Enga, Western Highlands, Jiwaka and Simbu trained in displacement data, vulnerability assessment and response planning. [PNG-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-04-09 reports: Bougainville's community-based disaster-risk plan launched in 2024; community work identified 12 disaster-prone areas and informed 12 projects. [PNG-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-04-09 reports: In nine of 24 vulnerable island and atoll communities, grants supported livelihood, food, sanitation and ecosystem activities; ward committees oversee transport dinghies, with over 16,000 people listed as expected beneficiaries. [PNG-E-104]

  - FCV mediator: Rugged terrain, weak transport, uneven provincial capacity, insecurity, and limited remote-service reach
  - Possible consequence: Repeated isolation and unequal recovery may deepen deprivation and grievance, but climate pressure does not automatically cause violence

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The 2025 assessment reports that conflict displacement in Hela and Southern Highlands can be recurrent or protracted, complicating safe return, services, and recovery from subsequent hazards. [PNG-E-007]
- At one Port Moresby-area site, IDMC's 2022 survey found mixed flood and insecurity displacement reasons and interruptions to schooling; later school attendance improved, while access to services remained uneven. [PNG-E-008]
- ODI finds that churches, wantok ties, customary leaders, women's and youth groups, and other community networks provide information, mediation, shelter, material support, and recovery assistance, while also varying in inclusiveness and resilience under repeated shocks. [PNG-E-015]
- Source dated not stated reports: HJP2 aims to combine community conflict mediation, inclusive governance and services with local capacity to prepare for and adapt to climate-induced disasters, targeting women, youth, people with disabilities and displaced populations. Scenario: Expected programme outcomes during January 2026–April 2028. [PNG-E-106]
