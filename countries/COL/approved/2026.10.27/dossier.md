## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2026-09-12 reports: MinVivienda screened 558 municipalities and non-municipal areas as high or very high water and sanitation risk ahead of El Niño: 111 very high, 447 high; past impacts concentrated in Andean and Caribbean regions. Scenario: Possible El Niño conditions through the first quarter of 2027. [COL-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-05-15 reports: In May 2026, temperature anomalies reached +4.2°C in Valledupar, +4.0°C in Santa Marta, +4.4°C in Barrancabermeja and +3.3°C in Quibdó; 51.1% of active fire-alert municipalities were in the Caribbean, notably La Guajira and Magdalena. [COL-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-03 reports: Guaviare participants reported 2023–2024 heat and drought reduced water; some sources disappeared, while deforestation and mining further contaminated it. [COL-E-101]
- FEWS NET's June 2026 outlook describes recurrent drought and depleted coping capacity among poor Wayuu and agropastoral households in La Guajira; it separately forecasts worsening rainfall/soil moisture and livestock/crop pressures through January 2027. The outlook is not a current household census. [COL-E-104]
- As of June 2026, FEWS NET describes above-average rainfall earlier in the year, flood risk and localized insecurity constraining river transport, crops and access in Chocó, especially for poor Indigenous and Afro-Colombian riverine households. Continued impacts through January 2027 are a forecast, not observed later outcomes. [COL-E-105]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The Guaviare study describes existing dissidents exploiting El Niño dryness for deforestation revenue and negotiating leverage, not climate causing an insurgency. [COL-P-101; COL-E-102]
  - Documented impact: Conditions more favorable for clearing forest in the Colombian Amazon.
  - FCV mediator: Existing dissident factions regulate and profit from land transactions, deforestation and cattle economies, amid shifting negotiations and state control.
  - Possible consequence: Armed actors can exploit dry conditions for revenue and bargaining leverage, as described by the study; no quantified increase in violence is established.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COL-E-102'], 'impact': ['COL-E-102'], 'mediator': ['COL-E-102'], 'consequence': ['COL-E-102']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Political negotiations, group fragmentation, commodity incentives, land speculation and enforcement changes also influence behavior.']
  - Uncertainty: Qualitative subregional study using El Niño as a proxy; not attribution of the event to anthropogenic warming or causal identification of armed-group behavior. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
- Insecurity and territorial control restrict environmental response and reforestation in the Guaviare study area, not all Colombian adaptation. [COL-P-102; COL-E-101, COL-E-103]
  - Documented impact: Communities require water conservation, restoration and environmental response.
  - FCV mediator: Armed-group territorial control and insecurity limit state access and environmental operations.
  - Possible consequence: Environmental enforcement and reforestation responses are constrained, weakening support for longer-term resilience.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COL-E-101'], 'impact': ['COL-E-101', 'COL-E-103'], 'mediator': ['COL-E-103'], 'consequence': ['COL-E-103']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Agency budgets, technical capacity, weak state presence and resource shortages also limit response.']
  - Uncertainty: Subregional participant accounts, not a national estimate of conflict blocking all adaptation or a measured resilience effect. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
