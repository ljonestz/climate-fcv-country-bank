# Cambodia Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Three-commune evidence shows differing hazards and migration mediated by poverty, debt and employment, rather than direct hazard-triggered migration. [KHM-E-101, KHM-E-102]
- Tonle Sap's forced settlement illustrates how lost mobility and fishing access can increase exposure; WASH risks and local adaptation networks add capacity detail without causal outcome claims. [KHM-E-105, KHM-E-109, KHM-E-111, KHM-E-112, KHM-P-101]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The release reports 2015–16 drought affected 18 of Cambodia’s 25 provinces and over 2.5 million people. [KHM-E-001]
- The release says September 2022 rainfall flooded 14 provinces, affecting about 85,000 households; landslides prompted 5,000 household evacuations. [KHM-E-003]
- NDC 3.0 reports major floods recur about every five years, severe agricultural drought has a 4% median annual probability, and the southern plains face severe drought roughly every five to six years. [KHM-E-005]
- UNICEF and SIWI identify 282 communes (17.1%) in drought-prone areas with below-average basic drinking-water access, concentrated around the northwest, Tonle Sap and southern Mekong plains. [KHM-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The release estimates 2019 El Niño drought-related rice-production losses at US$100 million. [KHM-E-002]
- The CCDR projects worsening flood risk and heat stress; without proper adaptation and mitigation, climate costs could reach 9% of GDP by 2050 and poverty rise 6 percentage points by 2040. [KHM-E-004]
- The NDC identifies low-income households along the Mekong, Tonle Sap Basin, and northwest Cambodia as disproportionately exposed to floods, drought, and heat, with risks to agricultural livelihoods. [KHM-E-006]
- In the July 2023 three-commune study, respondents described drought/floods in Kanhchor, heatwaves in Sre Ktum and windstorms in Kbal Romeas; these are reported local hazards, not measured hazard frequencies. [KHM-E-101]
- The study says weather-related crop and property losses reduce income; poverty, debt and job scarcity mediate work migration, while hazards were not direct triggers. [KHM-E-102]
- The 2024 ethnographic article describes the 2018 forced relocation of three Tonle Sap floating communities in Kampong Chhnang—Chhnok Tru, Kampong Preah and Seh Slab—home to more than 4,000 people. [KHM-E-108]
- The authors describe altered flood pulses and fisheries decline amid dams, climate change, intensive agriculture and land development; individual drivers are not isolated. [KHM-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NDC 3.0 sets out climate-risk assessment and inclusive, climate-resilient WASH frameworks and provincial action plans for all 25 provinces, with a 2035 target. [KHM-E-007]
- UNICEF and SIWI recommend climate-resilient design standards, flood and drought contingency plans, stronger provider capacity and dedicated WASH adaptation financing. [KHM-E-107]
- Across eight Tonle Sap villages, 96% of sampled households reported drought; common responses were changing planting time (65%), while only 12% increased irrigation. [KHM-E-111]
- Perceived peer effects were positively associated with multiple adaptive practices, but not irrigation or fertilizer choices; authors cite resource-access and infrastructure constraints. [KHM-E-112]
- NGO and private-vendor ties moderated peer effects in the study models; government ties did not change the modeled relationship with adaptation intensity. [KHM-E-113]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The assessment identifies weak infrastructure standards, maintenance funding and institutional coordination as constraints on climate-resilient WASH services during floods and droughts. [KHM-E-106]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Forced settlement of Tonle Sap floating communities restricted seasonal mobility and fishing access, increasing vulnerability to water variability. [KHM-P-101; KHM-E-108, KHM-E-109, KHM-E-110]
  - Documented impact: The ethnography describes increased exposure and reduced fishing access after forced settlement.
  - FCV mediator: Eviction and fixed relocation removed households' ability to move with seasonal water and fish.
  - Possible consequence: Narrower livelihood and mobility options can constrain coping with water variability; no climate-induced violence is inferred.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- In 2023, Sre Ktum received over USD 70,000 through REDD+ across three community protected areas; WCS, WWF and the commune discussed livelihood-diversification uses. [KHM-E-103]
- UNICEF/SIWI's August 2024 assessment describes a risk of longer water-collection journeys during shortages exposing women and girls to violence and lost school/work time. [KHM-E-105]
- The Tonle Sap ethnography describes forced settlement preventing seasonal movement with floodwaters, narrowing fishing access and increasing flood/storm exposure and travel costs. [KHM-E-109]
