## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-06 reports: Lowlands around Zeta, Bjelopavlici, Bojana and Lim have experienced flood damage to crops and infrastructure. [MNE-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: A 2022 hailstorm damaged vineyards and orchards in Zeta Plain; Plantaze estimated losses at €8 million. [MNE-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: The report models flood losses above baseline under RCP 2.6, 4.5 and 8.5; the heat-stressor intensity rises in all three scenarios. Scenario: RCP 2.6, RCP 4.5 and RCP 8.5; modeled future risk. [MNE-E-111]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: The 2011 drought affected Zeta and Bjelopavlici; 2017-2018 drought lowered Morača and Zeta river and Skadar Lake levels, harming farming, fisheries and power generation. [MNE-E-112]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The report says most 2022 wildfires occurred along the coast and in Podgorica municipality; Montenegro was largely spared in 2023. [MNE-E-115]
- Source dated 2023 reports: In August 2023, Podgorica recorded its first 24-hour period with temperatures never below 30°C since records began. [MNE-E-116]
- Source dated 2023 reports: January 2023 heavy rain triggered landslides and damaged roads and bridges in Pljevlja, Mojkovac and Rožaje; a person died in Herceg Novi. [MNE-E-118]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-06 reports: The plan identifies Roma and Egyptian women, women with disabilities, unemployed women and older women as climate-vulnerable groups. [MNE-E-104]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: The 2020 plan identifies the coastal and northern mountain regions as particularly drought-vulnerable, with risks to crop yields and farm income. [MNE-E-107]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: The plan says poorer communities have less ability to respond and depend on outside support; rural women carry much food-production work. [MNE-E-108]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Dry months in 2022 reduced hydropower and nearly all power relied on the Pljevlja coal plant, a major air-pollution source. [MNE-E-117]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2020-11 reports: The plan proposes drought authority and advisory-board roles, clearer warning communication, field reporting and civil-society links with communities. [MNE-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-06 reports: Municipal farm-recovery budgets are limited; local relief units lack reliable data on remote, older, disabled, single-woman and Roma/Egyptian households. [MNE-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: The plan rates climate-adaptation capacity as basic and says coastal and mountain tourism lacks adequate operational risk and crisis plans. [MNE-E-106]
- Source dated 2020-11 reports: The plan described drought management as inadequate in 2020, while assigning the hydrometeorological institute monitoring, forecasting and warning functions. [MNE-E-109]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: More than 30 social-protection programs cover over half the population, but climate-shock support is limited and municipal disaster aid is often ad hoc. [MNE-E-113]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: National, municipal and operational rescue plans exist, but the report says municipal preparedness planning needs strengthening. [MNE-E-114]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The 2025 plan links projected dryness and shared water demand to possible water-use disputes, not observed violence. [MNE-P-101; MNE-E-101]
  - Documented impact: Reduced agricultural and public water availability in the Zeta and Bjelopavlici plains and coastal areas.
  - FCV mediator: Competing agricultural, public-supply and tourism water uses place pressure on a shared resource.
  - Possible consequence: The 2025 plan warns of possible water-use disputes, particularly involving tourism; it does not report violence.
