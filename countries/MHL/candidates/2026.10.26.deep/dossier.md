# Marshall Islands Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Majuro consultations identify access barriers around Ajeltake–Laura, differentiated high-tide support gaps and participant-reported heat/GBV concerns; these are local accounts, not national prevalence. [MHL-E-101, MHL-E-102]
- Leasehold incentives and conditional relocation/resource pressures matter for adaptation design. Community solidarity and communication outputs add capacity, with seawall progress retained at its April 2025 vintage. [MHL-E-103, MHL-E-104, MHL-E-107, MHL-E-108, MHL-E-110, MHL-E-112]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Majuro's concentration of people, government and economic activity increases exposure to coastal erosion, flooding, rising seas and drought. [MHL-E-001]
- The city-risk synthesis links narrow-atoll settlement concentration and outer-island migration to the need for risk-informed urban planning in Majuro. [MHL-E-006]
- Source dated 2023-12-05 reports: The assessment cites a greater likelihood of severe drought in southern atolls by 2050 under RCP8.5, while flagging high uncertainty. [MHL-E-106]
- Source dated 2018-06-22 reports: GFDRR says the assessment analyzed waves, storm surges, typhoons and tsunamis for Majuro and Ebeye, with detailed inundation and erosion risk analysis for Ebeye. [MHL-E-113]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- UNDP describes saline groundwater, degraded land and coastal erosion as connected atoll pressures. [MHL-E-003]
- Source dated 2023 reports: In Majuro, inundation disrupts livelihoods and can block Ajeltake-Laura youth from school or town; women, youth and disabled residents reported inadequate high-tide support. [MHL-E-101]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Most consulted Majuro participants favored in-place adaptation; 10% of Day-in-Life respondents considered migration after an extreme sea-level briefing. [MHL-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Proposed resilient public buildings, emergency facilities and planning reforms aim to protect services and livelihoods in Majuro. [MHL-E-002]
- Recharge measures and nature-based coastal buffers are proposed interventions, not verified protection outcomes. [MHL-E-005]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The preparation framework envisages community committees and women's leadership in water and land stewardship. [MHL-E-004]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Majuro business participants said leasehold land weakens incentives to invest in flood-proofing buildings that eventually revert to landlords. [MHL-E-103]
- The April 2025 World Bank ISR reports completion of 63 emergency communication stations by January and training of 1,369 local operators, including 550 women. [MHL-E-110]
- Source dated 2025-04-15 reports: By February 2025, national and local climate/disaster working groups had been operationalized, and the National Adaptation Plan incorporated coastal vulnerability assessment results. [MHL-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-04-15 reports: At the April 2025 mission, the Ebeye seawall contractor was mobilized and works were reported on schedule; protection outcomes were not yet reported. [MHL-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Outer-island livelihood stress may interact with uneven land access and crowded urban services; mobility can also improve opportunities. [MHL-P-101; MHL-E-107, MHL-E-108]
  - Documented impact: The UNDP assessment describes existing movement toward crowded Majuro and Ebeye.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal land access and limited urban services can mediate settlement and resource pressures.
  - Possible consequence: Poorly managed movement could increase grievances and resource competition; violence is not established.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['MHL-E-108'], 'impact': ['MHL-E-108'], 'mediator': ['MHL-E-107', 'MHL-E-108'], 'consequence': ['MHL-E-108']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Employment, education, services and historical relocation also drive mobility.']
  - Uncertainty: Source-stated prospective mechanism in the 2023 assessment, not causal attribution or a forecast of violence.
