## Climate pressures and exposure

- One model estimates over 4.2 million people, including 538,000 in N’Djamena, face at least 10 cm inundation in a 100-year flood. [TCD-E-001]
- The study identifies 2012, 2022 and 2024 among Chad’s notably damaging flood years. [TCD-E-003]
- The update reports slightly more drought events in 2002–2022 than in 1981–2001, with each event affecting 14.5% of the population on average. [TCD-E-004]
- Source dated 2026-09-07 reports: FAO’s 7 September 2026 brief reports that erratic rains and dry spells left poor satellite vegetation conditions by mid-August in Logone Occidental, Logone Oriental, Mandoul, Moyen-Chari, Lac, Batha, Wadi Fira and Ouaddaï. [TCD-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-02-09 reports: UNICEF reports 2025 floods killed people and displaced residents in Mandoul, Moyen-Chari, Salamat and Wadi Fira; the season was milder than 2022 and 2024. [TCD-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Rural vulnerability is tied to rain-fed farming and less durable homes; recent urban arrivals often settle in low-lying areas with weak drainage. [TCD-E-002]
- Source dated 2026-09-07 reports: FAO estimated 2025 cereal output at 3 million tonnes, 10% above its five-year average, but reported lower output in parts of the Sudanian zone and localized shortfalls where civil insecurity disrupted farming in Lac and southern areas. [TCD-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-05-26 reports: The World Bank says N’Djamena, at the Chari–Logone confluence, faces high flood risk from intense rains, rapid urban growth, clogged drains and limited emergency capacity; risk heightened after the 2022 floods and ahead of the 2024 rainy season. [TCD-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-08 reports: UNFPA reports torrential July 2024 rains flooded western and southern Chad, affecting over 6,300 people and displacing some; it says displacement would impede women’s and girls’ service access and increase protection risks amid broader insecurity and intercommunal tensions. [TCD-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The report cites 11% electricity and 12% basic-sanitation access and says service deficits, poverty and governance constraints limit coping capacity. [TCD-E-005]
- Source dated 2026-02-09 reports: UNICEF reports that the Humanitarian Country Team supported a national contingency plan and stocks after 2025 floods. [TCD-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-05-26 reports: The World Bank reports that 2024 PILIER works cleared and repaired 350 km of N’Djamena drains, deployed 12 pumps, trained about 30 technicians, and provided neighborhood maps and pre-positioned supplies benefiting 9,858 households; the city remained largely unharmed during high river levels. [TCD-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-02-09 reports: Citing OCHA, UNICEF says access restrictions prevented assessment or aid during displacement events in Logone Occidental, Ouaddaï and Mayo Kebbi Ouest; armed groups remained active on Lake Chad islands. [TCD-E-106]

## Bibliography and review decision

- World Bank Sahel Adaptive Social Protection Program Technical Paper Series; Pierre E. Biscaye (external contributor). Analysis of the Frequency and Impacts of Flooding in Chad. 2026-02 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099045002052633793/pdf/IDU-be7cbe70-175d-49ef-81a1-123fe8928825.pdf [TCD-SRC-001] Methodology: External technical study compares flood-hazard models, survey reports and satellite-detected floods, then maps population exposure using administrative and population datasets. Limitations: Modeled exposure is not observed damage; estimates vary by flood model and population data. Some satellite years are missing and built-up areas can be harder to detect.
- World Bank. Chad Economic Update: Improving Resilience to Floods. 2023-04 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099070623113040139/pdf/P179299084fd380990a9b10385fd104b6d4.pdf [TCD-SRC-002] Methodology: Economic update combining flood-risk context, service data and climate-impact modeling summarized from the Sahel CCDR. Limitations: The report relies partly on cited data; conflict and displacement statements do not evaluate specific flood events causally.
- Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. FAO GIEWS Country Brief on Chad. 2026-09-07 (date basis: document-version). https://www.fao.org/giews/countrybrief/country.jsp?code=TCD [TCD-SRC-101] Methodology: FAO GIEWS combines cereal-balance, price, Cadre Harmonisé and satellite crop-monitoring information. Limitations: This dated brief is a snapshot; its 2026 seasonal outlook is not a verified final harvest result.
- World Bank. When the Waters Rise: How the City of N’Djamena, Chad, Prepared for Floods and Averted Disaster. 2026-05-26 (date basis: publication). https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2026/05/26/when-the-waters-rise-how-the-city-of-n-djamena-chad-prepared-for-floods-and-averted-disaster [TCD-SRC-102] Methodology: World Bank results brief describes PILIER activities and reported 2024 results. Limitations: Project results are self-reported; the page provides no counterfactual or independent evaluation of flood losses avoided.
- United Nations Children’s Fund. Chad Humanitarian Situation Report No. 2 (End of Year), 31 December 2025. 2026-02-09 (date basis: publication). https://www.unicef.org/media/178776/file/Chad-Humanitarian-Situation-Report-No.2%28End-of-Year%29-31-December-2025.pdf.pdf [TCD-SRC-103] Methodology: UNICEF operational annual report. Limitations: Some OCHA data are relayed; flood data lack household detail.
- United Nations Population Fund. Chad Situation Report #1 – July 2024. 2024-08 (date basis: publication). https://www.unfpa.org/resources/chad-situation-report-1-july-2024 [TCD-SRC-104] Methodology: UNFPA operational situation report summarizing reported flood impacts, displacement and service delivery. Limitations: The brief gives no named flood-affected provinces or detailed breakdown of affected women and girls.
