## Climate pressures and exposure

- NAP says drought can reduce water quantity and quality; many rural communities rely on a single river or stream, and resulting scarcity affects users across provinces. [ZMB-E-003]
- NAP states higher temperatures and drought contribute to more frequent forest-fire risk in Southern, Western, Eastern and Central provinces. [ZMB-E-006]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Under the CCDR's BAU dry-hot scenario, drought risk rises fivefold by mid-century and climate change lowers annual GDP about 6 percent by 2050, affecting agriculture, hydropower and labor productivity. [ZMB-E-001]
- NAP links drought yield losses to lower smallholder income from crop, livestock and fish sales, with little adaptation finance in Southern, Western and Eastern provinces. [ZMB-E-005]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NAP rates drought-related groundwater exposure high and says low-rainfall areas have lower adaptive capacity because droughts occur frequently, including Southern and Eastern provinces in agro-ecological region I. [ZMB-E-004]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- CCDR says shock-responsive social protection backed by disaster risk financing has cushioned recent drought impacts; it recommends scaling early warning and adaptive social protection. [ZMB-E-002]
