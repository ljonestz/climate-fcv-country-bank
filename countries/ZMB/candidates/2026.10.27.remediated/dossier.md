## Climate pressures and exposure

- NAP says drought can reduce water quantity and quality; many rural communities rely on a single river or stream, and resulting scarcity affects users across provinces. [ZMB-E-003]
- NAP states higher temperatures and drought contribute to more frequent forest-fire risk in Southern, Western, Eastern and Central provinces. [ZMB-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-05-08 reports: The 2023/2024 season’s late onset, dry spells and heat affected crops and livestock; over nine million people in 84 of 117 districts were affected, with Central, Eastern, Southern and Western provinces hardest hit. [ZMB-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-01-30 reports: Late-December 2025 floods in Lundazi and Lumezi submerged Lundazi’s treatment plant, leaving over 40,000 people without safe water; UNICEF describes child displacement, missed school, crop losses and greater illness risk. [ZMB-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Under the CCDR's BAU dry-hot scenario, drought risk rises fivefold by mid-century and climate change lowers annual GDP about 6 percent by 2050, affecting agriculture, hydropower and labor productivity. [ZMB-E-001]
- NAP links drought yield losses to lower smallholder income from crop, livestock and fish sales, with little adaptation finance in Southern, Western and Eastern provinces. [ZMB-E-005]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The 2023/2024 drought affected over nine million people in more than 70% of 117 districts; over three million children were food insecure and more than 51,000 faced severe-wasting risk. [ZMB-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: Among respondents, 42.3% felt excluded from decisions and 12.4% reported discrimination or access barriers; 87% reported no barriers. [ZMB-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-03-20 reports: FAO reports that drought-damaged crops, dried water sources and livestock losses persisted in Gwembe and Sinazongwe after above-average 2025/2026 rains supported crop recovery; some households entered later seasons with depleted herds and assets. [ZMB-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NAP rates drought-related groundwater exposure high and says low-rainfall areas have lower adaptive capacity because droughts occur frequently, including Southern and Eastern provinces in agro-ecological region I. [ZMB-E-004]
- Source dated 2026-03-20 reports: FAO and partners distributed 1,312 goats to vulnerable farmers in Gwembe and Sinazongwe, reporting about 1,600 people reached, a majority women; support also included animal husbandry, disease-control and climate-smart agriculture training. [ZMB-E-110]
- Source dated 2026-03-20 reports: A woman farmer from Luumbo Camp, Munyumbwe, told FAO she had lost livestock in the drought and received replacement goats; the project says a pass-on arrangement is intended to extend offspring to other households. [ZMB-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- CCDR says shock-responsive social protection backed by disaster risk financing has cushioned recent drought impacts; it recommends scaling early warning and adaptive social protection. [ZMB-E-002]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: In 2025, UNICEF rehabilitated 86 boreholes (34 at health facilities) in drought-affected districts, benefiting 21,500; a separate cholera response restored water to 4,108 through seven boreholes. [ZMB-E-104]
- UNICEF reports drought cash delivery through the Social Cash Transfer system, with aggregate reach including regular and emergency households; reported avoided asset sales are not an independently evaluated effect. [ZMB-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: Among 950 surveyed assistance recipients, 66.7% said aid met household priorities; 27.3% disagreed, citing unmet needs, exclusion or access barriers. [ZMB-E-106]
- The recipient survey reports different protection-service perceptions in Lunga and Gwembe and identifies remoteness, roads and communications as constraints. [ZMB-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-01-30 reports: UNICEF reported orienting over 75 community volunteers across four flood-affected health facilities and 11 additional facilities remotely; three hand pumps were provided for installation and three clinic boreholes remained planned. [ZMB-E-113]
