## Climate-FCV pathways

- A secondary-cited 2004 Songkhla account describes drought-related food scarcity interacting with an existing insurgency's violent raids, not drought creating the insurgency. [THA-P-101; THA-E-107]
  - Documented impact: Expected rice-output losses and farmers stockpiling rice in fields, as described in the chapter's cited case.
  - FCV mediator: An existing insurgent organization sought control of scarce food resources through raids.
  - Possible consequence: The chapter reports violent raids and villagers killed during that drought period; no causal effect size is retained.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['THA-E-107'], 'impact': ['THA-E-107'], 'mediator': ['THA-E-107'], 'consequence': ['THA-E-107']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Existing insurgency, political history, targeting decisions and other concurrent conditions are central; drought is not established as the origin of conflict.']
  - Uncertainty: Secondary-cited historical account in a document-analysis chapter; underlying incident records and the reported percentage change were not independently verified. Not causal identification or a current forecast.

## Bibliography and review decision

- Office of Natural Resources and Environmental Policy and Planning, Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment, Thailand. Thailand's National Adaptation Plan (NAP). 2023-11 (date basis: document-version). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/NAP_THAILAND_2024.pdf [THA-SRC-101] Methodology: National plan reproduces ONEP consultant models and sectoral risk assessments for climate adaptation planning. Limitations: Provincial top-ten lists are model outputs attributed to ONEP 2021, not measured current impacts; the settlements-and-security sector label is not a conflict indicator.
- World Bank Group. Thailand Country Climate and Development Report. 2025 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099063025043513963/pdf/P507208-2e39fbbd-4d16-4660-a7eb-9c99c88864f0.pdf [THA-SRC-102] Methodology: World Bank country assessment with biophysical modeling and policy analysis; Industrial Economics supported physical-risk modeling. Limitations: Model scenarios and dates are stated claim-by-claim; the Khon Kaen institutional case describes planning processes, not evaluated climate-resilience outcomes.
- Springer Nature, in Risks, Resilience and Interdependency. Climate Change Adaptation Implementation and Governance in Thailand: Increased Resilience and Potential Conflicts. 2025-07-19 (date basis: publication). https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-7167-0_4 [THA-SRC-103] Methodology: Qualitative text analysis of policies, government data, public records and climate/conflict literature. Limitations: Document review, not primary causal research; authors say Thailand linkages remain understudied.
- Save the Children Thailand. Gender Equality and Social Inclusion Analysis (Thailand, 2025). 2025 (date basis: publication). https://resourcecentre.savethechildren.net/document/gender-equality-and-social-inclusion-analysis-thailand-2025 [THA-SRC-104] Methodology: Publisher abstract describes the study as mixed-methods; extracted findings are limited to that official abstract. Limitations: Full report PDF was not retrievable; sample, detailed methods and locality-level results were not inspected, and the abstract does not establish causal effects.
- United Nations in Thailand / UN Women. Women leading peace and climate resilience in southern Thailand. 2026-04-02 (date basis: publication). https://thailand.un.org/en/314423-women-leading-peace-and-climate-resilience-southern-thailand [THA-SRC-105] Methodology: Qualitative UN country feature profiles women and community organizations, with named-site observations, participant accounts and captions. Limitations: A feature story, not representative monitoring or climate-attribution research; local event estimates and reported experiences are not population-level measures.
