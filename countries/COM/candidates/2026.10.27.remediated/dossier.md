- Island water-source differences and named 2024 flood localities deepen local exposure; Grand Moroni's reliability and affordability deficits are distinct from national rainwater dependence. [COM-E-101, COM-E-104, COM-E-108]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC reports 11 tropical depressions or cyclones in 2018–2023; Cyclone Kenneth caused damage valued at 14% of GDP and affected over 345,000 people, including 12,000 displaced. [COM-E-001]
- The 2002 communication projected a 1°C rise in mean annual temperature and about 20 cm sea-level rise by 2050. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify it. [COM-E-004]
- Source dated 2024-09 reports: The assessment projects dry-season rainfall to fall 47% by 2090 and says more irregular precipitation may prolong drought and worsen freshwater scarcity. Scenario: The cited UNICEF passage does not specify an emissions scenario. [COM-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-09-29 reports: May 2024 heavy rains flooded Fomboni and Wanani (Mohéli), Vouani and Moya (Anjouan), and Bangoi and Moroni (Grande Comore); UNDP reports 66,835 people affected across the islands and three deaths. [COM-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The communication says open-field farming with little vegetation is more erosion-prone than traditional mixed agroforestry with persistent soil cover. [COM-E-005]
- Source dated 2024-09 reports: UNICEF reports about half Grande Comore's upland population relies on rainwater; Anjouan and Mohéli households mainly use gravity-fed rivers that often dry seasonally. [COM-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-04-15 reports: The Bank says climate impacts add to population and resource pressures in Grand Moroni, which receives most national rural-to-urban migration; internal migration accounts for an estimated 78% of city population growth. [COM-E-107]
- The April 2025 PID reports unreliable, unaffordable or unsafe water in Grand Moroni, with piped distribution about two hours daily and costly tanker alternatives. Its separate 35.8% rainwater-dependence estimate is national. [COM-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC organizes adaptation around water management, climate-resilient agriculture and food security, coastal and marine systems, infrastructure and habitat, and health. [COM-E-003]
- The World Bank overview says poor infrastructure, especially in coastal and urban areas, exacerbates natural-disaster impacts. [COM-E-006]
- Source dated not stated reports: IAWASuR aims to benefit 75,000 people (51% women) across Anjouan, Mohéli and Grande Comore through watershed restoration, climate-resilient water infrastructure, climate-smart governance and institutional capacity-building. [COM-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-09-29 reports: The Civil Protection directorate activated the national contingency plan and a fixed command post; nine multisector teams from national actors, Red Cross, MSF and UN agencies conducted initial assessments. [COM-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-09-29 reports: UNDP reports KMF 77 million funded 45-day river/drain clearance and rock removal in named communities; it later reports families in Mitsoudje returned home after channel clearing. [COM-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-04-15 reports: The Bank says years of political instability damaged the water sector, leaving aged infrastructure and persistent gaps in water management, investment, oversight, cost recovery and maintenance. [COM-E-109]
