# Sudan Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Sudan faces a robust warming signal, but rainfall change is spatially heterogeneous and substantially more uncertain than temperature change. Supporting evidence: SDN-E-001-E-005, E-016; pathways SDN-P-001 and P-002. The North Darfur finding of increased rainfall is particularly important as a warning against treating “drought” or “declining rainfall” as a uniform national trend. (UNFCCC1) [SDN-E-001, SDN-E-002, SDN-E-003, SDN-E-004, SDN-E-005, SDN-E-016, SDN-P-001, SDN-P-002]
- The best-supported climate-FCV mechanisms are mediated through livelihood sensitivity, pastoral mobility and resource governance; current Sudanese violence, famine and displacement cannot credibly be reduced to climate causation. Supporting evidence: SDN-E-011-E-015, E-017, E-022-E-023; pathways SDN-P-001-P-004. (Climate-Diplomacy4) [SDN-E-011, SDN-E-012, SDN-E-013, SDN-E-014, SDN-E-015, SDN-E-017, SDN-E-022, SDN-E-023, SDN-P-001, SDN-P-002, SDN-P-003, SDN-P-004]
- Since April 2023, FCV conditions themselves have become an exceptionally strong constraint on climate resilience through displacement, destroyed services, market interruption, agricultural disruption, access restrictions and fragmented implementation capacity. Supporting evidence: SDN-E-019-E-025, E-030; pathways SDN-P-004-P-006. (World Food Programme15) [SDN-E-019, SDN-E-020, SDN-E-021, SDN-E-022, SDN-E-023, SDN-E-024, SDN-E-025, SDN-E-030, SDN-P-004, SDN-P-005, SDN-P-006]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate-projection gap. The most traceable national projection system located is still based on CMIP5 and four RCPs. A comparable, publicly documented CMIP6/downscaled national assessment with state/basin-level uncertainty was not identified in the verified source set. (UNFCCC1) []
- Observation/access gap. Current-war insecurity makes it difficult to know whether meteorological, hydrological and agricultural observation networks remain functional and spatially representative. []
- Geographic/population gap. Darfur and Kordofan are relatively prominent in climate-security research, but large parts of Sudan have limited current local evidence. Accessible populations must not stand in for populations in besieged or inaccessible areas. The IPC itself documents the severity of such data constraints. (IPCinfo5) []
- Causal-attribution gap. No verified source establishes a national causal effect whereby anthropogenic climate change causes current armed conflict. Specialist studies consistently retain governance, marginalization, conflict history and resource institutions as independent drivers. (Climate-Diplomacy4) []
- FCV-to-climate quantification gap. There is strong qualitative/operational evidence that conflict harms food systems, infrastructure and delivery, but limited quantitative work estimates how much this reduces adaptive capacity or increases losses from a specified future climate hazard. []
- Differentiated-vulnerability gap. Women and girls, IDPs, pastoralists and farmers are represented, but rigorous comparisons by gender, disability, age, ethnicity, livelihood, wealth and displacement status remain sparse. (World Bank13) []
- Post-war evaluation gap. The strongest identified independent-style adaptation evaluation predates April 2023. Current World Bank ratings demonstrate implementation monitoring, not independent impact or peace-effectiveness evaluation. (UNDP IEO Evaluation Resource Center11) []
- Unintended-effect gap. Little evaluated evidence was found on whether new water points, land rehabilitation, cash/finance instruments or aid distribution alter resource access, elite capture, exclusion, mobility or local conflict. []
- Contradictory rainfall evidence. The NAP six-station historical analysis finds no long-term precipitation trend; the NDC describes lower rainfall nationally; Al Fashir 2000-2024 shows increased annual rainfall with high variability. These results concern different datasets, periods and scales and should remain unresolved rather than forced into a single trend. (UNFCCC1) []
- Dust/ecosystem attribution gap. The country priority domain identifies dust and ecosystem pressures, but the verified set does not establish a sufficiently traceable current climate-attribution chain for dust events or many observed forms of land degradation. []
- *None wholly absent. All nine controlled evidence classes have at least traceable support. However, response-performance and especially resilience-peace-capacity remain only partially covered because current independent evaluations and demonstrated peace outcomes are lacking. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Historical warming at 5/6 NAP stations. [SDN-E-001]
- No common long-term rainfall trend in NAP station sample. [SDN-E-002]
- NAP projection basis: CMIP5, 22 GCMs, four RCPs. [SDN-E-003]
- Warming robust; rainfall direction uncertain. [SDN-E-004]
- NDC reports broad climate pressures nationwide. [SDN-E-005]
- North Darfur local record shows warming plus wetter/highly variable rainfall. [SDN-E-016]
- War has produced massive displacement and exposure. [SDN-E-019]
- Acute hunger remained extreme in mid-2026. [SDN-E-021]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Rainfed crop sensitivity is a national adaptation concern. [SDN-E-006]
- In North Darfur, conflict effects on crop yield exceeded rainfall effects in study results. [SDN-E-017]
- War-era service collapse raises climate sensitivity. [SDN-E-020]
- El Fasher famine pathway was conflict/access-driven. [SDN-E-022]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate stress can worsen livelihood insecurity, but any FCV effect is mediated by markets, assets, governance and conflict conditions rather than being automatic. (UNFCCC2) [SDN-P-001; SDN-E-001, SDN-E-005, SDN-E-006, SDN-E-007, SDN-E-012, SDN-E-014, SDN-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Crop/livestock sensitivity and household coping pressures.
  - FCV mediator: Income and food-security loss; market dependence; depletion of assets.
  - Possible consequence: Where households lack alternatives, livelihood deterioration could increase displacement, grievance, harmful coping or susceptibility to wider instability.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed conflict', 'inflation', 'input prices', 'market closure', 'displacement', 'land governance and poverty']
  - Uncertainty: Complete climate -> violence chain is not demonstrated.
- Climate variability may modify pastoral mobility and resource contact, but governance and political-security conditions determine whether such contact becomes conflictual. (CGSpace9) [SDN-P-002; SDN-E-001, SDN-E-005, SDN-E-007, SDN-E-011, SDN-E-015, SDN-E-028]
  - Documented impact: Pressure on livestock systems and possible altered seasonal movements.
  - FCV mediator: Migration timing, livestock corridors, land access, water points and farmer-herder institutions.
  - Possible consequence: Earlier or redirected livestock movement may raise localized conflict risks where crops, routes or water points overlap and governance mechanisms fail.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SDN-E-001', 'SDN-E-005'], 'impact': ['SDN-E-007'], 'mediator': ['SDN-E-001'], 'consequence': ['SDN-E-015']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Tenure arrangements', 'political mobilization', 'armed groups', 'weapons', 'livestock-route encroachment', 'demographic pressures and historical disputes']
  - Uncertainty: Climate-specific contribution relative to these drivers is poorly quantified.
- Climate-sensitive livelihood mobility is plausible, but Sudan's current mass displacement must not be attributed to climate without direct evidence. (NUPI8) [SDN-P-003; SDN-E-012, SDN-E-013, SDN-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Potential livelihood loss and climate-sensitive migration.
  - FCV mediator: Movement into areas with constrained land, services and employment.
  - Possible consequence: Host-area tension could increase where access and institutions are inadequate.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SDN-E-012'], 'impact': ['SDN-E-012'], 'mediator': ['SDN-E-013'], 'consequence': ['SDN-E-012']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['The current displacement crisis is primarily a direct consequence of war and violence']
  - Uncertainty: Very high for any attempt to allocate current displacement quantitatively to climate.
- In current conflict zones, armed violence and access constraints are demonstrably undermining the production, food and service systems on which climate resilience depends. (IPCinfo5) [SDN-P-004; SDN-E-017, SDN-E-021, SDN-E-022, SDN-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Siege, blocked roads, destroyed water infrastructure, disrupted planting and markets.
  - FCV mediator: Armed conflict and humanitarian/commercial access.
  - Possible consequence: Households can lose assets, nutrition and coping capacity, leaving them more susceptible to later drought, heat, floods and disease.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SDN-E-017'], 'impact': ['SDN-E-017', 'SDN-E-023'], 'mediator': ['SDN-E-017'], 'consequence': ['SDN-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Macroeconomic collapse and pre-existing poverty interact strongly']
  - Uncertainty: Future climate consequences of depleted coping capacity are inferred.
- War compounds pre-existing adaptation-capacity gaps and shifts delivery toward decentralized and humanitarian structures. (UNFCCC2) [SDN-P-005; SDN-E-009, SDN-E-010, SDN-E-020, SDN-E-024, SDN-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Federal implementation capacity is unreliable in current World Bank project arrangements; state/local structures and third-party actors carry delivery functions.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict, institutional fragmentation, security constraints and reduced fiscal resources.
  - Possible consequence: Climate adaptation, WASH, agriculture, health and early-warning investments may be delayed, uneven or inaccessible.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Pre-war underinvestment and capacity deficiencies already existed']
  - Uncertainty: Project evidence cannot establish equal levels of impairment in every state.
- Conflict can undermine the ecological and governance assets needed for later adaptation, but the evidence is Darfur-specific and partly historical. (Humanitarian Practice Network12) [SDN-P-006; SDN-E-011, SDN-E-030]
  - Documented impact: Darfur evidence indicates conflict accelerated environmental degradation and weakened traditional environmental-management arrangements.
  - FCV mediator: Armed violence, institutional erosion, displacement and contested resource governance.
  - Possible consequence: Degraded land and governance could leave livelihoods less able to absorb later climate shocks.

  - Uncertainty: Foundational source is old; environmental degradation cannot automatically be attributed to climate.
- Water projects are potentially peace-supporting only when their governance and distribution are conflict-sensitive; infrastructure alone is not evidence of peace impact. (Climate-Diplomacy4) [SDN-P-007; SDN-E-005, SDN-E-028, SDN-E-029]
  - Documented impact: Water access is a recurring livelihood and resource-management concern.
  - FCV mediator: Whether infrastructure and access are shared, governed and perceived as legitimate.
  - Possible consequence: Jointly managed water systems could reduce friction and support community stabilization.

  - Documented impact: A pre-war programme delivered climate information, insurance and finance-related adaptation activities and received generally positive terminal-evaluation ratings.
  - FCV mediator: Household capacity to manage shocks without distress asset sales, displacement or severe consumption cuts.
  - Possible consequence: Better financial and information capacity could reduce erosive coping and therefore some forms of fragility exposure.

  - Uncertainty: No peace/conflict outcome was evaluated; programme predates April 2023.

- Direct “climate war” causality is unsupported. [SDN-E-011]
- Three mediated Climate-FCV mechanisms. [SDN-E-012]
- Displacement and mobility can mediate climate-security risk. [SDN-E-013]
- Climate-security pathways center on livelihoods and resources. [SDN-E-014]
- Mobility timing can mediate farmer-herder risk. [SDN-E-015]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National climate-policy material should be separated from subnational evidence. The strongest current FCV/humanitarian observations concern Darfur and Kordofan, while the World Bank project evidence is tied to selected delivery areas including Kassala, Northern and River Nile states. North Darfur's Al Fashir station finding - warming alongside increased but highly variable rainfall in 2000-2024 - must not be extrapolated to Sudan as a whole. (CMI - Chr. Michelsen Institute10) []
- El Fasher and Kadugli illustrate why climate and conflict mechanisms require local diagnosis. In Kadugli, the IPC review recorded broadly adequate 2025 rainfall alongside conflict-driven planting and supply disruption; in El Fasher, siege and blockade dominated the food-security mechanism. (IPCinfo5) []
- Khartoum's principal evidence in this register concerns large-scale returns into heavily damaged infrastructure and service environments rather than a climate-specific mechanism. Darfur/Kordofan, Khartoum, eastern states, Nile states, pastoral corridors, displacement settlements and urban settings therefore should remain analytically distinct. (UNHCR3) []
- Areas poorly represented by recent climate-FCV evidence include substantial parts of northern and eastern Sudan outside project sites, many conflict-controlled rural localities, Red Sea/coastal communities in current conditions, and locations from which systematic observation has become impossible. []
- *Relatively well covered: agriculture, pastoralism/livestock, food security, water/WASH, humanitarian delivery, basic services, displacement and climate adaptation institutions. []
- *Partially covered: public health, gender, land/resource governance, climate information, insurance/finance and ecosystem resilience. []
- *Thin or absent in this packet: current urban heat impacts, energy-system climate resilience, hydropower during the war, dust-storm attribution, contemporary coastal/sea-level impacts, fisheries, detailed groundwater monitoring, disability-specific vulnerability and independently evaluated peacebuilding effects of adaptation. []
