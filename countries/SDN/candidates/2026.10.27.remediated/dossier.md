- Climate-projection gap. The most traceable national projection system located is still based on CMIP5 and four RCPs. A comparable, publicly documented CMIP6/downscaled national assessment with state/basin-level uncertainty was not identified in the verified source set. (UNFCCC1) []
- Observation/access gap. Current-war insecurity makes it difficult to know whether meteorological, hydrological and agricultural observation networks remain functional and spatially representative. []
- Geographic/population gap. Darfur and Kordofan are relatively prominent in climate-security research, but large parts of Sudan have limited current local evidence. Accessible populations must not stand in for populations in besieged or inaccessible areas. The IPC itself documents the severity of such data constraints. (IPCinfo5) []
- Causal-attribution gap. No verified source establishes a national causal effect whereby anthropogenic climate change causes current armed conflict. Specialist studies consistently retain governance, marginalization, conflict history and resource institutions as independent drivers. (Climate-Diplomacy4) []
- FCV-to-climate quantification gap. There is strong qualitative/operational evidence that conflict harms food systems, infrastructure and delivery, but limited quantitative work estimates how much this reduces adaptive capacity or increases losses from a specified future climate hazard. []
- Differentiated-vulnerability gap. Women and girls, IDPs, pastoralists and farmers are represented, but rigorous comparisons by gender, disability, age, ethnicity, livelihood, wealth and displacement status remain sparse. (World Bank13) []
- Post-war evaluation gap. The strongest identified independent-style adaptation evaluation predates April 2023. Current World Bank ratings demonstrate implementation monitoring, not independent impact or peace-effectiveness evaluation. (UNDP IEO Evaluation Resource Center11) []
- Unintended-effect gap. Little evaluated evidence was found on whether new water points, land rehabilitation, cash/finance instruments or aid distribution alter resource access, elite capture, exclusion, mobility or local conflict. []
- Contradictory rainfall evidence. The NAP six-station historical analysis finds no long-term precipitation trend; the NDC describes lower rainfall nationally; Al Fashir 2000-2024 shows increased annual rainfall with high variability. These results concern different datasets, periods and scales and should remain unresolved rather than forced into a single trend. (UNFCCC1) []
- Dust/ecosystem attribution gap. The country priority domain identifies dust and ecosystem pressures, but the verified set does not establish a sufficiently traceable current climate-attribution chain for dust events or many observed forms of land degradation. []
- *None wholly absent. All nine controlled evidence classes have at least traceable support. However, response-performance and especially resilience-peace-capacity remain only partially covered because current independent evaluations and demonstrated peace outcomes are lacking. []
- No source located in this search established that ongoing conflict stopped implementation or delivery of a named climate-adaptation project; the documented reverse links concern agricultural, pastoral, and resource access. []
- NUPI's 2022 climate-security pathway is a secondary synthesis with explicit multi-driver caveats and predates the 2023 war; it is not an explanation of current conflict. []
- FAO's latest state-level rainfall/drought evidence does not provide district or household disaggregation, and it notes data gaps for the national lean-season projection. []
- SAMRA's access in conflict-affected states relied on remote consultations; its consultation percentages are not representative household estimates. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Six Sudan stations over 1961-2010 showed a fairly strong warming trend except Kadugli, which showed slight cooling. [SDN-E-001]
- The same six-station assessment found strong precipitation seasonality, a north-south gradient and interannual variability but no long-term precipitation trend. [SDN-E-002]
- The NAP used 22 CMIP5 GCMs and RCP2.6/4.5/6.0/8.5, with BCSD downscaling for future scenarios. Scenario: to 2100 [SDN-E-003]
- For Port Sudan, the NAP's ensemble mean indicated warming up to roughly 3°C by 2100, while projected precipitation ranged across both wetter and drier outcomes; the other five stations showed a similar uncertainty pattern. Scenario: to 2100; RCP ensemble [SDN-E-004]
- The updated NDC reports increased rainfall variability, lower rainfall, rising temperatures and flood/drought extremes across all 18 states. [SDN-E-005]
- At Al Fashir station, 2000-2024 maximum and minimum temperatures increased, while rainfall showed an increasing trend and a 45.45% coefficient of variation. [SDN-E-016]
- UNHCR reported about 9 million people displaced within Sudan and 4.4 million across borders by April 2026. [SDN-E-019]
- FAO/WFP reported 14 areas in North Darfur, South Darfur and South Kordofan at risk of Famine through September 2026; about 19.5 million people had faced IPC Phase 3+ through May 2026. Scenario: Jun-Sep 2026, some risk through Jan 2027 [SDN-E-021]
- The fact sheet reports that 2020 floods and landslides affected about 830,000 people and destroyed homes and crops; farmland was inundated in North Darfur, Khartoum, Blue Nile, West Darfur, and Sennar. [SDN-E-101]
- FAO's 24 September 2026 brief reports seasonal rainfall roughly 20% below average in Sennar and 30% in Gedaref, with agricultural-stress monitoring indicating severe drought on up to 55% and 70% of cropland, respectively. [SDN-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The NDC reports crop-suitability and yield problems and cites modelling of continuing impacts on major crops and the Kordofan gum-Arabic belt during 2030-2060. [SDN-E-006]
- The North Darfur study concluded that conflict impeded crop yield more than rainfall variability in its 2000-2024 analysis. [SDN-E-017]
- In May 2026, FAO/WFP/UNICEF estimated around 40% of health facilities were non-functional, 17 million people lacked safe drinking water and 24 million lacked adequate sanitation. [SDN-E-020]
- In El Fasher, the IPC FRC identified siege and near-total blockade, with restrictions on goods and destruction of water infrastructure, as the key driver of the 2025 famine finding. [SDN-E-022]
- The 2026 UNDP-ITC report describes conflict and displacement restricting pastoral routes and water points, pushing livestock onto cropland and aggravating farmer-pastoralist tensions in Darfur/Kordofan. [SDN-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Farmers in Kordofan, Darfur and Gedarif were reported to use dry-season night grazing to reduce livestock heat exposure. [SDN-E-007]
- The NDC identifies inadequate project preparation, weak state-level adaptation planning, unfinished enabling-environment work and absence of a dedicated NAP M&E system as implementation gaps. [SDN-E-009]
- Surveyed North Darfur households reported migration, asset sales, borrowing, reduced food intake and social support as coping strategies, alongside improved/early-maturing seeds among adaptation practices. [SDN-E-018]
- The same evaluation recorded risks including unclear institutional responsibilities, weather-station security and incomplete inclusion of pastoralists in an insurance programme. [SDN-E-027]
- Earlier Darfur analysis found that armed conflict itself accelerated environmental degradation and weakened traditional environmental management while political/economic marginalization remained an independent conflict driver. [SDN-E-030]
- UNDP's 2020-2027 project page proposes water yards, sand dams and hafirs and describes reduced resource competition as an intended benefit, not an independently evaluated peace effect. [SDN-E-103]
- Conflict restricted field access especially in Greater Darfur, Greater Kordofan, and Blue Nile; input shortages, high prices, and reduced credit also hindered farming. [SDN-E-105]
- The 2026 UNDP-ITC consultation report describes large productivity losses and unsafe harvesting in conflict-affected Darfur/Kordofan, with climate variability adding yield pressure. [SDN-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The NDC states that HCENR was consolidated as the national environmental council under 2020 legal amendments and identifies 12 adaptation priorities costing roughly US$3.85 billion. [SDN-E-008]
- The 2026 World Bank Thabat project paper says federal institutions cannot be relied upon for implementation because of conflict, while state/local institutions remain more operational. [SDN-E-024]
- Thabat's World Bank ISR recorded “Satisfactory” progress toward its PDO as of 20 January 2026, while overall project risk remained High and M&E was Moderately Satisfactory. [SDN-E-025]
- A 2021 UNDP/GEF terminal evaluation rated overall project outcomes and effectiveness Satisfactory, implementation/execution Moderately Satisfactory and sustainability Moderately Likely, but financial-resource sustainability Moderately Unlikely. [SDN-E-026]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Adelphi concludes that climate-related changes in natural-resource availability do not directly or automatically produce conflict and identifies marginalization, conflict history and governance failures as independent drivers. [SDN-E-011]
- Adelphi identifies resource competition, displacement/livelihood loss and inadequate institutional responses as three mediated routes through which climate/environmental stress can interact with fragility. [SDN-E-012]
- NUPI/SIPRI assesses that displacement can sharpen climate vulnerability and that migration changes associated with resource availability may increase local-conflict risks in some locations. [SDN-E-013]
- CGIAR's pathway analysis identifies livelihood/food-security and resource-access/availability as its two main climate-security pathways. [SDN-E-014]
- CGIAR describes earlier or altered pastoral movement and livestock-route pressures as mechanisms that can increase contact and disputes with settled farmers where land/water governance is weak. [SDN-E-015]
- In Kadugli, the FRC reported that 2025 seasonal rainfall was generally adequate, although delayed, while hostilities disrupted planting and access to supply roads. [SDN-E-023]
- Adelphi identifies locally appropriate water infrastructure, water governance and monitoring as potential entry points, noting that water access can become a focal point for community stabilization. [SDN-E-028]
- NUPI/SIPRI recommends locally owned adaptation, natural-resource conflict management, gender-differentiated responses and participation of women/youth in environmental peacebuilding and early warning. [SDN-E-029]
- NUPI's May 2022 synthesis describes resource competition and intercommunal tensions in West Darfur, mediated by political interests, exclusion, land use and regional conflict; it does not offer a direct climate-conflict causal estimate. [SDN-E-102]
