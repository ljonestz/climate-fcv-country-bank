# Cameroon Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Cameroon’s Climate-FCV risk is geographically differentiated rather than nationally uniform. Northern agro-pastoral/resource pathways, Anglophone conflict and delivery constraints, eastern refugee-hosting pressures and coastal/urban climate exposure should be assessed separately. Supporting evidence: CMR-E-001-E-007, E-014-E-018; CMR-P-001-P-004. [CMR-E-001, CMR-E-002, CMR-E-003, CMR-E-004, CMR-E-005, CMR-E-006, CMR-E-007, CMR-E-014, CMR-E-015, CMR-E-016, CMR-E-017, CMR-E-018, CMR-P-001, CMR-P-002, CMR-P-003, CMR-P-004]
- The most credible Climate-FCV formulation is mediated and bidirectional. Climate variability can stress livelihood/resource systems, while conflict, displacement, governance and access constraints can independently weaken coping, municipal services and adaptation delivery. Supporting evidence: CMR-E-008-E-018, E-024; CMR-P-001-P-006. [CMR-E-008, CMR-E-009, CMR-E-010, CMR-E-011, CMR-E-012, CMR-E-013, CMR-E-014, CMR-E-015, CMR-E-016, CMR-E-017, CMR-E-018, CMR-E-024, CMR-P-001, CMR-P-002, CMR-P-003, CMR-P-004, CMR-P-005, CMR-P-006]
- Cameroon has meaningful institutional and programme capacity, but effectiveness is uneven and evaluative evidence cautions against equating plans or outputs with resilience. Supporting evidence: CMR-E-012-E-013, E-019-E-026; CMR-P-005, P-007, P-008. [CMR-E-012, CMR-E-013, CMR-E-019, CMR-E-020, CMR-E-021, CMR-E-022, CMR-E-023, CMR-E-024, CMR-E-025, CMR-E-026, CMR-P-005, CMR-P-007, CMR-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: NDC 3.0’s readily locatable adaptation projections substantially inherit earlier material; transparent model ensembles, scenarios, uncertainty ranges and current subnational downscaling need independent verification. []
- Causal Climate-FCV evidence: neither the Far North nor North-West evidence estimates the independent causal contribution of climate variables to violence. []
- Geographic coverage: direct evidence is particularly weak for the East/refugee-hosting context, Adamawa, southern forest areas, coastal fisheries and informal coastal settlements. []
- Differentiated vulnerability: sex-, age-, disability-, ethnicity/indigeneity- and displacement-disaggregated climate-resilience evidence remains incomplete. []
- FCV-to-climate direction: project evidence establishes that insecurity can impede delivery, but there is little causal measurement of how much conflict reduces climate-adaptation outcomes or increases future hazard losses. []
- Response evaluation: long-term adaptation outcomes, durability after project closure and unintended effects are poorly covered even where output evaluations exist. []
- Peacebuilding: the reviewed Lake Chad peacebuilding monitoring relies heavily on beneficiary/implementer reporting; independent evaluation of conflict outcomes is absent. []
- Local institutions: customary, pastoral, municipal and community institutions are mentioned in local studies, but comparative evidence on which governance arrangements successfully prevent escalation is missing. []
- Sector gaps: forests, fisheries and hydropower require stronger sector-specific evidence. []
- Hazard attribution: rainfall can trigger landslides, but geology, land use, drainage and infrastructure conditions need to remain separate; this packet contains no event-attribution analysis supporting climate-change attribution for individual landslides. []
- NAP verification: the official MINEPDED PNACC publication page was accessible, but the underlying attachment was not parsed and therefore was not used for substantive atomic claims. (Minepded) []
- *None are wholly absent, but coping-capacity, direct-climate-fcv and resilience-peace-capacity are only partially covered and should not be treated as nationally representative. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Strong long-run northern warming projected. [CMR-E-001]
- Rainfall change is spatially heterogeneous. [CMR-E-002]
- Multiple climate hazards affect Cameroon. [CMR-E-004]
- Far North has >510k estimated IDPs. [CMR-E-005]
- Nearly 493k NW/SW IDPs. [CMR-E-006]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Agriculture/livestock/water are climate-sensitive. [CMR-E-003]
- Acute hunger concentrated in crisis regions. [CMR-E-007]
- FCV and displacement strain service systems. [CMR-E-011]
- Rainfall variability pressures pastoral mobility locally. [CMR-E-017]
- Herd pressure is an independent co-driver. [CMR-E-018]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Coping capacity is strained in crisis settings. [CMR-E-008]
- Formal multi-level climate governance exists. [CMR-E-012]
- Completed drainage produced measurable flood-protection output. [CMR-E-025]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Humanitarian delivery capacity fell materially. [CMR-E-009]
- Decentralized delivery capacity is constrained. [CMR-E-010]
- Implementation constraints remain substantial. [CMR-E-013]
- Programme did not measurably improve shock recovery. [CMR-E-020]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-related livelihood stress may aggravate already-governed resource disputes; it is not demonstrated as a sufficient cause of violence. (Refugees International) [CMR-P-001; CMR-E-014, CMR-E-015, CMR-E-016]
  - Documented impact: Reported reductions/disruptions in pasture, livestock water, crops and fishing/farming livelihoods.
  - FCV mediator: Competition over water, channels, grazing areas and crops, interacting with local resource-sharing rules.
  - Possible consequence: Under adverse governance and security conditions, these pressures may contribute to intercommunal clashes and displacement.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-015'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-015'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-014', 'CMR-E-015'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-014']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed actors', 'political connections', 'population growth', 'historical intergroup relations', 'insurgency', 'local governance']
  - Uncertainty: Interview-based evidence cannot estimate the climate contribution to violence.
- Climate shocks occur inside a conflict-displacement system that can weaken coping, rather than independently producing acute hunger. (UNHCR Data Portal) [CMR-P-002; CMR-E-005, CMR-E-007, CMR-E-008, CMR-E-009]
  - Documented impact: Acute food insecurity and constrained household coping.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict displacement, loss/restriction of land and livelihood access, concentration of needs.
  - Possible consequence: Displaced and host households may become less able to absorb subsequent climate or price shocks.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-007', 'CMR-E-008'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-007'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-005', 'CMR-E-008'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-005']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Inflation/high food prices', 'poverty', 'aid funding', 'disease', 'market disruption']
  - Uncertainty: No reviewed study decomposes the relative climate/conflict contributions.
- In Kilum-Ijim, climate variability interacts with herd pressure and land governance rather than operating as a stand-alone conflict driver. (Frontiers) [CMR-P-003; CMR-E-017, CMR-E-018, CMR-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Poorer pasture and longer livestock movements.
  - FCV mediator: Crop/grazing encroachment, livestock density, crop damage and land-tenure pressures.
  - Possible consequence: These interacting pressures may aggravate farmer-pastoralist conflict.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-017'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-017'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-018'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-018']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Herd growth', 'overgrazing', 'agricultural expansion', 'tenure arrangements']
  - Uncertainty: No national extrapolation is justified.
- Recent project evidence directly shows insecurity delaying or narrowing resilience investment. (World Bank) [CMR-P-004; CMR-E-010, CMR-E-024, CMR-E-025, CMR-E-026]
  - Documented impact: Need for resilient urban infrastructure and services.
  - FCV mediator: Security problems restricted implementation and caused reductions in project scope.
  - Possible consequence: Conflict may leave some exposed urban communities with delayed or incomplete resilience investments.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-025'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-025'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-024'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-024', 'CMR-E-026']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Administrative delay', 'procurement', 'counterpart funding and staffing also constrained implementation']
  - Uncertainty: Project experience cannot be generalized to every municipality.
- Cameroon has an increasingly articulated climate architecture, but implementation bottlenecks may weaken conversion of policy into local resilience. (UNFCCC) [CMR-P-005; CMR-E-001, CMR-E-004, CMR-E-010, CMR-E-012, CMR-E-013]
  - Documented impact: Climate policies require sectoral/local implementation.
  - FCV mediator: Institutional fragmentation, limited decentralization capacity, fiscal constraints and weak data systems.
  - Possible consequence: Adaptation may be slower, less spatially targeted or less sustainable.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-001', 'CMR-E-004'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-001'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-010', 'CMR-E-013'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-001']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Fiscal space', 'normal administrative complexity and project design']
  - Uncertainty: No reviewed nationwide study estimates adaptation losses attributable to governance fragmentation.
- Assistance capacity is itself a mediator of whether multi-causal shocks become deeper household crises. (World Food Programme) [CMR-P-006; CMR-E-007, CMR-E-008, CMR-E-009]
  - Documented impact: Severe food insecurity in conflict-affected regions.
  - FCV mediator: Displacement and insecurity elevate needs while donor shortages reduce delivery.
  - Possible consequence: Reduced aid coverage could increase harmful coping or deepen vulnerability to subsequent shocks.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-007'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-007'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-007'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-007']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Prices', 'harvest conditions', 'household assets and market access']
  - Uncertainty: Downstream conflict/stability effects have not been evaluated.
- Evidence suggests local resource institutions can be peace-supporting, but the measured magnitude of their effect remains uncertain. (MPTF Gateway) [CMR-P-007; CMR-E-019, CMR-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Water/pasture access can be a focus of community disputes.
  - FCV mediator: Quality of dialogue, dispute management and inclusive resource rules.
  - Possible consequence: Stronger local institutions may reduce escalation even under resource stress.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-019'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-019'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-019'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-019']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Wider security trends and other interventions may explain perceived conflict reductions']
  - Uncertainty: No independent counterfactual evaluation of peace outcomes.
- Measurable livelihood gains did not automatically produce measurable shock resilience. (IFAD) --- [CMR-P-008; CMR-E-020, CMR-E-021]
  - Documented impact: AEP-Youth improved measured food security but did not improve measured shock-recovery ability.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict/insecurity was explicitly included among non-climate shocks in the evaluation framework.
  - Possible consequence: Economic or food-security improvements should not automatically be interpreted as climate/FCV resilience.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['CMR-E-020'], 'impact': ['CMR-E-020'], 'mediator': ['CMR-E-020'], 'consequence': ['CMR-E-020']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Differential shock exposure among participants and comparison households']
  - Uncertainty: Programme geography excludes major Far North and East crisis contexts.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Immediate clash triggers involved resource-use incidents. [CMR-E-014]
- Climate stress was reported as an aggravating mediator. [CMR-E-015]
- Simple “ever-shrinking lake causes conflict” narrative is inadequate. [CMR-E-016]
- Local institutions provide some risk-reducing capacity. [CMR-E-019]
- Beneficiaries reported peacebuilding benefits. [CMR-E-023]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- *National evidence is strongest for policy, climate-governance architecture, displacement aggregates and humanitarian needs. It is not sufficient to infer a single national Climate-FCV mechanism. (UNHCR Data Portal) []
- *Far North: strongest direct Climate-FCV evidence, but the reviewed Logone Birni evidence is commune-specific. Current displacement evidence and humanitarian pressures are much broader than that case study. []
- *North-West/South-West: current displacement and conflict-related project-delivery constraints are well documented. The Kilum-Ijim pastoral study covers only a small North-West setting and cannot characterize the Anglophone crisis generally. []
- *East/Adamawa: WFP identifies substantial food-security needs and refugee-hosting pressures are part of the national humanitarian setting, but this review found little direct, well-located evidence demonstrating climate-FCV mechanisms there. []
- *Coastal/urban areas: NDC evidence supports flood/sea-level exposure and the World Bank ICR provides evaluated urban resilience evidence, especially around drainage. Evidence linking coastal climate pressures to FCV outcomes is weak. []
- Important underrepresented populations include some refugee-host communities, forest-dependent and fisheries-dependent groups, people with disabilities, indigenous groups, and differentiated experiences of women and girls outside specific programme samples. []
- Agriculture, pastoralism, water, food security, municipal infrastructure and humanitarian delivery are relatively well covered. Forests appear primarily in national policy; fisheries are present mainly through the Logone case; hydropower has little traceable Climate-FCV evidence in this packet. Health and education appear chiefly as affected services rather than as independently investigated climate-FCV sectors. []
