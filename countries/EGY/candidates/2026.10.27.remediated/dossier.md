## Climate pressures and exposure

- The 2025 BTR cites severe 2010 floods as displacing thousands and damaging more than 4,000 houses. This is historical disaster exposure, not a current displacement estimate or attribution to anthropogenic climate change. [EGY-E-103]
- Source dated 2022-11-08 reports: The World Bank estimates over 80% of residents in Egypt's 14 major cities are exposed to at least one major climate risk; the Nile Delta is about one metre above mean sea level. [EGY-E-104]
- Source dated 2022-11-08 reports: The report projects that lower Nile inflows could reduce irrigated land by 22%, output per irrigated hectare by 11% and agricultural employment by 9%; its extreme-water-scarcity estimate is 2033 if resources and population trends persist. Scenario: The summary states the water-scarcity assumption but does not identify a climate scenario for these figures. [EGY-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The September 2025 BTR describes historical salinity intrusion and damage to Rosetta's coastal agriculture and freshwater. Its salinity unit and underlying studies need confirmation before the percentage is reused. [EGY-E-101]
- Source dated 2022-11-08 reports: The report says poor urban residents are more likely to live on hazardous sites or in slums, face higher pollution and congestion, and lack assets or skills to cope with shocks. [EGY-E-105]
- Source dated 2023-06-26 reports: The 2023 NDC reports that 95% of Egypt's population lives in the Nile Valley and Delta; it records 55.5 BCM annual Nile supply against estimated needs of 114 BCM and a 97% external renewable-water dependency ratio. [EGY-E-107]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The 2025 BTR reports an irrigation-modernization programme covering a stated four-million-feddan ambition, with an expected 20% water-efficiency gain. These are reported implementation scope and targets, not independently verified achieved savings. [EGY-E-102]
- Source dated not stated reports: The coastal project also proposes small livelihood pilots with civil-society and research partners, including greenhouses and fishing-net support for local residents and small farmers. [EGY-E-109]
