# Mauritius Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Flood-planning examples name localities around Port Louis and elsewhere, while Baie du Nord on Rodrigues provides specific fisheries, water-access and household adaptation evidence. [MUS-E-108, MUS-E-109, MUS-E-110, MUS-E-112, MUS-E-113, MUS-E-114]
- Poverty, insecure tenure, disability and unequal access to finance and decisions mediate climate vulnerability. Reported social provisions and coastal mapping are not effectiveness evaluations. [MUS-E-103, MUS-E-104, MUS-E-105, MUS-E-106, MUS-E-107]
- Rodrigues interviews describe political divisions affecting adaptation acceptance. October 2026 water-service violence is retained as contextual evidence, not a climate-caused conflict claim. [MUS-E-116, MUS-E-117, MUS-E-118, MUS-E-119]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No inspected primary source explicitly attributes the October 2026 water-service protests to climate change; ACLED reports water cuts, cost-of-living pressures and a police shooting, so no climate-to-violence chain is asserted. []
- The Rodrigues study reports political-party divisions affecting acceptance of adaptation measures but does not identify armed conflict, violence or insecurity; no source directly documents insecurity interrupting adaptation delivery. []
- The full Mauritius CCDR and BRGM Phase 2 coastal-risk volumes could not be opened by the web reader because of file-size limits. The BRGM claim is limited to the directly inspected Ministry annual-report summary; detailed district-level coastal erosion projections remain unverified. []
- The Government flood webpage reports 16 high-risk sites but enumerates 15 numbered entries; the extraction names listed localities without relying on the inconsistent total. []
- Baie du Nord's fisheries and household adaptations are documented in a UNDP programme narrative rather than an independent impact evaluation; reported income and water outcomes are not independently verified. []
- No complete climate-FCV pathway is assembled. Political acceptance of adaptation is governance evidence, while water-service violence has no inspected climate attribution. []
- The same locality or island can have several pressures without those pressures establishing a causal chain. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: The NDC records prolonged dry months in 2020/21, 2022/23 and 2024/25, compared with episodes in 1998/99 and 2010/11; it attributes increased frequency to climate change. [MUS-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: The regional climate projection cited in the NDC estimates a further 13% decline in average rainfall by 2050. [MUS-E-102]
- Source dated not stated reports: The page reports that Port Louis flash floods killed 11 people in 2013; Cyclone Belal caused 2024 flooding, two deaths and damage to infrastructure and private property. [MUS-E-113]
- Source dated not stated reports: The plan lists flood-prone localities including Roche Bois, Port Louis reaches, Grand Baie-Pereybere, Flacq, Vacoas, Quatre Bornes, Flic en Flac, Mapou, Piton and Cottage. [MUS-E-114]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: Forum participants said poverty limits suitable housing and safety-net access; untitled people in precarious high-risk areas face stigma and barriers to post-disaster assistance. [MUS-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: The NDC identifies women, youth, children, marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and people with health conditions as disproportionately affected; unequal access to land, finance, information and decisions compounds risk. [MUS-E-104]
- Source dated not stated reports: Baie du Nord fishers report a 75% decade-long decline in octopus and reef catches; most of the village's 350 residents rely on the ocean for food and livelihoods. [MUS-E-108]
- Source dated not stated reports: Project consultations found that reduced-rainfall dry months left villagers relying on trucked water and paying for storage; a steadier supply was a priority. [MUS-E-109]
- Source dated not stated reports: Farmers interviewed named water stress, saline intrusion, limited technical knowledge and credit, costly inputs, and weak agricultural extension as adaptation barriers. [MUS-E-117]
- In its October 2026 account, ACLED reported at least 15 water-protest events in the week after 5 October around Vacoas-Phoenix and Bambous; over two-thirds involved violence. It identifies water cuts, living-cost pressures and a police shooting, without attributing the shortages to climate change. [MUS-E-119]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: The NDC reports a Social Register and 166 cyclone/flood evacuation centres, plus financial and food aid, social housing, and specialized vehicles for people with disabilities. [MUS-E-105]
- Source dated not stated reports: Older planters drew on past flood experience and used canals as prevention; younger planters reported being overwhelmed during prolonged 2018 rains. [MUS-E-116]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated not stated reports: The Ministry reports 33 coastal rehabilitation priority sites spanning 31 km, with hard, soft and nature-based approaches; the report does not name the sites. [MUS-E-106]
- Source dated not stated reports: Government reports hotspot field surveys and completed GIS risk maps for current conditions and 2050/2100 impacts; stakeholders were trained to use them in June 2025. [MUS-E-107]
- Source dated not stated reports: Participatory mapping combined village knowledge and data-rich maps to identify low-lying areas prone to sea-level rise and flooding; it recommended future development at least 2 metres above present sea level. [MUS-E-110]
- Source dated not stated reports: Poultry, agriculture and financial-management training supported local women and families; UNDP reports monthly income rose nearly 40% for 16 families. [MUS-E-111]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP reports 2,000-litre tanks installed at 38 households, reducing truck-water dependence and increasing water available for gardens and livestock. [MUS-E-112]
- Source dated not stated reports: The plan recommends evacuation protocols, accelerated drainage works, improved maps and zoning, and nature-based drainage; the page does not report their effectiveness. [MUS-E-115]
- Source dated not stated reports: Respondents described octopus closures, marine reserves and plastic bans as sometimes imposed; adaptation acceptance varied with local party allegiance. [MUS-E-118]
