# Bhutan Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- District/gewog indices identify differentiated vulnerability, while Dechencholing and Punakha cases show historical displacement and service-access losses; indices are older and cases are not representative. [BTN-E-101, BTN-E-102, BTN-E-103, BTN-E-107, BTN-E-108]
- Water-system delivery gaps and women's participation barriers add institutional depth. Mobility evidence is anecdotal and wildlife interactions are not human FCV. [BTN-E-105, BTN-E-106, BTN-E-110, BTN-E-111, BTN-E-112]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The assessment identifies glacial lake outburst floods, flash floods, landslides, forest fires, and windstorms alongside slower-onset glacial retreat and ecosystem degradation, with implications for water, food, energy, infrastructure, health, and livelihoods. [BTN-E-003]
- The report records heavy rain on 4-5 October 2025 followed by flash floods, landslides, swollen rivers, and windstorms; it notes crop, livestock, home, cultural-site, infrastructure, and hydropower impacts, one death, and two people missing, with Samtse, Chukha, Haa, and Dagana among the most affected districts. [BTN-E-004]
- The World Bank release says hotter and drier conditions could affect hydropower, tourism, and agriculture and produce economic losses by 2050; it reports that adaptation and diversification could reduce projected losses by at least 40 percent. [BTN-E-005]
- Thimphu is projected as the highest future-risk area, associated with in-migration, urbanisation and population growth. [BTN-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Bhutan's mountainous setting, reliance on hydropower and agriculture, and limited resources and capacity shape its climate vulnerability. [BTN-E-001]
- The release reports that about 65 percent of Bhutan's population is exposed to at least one natural hazard and identifies Zhemgang and Mongar among districts with higher risk; floods, landslides, and heat disproportionately affect vulnerable people. [BTN-E-006]
- The 2022 vulnerability assessment's present-risk index ranks Samtse, Monggar, Sarpang, Punakha and Dagana highest using older administrative indicators; not a 2026 risk or loss ranking. [BTN-E-101]
- The 2022 assessment ranks Lunana gewog in Gasa highest in socioeconomic vulnerability, citing low electricity and roughly19%concrete housing in its underlying data. [BTN-E-103]
- Assessment links climate-driven wetland and habitat shifts with increased human-wildlife conflict. [BTN-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC reports that the 12th Five-Year Plan includes climate resilience and disaster preparedness priorities and describes national coordination arrangements for climate action. [BTN-E-002]
- The 2022 assessment gives Zhemgang, Dagana and Gasa the lowest adaptive-capacity index scores; slower recovery is an inference, not measured outcomes. [BTN-E-102]
- The NAP prioritizes protecting and managing water sources, water-user groups, local stakeholder capacity, research and integrated water management across sectors. [BTN-E-109]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- 2025 Punakha floods cut Gasa road access for over two weeks; bridge loss affected 42 households, while budget gaps delayed response and residents supplied most repair labour. [BTN-E-108]
- The 2026 ACREWAS midterm review reports11,544beneficiaries (45.7%female),3,220trained people and4,426hectares under climate-resilient management; these are interim programme indicators. [BTN-E-110]
- At the 2026 ACREWAS midterm, assured irrigation/domestic-water access reached5.6%of its interim target, with systems still under construction; final water-service benefits were not yet measurable. [BTN-E-111]
- The 2026 ACREWAS review describes women interested in water-user associations but constrained by gender roles, lower formal education and care burdens; sustained participation effects were not evaluated. [BTN-E-112]
