## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The report associates El Niño with more flooding on the Costa and drought in the Sierra; agriculture and fisheries’ large employment and export shares add exposure. [PER-E-101]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The diagnostic’s approximately 5-km water-balance model found deficits in 72 of 231 basins; six additional basins could enter deficit under its RCP8.5 projection. Scenario: The report labels the additional-basin map RCP8.5 for 2030 and describes broader model projections for 2030 and 2050. [PER-E-104]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Under possible summer El Niño/La Niña rainfall anomalies, the plan rates 78 districts across 12 departments very high flood risk, covering 2.396 million people; Annex 4 names Belén and Piura districts including Catacaos and Tambogrande. Scenario: Potential January–March rainfall anomalies associated with historical El Niño and La Niña episodes; a risk scenario, not a dated event forecast or climate-change projection. [PER-E-107]
- Source dated 2012-06 reports: Huascarán Park’s head said glacier retreat, warming and erratic rain were weakening Cordillera Blanca wetlands’ and pastures’ water regulation; low-income settlers and illegal miners were entering sensitive highlands exposed to glacial-lake outbursts. [PER-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The report says informality, inequality and low safe-water/sanitation access increase climate welfare vulnerability; 73% of employed people had informal jobs in 2019, with worker poverty rates over four times formal peers. [PER-E-102]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The report identifies the Rimac Basin as the sharpest deficit: Lima’s 11 million residents rely on Atlantic-region transfers for several months yearly; north-coast irrigated agriculture is also threatened by scarcity. [PER-E-105]
- Source dated 2023 reports: In Sierra and Alta Selva, where half of rural residents are poor, only about 20% of cultivated land is irrigated, leaving farming exposed to rainfall shifts; most Costa agricultural land is irrigated. [PER-E-106]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025 reports: For 2025–2027, the plan assigns INDECI technical assistance to reactivate or optimize 43 community early-warning systems and strengthen 122 others for intense-rain hazards. [PER-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The report describes weak national–regional coordination and limited subnational DRM participation; only 13 regions, 28 provinces and 39 districts had prevention plans, while central authorities led the 2017 El Niño emergency outside the DRM framework. [PER-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: MINAM prioritized 39 cases, OEFA 23 and SERNANP 14; MINAM reported 342 of 379 commitments across 35 cases complete, 25 in process, 12 pending, and 100 alerts issued during 2025. [PER-E-109]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: Authorities and residents of Ocuviri and Vila Vila, Puno, demanded environmental remediation of the Hatun Ayllu basin and a plenary session involving ministers and other senior officials. [PER-E-110]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The Llallimayo case covers Ayaviri, Cupi, Llalli and Umachiri in Melgar, Puno; an environmental-recovery worktable expired in December 2025 after a 2020 rights action was suspended, and local actors sought a replacement development table. [PER-E-111]

## Bibliography and review decision

- World Bank Group. Peru Country Climate and Development Report. 2022-11 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099220512062228587/pdf/P1771370b3ea6109108b4403bb881f18f1d.pdf [PER-SRC-101] Methodology: World Bank synthesis of climate, socioeconomic and institutional diagnostics with referenced quantitative estimates. Limitations: Dated 2022 with mixed indicator vintages; a national development report, not a conflict study, and attribution varies by estimate.
- World Bank. Peru: Strategic Actions Toward Water Security. 2023 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099060623153520241/pdf/P1736360229c810820982e0e2287df37745.pdf [PER-SRC-102] Methodology: Water-security diagnostic using local hydrological data, satellite evapotranspiration, demand databases and SENAMHI climate projections. Limitations: Mixed study vintages and model assumptions; basin deficits are not 2026 measurements, and conflict discussion does not establish climate attribution.
- Presidencia del Consejo de Ministros (PCM), with CENEPRED. Plan Multisectorial ante Lluvias Intensas y Peligros Asociados 2025–2027. 2025 (date basis: publication). https://sigrid.cenepred.gob.pe/sigridv3/storage/biblioteca/18525_plan-multisectorial-ante-lluvias-intensas-y-peligros-asociados-2025-2027.pdf [PER-SRC-103] Methodology: Government risk plan combines hazard susceptibility, district exposure and vulnerability with agency intervention targets. Limitations: ENSO-based risk scenario is not an event forecast or anthropogenic attribution; intervention targets are not verified delivery outcomes.
- Ministerio del Ambiente (MINAM), Oficina General de Asuntos Socio-Ambientales (OGASA). Informe N.º 12-2025-OGASA: Conflictos socioambientales, diciembre 2025. 2025-12 (date basis: publication). https://sinia.minam.gob.pe/sites/default/files/archivos/public/docs/Informe%20OGASA-DIC2025.pdf [PER-SRC-104] Methodology: Official monthly administrative monitoring of socio-environmental cases, alerts and agency commitments. Limitations: Agency case and commitment records are not independent verification, climate attribution or impact evaluation.
- Foundation for Environmental Security and Sustainability (FESS), prepared for USAID Office of Conflict Management and Mitigation. Follow the Water: Emerging Issues of Climate Change and Conflict in Peru. 2012-06 (date basis: publication). https://climateandsecurity.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/pnaec328.pdf [PER-SRC-105] Methodology: Original field interviews in Lima, Huaraz/Santa River Basin and highland Arequipa. Limitations: 2012 discussion paper expressly not official USAID; qualitative interviews, no causal evaluation.
