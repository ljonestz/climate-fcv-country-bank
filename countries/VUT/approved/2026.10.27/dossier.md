## Climate pressures and exposure

- Cyclone Pam damaged remote-island roads, schools and public assets; flood-prone roads and weak school buildings can isolate communities and disrupt services. [VUT-E-001]
- Source dated 2025-09-18 reports: During 1971–2021, the study counted 2.31 cyclone tracks per season within 500 km of Torba, 2.30 of Sanma and 2.45 of Penama. [VUT-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-09-18 reports: The severe-cyclone share rose in all six provinces; mean wind-speed increases were significant for Tafea (18.1%), Penama (13.4%) and Shefa (13.0%). [VUT-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-09-18 reports: Under CMIP5 RCP8.5, the illustrative severe-cyclone intensity of Pam has a shorter projected return period in 2071–2100 than 1971–2000: 77 to 62 years in Torba and 68 to 51 in Penama. Scenario: CMIP5 RCP8.5 high-emissions pathway; 2071–2100 compared with 1971–2000. [VUT-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Damaged decentralized energy systems disrupt water, sanitation and other services, with differentiated burdens on women, children, older people and persons with disabilities. [VUT-E-004]
- Source dated 2025-03-25 reports: The UNDP account attributes clean-water difficulty on Tongariki and Buninga to saltwater intrusion from rising seas; the boat-accessible islands lack natural streams and rely on rainwater. [VUT-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-03-25 reports: Residents walk long distances in intense heat carrying water containers that can exceed 20 kg; the account says this is especially treacherous for older people and people with disabilities. [VUT-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The proposed energy-resilience work combines institutional capacity, risk-informed design and community-level pilots for remote communities. [VUT-E-005]
- Source dated 2025-03-25 reports: A locally assessed rainwater system was designed for 134 households (107 Tongariki; 27 Buninga), with raised and reinforced tanks; materials had arrived, but household service was still a future hope. [VUT-E-106]
- Source dated 2018 reports: The policy recommends community-led relocation negotiations, free prior and informed consent of customary landholders, land-dispute grievance channels and host-community support as displacement safeguards. [VUT-E-109]
- Source dated 2024 reports: In 2024, 23 of 29 Area Council climate officers were in place; 40 communities developed adaptation plans, while final documentation remained due in 2025. [VUT-E-110]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Two solar freezers were procured for fisheries cold storage in Mota Lava and Mataso/Makira. [VUT-E-113]
- Source dated 2024 reports: A solar dryer pilot for root crops and vegetables was introduced in Nguna/Pele. [VUT-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The programme reports reconstructed roads, schools and public facilities, improved all-weather access and local-contractor employment. [VUT-E-002]
- Needs-based selection, partner coordination, community involvement and local technical training were reported features of the reconstruction approach. [VUT-E-003]
- Source dated 2018 reports: For communities facing repeated floods, provincial governments sometimes arranged temporary stays with host communities; the policy describes assistance procedures as unclear and responses as ad hoc. [VUT-E-108]
- Source dated 2024 reports: Airline liquidation forced slow, infrequent sea travel to remote targets and disrupted some activities; recruitment and communication also delayed. [VUT-E-111]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The Ministry says parliamentary dissolution and political instability disrupted climate-project implementation. [VUT-E-112]
