## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC says coastal and marine ecosystems face storms, flooding, erosion and sea-level rise, with growing freshwater scarcity and declining marine productivity. Scenario: Qualitative long-term hazards; no climate scenario is specified at this locator. [GNB-E-001]
- The NDC reports mangroves cover about 22% of the country and declined approximately 19% in 2001–2021; they support fisheries and many livelihoods. [GNB-E-002]
- NC4 reports coastal displacement from land and housing loss, farmer crop failures amid unpredictable rainfall, and threats to Bijagós ecosystems. [GNB-E-005]
- For 2016–2045, the NDC projects coastal warming of 1.2°C under RCP4.5 and 1.3°C under RCP8.5, versus 1.4°C and 1.5°C inland; rainfall is projected to rise slightly or stagnate, with local exceptions. Scenario: RCP4.5 and RCP8.5; 2016–2045. [GNB-E-007]
- Source dated 2022-09-12 reports: In Djobel, 2017 storm surges, heavy rain and high-tide waves destroyed most rice-field infrastructure; ten families became houseless and damaged dikes left some children travelling to school by canoe or swimming. [GNB-E-105]
- Source dated 2022-10-03 reports: WFP reports that floods since 2015 affected over 170,000 people in southern coastal zones and eastern plains and destroyed 8 percent of total rice production. [GNB-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- NC4 identifies vulnerabilities in coastal, Bijagós, inland agricultural, and riverside areas, linked to poverty, natural-resource dependence, and institutional and infrastructure weaknesses. [GNB-E-004]
- The NDC describes the interior as prone to longer droughts, fires, floods, and water shortages; the coastal zone holds about 70% of the population and faces saline intrusion in agricultural fields. Scenario: Qualitative regional risk description; scenario not specified at this locator. [GNB-E-008]
- Source dated 2024-04 reports: Flooding puts national roads N1, N2 and N5 at risk; rainy-season road impassability restricts rural access to markets, water, schools and health care, especially for women. [GNB-E-103]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC proposes 50 km of coastal and riverine anti-salt dykes by 2035, prioritizing high-risk areas with predominantly women residents; mangrove restoration is also targeted. Scenario: Government NDC target for 2035; the passage also proposes mangrove restoration targets. [GNB-E-003]
- Source dated 2022-09-12 reports: Most young people return to Djobel seasonally for field work, leaving dike repair and maintenance to a diminishing number who stay. [GNB-E-108]
- Source dated 2022-10-03 reports: WFP plans community watershed, water, climate-smart agriculture and forest/mangrove management for rural resilience, including women and young farmers. Scenario: WFP country-plan commitments for 2023–2027; no results are reported at this locator. [GNB-E-110]
- Source dated 2022-10-03 reports: WFP reports only 0.9 percent of the population received a social-protection benefit; the 88-island geography further hinders universal service coverage for hard-to-reach communities. [GNB-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- NC4 identifies weak cross-sector coordination, insufficient data and climate finance, and a lack of compensation or insurance mechanisms for climate-related loss and damage. [GNB-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-04 reports: CCDR says political instability challenges implementation and sustainability of comprehensive strategies for climate impacts and development needs. [GNB-E-104]
- The Djobel researchers describe an upland territorial dispute with neighbouring Arame that constrained efforts to establish safer settlement space. [GNB-E-106]
- Source dated 2022-09-12 reports: After years of requests for assistance and the 2017 flood and dike rupture, the authors report no effective technical or financial support for climate adaptation from state, NGOs or donors. [GNB-E-107]
- Source dated 2024-07 reports: INM-GB operated 19 classic weather stations, only 8 operational and none automatic; Bissau Airport, Bolama and Bafatá issued observations every three hours during daytime. [GNB-E-112]
- Source dated 2024-07 reports: The diagnostic rates overall INM-GB capacity at level 1 and says financial constraints and political instability stalled or discontinued some hydromet projects. [GNB-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- In Djobel, flood exposure and pressure to retreat interact with an older upland land dispute that constrains safer settlement; the case is local and multi-causal. [GNB-P-101; GNB-E-102, GNB-E-105, GNB-E-106, GNB-E-107, GNB-E-108]
  - Documented impact: Damage to rice-field/dike systems and pressure to establish safer settlement space.
  - FCV mediator: Longstanding contested upland access, unresolved resource rights, limited support and labour constraints.
  - Possible consequence: The case documents obstacles to safer settlement/adaptation and renewed land tensions; it does not isolate climate change as the origin of the dispute.
