## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2020 reports: The plan's 2015 assessment found over 50% precipitation shortfalls in parts of Ngamiland, Ghanzi and Kgalagadi; other high-risk areas had 25-50% deficits. [BWA-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025 reports: The CCDR reports poorer villages face more excessive or deficient rainfall; poorer smallholders are highly exposed to food insecurity and unstable livelihoods and lack resources to adapt. [BWA-E-101]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Highest drought-vulnerability pockets are Southern District, Kgalagadi and western Ghanzi; Ngamiland Delta subdistrict and Francistown combine high flood risk with many poor households. [BWA-E-102]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Kalahari communal pastoralists are disproportionately affected by land privatization; Ngamiland, Chobe and Central combine high climate impacts with social exclusion, whose reported drivers include process-legitimacy and social-cohesion challenges. [BWA-E-104]
- Source dated 2010-10 reports: The plan says drought concentrates people and wildlife around remaining water points and identifies the Boteti River as an existing Makgadikgadi human-wildlife-conflict focus. [BWA-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-02-03 reports: The Acting DWNP Director said climate-change effects exacerbate human-wildlife conflict; agricultural land demand and settlement encroachment into wildlife habitat were additional drivers. [BWA-E-114]
- Source dated 2025-02-03 reports: The official said crop and livestock losses, injuries and deaths damage livelihoods and can trigger resentment and retaliatory animal killing; he reported eight deaths each year in 2022-2024 and 8, 17 and 14 injuries. [BWA-E-115]
- Source dated 2022-08-25 reports: In Xhumaga beside Makgadikgadi National Park, drought dried the seasonal river; a broken boundary fence and failed backup borehole left people and elephants sharing water, amid crop damage and reported trampling. [BWA-E-116]
- Source dated 2022-08-25 reports: Xhumaga women responsible for household water collection were especially exposed to human-wildlife conflict shifts; households had little capital for climate-adaptive planning. [BWA-E-117]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025 reports: In Ngamiland East, smallholders use hybrid seeds, changed planting and water harvesting; access to credit remains a barrier to investment. [BWA-E-103]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The CCDR says women's water-collection and natural-resource roles heighten climate exposure; Bogakhwe knowledge, drought-tolerant crops and livelihood diversification support adaptation, while female-headed households in Kweneng show resilience and women report less institutional confidence. [BWA-E-105]
- Source dated 2020 reports: Proposed drought measures include integrated water management to mitigate upstream-downstream water-user conflicts and coordinate users, communities and sectors. [BWA-E-110]
- Source dated 2022-08-25 reports: Ramotswa households had more formal employment and livelihood diversity, and greater capacity to withstand drought and flood shocks than Xhumaga, but faced dry taps, billing, maintenance and outage barriers. [BWA-E-118]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2020 reports: In 2020 the plan described drought monitoring and impact assessment as reactive; it noted sparse hydrometeorological stations and missing data, and proposed community-engaged early warning. [BWA-E-108]
- Source dated 2020 reports: The plan describes presidential drought declarations after interministerial assessment and lists possible public works, under-five clinic feeding and farm-subsidy responses. [BWA-E-109]
- Source dated 2010-10 reports: At Gweta, Phuduhudu, Rakops and Mmatshumo, focus groups said low or late compensation left farmers bitter toward wildlife and government; the plan judged direct compensation ineffective as appeasement. [BWA-E-113]
