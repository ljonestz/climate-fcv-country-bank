# Somalia Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Jambaluul/Buula Madino provide named examples of conflict restricting farms and markets during drought, while cooperative attendance data show a programme-participation constraint. [SOM-E-106, SOM-E-107, SOM-P-101]
- Water access conditions uptake in named El Barde communities. Resource committees and women's land access provide reported peace/institutional capacities, with short-duration and non-counterfactual limitations. [SOM-E-108, SOM-E-110, SOM-E-111, SOM-E-112]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate-projection gap: temperature trends are comparatively robust, but precipitation extremes, river flooding, cyclone hazards and local coastal effects have much higher uncertainty. The 2022 climate profile warns that river-flood projections are not reliable enough for long-term planning. []
- Geographic/access gap: insecurity and humanitarian-access restrictions create uneven observation. Current evidence is stronger in monitored/accessible areas; conditions in inaccessible territories cannot safely be inferred from them. IPC explicitly records evidence scarcity due to access limitations. []
- Population differentiation gap: women and children appear in programme and nutrition data, but robust evidence differentiating climate-FCV mechanisms by gender, clan/minority status, disability, age, IDP status and wealth remains incomplete. NDC inclusion commitments should not be mistaken for evidence that implementation currently overcomes these inequalities. (InforMEA) []
- Evaluation/unintended-effect gap: IEG and WFP provide meaningful independent evaluation, but few sources rigorously test whether climate/resilience programming unintentionally altered land competition, exclusion, clan relations, displacement incentives or conflict dynamics. (World Food Programme) []
- Causal uncertainty: the dedicated climate-security studies rely on qualitative process tracing, synthesis and consultation rather than quasi-experimental causal identification. The EIP explicitly says its pathways do not establish a direct causal climate-security relationship. (SIPRI) []
- FCV-to-climate evidence gap: there is good evidence that security considerations affect programme geography and that fiscal/institutional fragility affects sustainability, but less quantitative evidence measuring how much adaptation, recovery or service delivery is lost because of armed conflict or territorial inaccessibility. (UNDP Climate Change Adaptation) []
- NAP verification gap: the official UNFCCC NAP publication record is verified, but the full internal document did not reliably load, so its detailed targets, institutional arrangements and subnational priorities are LOCATOR NOT VERIFIED in this packet. (UNFCCC) []
- Response-outcome gap: preparedness-plan activation, early-warning dissemination and NDC/NAP institutions are verified, but avoided losses, improved long-term adaptive capacity and peace effects are not yet demonstrated merely by activation or institutional creation. []
- Temporal volatility: 2026 displacement, food security, humanitarian financing, conflict-control and access conditions can change within weeks or months. The May IPC and July UNHCR assessments should therefore be treated as dated snapshots. []
- *None are wholly absent, but three are only partially and unevenly covered []
- coping-capacity: household-level positive and negative coping evidence is thin []
- direct-climate-fcv: traceable qualitative evidence exists, but strong causal evidence is absent []
- resilience-peace-capacity: capacities and intended mechanisms are documented, but evaluated peace outcomes are weak. []
- The Somalia HNRP is a dated operational snapshot; access, displacement, needs and conflict conditions may change quickly, and some inaccessible areas are likely under-observed. []
- The BRCiS III baseline PDF was too large for the available reader; claims use the accessible endline evaluation and its stated baseline comparisons, not the unavailable baseline text. []
- The BRCiS results cover 21 programme districts, not all Somalia; the endline is non-counterfactual and includes self-reported outcomes. []
- SIPRI's local conflict-reduction claim relies on an unpublished IOM Galmudug final report; the Hirshabelle evaluation was pending when SIPRI wrote, and project duration was only 18 months. []
- No evidence in these new sources estimates nationally the amount of climate adaptation or resilience programming prevented by insecurity; the HNRP documents humanitarian access restrictions and BRCiS documents specific activity/market participation barriers. []
- Subnational evidence is concentrated in south-central programme areas and 2025 drought-affected regions; current climate-security outcomes for coastal communities, fisheries, minority groups and people in inaccessible territories remain thin. []
- The local Afgoye case is not assigned the report's broader30%market-access statistic. []
- Cooperative meeting barriers apply to 526 members, not all 3,889 surveyed households. []
- The expired 2025 year-end displacement projection was excluded from operational evidence. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Substantial warming is projected. [SOM-E-001]
- Rainfall variability is more certain than rainfall magnitude. [SOM-E-002]
- Coastal sea level rises under both scenarios. [SOM-E-003]
- Q1 2026 drought was severe but spatially uneven. [SOM-E-004]
- In Q1 2026, low river flows and groundwater dependence raised drought-related water and irrigation concerns while mapped river breakages showed concurrent flood exposure. [SOM-E-005]
- IPC's April-June 2026 Burhakaba famine-risk scenario was conditional, not an observed Famine classification. [SOM-E-011]
- In Q2 2026, UNHCR monitoring attributed nearly 90% of recorded displacement to conflict and drought together. [SOM-E-012]
- The January 2026 HNRP describes 2025 northern drought, depleted Puntland berkads and Hirshabelle borehole-repair needs; these are historical operational figures, not uniform or current state conditions. [SOM-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The 2025 Deyr harvest was 75% below the preceding five-year average, while the 2025 Gu harvest was above average. [SOM-E-006]
- During the 2025 Deyr failure, water and pasture shortages drove earlier and longer livestock movements. [SOM-E-007]
- Between September 2025 and January 2026, selected cereal markets recorded sharp price rises alongside drought-linked production losses. [SOM-E-008]
- For April-June 2026, IPC estimated 6.0 million people in Phase 3+ and attributed deterioration to interacting climatic, economic, conflict and assistance factors. [SOM-E-009]
- Child malnutrition reflects multiple interacting pressures. [SOM-E-010]
- In BRCiS programme households, reported drought exposure rose from 15% at 2024 baseline to 67% at 2026 endline; this is not national prevalence. [SOM-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Insecurity directly influenced adaptation-programme geography. [SOM-E-016]
- National policy explicitly frames adaptation as conflict-sensitive. [SOM-E-017]
- Early-warning infrastructure supports anticipatory coping. [SOM-E-021]
- A national crisis-governance mechanism was activated in 2026. [SOM-E-022]
- The January 2026 HNRP describes 2025 checkpoints, hostilities, IEDs and damaged roads restricting humanitarian routes in Shabelle, Hiraan, Bay, Bakool and Lower Juba. [SOM-E-103]
- At BRCiS 2026 endline,52.1%of526surveyed cooperative members reported conflict/insecurity preventing meeting attendance; this is not a share of all households. [SOM-E-107]
- The June 2026 BRCiS evaluation contrasts water-supported gardens in El Barde Town/Emilow with low adoption in water-scarce Higlowayn, Lawareeg and Ayeeyo. [SOM-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Institutional capacity constrains adaptation. [SOM-E-015]
- Rural resilience projects produced substantial outputs but missed some targets. [SOM-E-018]
- Fragility and fiscal limits threaten service sustainability. [SOM-E-019]
- Emergency response performed strongly in some areas but resilience work faced constraints. [SOM-E-020]
- BRCiS's programme-panel resilience index rose from 3.24 to 3.71 between 2024 and 2026, with larger market/ecosystem than leadership gains; no counterfactual impact is estimated. [SOM-E-109]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Drought-driven livelihood and mobility changes can raise local resource pressures, but whether these become conflict depends on governance, historical relations and institutions. [SOM-P-001; SOM-E-004, SOM-E-007, SOM-E-013]
  - Documented impact: Water/pasture depletion, livestock stress and earlier/farther pastoral mobility.
  - FCV mediator: Access rules for water, grazing land and migration routes; land/resource governance; pre-existing grievances.
  - Possible consequence: Where governance and dispute-resolution institutions are weak or contested, increased concentrations of livestock and altered mobility may intensify local resource disputes or pre-existing tensions.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-004'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-007'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-013'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Land tenure ambiguity', 'clan relations', 'political representation', 'armed actors', 'historical grievances', 'land degradation unrelated to climate', 'demographic change and livestock-market conditions']
  - Uncertainty: No national evidence establishes a fixed drought -> conflict relationship; the best direct pathway study is geographically restricted.
- 2026 food insecurity reflects climate-linked production stress interacting with conflict, markets, disease, services and humanitarian financing. [SOM-P-002; SOM-E-006, SOM-E-008, SOM-E-009, SOM-E-010]
  - Documented impact: Severely reduced Deyr crop output and livestock productivity.
  - FCV mediator: Food markets, purchasing power, conflict disruption, currency conditions and humanitarian assistance.
  - Possible consequence: Combined production and market shocks may deepen food insecurity, distress coping and displacement, increasing vulnerability in already fragile communities.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-006'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-006'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-008', 'SOM-E-009'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-009', 'SOM-E-010']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed conflict', 'international commodity/fuel prices', 'Somali-shilling disruption', 'poverty', 'disease', 'aid shortfalls and service deficits']
  - Uncertainty: IPC classifications integrate multiple drivers and do not assign an independent causal percentage to climate.
- Drought and conflict jointly drive displacement; resulting FCV effects depend on urban/service and land-governance capacity. [SOM-P-003; SOM-E-009, SOM-E-012, SOM-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Livelihood deterioration and displacement.
  - FCV mediator: Settlement access, urban service capacity, land/property access, livelihoods and host-IDP relations.
  - Possible consequence: Large displacement flows may increase demand for water, housing, livelihoods and municipal services and can aggravate vulnerability or grievances where delivery and land governance are weak.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-009'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-012'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-019'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-012', 'SOM-E-019']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Direct conflict displacement', 'eviction', 'insecurity', 'poverty and established patterns of urbanization']
  - Uncertainty: UNHCR reports conflict and drought jointly; it does not allocate the “nearly 90%” figure between them or establish the downstream grievance pathway. (UNHCR Data Portal)
- Insecurity can directly narrow where adaptation programmes operate, making FCV an obstacle to climate resilience. [SOM-P-004; SOM-E-015, SOM-E-016, SOM-E-017, SOM-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Need for climate planning and local delivery.
  - FCV mediator: Insecurity, territorial access, institutional fragility and operating costs.
  - Possible consequence: Insecure or inaccessible areas may receive less consistent adaptation programming, monitoring and service delivery, producing geographically unequal resilience.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-017'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-017'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-016', 'SOM-E-019'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-016']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Implementation sequencing', 'donor choices', 'administrative capacity and infrastructure']
  - Uncertainty: The observed UNDP programme-design choice cannot quantify all adaptation excluded by insecurity. (UNDP Climate Change Adaptation)
- Institutional and fiscal fragility can make climate-resilience gains difficult to sustain even where projects work. [SOM-P-005; SOM-E-004, SOM-E-009, SOM-E-018, SOM-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Recurring demand for water, social protection, food-security and recovery services.
  - FCV mediator: Limited fiscal space, contested federal arrangements, institutional capacity and absorptive constraints.
  - Possible consequence: Rapidly expanding crisis/resilience portfolios may overload systems or become fiscally unsustainable even when individual projects deliver useful outputs.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-004', 'SOM-E-009'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-018'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-019'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-019']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Donor dependence', 'programme design', 'macroeconomic conditions and implementation quality']
  - Uncertainty: IEG evidence is specific to the World Bank portfolio rather than all adaptation financing.
- Somalia has meaningful anticipatory and community resilience capacities, but their effect on FCV outcomes remains under-evaluated. [SOM-P-006; SOM-E-004, SOM-E-005, SOM-E-006, SOM-E-009, SOM-E-018, SOM-E-021, SOM-E-022, SOM-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Water, pasture and food-security deterioration.
  - FCV mediator: Information flows, anticipatory financing, government/humanitarian coordination and community resource management.
  - Possible consequence: Earlier and better-coordinated response could protect livelihoods and services and thereby reduce some pressures that otherwise feed displacement or grievance; conflict-prevention effects are not independently demonstrated.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-004', 'SOM-E-005'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-006', 'SOM-E-009'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-021', 'SOM-E-022'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-018']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Assistance volume', 'access', 'conflict dynamics', 'market shocks and household resources']
  - Uncertainty: Early-warning use and preparedness activation are documented more strongly than avoided losses or conflict effects.
- In Jambaluul/Buula Madino, conflict blocked farms and market routes and compounded drought-related livelihood losses. [SOM-P-101; SOM-E-106]
  - Documented impact: Residents described agricultural losses and disrupted livelihoods.
  - FCV mediator: Recurring armed conflict blocked access to farms and roads to markets in Jambaluul and Buula Madino.
  - Possible consequence: Insecurity compounds drought losses and restricts households' ability to sustain food and income access.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SOM-E-106'], 'impact': ['SOM-E-106'], 'mediator': ['SOM-E-106'], 'consequence': ['SOM-E-106']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Input and food prices, aid changes, market demand and other livelihood constraints may also influence losses.']
  - Uncertainty: Named-community qualitative account in a non-counterfactual programme evaluation; no separate climate/conflict effect size or national extrapolation.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Climate stress can amplify local conflict drivers rather than directly cause conflict. [SOM-E-013]
- Climate-security effects are mediated and embedded in pre-existing fragility. [SOM-E-014]
- Climate resilience and peace-supporting capacity exist, but evidence is mainly output-level. [SOM-E-023]
- The June 2026 BRCiS evaluation reports Jambaluul and Buula Madino residents in Afgoye describing conflict blocking farms and market roads, compounding drought losses. [SOM-E-106]
- SIPRI's 2024 report relays IOM's unpublished Galmudug evaluation claim that resource committees reduced conflicts through faster dispute response and broader participation. [SOM-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-05 reports: In Duqaqo, women trained in environmental peacebuilding proposed a drip-irrigated communal farm; after leaders resisted, a peaceful demonstration led to land being designated. [SOM-E-111]
- Source dated 2024-05 reports: SIPRI warns that water projects could overuse groundwater or trigger competition over borehole and reservoir control, especially during drought or dry seasons. [SOM-E-112]
