- The retained national projection source is the 2021 Adaptation Communication using CMIP5/RCP8.5; the newer physical-risk baseline remains incomplete. []
- Geographic/population gaps: farmer-herder and Lake Chad findings are geographically specific; evidence is too thin to build comparable pathways for every geopolitical zone. []
- Evaluation gaps: independent outcome evaluation is available for NEWMAP, but not in this packet for the 2026 anticipatory flood mechanism, recent livestock reforms, community peacebuilding, NAP implementation, or Lagos/coastal adaptation. []
- Unintended effects: IEG's NEWMAP review has a section for unintended impacts but records no assessed unintended impact there; broader maladaptation and distributional effects remain weakly evaluated. (World Bank3) []
- Differentiated vulnerability: the Adaptation Communication itself reports gaps in sex-disaggregated/resource-control data and limited documentation of Indigenous/traditional participation. []
- Causal uncertainty: the climate -> farmer/herder-conflict pathway has meaningful evidence but cannot isolate climate from land governance, agricultural expansion, arms, criminality, politics, identity and historical relations. []
- FCV -> climate/adaptation direction: evidence exists for Lake Chad livelihood restrictions and contemporary humanitarian-access constraints, but nationwide causal evidence on how insecurity changes adaptation outcomes is weak. []
- Food-security attribution: current WFP and IPC evidence strongly documents conflict, access, displacement, disease, weak health systems and funding constraints. Climate should not be inserted as a proximate driver where those current analyses do not establish it. (World Food Programme8) []
- Coastal attribution: sea-level rise is relevant, but specific erosion/flood losses can also depend heavily on drainage, jetties, sand mining, land-use planning, infrastructure and maintenance. []
- Access limitations: official Nigeria-hosted copies of the 2021 NDC and climate-policy PDFs returned gateway errors during verification; the UNFCCC-hosted Adaptation Communication and NDC registry were used instead. []
- No inspected source establishes climate as a cause of armed violence or farmer-herder conflict; UNICEF describes concurrent or interacting pressures. []
- The NiMet 2025 State of the Climate PDF was too large for direct web reading, the NIHSA 2025 flood-outlook link returned 404, and the UN 2024 results PDF was also too large; their search-index text was excluded. []
- The UNICEF report is a historical mid-year 2025 operational snapshot; response outputs and coordination pilots are not independent effectiveness evaluations. []
- New Gumsuri and Northwest findings are a mid-2025 operational snapshot, not current access guidance or evidence that climate caused armed-group activity. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Nigeria's mean annual rainfall is about 3,000 mm/year in the south and 500-1,000 mm/year in the north, where the dry season can last up to seven months. [NGA-E-001]
- The Adaptation Communication reports national average warming of 1.4-1.9°C between 1941 and 2000 and rainfall declines of 2-8 mm across most of Nigeria, with increases of 2-4 mm in some locations. [NGA-E-002]
- Under a CMIP5 RCP8.5 ensemble, mean annual temperature is projected to rise 1.81°C in 2040-2059, with a stated range of 1.25-2.76°C, and about 3°C by the 2090s, range 1.4-4.6°C. Scenario: 2040-2059 and 2090s; CMIP5 RCP8.5 ensemble [NGA-E-003]
- The Adaptation Communication expects northern savanna areas to receive less rainfall, with lower soil moisture, greater drought probability and greater interannual variability as temperatures increase. Scenario: future; horizon not specified in this passage [NGA-E-004]
- Nigeria's coastal change is described as resulting from both natural and anthropogenic processes; anticipated 0.5-1 m sea-level rise would worsen inundation, erosion and storm-surge problems. Scenario: future; 0.5-1 m SLR, horizon unspecified [NGA-E-005]
- NEMA's 2024 EOC dashboard recorded floods affecting 217 LGAs in 34 states: 1,373,699 people affected, 740,734 displaced, 321 deaths and 2,854 injuries. [NGA-E-007]
- A July 2026 NEMA assessment in six Surulere communities found homes and businesses inundated and livelihoods disrupted; it attributed flooding largely to intense rainfall, blocked drains and inadequate drainage maintenance. [NGA-E-008]
- NEMA reported that the September 2024 Maiduguri flood resulted from excess Alau Dam waters and was exacerbated when a spillway collapsed. [NGA-E-009]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Agriculture and hydropower are identified as particularly vulnerable to higher temperatures and more variable rainfall because crop/livestock production and water-flow predictability can be disrupted. Scenario: future, unspecified [NGA-E-006]
- UNHCR's 1 March 2025 snapshot recorded 3,575,114 IDPs: 2,252,348 in the Northeast, 718,126 Northwest and 604,640 North Central; in the Northeast it attributed 92% to insurgency and 6% to communal clashes. [NGA-E-010]
- WFP reported in July 2026 that more than 17 million people across nine conflict-affected northern states were in crisis, emergency or catastrophic hunger categories; Borno alone had more than 3 million acutely food-insecure people. [NGA-E-012]
- IPC estimated nearly 6.4 million children aged 0-59 months would suffer acute malnutrition through September 2026, including 2 million with SAM, plus nearly 786,000 pregnant/breastfeeding women needing treatment, across 204 analysed LGAs. Scenario: Oct 2025-Sep 2026 [NGA-E-014]
- IPC identifies disease, weak health systems, inadequate food consumption and insecurity linked to armed conflict, insurgency and farmer-herder clashes as key current malnutrition drivers in its analysed northern regions. [NGA-E-015]
- Source dated 2025-07-30 reports: The report describes combined conflict and early flooding across BAY; late-June rain submerged over 70% of Gumsuri in Damboa LGA, displacing hundreds. [NGA-E-101]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- WFP reported that attacks and illegal checkpoints were disrupting major supply routes, 15 additional areas had become partially inaccessible, and funding constraints left WFP able to support 740,000 of 6.2 million food-insecure people in three northeastern states. [NGA-E-013]
- Nigeria's NAP Framework is designed to coordinate medium- and long-term adaptation needs and bring together planning across national, state and local government scales. [NGA-E-016]
- In June 2026 Nigeria directed activation of a NEMA “Fusion and Trigger Room” for anticipatory flood action, alongside approved intervention funding and prioritization of high-risk areas. [NGA-E-020]
- IEG found that eight states had established erosion and watershed-management agencies and that the project created 54 community groups for solid-waste management; sustaining benefits depended on maintenance and political commitment. [NGA-E-023]
- Source dated 2025-07-30 reports: Rainy-season onset coincided with armed-group illegal taxes and restricted farmland access; localized floods destroyed homes and caused further displacement. [NGA-E-102]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Nigeria's Adaptation Communication identifies funding and skills constraints, weak coordination, weak target-setting/M&E, and insufficient subnational - especially local-government - and Indigenous participation as barriers to NAP implementation. [NGA-E-017]
- The Adaptation Communication states that some climate-policy documents do not provide sex-disaggregated data and that information on resource access, ownership/control and decision-making was not adequately generated. [NGA-E-018]
- In June 2026 NEMA began reviewing the National Disaster Management Framework and Response Plan to strengthen coordination, early warning/action, integration of climate and insecurity, and inclusion of vulnerable groups. [NGA-E-019]
- The IEG review rated NEWMAP's overall outcome Moderately Satisfactory because, although outputs were achieved, the critical erosion-vulnerability outcome was not independently measured adequately. [NGA-E-021]
- IEG recorded 92 treated gully/erosion complexes, 30,627.96 ha under sustainable landscape-management practices and 65 participatory sub-watershed plans, but these are principally outputs. [NGA-E-022]
- Source dated 2025-07-30 reports: UNICEF and disaster authorities held simulation workshops in Zamfara, Sokoto and Kebbi; a State-led coordination framework was being piloted in Zamfara. [NGA-E-104]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate stress may shape pastoral mobility, but governance and other conflict drivers determine whether mobility becomes violent competition. [NGA-P-001; NGA-E-003, NGA-E-004, NGA-E-024, NGA-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Pressure on pastoral livelihoods/grazing resources and changing mobility patterns.
  - FCV mediator: Movement into farming zones; land/resource access; weak land/livestock governance; small-arms and wider political/social conditions.
  - Possible consequence: Farmer-herder disputes may become more difficult to manage and, where governance and security conditions permit escalation, may become violent.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-003', 'NGA-E-004'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-024'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-024', 'NGA-E-025'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-024']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['agricultural expansion', 'land-tenure changes', 'population pressure', 'weapons', 'banditry/crime', 'political and ethnic mobilization', 'impunity', 'historical farmer-pastoral relations']
  - Uncertainty: Climate is neither necessary nor sufficient for violence; spatial evidence directly cautions against a monotonic climate-conflict relationship. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
- The geographic location of climate stress and the location of violence can diverge because mobility transfers pressures across space. (SIPRI2) [NGA-P-002; NGA-E-024, NGA-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Seasonal or longer-distance pastoral movement toward less climate-exposed areas.
  - FCV mediator: Destination-area competition and institutions governing access.
  - Possible consequence: Conflict risk may rise in places where climate impacts are less acute if those areas receive mobile livelihood groups and resource governance is weak.

  - Alternative explanations: ['pre-existing insecurity', 'land scarcity unrelated to climate', 'commercial farming', 'criminality and weapons']
  - Uncertainty: One spatial study cannot establish a national law-like relationship. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
- FCV can increase climate vulnerability by restricting access to the resources on which coping livelihoods depend. [NGA-P-004; NGA-E-026]
  - Documented impact: Fishers and pastoralists require access to water, fisheries and pasture.
  - FCV mediator: Military/security restrictions, forced evacuation, movement limitations and market closures.
  - Possible consequence: Conflict measures can reduce coping options and increase vulnerability to subsequent climate stress.

  - Alternative explanations: ['wider market collapse', 'poverty and ecological change']
  - Uncertainty: Evidence is narrow and older; national generalization is inappropriate. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
- Conflict demonstrably constrains food-assistance delivery; its effect on climate resilience is plausible but not separately quantified. (World Food Programme8) [NGA-P-005; NGA-E-012, NGA-E-013, NGA-E-014, NGA-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Violence displaces people from farmland and obstructs humanitarian supply routes.
  - FCV mediator: attacks, illegal checkpoints, partial inaccessibility and displacement.
  - Possible consequence: Reduced assistance and market/land access can deepen hunger and reduce households' capacity to withstand additional shocks, including climate shocks.

  - Alternative explanations: ['food prices', 'weak health systems', 'disease', 'funding shortages and household poverty']
  - Uncertainty: Current IPC evidence does not identify climate as a proximate malnutrition driver. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
- Institutional fragmentation is a documented adaptation constraint, but any downstream FCV effect remains unproven. (UNFCCC1) [NGA-P-006; NGA-E-016, NGA-E-017, NGA-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Need for cross-sector, multi-level adaptation.
  - FCV mediator: Weak coordination, limited local-government involvement, inadequate finance/skills and weak M&E.
  - Possible consequence: Adaptation may be delayed, fragmented or uneven, potentially leaving vulnerable/conflict-affected areas with lower resilience.

  - Uncertainty: No evidence reviewed shows these adaptation gaps directly producing violence. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
- Nigeria is strengthening anticipatory flood mechanisms, but their real-world effectiveness remains an evaluation gap. (NEMA14) [NGA-P-007; NGA-E-007, NGA-E-019, NGA-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Nigeria has recently experienced large flood exposure and displacement.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- SIPRI's review finds that changing pastoral mobility is an important mediator between climate-affected livelihood conditions and farmer-herder violence in Nigeria's Middle Belt, with poor/exploitative governance increasing escalation risk. [NGA-E-024]
- SIPRI reports a Nigeria study in which northern areas were more climate-vulnerable but less farmer-herder-conflict-vulnerable, while some adjacent areas were less climate-vulnerable yet more conflict-vulnerable. [NGA-E-025]
- SIPRI's Nigeria/Lake Chad evidence describes armed conflict and military operations restricting fishing, movement, water and pasture access, thereby worsening livelihood insecurity and vulnerability to climate effects. [NGA-E-026]
- SIPRI identifies stronger livelihood resilience and sustainable livelihood adaptation as potential entry points for reducing the likelihood that climate-related livelihood deterioration contributes to violent conflict. [NGA-E-027]
- Source dated 2025-07-30 reports: In BAY, service access declined amid conflict, displacement and climate shocks; flood risk contaminated water and damaged health infrastructure, limiting care and raising disease risks. [NGA-E-103]
