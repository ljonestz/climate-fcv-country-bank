# Nigeria Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Nigeria has substantial and geographically differentiated physical climate risk, but climate-FCV evidence is much narrower than the physical-risk evidence. [NGA-E-001, NGA-E-002, NGA-E-003, NGA-E-004, NGA-E-005, NGA-E-006, NGA-E-024, NGA-E-025, NGA-E-026, NGA-P-001, NGA-P-002, NGA-P-003]
- The best-supported Climate-FCV mechanisms operate through livelihoods, mobility, land/resource governance, displacement and service access - not direct climate -> violence causation. [NGA-E-010, NGA-E-011, NGA-E-012, NGA-E-013, NGA-E-014, NGA-E-015, NGA-E-024, NGA-E-025, NGA-E-026, NGA-E-027, NGA-P-001, NGA-P-002, NGA-P-004, NGA-P-005]
- Nigeria has formal climate and disaster institutions and demonstrable project outputs, but implementation, local-level capacity, M&E and independently verified outcomes remain material constraints. [NGA-E-016, NGA-E-017, NGA-E-018, NGA-E-019, NGA-E-020, NGA-E-021, NGA-E-022, NGA-E-023, NGA-P-006, NGA-P-007, NGA-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: the best page-verified national projection source available here is the 2021 Adaptation Communication using CMIP5/RCP8.5. A World Bank Climate Risk Country Profile surfaced in search but its PDF returned a 403 when opened, while the 2026 CCDR PDF could not be checked page by page. The newer physical-risk baseline therefore remains incomplete. []
- Geographic/population gaps: farmer-herder and Lake Chad findings are geographically specific; evidence is too thin to build comparable pathways for every geopolitical zone. []
- Evaluation gaps: independent outcome evaluation is available for NEWMAP, but not in this packet for the 2026 anticipatory flood mechanism, recent livestock reforms, community peacebuilding, NAP implementation, or Lagos/coastal adaptation. []
- Unintended effects: IEG's NEWMAP review has a section for unintended impacts but records no assessed unintended impact there; broader maladaptation and distributional effects remain weakly evaluated. (World Bank3) []
- Differentiated vulnerability: the Adaptation Communication itself reports gaps in sex-disaggregated/resource-control data and limited documentation of Indigenous/traditional participation. []
- Causal uncertainty: the climate -> farmer/herder-conflict pathway has meaningful evidence but cannot isolate climate from land governance, agricultural expansion, arms, criminality, politics, identity and historical relations. []
- FCV -> climate/adaptation direction: evidence exists for Lake Chad livelihood restrictions and contemporary humanitarian-access constraints, but nationwide causal evidence on how insecurity changes adaptation outcomes is weak. []
- Food-security attribution: current WFP and IPC evidence strongly documents conflict, access, displacement, disease, weak health systems and funding constraints. Climate should not be inserted as a proximate driver where those current analyses do not establish it. (World Food Programme8) []
- Coastal attribution: sea-level rise is relevant, but specific erosion/flood losses can also depend heavily on drainage, jetties, sand mining, land-use planning, infrastructure and maintenance. []
- Access limitations: official Nigeria-hosted copies of the 2021 NDC and climate-policy PDFs returned gateway errors during verification; the UNFCCC-hosted Adaptation Communication and NDC registry were used instead. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Strong baseline north-south rainfall gradient. [NGA-E-001]
- Historical warming and spatially mixed rainfall trend reported. [NGA-E-002]
- Substantial warming projected under RCP8.5. [NGA-E-003]
- Northern drought/variability risk is projected to rise. [NGA-E-004]
- Coastal climate risk compounds existing human-driven pressures. [NGA-E-005]
- 2024 floods produced wide national exposure and displacement. [NGA-E-007]
- Lagos urban flooding was a compound rainfall-infrastructure event. [NGA-E-008]
- Infrastructure failure materially amplified Maiduguri flood impacts. [NGA-E-009]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Climate-sensitive livelihoods and water-dependent production face material exposure. [NGA-E-006]
- Conflict dominates documented displacement in the Northeast. [NGA-E-010]
- Flood exposure overlaps an established FCV-displacement context in the Northeast. [NGA-E-011]
- Current hunger is severe in conflict-affected northern states. [NGA-E-012]
- Children and pregnant/breastfeeding women face highly differentiated nutritional vulnerability. [NGA-E-014]
- Current malnutrition is explicitly multi-causal; climate is not listed as the proximate driver in this analysis. [NGA-E-015]
- Climate-development losses could be large without further adaptation. [NGA-E-028]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Insecurity and funding constraints directly impede humanitarian delivery. [NGA-E-013]
- A multi-level formal adaptation framework exists. [NGA-E-016]
- Anticipatory flood mechanisms have been activated. [NGA-E-020]
- Formal and community resilience institutions were created, with sustainability risks. [NGA-E-023]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Adaptation implementation capacity remains materially constrained. [NGA-E-017]
- Gender-differentiated vulnerability evidence remains incomplete. [NGA-E-018]
- Disaster-management architecture is being updated for compound risks. [NGA-E-019]
- Adaptation project outputs were strong but outcome evidence was insufficient. [NGA-E-021]
- NEWMAP produced substantial physical/planning outputs. [NGA-E-022]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate stress may shape pastoral mobility, but governance and other conflict drivers determine whether mobility becomes violent competition. [NGA-P-001; NGA-E-003, NGA-E-004, NGA-E-024, NGA-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Pressure on pastoral livelihoods/grazing resources and changing mobility patterns.
  - FCV mediator: Movement into farming zones; land/resource access; weak land/livestock governance; small-arms and wider political/social conditions.
  - Possible consequence: Farmer-herder disputes may become more difficult to manage and, where governance and security conditions permit escalation, may become violent.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-003', 'NGA-E-004'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-024'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-024', 'NGA-E-025'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-024']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['agricultural expansion', 'land-tenure changes', 'population pressure', 'weapons', 'banditry/crime', 'political and ethnic mobilization', 'impunity', 'historical farmer-pastoral relations']
  - Uncertainty: Climate is neither necessary nor sufficient for violence; spatial evidence directly cautions against a monotonic climate-conflict relationship.
- The geographic location of climate stress and the location of violence can diverge because mobility transfers pressures across space. (SIPRI2) [NGA-P-002; NGA-E-024, NGA-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Seasonal or longer-distance pastoral movement toward less climate-exposed areas.
  - FCV mediator: Destination-area competition and institutions governing access.
  - Possible consequence: Conflict risk may rise in places where climate impacts are less acute if those areas receive mobile livelihood groups and resource governance is weak.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-024'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-024'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-024'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-024']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['pre-existing insecurity', 'land scarcity unrelated to climate', 'commercial farming', 'criminality and weapons']
  - Uncertainty: One spatial study cannot establish a national law-like relationship.
- Floods can compound humanitarian needs where conflict has already displaced populations and weakened livelihoods. (UNHCR10) [NGA-P-003; NGA-E-010, NGA-E-011]
  - Documented impact: Flood exposure affecting more than 480,000 people in the Northeast in 2024.
  - FCV mediator: Existing violence, insecurity and large-scale displacement.
  - Possible consequence: Flood shocks can impose greater shelter, livelihood, health and service burdens on already displaced/conflict-affected communities.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-010'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-010'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-010'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-010']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['infrastructure quality', 'exposure', 'poverty', 'dam/drainage management']
  - Uncertainty: Co-occurrence establishes compounding vulnerability, not a climate -> conflict causal pathway.
- FCV can increase climate vulnerability by restricting access to the resources on which coping livelihoods depend. [NGA-P-004; NGA-E-026]
  - Documented impact: Fishers and pastoralists require access to water, fisheries and pasture.
  - FCV mediator: Military/security restrictions, forced evacuation, movement limitations and market closures.
  - Possible consequence: Conflict measures can reduce coping options and increase vulnerability to subsequent climate stress.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-026'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-026'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-026'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-026']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['wider market collapse', 'poverty and ecological change']
  - Uncertainty: Evidence is narrow and older; national generalization is inappropriate.
- Conflict demonstrably constrains food-assistance delivery; its effect on climate resilience is plausible but not separately quantified. (World Food Programme8) [NGA-P-005; NGA-E-012, NGA-E-013, NGA-E-014, NGA-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Violence displaces people from farmland and obstructs humanitarian supply routes.
  - FCV mediator: attacks, illegal checkpoints, partial inaccessibility and displacement.
  - Possible consequence: Reduced assistance and market/land access can deepen hunger and reduce households' capacity to withstand additional shocks, including climate shocks.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-012'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-012'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-012'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-012']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['food prices', 'weak health systems', 'disease', 'funding shortages and household poverty']
  - Uncertainty: Current IPC evidence does not identify climate as a proximate malnutrition driver.
- Institutional fragmentation is a documented adaptation constraint, but any downstream FCV effect remains unproven. (UNFCCC1) [NGA-P-006; NGA-E-016, NGA-E-017, NGA-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Need for cross-sector, multi-level adaptation.
  - FCV mediator: Weak coordination, limited local-government involvement, inadequate finance/skills and weak M&E.
  - Possible consequence: Adaptation may be delayed, fragmented or uneven, potentially leaving vulnerable/conflict-affected areas with lower resilience.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-016'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-016'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-016'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-016']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['fiscal constraints', 'wider public-sector capacity', 'macroeconomic conditions and infrastructure deficits']
  - Uncertainty: No evidence reviewed shows these adaptation gaps directly producing violence.
- Nigeria is strengthening anticipatory flood mechanisms, but their real-world effectiveness remains an evaluation gap. (NEMA14) [NGA-P-007; NGA-E-007, NGA-E-019, NGA-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Nigeria has recently experienced large flood exposure and displacement.
  - FCV mediator: preparedness and institutional coordination can affect whether shocks translate into emergency displacement and service overload.
  - Possible consequence: Effective early warning, anticipatory financing and evacuation could reduce losses and displacement.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-007'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-007'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-007'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-007']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['drainage and dam conditions', 'local development patterns', 'household resources']
  - Uncertainty: Current evidence demonstrates activation, not avoided mortality or displacement.
- NEWMAP built tangible resilience capacities, but independent evidence of the intended reduction in erosion vulnerability was incomplete. (World Bank3) [NGA-P-008; NGA-E-021, NGA-E-022, NGA-E-023]
  - Documented impact: NEWMAP treated erosion sites and supported landscape-management planning.
  - FCV mediator: state capacity, local organization, maintenance and political commitment.
  - Possible consequence: Sustained institutions and watershed management may reduce vulnerability and protect livelihoods, helping limit shock-related disruption.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NGA-E-021'], 'impact': ['NGA-E-021'], 'mediator': ['NGA-E-021'], 'consequence': ['NGA-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['other investments', 'site-specific geomorphology and independent environmental changes']
  - Uncertainty: IEG explicitly found insufficient outcome evidence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Mobility and governance mediate climate-farmer/herder conflict links. [NGA-E-024]
- Climate exposure and farmer-herder conflict are not spatially monotonic. [NGA-E-025]
- Conflict can directly reduce livelihood-based adaptive capacity. [NGA-E-026]
- Livelihood resilience is a plausible peace-supporting capacity. [NGA-E-027]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National physical-climate evidence should not be used to erase Nigeria's ecological and FCV differences. The accessible evidence distinguishes arid/semi-arid northern settings, the Middle Belt pastoral-farming interface, the Northeast/Lake Chad conflict and displacement context, and Lagos/coastal exposure. (UNFCCC1) []
- The Middle Belt farmer-herder evidence is subnational, and its mechanisms should not be projected onto the Niger Delta, Lagos, the Southeast or the entire North. Likewise, the 2026 Surulere flood assessment is evidence about six Lagos communities, not Nigerian urban flooding in general. []
- The Northeast combines insurgency-related displacement, humanitarian-access constraints, food insecurity and flood exposure. Northwest and North Central displacement have distinct conflict patterns and should not be subsumed under the Northeast insurgency model. []
- The Niger Delta/coastal zone is physically exposed to sea-level rise, inundation and erosion, but oil spills, pollution, gas flaring, sand mining and infrastructure-driven coastal change must remain distinct unless a source establishes a climate interaction. The Adaptation Communication itself recognizes several of these anthropogenic non-climate contributors. (UNFCCC1) []
- Important underrepresented areas include much of the Southeast, Northwest beyond displacement aggregates, smaller coastal settlements outside Lagos/Niger Delta, and state/local contexts without recent humanitarian or project evidence. []
- *Relatively well covered: agriculture and pastoralism; water/watersheds; disaster management; food/nutrition; humanitarian logistics; displacement; urban flooding; climate governance. []
- *Thinner: fisheries, energy/hydropower, coastal infrastructure performance, health beyond nutrition, land-tenure administration, informal/customary conflict resolution, market-price transmission, Niger Delta climate-pollution interaction, and evaluated urban adaptation. []
