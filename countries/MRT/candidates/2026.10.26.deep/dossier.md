## Climate pressures and exposure

- Report estimates 1.5°C warming since the 1950s and rainfall decline from 85 mm (1950) to 63 mm (2020); June 2020-November 2022 drought affected up to one-third of people and worsened food insecurity. [MRT-E-001]
- The report states that floods occur nearly once every two years, have destroyed villages and livestock, and on several occasions disrupted at least 10,000 livelihoods and caused about 10 deaths. [MRT-E-002]
- Source dated 2024 reports: UNICEF estimates about half of Hodh El Chargui communities face severe or high drought risk. [MRT-E-107]
- Source dated 2025-09-09 reports: By end-August 2025, rainfall deficits persisted in Hodh Ech Chargui and Hodh El Gharbi; dry spells and floods caused localized crop losses. [MRT-E-113]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- World Bank modeling in the report estimates climate-related GDP losses of 9.3% by 2050 under its dry/pessimistic scenario without adaptation, compared with 5.7% with partial adaptation. [MRT-E-003]
- The G5 CCDR projects that under medium growth and its dry/pessimistic climate scenario without adaptation, Mauritania’s poverty rate in 2050 would be 0.2 percentage points above the no-climate baseline. [MRT-E-004]
- Source dated 2024-09-26 reports: The study identifies poverty, food insecurity, gender inequality, limited services and youth employment as peacebuilding pressures. [MRT-E-101]
- Source dated 2024 reports: UNICEF rates child climate exposure and vulnerability high or very high in 11 wilayas; six highest-risk are Hodh El Chargui, Gorgol, Hodh El Gharbi, Guidimakha, Tagant and Assaba; poorest, least-served children face greatest risks. [MRT-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-08-02 reports: As of 30 June 2024, Mbera hosted 108,059 registered refugees; women and children were 83% of camp registrants. [MRT-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-08-02 reports: UNHCR reports Mali's humanitarian-security crisis drove arrivals into Hodh Chargui, with camp saturation sending many to neighboring villages and straining services and natural resources in a poor, climate-shock-vulnerable region. [MRT-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-09-09 reports: For June-August 2025, FAO reports about 44,000 refugees in Hodh Ech Chargui faced acute food insecurity, including over 6,000 in Phase 4; 17% of resident Mauritanians needed aid. [MRT-E-114]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Around Mbera, climate-related resource pressure can interact with longstanding grievances and grazing/water access; tensions are conditional, not inevitable. [MRT-P-101; MRT-E-102, MRT-E-103, MRT-E-105]
  - Documented impact: DPPA describes altered transhumance and pressure around refugee-hosting areas outside Mbera.
  - FCV mediator: Longstanding resentments, grazing competition and unequal representation/access among arrival cohorts.
  - Possible consequence: Potential host–refugee or inter-herder tensions; no climate-attributable violence incidence is estimated.

  - Uncertainty: Source-stated conditional pathway in an executive summary; underlying incident evidence not reviewed, not proof that climate caused armed violence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2024-09-26 reports: The study links changing rainfall and altered traditional transhumance to farmer-herder and inter-herder conflict. [MRT-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-09-26 reports: Women and youth face marginalization and security risks amid livelihood disruption and displacement. [MRT-E-104]
- Source dated 2024 reports: UNICEF links drought and water scarcity to rainfed-crop and livestock losses, child poverty and malnutrition, rural out-migration, and possible abuse or exploitation when families send children to cities. [MRT-E-108]
