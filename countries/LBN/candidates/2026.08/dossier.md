# Lebanon Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Lebanon has material physical climate risks, particularly around heat, declining precipitation, drought and water security, but vulnerability is amplified by service, fiscal and institutional weaknesses rather than determined by climate alone. [LBN-E-001, LBN-E-002, LBN-E-003, LBN-E-004, LBN-E-005, LBN-E-006, LBN-E-007, LBN-E-012, LBN-E-013, LBN-E-014, LBN-E-015, LBN-E-016, LBN-E-017, LBN-P-001, LBN-P-002, LBN-P-003]
- The strongest available Climate-FCV evidence supports mediated and conditional pathways through water services, livelihoods, affordability, governance and pre-existing social relations; it does not demonstrate a general climate -> conflict causal effect. [LBN-E-013, LBN-E-014, LBN-E-015, LBN-E-016, LBN-E-017, LBN-E-018, LBN-E-019, LBN-E-020, LBN-P-001, LBN-P-002, LBN-P-005, LBN-P-006]
- FCV conditions clearly constrain resilience: recent conflict damage, displacement, economic collapse and weak public finances increase reconstruction burdens and impede climate-sensitive service delivery, while evaluated programme evidence shows both local peace-supporting capacity and risks from unequal assistance allocation. [LBN-E-003, LBN-E-007, LBN-E-008, LBN-E-009, LBN-E-010, LBN-E-011, LBN-E-018, LBN-E-021, LBN-E-022, LBN-P-003, LBN-P-004, LBN-P-005, LBN-P-007]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: The verified CCDR release provides major water and macroeconomic projections but not the underlying emissions scenario/model detail. More granular verified temperature, precipitation, wildfire, flood and sea-level projections are needed before populating a scenario-specific physical-risk layer. []
- Geographic/population differentiation: There is good urban/rural and some nationality/governorate differentiation, but much less traceable evidence for gender, disability, age, migrant workers, Palestine refugees, informal-settlement typologies and specific coastal/mountain livelihood groups. []
- Adaptation evaluation: The 2025 NAP, NDC 3.0 and current water/reconstruction operations are too recent - or insufficiently evaluated - to support strong effectiveness claims. Plans, financing and beneficiary targets remain intended capacity, not demonstrated outcomes. []
- Unintended effects: LHSP provides strong evidence that geographically uneven infrastructure assistance can generate grievances, but equivalent monitoring of climate/adaptation projects is lacking. Whether new resilience investments produce exclusion, elite capture, maladaptation or conflict-sensitive benefits remains an open question. []
- Causal uncertainty: The national climate-FCV evidence is dominated by perceptions and expert/programme synthesis. The reviewed sources do not identify an independent causal effect of temperature, rainfall or drought on violent events. []
- FCV-to-climate quantification: The qualitative/recovery evidence strongly supports reduced fiscal, infrastructure and institutional capacity after crisis/conflict, but few sources quantify how much this increases mortality, economic loss or service disruption during a given climate hazard. []
- Water-conflict generalization: The Bcharreh-Denniyeh/Qurnat al-Sawda case contains important governance lessons but cannot be generalized to all Lebanese water disputes; historical claims, property rights and local politics require separate tracing. []
- Food-security attribution: Recent acute food insecurity is documented, but the cited evidence identifies conflict, displacement and reduced assistance as principal immediate drivers. A climate share should not be inferred without agricultural/climate attribution evidence. []
- Current conflict/access conditions: Displacement and service conditions are fast-changing. Operational figures from June-July 2026 should be date-stamped and refreshed before screening a specific project location. (UNHCR Data Portal) []
- Source access limitation: The NDC 3.0 and NAP publication pages and download links were verified, but the detailed underlying PDFs were not independently parsed in this session. Detailed page-level policy claims therefore remain outside this packet. []
- *None are wholly absent, but four remain only partially covered: coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, response-performance, direct-climate-fcv, and resilience-peace-capacity. In particular, response-performance evidence is not climate-specific, and direct-climate-fcv evidence does not robustly establish causality. []

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-related water stress may aggravate tension through service failure and perceived inequity, but scarcity alone is not demonstrated to cause conflict. (World Bank) [LBN-P-001; LBN-E-001, LBN-E-006, LBN-E-013, LBN-E-014, LBN-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Water shortage, unreliable public supply and costly private alternatives.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal access, affordability, service failure, perceived unfairness and pre-existing group/resource competition.
  - Possible consequence: Where governance and distribution are already contested, additional water stress could worsen grievances or intergroup tensions.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-001'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-006', 'LBN-E-013', 'LBN-E-014'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-015'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-014']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Structural water-management deficits', 'seasonal rainfall concentration', 'insufficient storage', 'electricity failures', 'informal water markets', 'poverty', 'conflict damage and allocation decisions']
  - Uncertainty: No source reviewed estimates an independent causal effect of climate change on violent conflict.
- Climate-sensitive livelihood losses may compound job/resource competition, but political economy and labour-market conditions remain independent drivers. [LBN-P-002; LBN-E-013, LBN-E-015, LBN-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Self-reported livelihood/financial losses, especially among rural and economically precarious respondents.
  - FCV mediator: Informal employment dependence, reduced household income, competition over jobs/resources and existing economic crisis.
  - Possible consequence: Climate-sensitive livelihood shocks could intensify competition and social tension where labour markets and household finances are already stressed.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-013'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-013'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-013'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Inflation', 'economic collapse', 'legal/work restrictions', 'aid reductions', 'political tensions and conflict']
  - Uncertainty: Survey respondents attribute losses to climate; causal attribution was not independently tested.
- The strongest reverse pathway is that conflict and economic collapse weaken infrastructure and institutions needed to manage climate risk. (World Bank) [LBN-P-003; LBN-E-003, LBN-E-007, LBN-E-008, LBN-E-009]
  - Documented impact: Crisis-constrained public finances and conflict-damaged lifeline infrastructure.
  - FCV mediator: Reduced fiscal space, maintenance failures, implementation constraints and reconstruction burdens.
  - Possible consequence: Climate hazards may produce larger service and welfare impacts because institutions and infrastructure have less capacity to absorb shocks.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-003'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-003'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-003'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-003']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Long-standing infrastructure underinvestment and governance weaknesses predate recent hostilities']
  - Uncertainty: Sources demonstrate damage and capacity constraints more directly than they measure resulting changes in climate vulnerability.
- Climate shocks could compound food insecurity, but the recent acute burden cannot be credibly described as climate-driven. [LBN-P-004; LBN-E-011, LBN-E-013, LBN-E-020]
  - Documented impact: High acute food insecurity in 2025.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict, displacement, loss of purchasing power and reduced humanitarian assistance.
  - Possible consequence: Additional climate-related production or income shocks could compound food insecurity and competition for scarce assistance or employment.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-011'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-011'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-011'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-011']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['The cited 2025 evidence explicitly identifies conflict', 'displacement and reduced assistance - not climate - as the main immediate food-insecurity drivers']
  - Uncertainty: No reviewed source apportions the 2025 IPC burden quantitatively to climate.
- WASH projects can potentially serve as peace-supporting adaptation when infrastructure and dialogue are deliberately combined. [LBN-P-005; LBN-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Water access can become a focus of local stress and mistrust.
  - FCV mediator: Service-provider accountability, communication and intercommunity dialogue.
  - Possible consequence: Well-designed WASH investment may reduce tension while strengthening capacity to manage climate-related water stress.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-018'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-018'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-018'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-018']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Dialogue or wider political changes may have contributed independently']
  - Uncertainty: No counterfactual evaluation; the observed local experience does not establish general effectiveness.
- Climate stress may amplify contested water governance, but the causal core of documented local disputes is institutional and political as well as environmental. [LBN-P-006; LBN-E-006, LBN-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Local competition over springs/groundwater and high reliance on informal/private provision.
  - FCV mediator: Ambiguous land/water rights, weak enforcement, fragmented governance and perceived unequal allocation.
  - Possible consequence: Under worsening scarcity, unresolved institutional disputes could become harder to mediate and reduce cooperation needed for adaptation.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-006'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-006'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-006'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-006']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Historical property disputes', 'sectarian politics and governance failures can generate conflict independent of climatic change']
  - Uncertainty: Climate's contribution to the specific Bcharreh-Denniyeh conflict is not isolated.
- Adaptation finance itself can become conflict-sensitive when allocation is unequal; evaluated Lebanese development experience provides a concrete warning. [LBN-P-007; LBN-E-021, LBN-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Independent LHSP evaluation found that uneven infrastructure/solar support sometimes created intermunicipal grievances.
  - FCV mediator: Beneficiary selection, geographic targeting, transparency and perceived distributive fairness.
  - Possible consequence: Climate/adaptation investments could unintentionally intensify local grievances if benefits are visibly unequal and conflict sensitivity is weak.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBN-E-021'], 'impact': ['LBN-E-021'], 'mediator': ['LBN-E-021'], 'consequence': ['LBN-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Grievances may reflect pre-existing political', 'municipal or socioeconomic competition']
  - Uncertainty: LHSP was not a climate-adaptation programme, so transfer to climate programming is an explicitly cautious inference.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Climate-service/tension linkage is widely perceived. [LBN-E-014]
- Existing tensions mediate climate-risk perceptions. [LBN-E-015]
- Water projects may support local cohesion. [LBN-E-018]
- Local water conflict is governance-mediated, not climate-determined. [LBN-E-019]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- *Beirut and Mount Lebanon: Wave 22 reports high water-shortage exposure and very poor perceived water-service quality, while the SGBWSP specifically targets Greater Beirut/Mount Lebanon. These findings support an urban service-stress lens, not a national generalization. []
- *Bekaa and Akkar: Evidence highlights rural livelihood exposure, local water/service pressures, aid/labour tension mechanisms and evaluated examples of intermunicipal allocation grievances. These areas merit separate treatment from Beirut's infrastructure/service profile. []
- *North Lebanon/Bcharreh-Denniyeh: UNICEF's WASH/social-cohesion example and ALEF's Qurnat al-Sawda case are explicitly local. Neither supports a conclusion that water scarcity generally causes violence throughout Lebanon. []
- *South and Nabatiyeh: Recent direct conflict damage and access constraints are especially important. Wave 22 itself notes remote interviewing in Bint Jbeil and Marjeyoun because of access/safety constraints; the RDNA identifies the South/Nabatiyeh among the hardest-hit areas. Conflict damage must remain distinct from climate hazards even where both affect the same assets or communities. []
- *Unrepresented/thin areas and populations: Detailed evidence is comparatively thin for coastal communities, mountain wildfire exposure, people with disabilities, migrant workers, gender-specific adaptation barriers, Palestine refugee camps, and post-return conditions of IDPs at fine geographic scale. []
- *Strongest coverage: water resources and service delivery; electricity/energy; municipal/urban infrastructure; displacement/humanitarian systems; food security/agriculture; national adaptation governance. []
- *Moderate coverage: rural livelihoods, groundwater governance, transport/reconstruction, social cohesion and local institutions. []
- *Thin/absent: forests/wildfire outcomes, fisheries/coastal livelihoods, sea-level/coastal adaptation performance, waste-sector climate outcomes, public-health climate impacts, tourism adaptation performance and independently evaluated nature-based adaptation. []
