## Climate pressures and exposure

- The 2020 communication's satellite-based assessment for 1984–2017 describes differentiated drought-related crop damage in the Ararat Valley, southwestern Gegharkunik, Kotayk and Gegharkunik. Its damage-frequency categories are not reused as current annual probabilities. [ARM-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-11 reports: Flood losses relative to regional GDP are highest in Vayots Dzor, Tavush, Lori and Gegharkunik; the report also locates severe irrigation-water and land-degradation risks in high-poverty marzes. [ARM-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-11 reports: The CCDR links spatial disparities with fragility risks, notes high rural and border-region poverty, and says conflict with Azerbaijan raises risks across the economy; it does not identify climate change as causing conflict. [ARM-E-102]
- Source dated 2020 reports: By 2070, projected wheat yields fall about 19–20% in Ararat, Kotayk and Gegharkunik, potato yields 21% in Shirak, while wheat yields rise about 8% in Lori. Scenario: The yield tables describe climate-change projections without agrotechnical measures but do not identify the emissions pathway. [ARM-E-104]
- Source dated 2021-05-13 reports: The NAP says social norms can restrict women’s access to climate information, resources and opportunities, and calls for gender-responsive participation in adaptation decisions. [ARM-E-108]
- Source dated 2025-12-19 reports: The framework includes possible displacement among the human impacts of climate risks and places climate adaptation within a wider resilience and social-cohesion agenda. Scenario: UNSDCF 2026–2030 planning horizon; no climate scenario or displacement estimate is stated. [ARM-E-109]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Lori and Tavush hosted 7% of Nagorno-Karabakh refugees before the May 2024 floods; refugees and host households in Tashir and Alaverdi faced flood-related displacement and damage to homes, food supplies and essential services. [ARM-E-111]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Field discussions in Lori and Tavush found women-headed households more likely to miss flood compensation when homes or livelihood assets lacked official property registration. [ARM-E-112]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2021-05-13 reports: The 2021–2025 action list scheduled adaptation plans for pilot marzes Shirak and Tavush, then eight more marzes, integrating climate adaptation into regional development planning. [ARM-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2020 reports: The communication records a crop-insurance pilot launched in 2018: potatoes and cereals against hail and frost in Shirak, and fruit and grapes in Armavir and Ararat. [ARM-E-105]
- Source dated 2021-05-13 reports: The NAP identifies weak inter-agency coordination and information flows, poor climate integration in public bodies, and gaps in locally relevant risk data and local adaptation finance. [ARM-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-10 reports: Flooding damaged eight hydrological stations in the Debed and Aghstev basins, impairing water monitoring; the PDNA calls for modern flood forecasting, risk modeling and early warning, especially in remote areas. [ARM-E-113]

## Bibliography and review decision

- World Bank Group. Country Climate and Development Report: Armenia. 2024-11 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099110524115029619/pdf/P18076418d641b0e91954b113281d9a1165.pdf [ARM-SRC-101] Methodology: Opened the full 89-page CCDR; it combines spatial risk assessment, flood maps, household data and economic scenarios. Limitations: Mapped losses and projections are model-based; the report notes asset valuation uncertainty for flood-loss estimates.
- Ministry of Environment of the Republic of Armenia, with UNDP support. Fourth National Communication on Climate Change under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. 2020 (date basis: publication). https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2024-09/nc4_armenia_.pdf [ARM-SRC-102] Methodology: Opened the full government communication and reviewed its climate, agriculture and adaptation sections. Limitations: The yield tables do not identify an emissions pathway; reported programs are historical and not independently evaluated.
- Government of the Republic of Armenia, Ministry of Environment. National Action Program of Adaptation to Climate Change and the List of Measures for 2021–2025. 2021-05-13 (date basis: publication). https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2024-09/nap_publication_eng_web.pdf [ARM-SRC-103] Methodology: Opened the government decree and annex listing adaptation measures, responsible agencies, timelines and indicative financing. Limitations: The program states planned actions, not verified implementation or achieved outcomes; its 2021–2025 period has ended.
- Government of the Republic of Armenia and United Nations Country Team. United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework 2026–2030. 2025-12-19 (date basis: document-version). https://armenia.un.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/Armenia%20-%20UN%20Sustainable%20Development%20Cooperation%20Framework%202026%20-%202030%20%282%29.pdf [ARM-SRC-104] Methodology: Opened the signed framework and reviewed its national rationale and planned green-transition and disaster-risk measures. Limitations: This is a planning framework, not an evaluation; it does not name pilot municipalities or estimate climate-related displacement.
- Government of Armenia, United Nations and European Union. Armenia Floods: May 2024 Post-Disaster Needs Assessment. 2024-10 (date basis: document-version). https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2024-11/pdna_armenia_eng_final.pdf [ARM-SRC-105] Methodology: Opened the full Government–UN–EU assessment, including human impacts, gender, housing and disaster-risk sections. Limitations: Event assessment; no attribution to anthropogenic climate change or evaluation of long-term recovery.
