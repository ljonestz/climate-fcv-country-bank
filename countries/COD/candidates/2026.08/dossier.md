- The Democratic Republic of the Congo packet documents climate pressures, FCV mediators, and resilience constraints through reviewed public sources. [COD-E-001, COD-E-002, COD-E-003, COD-P-001, COD-P-002]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: National warming projections are usable, but precipitation projections span opposing outcomes, observational networks are sparse and the NAP explicitly calls for newer and better-targeted studies. []
- Geographic coverage: Direct Climate-FCV research is disproportionately concentrated in North/South Kivu and a small set of localities such as the Ruzizi Plain. []
- Population differentiation: Current evidence on women is stronger than evidence differentiated simultaneously by age, disability, displacement status, ethnicity, livelihood and territorial control; older NAP indicators also need updating. []
- Evaluation and unintended effects: Beyond the UNDP ICPE and project evaluations it synthesizes, there is little independent evidence on sustained adaptation outcomes, distributional impacts, conflict sensitivity or unintended effects. []
- Causal uncertainty: Climate-to-FCV evidence largely consists of qualitative mechanisms, local observations, expert synthesis and triangulation; causal effect sizes are not established. []
- FCV-to-climate evidence: The reverse pathway is qualitatively credible - access restrictions, displacement, governance breakdown and conflict financing weaken adaptation - but quantified effects on resilience/adaptation performance remain scarce. (SIPRI2) []
- Institutional depth: National policies and selected provincial/community programmes are documented, but territorial, customary and municipal capacity across 26 provinces is not systematically mapped. []
- Hydropower and water systems: More evidence is needed on climate impacts on hydropower reliability, basin operations and the interaction between hydrology, water allocation and infrastructure management. []
- Mining/climate interaction: Mining governance and conflict financing are documented, but environmental pollution and deforestation must remain distinct from climate impacts unless a specific causal climate mechanism is established. []
- Access/unverified underlying sources: All 12 registered sources were publicly accessible, but many synthesis documents cite underlying studies or datasets that were not independently promoted into this packet. Claims were therefore bounded to what the opened registered source itself supports rather than silently treating every secondary reference as independently verified. []
- *None entirely absent, but four remain only partially covered: coping-capacity, adaptive-capacity, response-performance, and resilience-peace-capacity. Direct-climate-fcv evidence is also geographically incomplete even though traceable support exists. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- RCP4.5 produces a clear long-run warming signal. [COD-E-001]
- Higher-emissions scenario implies substantially greater warming. [COD-E-002]
- National precipitation direction is uncertain. [COD-E-003]
- Recent floods caused multi-sector, multi-province losses. [COD-E-005]
- Conflict displacement directly reduces livelihood and market access. [COD-E-009]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Climate vulnerability spans major livelihood and service sectors. [COD-E-006]
- Acute food insecurity remains extremely large. [COD-E-008]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Gendered institutions constrain adaptation options. [COD-E-007]
- NDC adaptation ambition carries very large financing requirements. [COD-E-016]
- Financing and implementation gaps constrain national climate ambition. [COD-E-017]
- Customary/local institutions can be resilience assets but are uneven. [COD-E-023]
- Delivery shortfalls constrain both emergency coping and recovery. [COD-E-024]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Sparse observations limit confidence in national climate baselines. [COD-E-004]
- Eastern FCV conditions remained an active delivery constraint in mid-2026. [COD-E-010]
- DRC's revised 2030 mitigation target is mostly conditional. [COD-E-015]
- National climate-policy enabling systems improved. [COD-E-018]
- Stronger national frameworks have not consistently translated into local implementation. [COD-E-019]
- Evaluated adaptation assets showed sustainability and operationalization failures. [COD-E-020]
- New urban flood programme was still at an early output stage in 2025. [COD-E-021]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate-related livelihood stress may increase pressure on already contested land and water, but political and governance conditions determine whether tension escalates. [COD-P-001; COD-E-001, COD-E-003, COD-E-006, COD-E-011, COD-E-012]
  - Documented impact: Reduced or disrupted agricultural production and climate-sensitive livelihood stress.
  - FCV mediator: Existing land disputes, unequal access, weak resource governance and lack of alternative livelihoods.
  - Possible consequence: Livelihood pressure may intensify existing disputes or grievances over land and water in parts of eastern DRC.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Longstanding land tenure disputes', 'ethnic and political mobilization', 'armed groups', 'patronage', 'demographic pressure and market conditions independently generate conflict']
  - Uncertainty: SIPRI explicitly states that specific livelihood-to-conflict mechanisms remain less clear than the climate-to-livelihood link. (SIPRI2)
- In the Ruzizi Plain, climate-sensitive changes in pasture and livestock movement appear to interact with - not originate independently from - existing farmer-pastoralist conflict. [COD-P-002; COD-E-012, COD-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Altered livestock mobility and competition for water/pasture.
  - FCV mediator: Pre-existing farmer-pastoralist disputes, identity conflict and land-access arrangements.
  - Possible consequence: Greater tension, livestock theft or violence has been reported locally where altered movement intersects with existing disputes.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-012'], 'impact': ['COD-E-012'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-012'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-012']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Historical land and identity disputes', 'political competition and livestock governance']
  - Uncertainty: Local, largely qualitative evidence; it must not be generalized across South Kivu or nationally.
- Flood displacement can add pressure to fragile local service/resource systems, potentially worsening grievances where recovery is weak. [COD-P-003; COD-E-005, COD-E-011]
  - Documented impact: Housing/livelihood loss and displacement.
  - FCV mediator: Overcrowded sites, inadequate basic services, resource scarcity and weak post-disaster recovery.
  - Possible consequence: Displacement may create or intensify localized tensions between displaced and host communities where services/resources are already scarce.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-005'], 'impact': ['COD-E-005'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-005'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-005']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Conflict displacement is far larger in many eastern areas and may independently overwhelm services', 'poor planning and infrastructure condition flood losses']
  - Uncertainty: Disaster-displacement figures should not automatically be interpreted as anthropogenic-climate displacement.
- Insecurity can convert otherwise manageable climate shocks into deeper crises by removing access to land, markets, services and recovery assistance. (SIPRI2) [COD-P-004; COD-E-009, COD-E-010, COD-E-013, COD-E-024]
  - Documented impact: Households require access to farmland, markets, services and humanitarian support to cope.
  - FCV mediator: Armed-group control, insecurity, road blockage, displacement and restricted territorial access.
  - Possible consequence: Communities exposed to conflict may experience greater livelihood loss, food insecurity and slower recovery when climate shocks occur.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-009'], 'impact': ['COD-E-009'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-009'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-009']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Poverty', 'infrastructure deficits and commodity-price shocks can also reduce coping capacity outside active conflict']
  - Uncertainty: Effects on quantified adaptation outcomes have rarely been evaluated.
- FCV-linked extraction can undermine institutions needed for climate mitigation and adaptation, while remaining an independent environmental and political-economy driver. (NUPI3) [COD-P-005; COD-E-014, COD-E-017, COD-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Armed actors and political-economic networks exploit forests/minerals and damage environmental governance.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict financing, corruption, weak state authority and elite competition.
  - Possible consequence: Forest-management and resilience objectives may be harder to implement, increasing environmental vulnerability and weakening mitigation capacity.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-014'], 'impact': ['COD-E-014'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-014'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-014']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Agricultural expansion', 'charcoal dependence', 'commercial logging', 'regulatory design and global commodity demand also drive environmental loss']
  - Uncertainty: Environmental degradation should not be relabelled as climate damage; specific climate consequences require separate evidence.
- Flood losses place additional pressure on weak urban service systems, but any transition from service stress to FCV outcomes remains unproven. (World Bank5) [COD-P-006; COD-E-005, COD-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Damage to roads, water, electricity, housing and agricultural assets.
  - FCV mediator: Poverty, informal settlement, weak drainage/DRM capacity, fiscal limitations and weak coordination.
  - Possible consequence: Repeated losses could deepen service deficits, recovery burdens and public grievances; violence is not demonstrated by the evidence reviewed.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-005'], 'impact': ['COD-E-005'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-005'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-005']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Settlement patterns', 'construction quality', 'drainage maintenance and urban governance']
  - Uncertainty: No source reviewed demonstrates that urban floods cause violence or conflict.
- Policy ambition exceeds demonstrated delivery capacity, leaving a gap between adaptation plans and sustained resilience outcomes. (IMF7) [COD-P-007; COD-E-016, COD-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Large planned adaptation requirements and implementation needs.
  - FCV mediator: Fiscal pressure, weak coordination, governance limitations and - where relevant - conflict-related access constraints.
  - Possible consequence: Delayed, incomplete or unsustained adaptation may leave households and infrastructure exposed to future shocks.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-016'], 'impact': ['COD-E-016'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-016'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-016']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Programme design', 'procurement', 'technical standards', 'COVID-19 and maintenance failures independently affect performance']
  - Uncertainty: National adaptation effectiveness has not been comprehensively evaluated.
- Local social institutions provide credible resilience and peace-supporting capacity, but their impact has not been rigorously quantified. (SIPRI2) [COD-P-008; COD-E-022, COD-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Communities face livelihood, displacement and resource-management challenges.
  - FCV mediator: Social cohesion, inclusion, local legitimacy and collective action.
  - Possible consequence: Well-supported women-, youth- and community-led initiatives could reduce vulnerability and strengthen peaceful problem-solving, although programme-level effects on violence are not established.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['COD-E-022'], 'impact': ['COD-E-022'], 'mediator': ['COD-E-022'], 'consequence': ['COD-E-022']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Community organizations may have limited resources', 'contested legitimacy or unequal participation']
  - Uncertainty: No reviewed evaluation measures whether these initiatives reduce conflict incidence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Resource governance mediates climate-related conflict risk. [COD-E-011]
- Climate stress may intensify existing Ruzizi resource conflicts through mobility. [COD-E-012]
- Conflict can erode adaptive capacity by blocking land, markets and services. [COD-E-013]
- FCV-related extraction can undermine environmental governance and resilience. [COD-E-014]
- Local women and youth provide climate/peace resilience capacity. [COD-E-022]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National climate-policy and projection sources provide broad country coverage, but the climate-security evidence is strongly concentrated in North Kivu and South Kivu, with the Ruzizi Plain providing the clearest local farmer-pastoralist pathway. Those findings should not be generalized to western, central or southern DRC. (SIPRI2) []
- *Eastern DRC requires internal differentiation. North/South Kivu evidence emphasizes armed groups, land disputes, displacement, resource exploitation and access constraints; Ituri and Tanganyika appear prominently in current humanitarian reporting but have less direct climate-security research in the registered sources. (World Food Programme1) []
- *Kongo Central/Muanda has distinct coastal-erosion and adaptation-programme evidence from UNDP evaluation. Findings concerning a retaining wall, early warning and livelihood interventions are programme/local findings and must not be extrapolated nationally. []
- *Urban flood risk is nationally important but city-specific exposure depends on drainage, settlement form, infrastructure quality and planning. The World Bank project concerns selected cities and should not be interpreted as evidence of identical flood conditions across the country's urban system. (World Bank5) []
- Important underrepresented areas include much of the central basin, northern and western provinces, many mining corridors outside the Kivus, rural riverine populations, people in inaccessible conflict-controlled territories, and populations for whom current gender/disability/age-disaggregated climate-risk data are unavailable. []
- Agriculture, land, food security, forests, water, urban infrastructure, transport and mining/resource governance receive meaningful coverage. Climate-sensitive livelihood pathways are especially well represented for eastern farming and pastoral settings. (SIPRI2) []
- Hydropower is covered more thinly: IMF analysis identifies potential drought-related electricity risks for mining and the NAP classifies energy as climate-sensitive, but this packet does not contain a detailed basin/hydropower operational study. (IMF7) Health and WASH appear through vulnerability, flood damage and humanitarian pressure, but evaluated climate-resilient health/WASH delivery is sparse. []
