# Democratic Republic of the Congo Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- The May 2025 Kasaba flood and South Kivu conflict displacement illustrate different local vulnerabilities, without attributing armed attacks to weather. [COD-E-101, COD-E-102]
- Poor WASH in return areas and conflict-restricted farming complicate coping; reported kits and agricultural assistance are outputs, not durable adaptation impacts. [COD-E-103, COD-E-105, COD-E-106]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Under RCP4.5, the NAP reports median annual-mean warming of 1.4°C for 2041-2070 and 1.7°C for 2071-2100 relative to 1981-2010. Scenario: RCP4.5; 2041-2070, 2071-2100 [COD-E-001]
- Under RCP8.5, median annual-mean warming is 2.1°C in 2041-2070 and 3.7°C in 2071-2100 relative to 1981-2010; IQRs are 1.8-2.4°C and 2.9-4.2°C. Scenario: RCP8.5; 2041-2070, 2071-2100 [COD-E-002]
- National annual-mean precipitation projections have broad uncertainty: under RCP8.5 for 2071-2100 the NAP gives a median −2.3% change but an IQR from −8.8% to +14%. Scenario: RCP8.5; 2071-2100 [COD-E-003]
- The World Bank reported that floods from Nov. 2023-Jan. 2024 affected more than 2.2 million people across 18 of 26 provinces and damaged homes, roads, electricity, water infrastructure and agricultural land. [COD-E-005]
- The same May 2026 FAO/WFP update reported more than 7.8 million IDPs and identified conflict-driven livelihood destruction, market disruption and restricted farmland access in North/South Kivu, Ituri and Tanganyika. [COD-E-009]
- Source dated 2025-06-02 reports: UNICEF reports flooding in Kasaba village, Fizi Territory, South Kivu, reportedly killed 62 people, including 47 children; it sent Red Cross Tanganyika to assess and identify response. [COD-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The NAP identifies agriculture, water resources, health, coastal zones and energy among the country's most climate-vulnerable sectors. [COD-E-006]
- In May 2026, FAO/WFP reported more than 26.5 million people struggling to meet basic food needs, including more than 3.6 million in IPC Emergency conditions. [COD-E-008]
- Source dated 2025-06-02 reports: UNICEF reports that fighting in Kalehe, Walungu, Fizi and Mwenga displaced civilians in South Kivu; ADF attacks in Fungula and Bapere, Lubero Territory, killed more than 20 people. [COD-E-102]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NAP identifies restricted mobility and lower access to education, credit/banking and land tenure among factors limiting women's adaptive capacity. [COD-E-007]
- The revised NDC estimates USD 23.08 billion for priority adaptation measures within its overall USD 48.68 billion budget. Scenario: 2021-2030 [COD-E-016]
- FBA's eastern DRC workshop identified customary/traditional authorities and local governance systems as potentially important for managing climate stresses and planning adaptation, while noting institutional weakness. [COD-E-023]
- FAO/WFP stated in May 2026 that humanitarian assistance remained critically below needs and emphasized safe, sustained access alongside longer-term livelihood support. [COD-E-024]
- The 24 June 2026 FAO brief attributes reduced planting area to conflict-related field-access constraints and displacement in eastern provinces. Its separate rainfall forecast does not establish an interaction effect. [COD-E-105]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- As of June 2026, IMF reporting continued to describe the security situation in eastern DRC as volatile, with severe displacement and food insecurity. [COD-E-010]
- The revised NDC sets a 2030 GHG reduction target of 21% below BAU, comprising 19% conditional and 2% unconditional action. [COD-E-015]
- UNDP's independent evaluation found that national and institutional frameworks for climate mitigation/adaptation and natural-resource management had been strengthened, including forest monitoring and support to NDC/REDD+ processes. [COD-E-018]
- The same evaluation found mixed decentralized natural-resource-governance performance, with delays and insufficient local engagement/ownership threatening some land-use and community-forestry processes. [COD-E-019]
- UNDP's independent evaluation found that several climate-resilience project achievements were not sustained; it recorded problems including an early-warning system that remained non-functional and deterioration/technical shortcomings in coastal-protection infrastructure. [COD-E-020]
- In the first World Bank ISR, the Urban Flood Resilience Project had 0 current beneficiaries from climate-resilient infrastructure, 0 beneficiaries from early-warning services and 0 completed key DRM instruments as of 15 Aug. 2025, while 26 people had been trained by September. [COD-E-021]
- Source dated 2025-06-02 reports: UNICEF says IDP returns extended cholera into areas with poor WASH infrastructure, complicating response; its UNiRR reached 5,352 households with WASH and shelter kits and provided free primary care in four health zones. [COD-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-06-24 reports: FAO reports supporting 55,500 crisis-affected families in North Kivu, South Kivu, Ituri and Tanganyika with a USD 10 million allocation, seeds, tools, livelihood inputs and cash assistance. [COD-E-106]

  - FCV mediator: Existing land disputes, unequal access, weak resource governance and lack of alternative livelihoods.
  - Possible consequence: Livelihood pressure may intensify existing disputes or grievances over land and water in parts of eastern DRC.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- SIPRI finds that climate-security risks in North and South Kivu are conditioned by governance and access to land and water, especially where resource access is already disputed. [COD-E-011]
- In the Ruzizi Plain, FBA/SIPRI sources report that drought/pasture and water pressures alter livestock movement and interact with existing farmer-pastoralist and identity disputes. [COD-E-012]
- SIPRI documents that insecurity, armed-group control and blocked routes can restrict farmland access and food supply chains, weakening livelihoods and increasing vulnerability to later climate shocks. [COD-E-013]
- Specialist evidence links armed-group and elite exploitation of forests and minerals in eastern DRC to environmental degradation and weakened resource governance. [COD-E-014]
- SIPRI documents women- and youth-led initiatives in eastern DRC involving peace committees, social cohesion, forest restoration, climate justice, sustainable agriculture and conservation. [COD-E-022]
