# Viet Nam Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Delta evidence distinguishes climate-income associations, modeled poverty risks and migration as a possible coping strategy, with separate nonrepresentative child consultations in Bac Lieu, Ca Mau and Soc Trang. [VNM-E-101, VNM-E-102, VNM-E-103, VNM-E-105, VNM-E-106, VNM-E-107]
- Northern Yagi evidence identifies specific protection mechanisms through WASH access and livelihood hardship, without turning planning risks into confirmed violence or trafficking incidents. [VNM-E-111, VNM-E-113, VNM-E-114, VNM-P-101, VNM-P-102]
- Response evidence documents evacuation, multisector assistance and feedback mechanisms rather than evaluated protection or avoided-loss outcomes. [VNM-E-112, VNM-E-115]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No reviewed source established a climate-to-armed-conflict pathway or that armed violence obstructed adaptation; this is a search gap, not evidence of absence. []
- The World Bank climate-income findings are Mekong Delta-wide associations and simulations, not estimates for each province or proof of causality. []
- UNICEF's climate perceptions are based on a school sample in three delta provinces and should not be read as population prevalence. []
- Typhoon Yagi reports describe disaster-related trafficking and gender-based violence risks, not confirmed incidents caused by the event. []
- The updated 2025 National Adaptation Plan PDF at UNFCCC was too large for the browser reader; no claims were taken from indexed snippets. []
- Geographic names refer to source-era provinces and the 2024 survey/response, before the 2025 administrative reorganization; they are not represented as a current province roster. []
- Student perceptions from six schools in three delta provinces are not representative province-level exposure or loss rates. Migration is multi-causal and is not itself FCV. []
- Protection pathways identify risk/exposure, not measured GBV or trafficking increases. No armed-conflict or reverse FCV-to-climate pathway was established in the reviewed sources. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-12 reports: Typhoon Yagi damaged about 245,000 homes and displaced families into temporary shelters; 2.65 million children lived in the 11 hardest-hit provinces. [VNM-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025 reports: Severe salinity exposure was associated with 17% lower per-capita income; flood and heat exposure with 7% and 6% lower income. [VNM-E-101]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Through 2050, modeled drought could slow poverty reduction, especially for poorer farming households with fewer assets and less coping capacity. [VNM-E-102]
- Source dated 2025 reports: Nearly 1.7 million people left the Delta in a decade; migration partly offsets climate-sensitive farm losses, but benefits are uneven. [VNM-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Surveyed children identified environmental damage, heat, storms, floods, drought and salt intrusion; 89.9% in Ca Mau cited environmental damage. [VNM-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Among surveyed students, 48.2% reported climate-related crop loss, 33.5% poverty risk, and 29.5% interrupted schooling or dropout. [VNM-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Soc Trang consultations linked drought and salinity to crop and income losses, work migration and schooling disruption for children left behind. [VNM-E-107]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The storm disrupted farm, fishery and forestry livelihoods, costing 2.3 million workdays and placing over 95,000 people at food-insecurity risk. [VNM-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-09-27 reports: Northern mountain provinces had nearly 18% average poverty versus 4.2% nationally; ethnic minorities were over 55% of the region’s population. [VNM-E-113]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025 reports: High-dyke systems gave wealthier farmers short-term benefits but reduced soil fertility, raised fertilizer dependence and contributed to land consolidation. [VNM-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Three-province findings identify missing child-specific indicators and targets in climate frameworks; schools and local authorities use participation mechanisms. [VNM-E-108]

- Source dated 2024-09-27 reports: Before landfall, authorities brought over 51,000 fishing boats and 220,000 crew to shore and evacuated nearly 53,000 people from vulnerable housing. [VNM-E-115]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Yagi damaged WASH; longer water journeys increased girls' reported GBV exposure, not confirmed incidents. [VNM-P-101; VNM-E-111]
  - Documented impact: WASH destruction and unsafe water access.
  - FCV mediator: Girls faced longer water-collection journeys where safe water access was already limited.
  - Possible consequence: Greater exposure to gender-based violence, not documented incident counts.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Pre-existing water-service gaps and protection inequalities shaped risk.']
  - Uncertainty: Agency-reported protection risk; no causal attribution study, incidence estimate or anthropogenic attribution of Yagi.
- Yagi-related livelihood loss compounded northern household hardship and potential trafficking risk; no incidents are attributed. [VNM-P-102; VNM-E-113, VNM-E-114]
  - Documented impact: Housing and livelihood damage in already poor northern provinces.
  - FCV mediator: Loss of assets and income reduced households' recovery options.
  - Possible consequence: The response plan identifies increased human-trafficking risk, not confirmed trafficking events.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Pre-existing poverty, remoteness and trafficking risks also matter.']
  - Uncertainty: Dated interagency planning diagnosis, not measured post-event trafficking or ethnic-group risk propensity.
