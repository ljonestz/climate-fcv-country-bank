## Climate pressures and exposure

- Mean annual warming is projected at 1.1–3.0°C by the 2060s and 1.6–5.3°C by the 2090s, fastest in the north and northwest; 2041–50 dry/hot and wet/warm cases differ in both temperature and rainfall. [GIN-E-001]
- The NDC lists drought, rainfall disruption, floods, heat, and sea-level rise; it reports 132,496 flood-affected people in 2024 and further major flooding near Conakry in summer 2025. [GIN-E-004]
- Source dated 2025-02-27 reports: In 2024, flood waves in Siguiri, Kouroussa, Kankan, Mandiana and Gueckedou disrupted livelihoods and required humanitarian assistance. [GIN-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Without accelerated adaptation, modelled 2050 GDP losses reach 11.6% under dry/hot conditions and 2.5% under wet/warm conditions. [GIN-E-002]
- Source dated 2025-02-27 reports: Floods account for over 75% of recorded natural hazards; the plan reports over 70,000 affected and needing aid in the prior five years. [GIN-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-02-27 reports: The plan links coastal sea-level rise to salinization, flooding, water shortages, crop and infrastructure damage; floods also affect northern Guinea. [GIN-E-103]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC names Conakry, Forécariah, Boffa, and Kamsar as exposed to coastal inundation, erosion, and saline intrusion; it calls for risk mapping and 5,000 hectares of mangrove restoration. [GIN-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-04-24 reports: IMF reports Guinea among 20 countries with the lowest climate-risk coping capacity on institutional and infrastructure factors, likely reflecting long-standing constraints and weak connectivity. [GIN-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-04-24 reports: IMF DIGNAD simulations find grant-financed resilient infrastructure plus improved public-investment efficiency yields the smallest modeled disaster-related GDP loss and debt increase. [GIN-E-107]
- Under SSP3-7.0 for 2050, the CCDR models urban flood damage increases without adaptation in six named cities; with modeled adaptation, increases are smaller, and Conakry/Kamsar losses fall below their historical baselines. [GIN-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- For 2026–2035, the NDC proposes interministerial and municipal coordination, local contingency plans, inclusive committees with at least 40% women, and linked early-warning and assistance protocols. [GIN-E-007]
- Source dated 2025-02-27 reports: The Red Cross Society of Guinea operates 38 prefectural/communal and 303 sub-prefectural committees covering disaster response, climate action, early warning and migration assistance. [GIN-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-02-27 reports: An August 2024 DREF allocation of CHF 467,835 supported Guinea Red Cross aid for 13,300 flood-affected people in Kankan region over four months, including cash, health, WASH and protection/accountability services. [GIN-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: The CCDR says education-adaptation strategies are underimplemented due to political instability and high costs; a 2013 health-adaptation plan was not implemented for lack of funds. [GIN-E-109]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The CCDR states that climate impacts aggravate Guinea’s fragility; it also identifies insecure tenure and land issues among fragility drivers linked in the report to rural poverty, gender inequality, and intercommunal violence. [GIN-E-003]
- The NDC says climate impacts interact with socioeconomic fragilities and contribute to internal mobility toward Conakry; it describes migration as a frequently cited adaptation strategy. [GIN-E-005]
- Source dated 2025-12 reports: CCDR says northern degradation/desertification threatens pastoral and farming communities, heightening intercommunal tensions; insecure tenure compounds rural poverty and gender inequality. [GIN-E-108]
