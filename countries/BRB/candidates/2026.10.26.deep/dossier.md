# Barbados Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Parish-level composite vulnerability and capacity differ from direct flood exposure; coastal transport examples name Speightstown, Holetown, Bridgetown and Oistins. [BRB-E-101, BRB-E-102, BRB-E-103, BRB-E-104, BRB-E-105]
- Beryl evidence identifies fishing assets and women processors/vendors as affected, while recovery accounts and proposed infrastructure support do not demonstrate restored incomes. [BRB-E-110, BRB-E-111, BRB-E-112, BRB-E-113, BRB-E-114]
- Coastal participation and enforcement weaknesses are dated institutional findings. No supported climate-violence or reverse-FCV pathway was found in the reviewed sources. [BRB-E-106, BRB-E-108, BRB-E-109]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No existing BRB country baseline directory was present in the assigned worktree. []
- No reviewed primary source links climate stress to armed conflict, violence or insecurity in Barbados, or documents conflict/insecurity disrupting climate adaptation; this is a search gap, not evidence of absence. []
- The ICZM plan's reported coastal user conflicts are linked to limited stakeholder participation, not explicitly to climate hazards or violence; the plan is dated 2020 and may not represent current institutional conditions. []
- Parish-level PDC composites are multi-hazard and do not reveal climate-specific household or social-group vulnerability within individual coastal sites; the coastal-transport corridor evidence lacks local flood counts. []
- The 2026 fisherfolk survey post does not disclose sample size or site-disaggregated results; underlying survey materials were not located. []
- The UNDP project page and World Bank PID report different Beryl vessel-damage estimates (>200 versus 240); neither estimate was reconciled against underlying damage records. []
- The IDB beach-erosion profile PDF exceeded the reader size limit and the 2026 Water Authority drought article returned 403; no claims rely on indexed-only content. []
- No mediated FCV chain is assembled: coastal-user conflicts are participation/governance context without a documented climate trigger. []
- Multi-hazard parish composites must not be presented as climate-specific rankings or household risk measurements. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024 reports: PDC estimates 40% of Barbados's population is exposed to flash flooding, 66% to extreme heat and 1% to coastal flooding; coastal-flood zones include 8% of critical infrastructure. [BRB-E-101]
- Source dated 2020-10 reports: The plan reports more frequent flooding after extreme rainfall raises disruption risk along coastal links among Speightstown, Holetown, Bridgetown and Oistins, affecting tourism and freight. [BRB-E-104]
- Source dated 2020-10 reports: The plan estimates that about 25% of the population and much infrastructure lie within a zone roughly 2 km from the coastal floodplain. [BRB-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-10-18 reports: The PID reports Beryl damaged 240 vessels, Bridgetown Port and marina breakwaters; 73% of hotels, apartments and guest houses and 40 houses also suffered damage. [BRB-E-110]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024 reports: The composite vulnerability ranking places Saint Michael and Saint Joseph highest, followed by Saint Andrew, Saint John and Saint Lucy among Barbados's 11 parishes. [BRB-E-102]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The VNR says CORVI found significant ecological and financial risks in the southern and western coastal urban corridor, reflecting reliance on marine and coastal activities threatened by climate change. [BRB-E-109]
- Source dated 2024-10-18 reports: About 3,230 fisherfolk work across roughly 30 sites; the PID says women processors and sellers faced post-Beryl pressure, selling reserves or importing fish for local demand. [BRB-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-02-05 reports: Fisherfolk surveyed across Barbados most often reported reduced income, disrupted market and service access, and damaged or lost boats and gear; landing-site representatives met to discuss recovery priorities. [BRB-E-113]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2024 reports: Island-capacity scores are highest in Saint James and lowest in Saint Joseph; Saint Andrew and Saint Lucy also rank near the bottom. [BRB-E-103]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP's Japan-funded project aims to upgrade select Bridgetown Fisheries Complex infrastructure and equipment, support affected livelihoods and strengthen the fishing community's disaster preparedness through capacity building. [BRB-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2020-10 reports: The plan's 2020 SWOT identifies weak climate-adaptation and disaster-management capacity, limited coastal enforcement and weak public participation. [BRB-E-106]
- Source dated 2023 reports: After Hurricane Elsa in 2021, the Government earmarked an initial BDS$54.5 million to repair or rebuild housing for the most vulnerable; the VNR estimates the hurricane's impact at 1% of GDP. [BRB-E-107]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The Government reports using the IDB-funded NCRIPP GIS platform and Coastal Risk Atlas to view hazard, vulnerability and risk maps for coastal and island-wide areas. [BRB-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-10-18 reports: The Government mobilized partners to relocate vessels, clear debris and repair Bridgetown Port; it established a Fisheries Benevolent Fund to support fisherfolk livelihoods. [BRB-E-112]
