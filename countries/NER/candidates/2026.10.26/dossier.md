# Niger Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Niger faces material and increasing climate pressure, but current food and livelihood crises cannot be reduced to climate scarcity. Supporting evidence: NER-E-001-004, E-014-021; pathways NER-P-001-003. In particular, the relatively strong 2025 aggregate cereal harvest alongside localized weather and insecurity impacts demonstrates why national averages and deterministic scarcity narratives are inadequate. (FAOHome) [NER-E-001, NER-E-002, NER-E-003, NER-E-004, NER-E-014, NER-E-015, NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018, NER-E-020, NER-E-021, NER-P-001, NER-P-003]
- The best-supported Climate-FCV mechanisms operate through livelihoods, mobility, land/water access, displacement, institutions and service delivery - not a direct climate -> violence relationship. Supporting evidence: NER-E-016-019, E-022-023, E-028-030; pathways NER-P-001-006 and P-008. (SIPRI) [NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018, NER-E-028, NER-E-029, NER-E-030, NER-P-001, NER-P-003, NER-P-004, NER-P-005, NER-P-006, NER-P-008]
- Niger has substantial adaptation policy architecture and some evaluated resilience gains, but implementation, financing, local capacity, data and current independent evaluation remain material constraints. Supporting evidence: NER-E-005-009, E-012-013, E-024-027; pathways NER-P-005-008. [NER-E-005, NER-E-006, NER-E-008, NER-E-009, NER-E-012, NER-E-013, NER-E-025, NER-E-026, NER-E-027, NER-P-005, NER-P-006, NER-P-007, NER-P-008]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate projections: the principal nationally specific projection evidence verified here uses CMIP5/RCP4.5-8.5. A current, downscaled CMIP6 assessment for Niger's livelihood zones, river basins and cities is needed before using precise future hazard estimates. [NER-E-001, NER-E-002, NER-E-003, NER-E-004]
- Causal Climate-FCV evidence: current Niger-specific longitudinal or quasi-experimental evidence remains scarce. The strongest Niger-specific field evidence located is from 2014; broader SIPRI evidence explicitly characterizes the West African literature as limited. (SIPRI) [NER-E-014, NER-E-015, NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018]
- Post-2023 institutional change: how Niger's changed political, fiscal, decentralization and international-financing environment affects NAP/NDC implementation at commune and regional levels has not been independently evaluated in the sources reviewed. [NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018, NER-E-020, NER-E-021, NER-E-025]
- FCV-to-climate direction: current sources demonstrate insecurity disrupting farms, markets, mobility and services, but rarely measure how much this reduces actual adaptation outcomes or increases climate-attributable loss. [NER-E-008, NER-E-009, NER-E-028, NER-E-029]
- Differentiated vulnerability: children, refugees, IDPs, women and pastoral groups appear in the evidence, but comparable quantitative analysis by sex, age, livelihood, disability, displacement status and wealth across hazards is missing. [NER-E-028, NER-E-029, NER-E-030]
- Mobility: evidence does not support treating migration or pastoral mobility as automatically climate-caused. Current data capable of separating climate, security, economic, family and border-policy motives were not verified in an accessible national dataset. [NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018]
- Farmer-herder relations: the most detailed Niger evidence is old. Current conditions following escalating insecurity and political change may differ substantially. [NER-E-026, NER-E-027]
- Response effectiveness: current UNICEF, UNHCR, FAO and national documents provide outputs, plans and operational reporting but generally do not establish counterfactual programme effectiveness. [NER-E-028, NER-E-029]
- Unintended effects: apart from IFAD's documented budget trade-off and older land-access/maladaptation findings, systematic evidence on adaptation creating exclusion, conflict, elite capture, groundwater depletion or livelihood displacement is thin. [NER-E-005, NER-E-006, NER-E-008, NER-E-009]
- Access/verification: the UNHCR PDF binary could not be rendered; claims were restricted to its accessible publication page. Screenshot rendering also intermittently failed for several otherwise text-accessible PDFs. [NER-E-020, NER-E-021]
- *None are entirely absent, but coping-capacity, response-performance, direct-climate-fcv and resilience-peace-capacity are only partially covered. Direct Climate-FCV and peace-capacity evidence is especially constrained by age, locality or regional scope. [NER-E-025, NER-E-026, NER-E-027]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Semi-arid, short rainy season. [NER-E-001]
- Rainfall recovered but variability rose. [NER-E-002]
- Material warming by 2030/2050. [NER-E-003]
- Fewer rainy days, potentially heavier rain/dry spells. [NER-E-004]
- Large workforce exposure to climate-sensitive livelihoods. [NER-E-011]
- FAO's 28 August 2026 brief reported 2025 aggregate cereal production at about 5.3 million tonnes, near the five-year average, after early dryness and later improvement. [NER-E-015]
- FAO reported in August 2026 that about 85% of forcibly displaced people were in Tillaberi, Diffa and Tahoua, increasing pressure on livelihoods and markets. [NER-E-018]
- During August-November 2025, IPC classified all of Diffa, selected Maradi departments and specified refugee sites in Phase 4 (Critical), showing sharply localized nutrition severity. [NER-E-021]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Humanitarian crisis is explicitly multi-driver. [NER-E-014]
- FAO estimated 2.42 million people in CH Phase 3+ during June-August 2026 and identified conflict as the main driver; more than 548,000 people were internally displaced in July 2026. [NER-E-017]
- For August 2025-July 2026, IPC projected 1.6 million children with acute malnutrition, including more than 410,000 severe cases, under interacting nutrition, disease, WASH, flood and insecurity pressures. [NER-E-020]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- National policy contains multiple adaptation instruments. [NER-E-005]
- Climate services target rural decision-making. [NER-E-009]

- Implementation relies heavily on capacity/finance. [NER-E-006]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Climate stress can alter livelihood strategies, but whether this becomes conflict depends strongly on land institutions, access rules and political-economic conditions. (ResearchGate) [NER-P-001; NER-E-002, NER-E-003, NER-E-004, NER-E-028, NER-E-029, NER-E-030]
  - Documented impact: Pressure on rainfed and pastoral livelihood strategies and adaptation choices.
  - FCV mediator: Cultivation encroachment, livestock mobility, access to wells/pasture, demographic pressure and weak/unequal land-governance implementation.
  - Possible consequence: Localized disputes or violence between resource users may become more likely where institutions fail to manage competing access.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-002'], 'impact': ['NER-E-028'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-028'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-028']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Population growth', 'cultivation policy', 'elite capture', 'commercial land use', 'weak law enforcement', 'historic grievances and wider insecurity']
  - Uncertainty: No current national causal estimate.
- Water or pasture scarcity is not sufficient to produce violence; conflict emerges through contested access and governance. (ResearchGate) [NER-P-003; NER-E-028, NER-E-030]
  - Documented impact: Changing pastoral mobility and water/pasture strategies.
  - FCV mediator: Agricultural encroachment, corridor obstruction, well access, unequal legal representation and weak Rural Code enforcement.
  - Possible consequence: Conflict risk may increase at specific encounter points between mobile herders and cultivators.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-028'], 'impact': ['NER-E-028'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-028'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-028']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Population pressure', 'commercialization', 'political patronage', 'cross-border pastoral agreements', 'criminality']
  - Uncertainty: Present-day magnitude and geographic distribution are not established.
- Current evidence more clearly demonstrates insecurity constraining climate-sensitive livelihoods than climate shocks directly causing insecurity. (FAOHome) [NER-P-004; NER-E-011, NER-E-016, NER-E-017]
  - Documented impact: Civil insecurity restricted access to fields and caused localized production shortfalls in Tillabéri and Diffa in 2025.
  - FCV mediator: Armed insecurity and mobility restrictions.
  - Possible consequence: Households with reduced farm access may possess less income, stocks and flexibility to absorb subsequent drought/flood shocks.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-011'], 'impact': ['NER-E-011'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-011'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-011']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Input prices', 'pests', 'local weather', 'soil fertility', 'market access']
  - Uncertainty: Household-level resilience losses are not quantified.
- FCV can erode the service base on which effective disaster response and adaptation depend. [NER-P-005; NER-E-013, NER-E-018, NER-E-025]
  - Documented impact: Displacement and insecurity increase needs while health, education and other services face access and funding constraints.
  - FCV mediator: Forced displacement, insecure roads/territories, concentrated urban demand and resource constraints.
  - Possible consequence: Reduced baseline service capacity may make subsequent climate emergencies harder to absorb.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-013'], 'impact': ['NER-E-013'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-013'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Chronic underinvestment', 'geography', 'rapid population growth and fiscal constraints']
  - Uncertainty: Direct climate-adaptation outcome evaluation is absent.
- An adaptation intervention should not be assumed peace-positive merely because it reduces climate exposure. (ResearchGate) [NER-P-006; NER-E-028, NER-E-029]
  - Documented impact: Farmers and pastoralists diversify, change mobility, intensify agriculture, build wells or monetize previously shared resources.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal capital, tenure, access and institutional representation.
  - Possible consequence: Some adaptation choices may shift costs or exclude other groups, creating grievances or disputes.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-028'], 'impact': ['NER-E-028'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-028'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-028']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Commercialization and demographic growth independent of climate']
  - Uncertainty: Contemporary prevalence is unknown.
- Early warning is a plausible resilience mechanism, but current evidence in this packet mostly demonstrates capacity and outputs rather than impacts. [NER-P-007; NER-E-005, NER-E-008, NER-E-009]
  - Documented impact: Niger's policies call for improved forecasting, early warning and locally accessible climate services; UNICEF reports anticipatory flood preparation.
  - FCV mediator: Timeliness and inclusiveness of information/assistance, local capacity and financing.
  - Possible consequence: Earlier action could reduce livelihood losses and pressure on crisis-response systems, provided services reach insecure and marginalized communities.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-005'], 'impact': ['NER-E-005'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-005'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-005']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Existing wealth', 'market access and infrastructure may dominate household outcomes']
  - Uncertainty: Current programme descriptions do not establish avoided losses or peace effects.
- Legitimate institutions may govern resource access and disputes more effectively, but the peace effect is supported only by old, localized evidence. [NER-P-008; NER-E-013, NER-E-029]
  - Documented impact: Resource-access disputes occur where mobility and cultivation overlap.
  - FCV mediator: Quality and legitimacy of local dispute-resolution institutions.
  - Possible consequence: Inclusive land commissions and mediation may reduce escalation, but the observed conflict-reduction account is old and limited to one community.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NER-E-013'], 'impact': ['NER-E-013'], 'mediator': ['NER-E-013'], 'consequence': ['NER-E-013']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Broader security conditions', 'local power relations and enforcement capacity']
  - Uncertainty: Louma finding is old and one-community evidence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- FAO's 28 August 2026 brief reported that civil insecurity continued to constrain field access in parts of Tillaberi and Diffa, causing localized production shortfalls. [NER-E-016]
- Adaptation can redistribute resource access and create tensions. [NER-E-028]
- Local inclusive land governance showed peace-supporting potential. [NER-E-029]
- Evidence does not justify deterministic climate -> violence claims. [NER-E-030]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National aggregates obscure substantial variation. Tillabéri and Diffa combine insecurity, displacement and agricultural-access problems; Tahoua also hosts substantial displaced populations and appears in older pastoral-resource evidence. Diffa stands out in the current IPC nutrition analysis, while Agadez appears in refugee-site nutrition evidence and has distinct pastoral/desert livelihood characteristics. (FAOHome) [NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018, NER-E-020, NER-E-021, NER-E-025]
- The 2014 findings on farmer-herder conflict, Rural Code implementation and Louma's land commission are local historical evidence and must not be represented as the current condition of all Tillabéri, Tahoua or Niger. (ResearchGate) [NER-E-028, NER-E-029]
- Lake Chad climate-security findings are relevant primarily to the eastern basin/Diffa context. SIPRI explicitly studies a transboundary region spanning Niger, Nigeria, Chad and Cameroon; its recruitment and resource-tension pathways are not national Niger estimates. (SIPRI) [NER-E-030]
- Tillabéri, Diffa and Dosso feature in UNICEF's 2026 evidence of conflict-related humanitarian/service constraints, while IPC identifies different patterns for malnutrition, including Diffa, selected Maradi departments and refugee sites. These different geographies should not be collapsed into a single “climate-FCV hotspot” category. [NER-E-020, NER-E-021]
- Important underrepresented settings include Niamey and secondary-city heat exposure, groundwater-dependent systems, Agadez desert/oasis livelihoods, Niger River floodplain communities beyond the available humanitarian reporting, and contemporary transhumance corridors outside the older field sites. [NER-E-010, NER-E-011, NER-E-012, NER-E-013]
- Coverage is strongest for agriculture, livestock/pastoralism, food security, humanitarian delivery, health/nutrition, water/WASH, land governance and DRM. Climate information and social protection/anticipatory action are reasonably represented at policy or programme-output level. [NER-E-014, NER-E-015, NER-E-016, NER-E-017, NER-E-018, NER-E-020, NER-E-021, NER-E-025, NER-E-026, NER-E-027, NER-E-028, NER-E-029]
- Coverage is thinner for groundwater quantity and governance, fisheries, urban heat/productivity, transport disruption, energy-system climate resilience, ecosystem services, private-sector adaptation and the distributional effects of adaptation finance. The NAP includes forestry, transport and wetlands as priorities, but this packet found less evaluated sector performance in those areas. (UNDP) [NER-E-005, NER-E-006, NER-E-008, NER-E-009, NER-E-010, NER-E-011, NER-E-012, NER-E-013]

Contribution Déterminée au Niveau National révisée. 2021 (date basis: publication). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/NDC/2022-06/CDN_Niger_R%C3%A9vis%C3%A9e_2021.pdf [NER-SRC-001] Methodology: National synthesis; CMIP5 ensemble of 29 models for cited projections; RCP4.5/RCP8.5 Limitations: Projection generation is now dated; some impact statistics originate in older national reports.
- Government of Niger / UNFCCC. Niger's Adaptation Communication (UNFCCC). 2025 (date basis: publication). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-08/Adaptation%20communication%20Niger.pdf [NER-SRC-004] Methodology: National synthesis of adaptation policies, programmes, gaps and support needs Limitations: Government self-assessment; project listings do not establish outcomes.
- World Bank. CPF FY26-FY31, Annex 5: Streamlined Country Diagnostic. 2026 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099842504102674841/pdf/SECBOS-47996674-b17e-4d12-95be-8378a61be412.pdf [NER-SRC-005] Methodology: Synthesis of socioeconomic, FCV, climate, institutional and sector evidence Limitations: Diagnostic synthesis, not a causal climate-security study; some estimates rely on earlier WB work.
- FAO. Niger - Emergency and Resilience (FAOHome). 2026 (date basis: publication). https://www.fao.org/emergencies/where-we-work/NER/en [NER-SRC-006] Methodology: FAO emergency assessment and programme data Limitations: Operational synthesis; headline counts may update; programme plans are not evaluations.
- FAO. GIEWS Country Brief: Republic of the Niger (FAOHome). 2026 (date basis: publication). https://www.fao.org/giews/countrybrief/country/NER/pdf/NER.pdf [NER-SRC-007] Methodology: Crop monitoring, cereal balance, prices, earth observation and Cadre Harmonisé evidence Limitations: Short early-warning product; projected CH figures subject to later updates.
- IPC. Acute Malnutrition Situation Aug.-Nov. 2025 and Projections to July 2026 (IPCinfo). 2026 (date basis: publication). https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1159831/ [NER-SRC-008] Methodology: IPC classification and multi-factor driver analysis Limitations: Projection periods are not observations; nutrition causation is multifactorial.
- UNHCR. Niger Operation Factsheet, January-June 2025 (UNHCR Data Portal). 2025 (date basis: publication). https://data.unhcr.org/en/documents/details/119022 [NER-SRC-010] Methodology: UNHCR operational reporting Limitations: Publication page accessible; PDF binary did not render in browser. Reported contributions are not independently evaluated outcomes.
- SIPRI. Climate Change and Violent Conflict in West Africa: Assessing the Evidence (SIPRI). 2022 (date basis: publication). https://www.sipri.org/publications/2022/sipri-insights-peace-and-security/climate-change-and-violent-conflict-west-africa-assessing-evidence [NER-SRC-013] Methodology: Systematic review of academic climate-conflict evidence Limitations: Regional and explicitly finds a limited research base.
- IFAD Independent Office of Evaluation. Niger Country Strategy and Programme Evaluation (ioe.ifad.org). 2021 (date basis: publication). https://ioe.ifad.org/en/w/niger-country-strategy-and-programme-evaluation-1-1 [NER-SRC-014] Methodology: Independent country strategy/programme evaluation Limitations: Covers IFAD, not Niger's entire adaptation portfolio; period ends 2020.
- USAID/FESS/Tetra Tech ARD. Climate Change and Conflict in Pastoralist Regions of Ethiopia, Kenya and Niger. 2014 (date basis: publication). https://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PBAAB109.pdf [NER-SRC-015] Methodology: Climate/livelihood synthesis with interviews, focus groups, institutional evidence and climate analyses Limitations: Foundational but old; some statistics and institutional conditions are obsolete; local findings cannot be generalized.
