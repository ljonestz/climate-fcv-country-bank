## Executive assessment

- Khanfar/Wadi Bana adds a concrete multi-driver water-allocation case; rainfall, war damage, diversion and governance must be read together, not as a single climate cause. [YEM-E-101]
- Al-Malakha's women-led mediation provides a historical capacity example, while Hodeidah, Shibam and Hadramaut activities are reported outputs or participant accounts. [YEM-E-102, YEM-E-103, YEM-E-104, YEM-E-105, YEM-E-106, YEM-E-108]
- Evaluation-summary findings caution that short timeframes, coherence and ownership constrain sustainability despite promising water-user capacities. [YEM-E-109]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Yemen's arid and semi-arid baseline varies sharply with topography: western highlands receive much more rainfall than eastern and interior zones, so national averages conceal basin and livelihood differences. [YEM-E-001]
- Official and World Bank assessments report rapid warming and project further heat, uncertain but potentially more variable or intense rainfall, and rising coastal water levels by mid-century. Scenario: Historical 1971-2020 and mid-century SSP3-7.0 and CMIP6 projections [YEM-E-002]
- World Bank spatial analysis estimates that about 16.7 million people are exposed to at least one of heat, drought, or flood, with hazard combinations and intensity differing across locations. Scenario: Policy-note spatial exposure modelling [YEM-E-003]
- The climate-health assessment models 433 health facilities reported operational as of 2023 in 190 of Yemen's 335 districts as exposed to at least 15 cm inundation in a 2050 1-in-100-year flood under SSP3-7.0, while conflict-damaged lifelines and access could convert physical exposure into service disruption. Scenario: 2050 SSP3-7.0, 1 percent annual-probability flood [YEM-E-016]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The World Bank diagnostic attributes Yemen's water deficit principally to demand exceeding renewable supply, inefficient use and largely unregulated groundwater abstraction; climate uncertainty compounds this structural crisis. [YEM-E-004]
- In its June 2026 analysis, IPC projected 5.37 million people, 51 percent of the analysed population, in Government-controlled areas in Phase 3 or worse during October-December 2026, including 1.79 million in Phase 4, while purposive fieldwork in six rural study areas documents differentiated asset, labour, water and mobility constraints affecting women, displaced people and poorer farmers. Scenario: IPC October-December 2026 projection published June 2026 [YEM-E-005]
- Source dated 2026-02-05 reports: In Wadi Bin Ali, Shibam, a farmer reported leaving fields uncultivated for months because seasonal spate irrigation, damaged canals and poor floodwater retention constrained production; beekeeping supplemented income. [YEM-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- A 2020 qualitative review of responses during the 2015-2020 conflict period found that reliance on private wells, tankers, generators and solar systems preserved some water and electricity access while fragmenting public systems and creating unequal affordability, maintenance and regulation risks. [YEM-E-006]
- Across six purposively selected rural study areas in Abyan, Hadramawt, Marib and Taiz, respondents reported switching to less water-demanding crops, maintaining communal spate and flood systems, diversifying income and migrating; solar pumps lowered fuel constraints but enabled capitalized farmers to pump longer and deeper, with unequal effects on downstream users and smallholders. [YEM-E-007]
- The NDC 3.0 Vision adopts an adaptation-first approach, names vulnerable groups, assigns sector goals and lead institutions, and sets a 2026-2035 roadmap, but does not establish that those arrangements are operational across divided institutions. [YEM-E-008]
- Source dated 2024-03-01 reports: A named farmer in Sanif Al-Monasarah, Al-Mansouriyah, said spillways prevented soil erosion and helped restore irrigation; a project account says the work enabled her to return to crop production after land degradation had stopped cultivation. [YEM-E-104]
- FAO's February 2026 Shibam feature reports greenhouse distribution and cash-for-work, with 102 facilities targeted for rehabilitation and participant accounts of more regular production. [YEM-E-106]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- UNDP evaluations find that local institutions and delivery partners retained meaningful implementation reach, while territorial access, staffing, maintenance finance, inclusion, monitoring, and exit arrangements constrained sustainability and equitable performance. [YEM-E-010]
- For the 2015-June 2021 response period, the independent inter-agency evaluation found major humanitarian reach alongside persistent weaknesses in needs evidence, targeting, accountability, localization, disability inclusion, outcome measurement and transition to durable local capacity. [YEM-E-011]
- Independent reviews of emergency public works and institutional resilience document delivery and employment outputs but find limited evidence on sustained welfare, maintenance, institutionalization, peace, or climate-resilience outcomes. [YEM-E-012]
- Source dated 2024-03-01 reports: In Al-Sukhnah and Al-Mansouriyah, Hodeidah, UNDP reports floods and heatwaves damaging farmland and crops; SFD reported 379 spillways across the districts, nearly 100 hectares benefited by reservoirs and 299 hectares protected by spillways. [YEM-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-04-24 reports: For flood-prone Mukalla and Seiyun, SIERY equipped local authorities to manage flood waste, improve drainage and rehabilitate rural roads; in Hadramaut, the project also rehabilitated irrigation canals for local agriculture. [YEM-E-108]
- Source dated not stated reports: FAO's evaluation summary says water-focused initiatives showed potential for area-based resilience and strengthened water-user associations, but short project periods, weak programme coherence and limited institutional ownership constrained sustainability; evidence of wider system change was limited. [YEM-E-109]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heat and rainfall stress can deepen livelihood insecurity where groundwater mismanagement, conflict-disrupted markets, and unequal assets narrow coping options. [YEM-P-001; YEM-E-002, YEM-E-004, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-007]

  - FCV mediator: Groundwater over-abstraction, inefficient irrigation, market disruption, asset inequality, displacement, and weak safety nets
  - Possible consequence: Livelihood erosion can intensify distress migration, exclusion, or local tension where households lack alternatives, but climate pressure is not a single cause of conflict

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Local assessments identify disputes around wells, irrigation, upstream-downstream allocation, land, and floodwater, but climate and water scarcity operate through governance, ownership, livelihoods, authority, and existing conflict rather than as single causes of violence. [YEM-E-013]
- The 2024 World Bank diagnostic states that conflict exacerbated Yemen's water-management crisis, while a 2020 infrastructure study documents conflict-era damage, fuel constraints and fragmented coping across water and electricity systems needed to manage climate hazards. [YEM-E-014]
- Water-user groups, customary mediation, community organizations, and local delivery institutions can support allocation, dispute management, and recovery when representation, legitimacy, financing, hydrological fit, and links to public authority are credible. [YEM-E-015]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: In Khanfar, climate-change-related rainfall shifts reportedly reduced Wadi Bana flow and irrigation; conflict had damaged or destroyed 85% of irrigation infrastructure by late 2023. Some farmers diverted water from downstream users, who feared violence. [YEM-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: In Al-Malakha, a dam serving about 350 farmers had been a violent conflict flashpoint. A women’s unit in the local WUA helped negotiate permitted shallow wells and a pipeline; the approach was replicated in 14 other villages with armed conflict. [YEM-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-04-24 reports: UNDP states that Yemen's climate and water pressures, compounded by conflict, have harmed resource management and impeded recovery and peacebuilding. [YEM-E-107]
