# Yemen, Rep. Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Yemen's structural aridity and highly uneven rainfall baseline are being compounded by rising heat, uncertain rainfall change, flood, drought, and coastal pressures, with exposure varying strongly among basins, highlands, coasts, cities, farms, and displacement sites. [YEM-E-001, YEM-E-002, YEM-E-003, YEM-E-016, YEM-P-001, YEM-P-002, YEM-P-003]
- Water, food, livelihood, health, and mobility risks are mediated by groundwater governance, agricultural demand, asset inequality, markets, damaged lifelines, displacement, territorial fragmentation, and humanitarian access; climate is not a single cause of water conflict or food insecurity. [YEM-E-004, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-006, YEM-E-007, YEM-E-009, YEM-E-013, YEM-E-014, YEM-P-001, YEM-P-002, YEM-P-003, YEM-P-004]
- National plans, local authorities, water-user and customary institutions, public works bodies, and humanitarian partners retain meaningful capacity, but territorial reach, inclusion, monitoring, maintenance, exit finance, and independently evaluated long-term outcomes remain weak or uneven. [YEM-E-008, YEM-E-010, YEM-E-011, YEM-E-012, YEM-E-015, YEM-P-005, YEM-P-006]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Localized climate, aquifer, groundwater-recharge, rainfall-intensity, coastal, and settlement exposure evidence is incomplete because observation systems are degraded and national projections have wide local uncertainty. [YEM-E-001, YEM-E-002, YEM-E-003, YEM-E-004, YEM-E-009, YEM-P-001, YEM-P-002, YEM-P-003]
- Current evidence from de facto authority areas, inaccessible districts, women, displaced people, persons with disabilities, pastoralists, and remote communities is uneven and often not comparable across territories. [YEM-E-005, YEM-E-007, YEM-E-010, YEM-E-011, YEM-P-001, YEM-P-004, YEM-P-005]
- Independent evaluations document delivery and implementation constraints but rarely establish durable maintenance, welfare, aquifer, livelihood, inclusion, climate-resilience, or peace outcomes. [YEM-E-010, YEM-E-011, YEM-E-012, YEM-E-015, YEM-P-005, YEM-P-006]
- The fcv-to-climate pathway is directly supported at system level, but quantitative estimates of conflict's additional effect on hazard losses, adaptation costs, and recovery duration are absent. [YEM-E-006, YEM-E-009, YEM-E-014, YEM-E-016, YEM-P-002, YEM-P-004]
- Water-dispute and mobility pathways remain conditional and local; stronger event-specific, longitudinal, and counterfactual evidence is needed before making national climate-conflict claims. [YEM-E-005, YEM-E-013, YEM-E-015, YEM-P-001, YEM-P-003, YEM-P-006]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Structural aridity and topography create sharply differentiated water baselines. [YEM-E-001]
- Heat, rainfall variability, flood, drought, and coastal pressures are expected to intensify unevenly. [YEM-E-002]
- Millions face spatially differentiated heat, drought, or flood exposure. [YEM-E-003]
- Health-facility flood exposure intersects with fragile lifelines and access. [YEM-E-016]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Groundwater governance and agricultural demand dominate structural water scarcity, with climate as a compounding pressure. [YEM-E-004]
- Food and livelihood vulnerability is severe, multi-driver, geographically partial, and socially differentiated. [YEM-E-005]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Decentralized coping preserves some services while fragmenting systems and access. [YEM-E-006]
- Local adaptations offer continuity but may deepen aquifer and equity risks. [YEM-E-007]
- The NDC Vision documents intended adaptive capacity, not delivered capacity. [YEM-E-008]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Fragmented authority and degraded monitoring constrain equitable water governance. [YEM-E-009]
- Local delivery capacity persists but is uneven and sustainability-constrained. [YEM-E-010]
- Large humanitarian reach coexists with targeting, accountability, localization, and outcome gaps. [YEM-E-011]
- Programme outputs are better evidenced than durable welfare, peace, or resilience effects. [YEM-E-012]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heat and rainfall stress can deepen livelihood insecurity where groundwater mismanagement, conflict-disrupted markets, and unequal assets narrow coping options. [YEM-P-001; YEM-E-002, YEM-E-004, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-007]
  - Documented impact: Crop and livestock stress, higher water demand, livelihood loss, food-price pressure, and reduced household coping
  - FCV mediator: Groundwater over-abstraction, inefficient irrigation, market disruption, asset inequality, displacement, and weak safety nets
  - Possible consequence: Livelihood erosion can intensify distress migration, exclusion, or local tension where households lack alternatives, but climate pressure is not a single cause of conflict

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Water-related conflict is conditional on governance, ownership, livelihoods, and authority. [YEM-E-013]
- Conflict and economic collapse erode systems needed for climate risk management. [YEM-E-014]
- Local institutions offer conditional resilience and dispute-management capacity. [YEM-E-015]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National evidence is complemented by basin, district, city, health-facility, programme, and six-community studies across parts of Abyan, Hadramawt, Dhamar, Marib, Taiz, Al-Hodeidah, Shabwah, and Sana'a; none of the local findings should be generalized nationally. [YEM-E-003, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-006, YEM-E-007, YEM-E-013, YEM-E-016, YEM-P-002, YEM-P-003, YEM-P-006]
- Government-controlled-area food analysis, limited access to de facto authority areas, degraded monitoring, and security restrictions create systematic territorial evidence gaps. [YEM-E-004, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-009, YEM-E-010, YEM-E-011, YEM-P-004, YEM-P-005]
- Water, agriculture, food security, livelihoods, health, energy, WASH, transport, social protection, public works, local governance, and humanitarian delivery are represented; coastal livelihoods, fisheries, urban heat, private-sector exposure, and ecosystem services remain thin. [YEM-E-004, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-006, YEM-E-010, YEM-E-011, YEM-E-016, YEM-P-001, YEM-P-002, YEM-P-004, YEM-P-005]
