Critical remaining gaps:
- Climate-projection gap: Accessible Libya-specific projections do not provide sufficiently detailed scenario, model-ensemble, spatial and uncertainty information for heat, drought, rainfall, sea level and compound extremes. SIPRI's projections are useful but secondary. (SIPRI) [LBY-E-003, LBY-E-004]
- Attribution gap beyond Storm Daniel: Event attribution is available for the September 2023 rainfall, but comparable Libya-specific attribution evidence for drought trends, groundwater depletion, heat impacts, coastal erosion and agricultural losses was not verified. (World Weather Attribution) [LBY-E-001, LBY-E-002, LBY-P-001]
- Geographic gap: Derna/eastern Libya is much better documented than many western and southern municipalities. Fezzan water-governance evidence is local and cannot be nationalized. [LBY-E-006, LBY-E-017, LBY-E-019, LBY-E-020, LBY-E-022, LBY-P-001, LBY-P-004, LBY-P-006]
- Population gap: There is insufficient comparable evidence separating impacts by gender, age, disability, income, migrant/refugee status, urban informality and livelihood type. UNICEF and UNHCR identify vulnerable groups, but longitudinal differentiated outcome data are scarce. [LBY-E-007, LBY-E-008, LBY-E-021, LBY-E-024, LBY-P-005, LBY-P-007]
- Evaluation gap: The 2025 UNDP evaluation provides independent evidence for one climate/energy programme, but the new drought strategy, water-security strategy, local water systems and post-Derna reconstruction lack comparable independent outcome evaluations. [LBY-E-011, LBY-E-012, LBY-E-013, LBY-E-014, LBY-E-015, LBY-E-016, LBY-P-008]
- Unintended-effect gap: There is limited systematic evidence on whether climate or reconstruction programmes reinforce territorial inequalities, authority competition, exclusion, maladaptation or unequal resource allocation. [LBY-E-012, LBY-E-015, LBY-E-022, LBY-E-024, LBY-P-006, LBY-P-008]
- Causal climate-FCV gap: Evidence that climate stress causes violence, recruitment or migration is substantially weaker than evidence that governance, conflict and institutional fragmentation shape vulnerability and response. (SIPRI) [LBY-E-018, LBY-E-019, LBY-E-021, LBY-P-003, LBY-P-005]
- FCV-to-adaptation quantification gap: Qualitative evidence that fragmentation weakens adaptation and recovery is strong, but there is little quantitative estimation of the size of these effects. [LBY-E-011, LBY-E-016, LBY-E-017, LBY-E-022, LBY-E-023, LBY-P-002, LBY-P-006]
- Formal climate-reporting gap: The UNFCCC National Communication table contained no listed Libyan submission at the time of verification. UN reporting indicates NDC development has advanced, but this research did not verify a finalized/submitted Libyan NDC. (UNFCCC) [LBY-E-011, LBY-E-025]
- Policy implementation gap: The 2026 drought and water strategies establish intentions and targets; financing, institutional ownership, municipal execution, maintenance and measurable resilience outcomes remain unresolved. (UNDP) [LBY-E-013, LBY-E-014, LBY-E-015, LBY-E-016, LBY-P-008]
- *None are wholly without traceable support. However, coping-capacity and resilience-peace-capacity remain only partially covered because current evidence is dominated by programme outputs and localized qualitative cases rather than independently evaluated household, community or peace outcomes. [LBY-E-015, LBY-E-019, LBY-E-020, LBY-P-004, LBY-P-008]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- On 10 September 2023, WWA estimated rainfall over its eastern Libya analysis region as a 1-in-300 to 1-in-600-year event. [LBY-E-001]
- For September 2023, WWA estimated up to 50 times greater likelihood and 50% greater intensity, while uncertainty included no detectable change. [LBY-E-002]
- By 2060, projected mean annual warming is 1.42 to 2.69 degrees C. [LBY-E-003]
- Drier averages can coexist with stronger extremes. [LBY-E-004]
- Coastal concentration increases exposure. [LBY-E-005]
- The September 2023 floods affected about 1.5 million people and initially displaced nearly 44,800 across 20 eastern municipalities. [LBY-E-006]
- By October 2025, UNHCR estimated 467,000 Sudanese refugees had arrived in Libya since April 2023. [LBY-E-008]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- In October 2023, UNICEF documented damaged WASH and health infrastructure and service disruption in flood-affected eastern Libya. [LBY-E-007]
- WFP's 2021-2022 exercise found 7 of 19 livelihood zones not resilient to climate change amid multiple non-climate constraints. [LBY-E-009]
- Weak basic services increase shock sensitivity. [LBY-E-010]
- UNICEF's October 2023 assessment documented differentiated protection, service and education impacts after Storm Daniel. [LBY-E-024]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- By mid-2025, local climate-action training had not produced implemented local plans or climate-resilient municipal projects. [LBY-E-012]
- A national drought-planning framework now exists. [LBY-E-013]
- Water strategy targets structural scarcity and system losses. [LBY-E-014]
- For 2025, UN reporting attributed more reliable climate-resilient water access to 642,600 people and reported 40 solar-powered installations plus technician training. [LBY-E-015]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- By mid-2025, Libya still lacked a submitted NDC and the evaluated programme had not produced a finalized comprehensive climate framework. [LBY-E-011]
- By June 2025, the evaluation reported 71% delivery, about US$1.3 million unspent, weak outcome monitoring and an overly ambitious implementation window. [LBY-E-016]
- SIPRI's September 2023 Derna account found fragmentation and securitized access impeded response and contributed to exclusion. [LBY-E-022]
- Political-economic structure constrains resilience capacity independently of climate. [LBY-E-023]
- Formal UNFCCC reporting remains incomplete. [LBY-E-025]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Derna demonstrates compound disaster risk, not simple climate causation. (World Weather Attribution) [LBY-P-001; LBY-E-001, LBY-E-002, LBY-E-006, LBY-E-007, LBY-E-017]
  - Documented impact: Flooding, dam failures, widespread physical damage, service disruption and displacement.
  - FCV mediator: Infrastructure maintenance, institutional fragmentation, disaster planning, warning interpretation and coordination.
  - Possible consequence: Extreme rainfall can produce much larger human and economic losses where dam safety, maintenance, emergency governance and exposure are inadequate.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBY-E-001', 'LBY-E-002'], 'impact': ['LBY-E-006', 'LBY-E-007'], 'mediator': ['LBY-E-017'], 'consequence': ['LBY-E-006', 'LBY-E-017']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Dam design', 'reservoir condition', 'land-use/exposure', 'infrastructure quality and emergency decisions independently affected losses']
  - Uncertainty: The magnitude of anthropogenic influence on the rainfall is highly uncertain; no evidence apportions total losses quantitatively among rainfall, dams and governance.
- FCV-related institutional fragmentation is a documented constraint on climate resilience. [LBY-P-002; LBY-E-003, LBY-E-004, LBY-E-011, LBY-E-012, LBY-E-016, LBY-E-017, LBY-E-023]
  - Documented impact: Weak/incomplete climate planning, degraded infrastructure and uneven emergency/recovery capacity.
  - FCV mediator: Divided governance, institutional rivalry, weak coordination and conflict legacy.
  - Possible consequence: Fragmentation may reduce the maintenance, planning and coordination needed to prevent climate shocks from becoming disasters.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBY-E-003', 'LBY-E-004'], 'impact': ['LBY-E-017'], 'mediator': ['LBY-E-011', 'LBY-E-023'], 'consequence': ['LBY-E-012', 'LBY-E-016', 'LBY-E-017']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Resource scarcity', 'technical skill gaps', 'financing', 'procurement and sanctions/external constraints may also affect implementation']
  - Uncertainty: Direct evidence varies by institution; national institutional weakness does not imply identical municipal performance.
- Water scarcity becomes an FCV risk through governance and livelihoods, not through climate alone. (SIPRI) [LBY-P-003; LBY-E-003, LBY-E-004, LBY-E-009, LBY-E-018, LBY-E-019]
  - Documented impact: Water stress, irrigation pressure and reduced rural livelihood resilience.
  - FCV mediator: Water allocation, livelihood dependence, inequality, institutional legitimacy and market conditions.
  - Possible consequence: Where access is perceived as inequitable and livelihood alternatives are weak, worsening water stress could increase grievances or local tension, but violence is not an automatic result.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBY-E-003', 'LBY-E-004'], 'impact': ['LBY-E-009'], 'mediator': ['LBY-E-019'], 'consequence': ['LBY-E-018']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Groundwater over-abstraction', 'allocation decisions', 'infrastructure losses', 'energy availability', 'food prices', 'political exclusion and armed competition']
  - Uncertainty: No strong national causal estimate links temperature or rainfall change to violent events.
- Water dependence is not intrinsically conflictual; institutions determine whether it divides or connects communities. (SIPRI) [LBY-P-004; LBY-E-014, LBY-E-019, LBY-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Dependence on shared, vulnerable water infrastructure.
  - FCV mediator: Allocation rules, control of pumps/infrastructure, perceptions of fairness and mutual dependence.
  - Possible consequence: Scarcity and shared infrastructure may produce either contestation or cooperation depending on governance arrangements and incentives.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBY-E-014'], 'impact': ['LBY-E-019'], 'mediator': ['LBY-E-019', 'LBY-E-020'], 'consequence': ['LBY-E-020']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Political rivalry', 'infrastructure condition and local power relationships can drive both service interruptions and cooperation independently of climate']
  - Uncertainty: Duration and transferability of cooperative arrangements are not evaluated.
- Climate may be one migration stressor, but Libya's mobility dynamics remain strongly multi-causal. (SIPRI) [LBY-P-005; LBY-E-008, LBY-E-009, LBY-E-021]
  - Documented impact: Livelihood disruption among some migrants and climate-sensitive communities.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict, income loss, migration policy, protection conditions, routes, markets and household resources.
  - Possible consequence: Climate shocks may contribute to decisions to move, but available Libyan evidence does not establish them as a dominant or sufficient cause of migration.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['LBY-E-021'], 'impact': ['LBY-E-009', 'LBY-E-021'], 'mediator': ['LBY-E-008', 'LBY-E-021'], 'consequence': ['LBY-E-021']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Armed conflict', 'persecution', 'employment', 'currency conditions', 'household networks and migration policy']
  - Uncertainty: The 25% climate-shock figure is sample-based association, not causal attribution.
- Political-security fragmentation affected who could deliver and receive recovery assistance. (SIPRI) [LBY-P-006; LBY-E-001, LBY-E-006, LBY-E-007, LBY-E-017, LBY-E-022]
  - Documented impact: Humanitarian, service and reconstruction needs after Derna.
  - FCV mediator: Competing authorities, checkpoints, securitized access, institutional rivalry and social/legal status.
  - Possible consequence: Fragmented security governance can delay aid and make recovery less inclusive, particularly for groups with weak protection or documentation.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Physical access damage', 'scale of destruction and administrative capacity also constrained response']
  - Uncertainty: Available evidence is predominantly qualitative regarding the magnitude of delay/exclusion.
- Climate exposure intersects with pre-existing service and protection inequalities. [LBY-P-007; LBY-E-001, LBY-E-003, LBY-E-004, LBY-E-005, LBY-E-007, LBY-E-008, LBY-E-010, LBY-E-022, LBY-E-024]
  - Documented impact: WASH, health and education disruption; unreliable services.
  - FCV mediator: Pre-existing public-service weakness, displacement status, poverty, documentation and gender/age-related vulnerability.
  - Possible consequence: Climate shocks may create larger welfare losses for people already facing weak services or barriers to assistance.

- Libya has emerging resilience assets, but implementation rather than policy adoption is the critical test. (UNDP) [LBY-P-008; LBY-E-003, LBY-E-004, LBY-E-011, LBY-E-012, LBY-E-013, LBY-E-014, LBY-E-015, LBY-E-020]
  - Documented impact: Recognition of planning, infrastructure and technical-capacity deficits.
  - FCV mediator: Institutional coordination, financing, local implementation capacity and shared-resource governance.
  - Possible consequence: The new drought and water strategies and local water/DRR investments could strengthen resilience and create practical cooperation incentives if implementation becomes sustained and inclusive.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Strategies may remain underfunded or fragmented', 'infrastructure investments may fail without maintenance and governance reform']
  - Uncertainty: Independent outcome evidence for 2025-2026 initiatives is not yet available.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- For September 2023, WWA found fragility contributed to poor dam maintenance and constrained planning and coordination. [LBY-E-017]
- Climate-livelihood stress is a conditional conflict-risk factor. [LBY-E-018]
- Water governance can mediate local grievance and disruption. [LBY-E-019]
- Shared water dependence can create cooperation incentives. [LBY-E-020]
- Climate exposure appears in some migrants' histories but does not establish climate-caused migration. [LBY-E-021]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National-level climate and institutional evidence should be kept separate from event-specific and local evidence. Derna and the surrounding eastern municipalities provide the strongest disaster and FCV-to-resilience evidence, but those findings cannot be assumed to describe Tripoli, Misrata, Fezzan or other municipalities. The RDNA itself covers 20 eastern municipalities and concentrates the greatest losses in a smaller subset. (World Bank) [LBY-E-001, LBY-E-002, LBY-E-006, LBY-E-007, LBY-E-017, LBY-E-022, LBY-E-024, LBY-P-001, LBY-P-006]
- Fezzan evidence on Great Man-Made River governance is useful for demonstrating plausible local water-governance pathways but should not be generalized to Libya's entire water system or to national conflict dynamics. Likewise, the local cooperation examples in the SIPRI synthesis demonstrate the possibility of resilience/peace-supporting institutions, not their nationwide prevalence. (SIPRI) [LBY-E-019, LBY-E-020, LBY-P-004]
- Coastal Libya has unusually high population concentration, creating exposure to coastal and sea-level hazards, whereas southern areas face distinctive groundwater, distance, infrastructure and service-access constraints. Recent UN programmes report water interventions in southern municipalities, but these outputs cannot establish equal coverage across the south. (UNDP) [LBY-E-005, LBY-E-014, LBY-E-015, LBY-P-004, LBY-P-008]
- Refugee and migrant vulnerability is geographically uneven and changes rapidly with conflict and migration routes. The large Sudanese influx documented in 2025 reflects the war in Sudan and border conditions; it must not be relabelled as climate displacement. (UNHCR) [LBY-E-008, LBY-E-021, LBY-E-022, LBY-P-005, LBY-P-006]
- Important underrepresented areas/populations include rural household-level vulnerability outside assessed livelihood zones; pastoral and customary institutions; informal settlements; people with disabilities; gender-disaggregated adaptation outcomes; and communities under different competing authorities for which comparable municipal capacity data are unavailable. [LBY-E-005, LBY-E-008, LBY-E-009, LBY-E-024, LBY-P-007]
- *Relatively well covered: water and groundwater, dams, disaster risk management, municipal WASH, agriculture/rural livelihoods, public-service delivery, humanitarian response and national climate/water planning. (UNDP) [LBY-E-003, LBY-E-004, LBY-E-006, LBY-E-007, LBY-E-009, LBY-E-013, LBY-E-014, LBY-E-015, LBY-E-017, LBY-E-019, LBY-E-020, LBY-E-022, LBY-P-001, LBY-P-003, LBY-P-004, LBY-P-006, LBY-P-008]
- *Partially covered: energy transition, urban climate resilience, coastal systems, ecosystem degradation and food systems. Energy governance appears in the independent UNDP evaluation and hydrocarbon dependence in World Bank evidence, but direct physical-climate effects on energy infrastructure are not comprehensively assessed. [LBY-E-005, LBY-E-011, LBY-E-016, LBY-E-023, LBY-P-002, LBY-P-007, LBY-P-008]
- *Thin or absent: fisheries, detailed pastoral systems, biodiversity/ecosystem services, public-health impacts of heat, climate-sensitive disease, quantified coastal asset exposure, customary resource governance, and independently evaluated private-sector adaptation. [LBY-E-005, LBY-E-009, LBY-E-018, LBY-E-021, LBY-E-024, LBY-P-003, LBY-P-005, LBY-P-007]
