## Climate pressures and exposure

- WWA estimated the 10 September 2023 extreme rainfall over its eastern Libya analysis region as approximately a 1-in-300 to 1-in-600-year event in the present climate. [LBY-E-001]
- For September 2023, WWA estimated warming may have made the Libya rainfall up to 50 times more likely and 50% more intense, while uncertainty includes no detectable change. [LBY-E-002]
- SIPRI reports a mean annual temperature of about 22.92 degrees C and projected warming of 1.42 to 2.69 degrees C by 2060. Scenario: 2060; underlying scenario not specified in accessible fact sheet [LBY-E-003]
- SIPRI reports projected mean annual precipitation decline of about 7% by 2050 while heavy-rainfall intensity is expected to increase. Scenario: 2050; scenario not specified in accessible fact sheet [LBY-E-004]
- UNDP reports that roughly 70% of Libya's population lives along the coast, placing substantial population and assets in areas potentially exposed to sea-level and coastal hazards. [LBY-E-005]
- The Derna RDNA summary estimated about 1.5 million people - 22% of Libya's population - were affected in the assessed flood area and nearly 44,800 people were displaced. [LBY-E-006]
- UNHCR estimated that about 467,000 Sudanese refugees had arrived in Libya since April 2023 by the time of its October 2025 update. [LBY-E-008]
- Source dated 2026-07-16 reports: FAO reports dry October-November 2025 weather delayed planting and reduced area; March 2026 rainfall improved some regions but remained well below average in cereal-producing Al Jabal al Akhdar. [LBY-E-101]
- FAO's official summary of its rapid geospatial assessment reports 36,245 flooded hectares in Benghazi following the September 2023 eastern Libya disaster. This is mapped flood exposure, not a ranking of fatalities or total human impacts. [LBY-E-104]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- UNICEF documented severe post-Storm-Daniel disruption to WASH and health facilities, including damaged water infrastructure, water shortages and non-functional or partially functional health facilities in affected eastern areas. [LBY-E-007]
- WFP's 2021-2022 resilience exercise found 7 of 19 assessed livelihood zones were not resilient to climate change, while agricultural access was also constrained by conflict, COVID-19, water and irrigation limitations. [LBY-E-009]
- The World Bank reports persistent weakness and regional disparities in health, education, water and electricity service delivery amid institutional fragmentation. [LBY-E-010]
- UNICEF's early post-Derna assessment identified women, girls and children among groups facing heightened protection and service risks after the flood and documented substantial education-facility damage. [LBY-E-024]
- The 16 July 2026 FAO brief forecasts 2026 cereal output of about 150,000 tonnes, almost 20% below the five-year average, and 2026/27 imports of 3.3 million tonnes; neither is verified final production. Scenario: FAO forecast; no emissions scenario. [LBY-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-07-16 reports: FAO reports 18 percent annual food inflation in May 2026; political and economic instability undermines livelihoods and services, while funding limits assistance to refugees, migrants and internally displaced people. [LBY-E-103]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Although the evaluated programme trained local actors in local climate action planning, the independent evaluation found no local climate action plans had subsequently been developed and implemented through that activity. [LBY-E-012]
- Libya's National Drought Management Strategy and Action Plan, published in January 2026, sets out a ten-year, multi-sector framework for integrated drought management. [LBY-E-013]
- The 2050 National Water Security Strategy was officially endorsed in April 2026; the launch source reports that over 90% of current water supply relies on non-renewable groundwater and sets goals including lower consumption, reduced groundwater dependence and network losses below 25%. [LBY-E-014]
- UN reporting in June 2026 stated that approximately 642,600 people had been reached with more reliable climate-resilient water systems, including 40 solar-powered pumping or treatment installations, and that local technicians had been trained. [LBY-E-015]
- The UN 2025 results report describes solar panels under WFP's Hayat project and a water-food nexus programme in Ubari, Ghat, Tahala and Alberkit: four desalination units and ten solar-powered extraction systems reportedly benefited more than 150,000 people. [LBY-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-05-03 reports: The report states restored wells provided water access to 75 percent of Qatroun's population, while a Yefren integrated water-resource plan delivered long-distance water to 49,000 residents, 52 percent of them women. [LBY-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The independent UNDP evaluation found a fragmented institutional landscape, weak coordination and limited technical capacity, and reported that by mid-2025 no finalized/endorsed NDC or comprehensive national adaptation/mitigation framework had resulted from the evaluated work. [LBY-E-011]
- The independent UNDP evaluation reported approximately 71% delivery by June 2025, about US$1.3 million in unspent EU funds, weaknesses in outcome-focused monitoring and an implementation window judged overly ambitious. [LBY-E-016]
- SIPRI reports that fragmentation, checkpoints and security competition impeded aspects of the Derna response and that migrants/refugees experienced exclusion or discrimination in parts of the recovery environment. [LBY-E-022]
- The World Bank identifies institutional fragmentation and hydrocarbon dependence as central structural constraints and reports continuing regional disparities in public services. [LBY-E-023]
- The UNFCCC National Communication registry listed no Libyan National Communication submission in the Libya row when accessed in August 2026. [LBY-E-025]
- Source dated 2025-01-19 reports: The assessment says unresolved conflicts in Murzuq and Kufra eroded cohesion and stalled reconciliation; climate adaptation and disaster-risk efforts are fragmented and underfunded, leaving communities vulnerable. [LBY-E-106]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Derna demonstrates compound disaster risk, not simple climate causation. (World Weather Attribution) [LBY-P-001; LBY-E-001, LBY-E-002, LBY-E-006, LBY-E-007, LBY-E-017]
  - Documented impact: Flooding, dam failures, widespread physical damage, service disruption and displacement.
  - FCV mediator: Infrastructure maintenance, institutional fragmentation, disaster planning, warning interpretation and coordination.
  - Possible consequence: Extreme rainfall can produce much larger human and economic losses where dam safety, maintenance, emergency governance and exposure are inadequate.

  - Documented impact: Weak/incomplete climate planning, degraded infrastructure and uneven emergency/recovery capacity.
  - FCV mediator: Divided governance, institutional rivalry, weak coordination and conflict legacy.
  - Possible consequence: Fragmentation may reduce the maintenance, planning and coordination needed to prevent climate shocks from becoming disasters.

  - FCV mediator: Water allocation, livelihood dependence, inequality, institutional legitimacy and market conditions.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- WWA found that conflict and state fragility contributed to deteriorating infrastructure and inadequate dam maintenance and constrained adaptation planning and disaster coordination before the Derna catastrophe. [LBY-E-017]
- SIPRI assesses that climate- and water-related livelihood pressures may increase risks of grievances or intercommunal tensions, while stressing that climate is rarely the principal driver of conflict in Libya. [LBY-E-018]
- SIPRI documents Fezzan examples in which perceived inequity in Great Man-Made River water management contributed to contestation and interference with pumps. [LBY-E-019]
- The same SIPRI assessment records that shared reliance on water infrastructure has also generated cooperation among communities and armed actors in maintaining access. [LBY-E-020]
- SIPRI reports that about 25% of migrants in an IOM sample had experienced a climate shock or stressor in the year before migrating, while the fact sheet treats migration as multi-causal. [LBY-E-021]
- Source dated 2025-01-19 reports: The UN names Sebha, Murzuq, Ghat, Kufra and Ubari among municipalities facing heat, water scarcity and drought, alongside service deficits, conflict and weak governance; women, youth and displaced people face disproportionate service gaps. [LBY-E-105]
