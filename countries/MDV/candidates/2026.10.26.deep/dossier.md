Critical remaining gaps:
- No inspected Maldives source explicitly links climate hazards to conflict or violence; no such pathway is asserted. []
- The full CCDR and CEA PDFs could not be inspected; projection and counterfactual methods are therefore not independently detailed here. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []
- The bank contains no completed mediated climate-FCV pathway; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- No reviewed primary source tied armed conflict or political violence to Maldives climate exposure or documented insecurity preventing a named adaptation activity; absence of evidence in this search is not evidence of no link. []
- The 2021 CCA's climate-related food-scarcity and transactional-sex pathway is a conditional historical risk statement, not a measured rate or present-day estimate. []
- The ESCAP report's exposure projections have model, population, land-use and infrastructure-location limitations; some islands are undercounted or unverified. []
- NDMA's 2024 incident total combines climate and non-climate hazards and its own report identifies remote-island reporting, demographic disaggregation, aid-monitoring and anticipatory-planning gaps. []
- The large government ECRFS Gender Assessment and Action Plan PDF was not fully accessible through web retrieval; only its official indexed excerpt was inspected and it is not used for claims in this file. []
- The CCA 2024 original was reported fully inspected by the collector, but the coordinator's publication-page requests timed out; independent reopening of those passages remains an approval check. []
- Disability-related exclusion is classified as sensitivity, not automatically as violence. The earlier CCA's exploitative-coping pathway is a conditional synthesis, not country-specific incidence. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The summary projects 0.5–0.9 m sea-level rise by 2100; 10-year floods could damage up to 3.3% of assets (US$0.7–1.1bn). [MDV-E-002]
- The NDC describes the country's low-lying narrow islands as exposed to sea-level rise and increasingly unpredictable rainfall and extreme weather, with risks to infrastructure, ecosystems, food and water security, and livelihoods. [MDV-E-005]
- Source dated 2024-11-28 reports: CMIP6 projects greatest rainfall and flood risk centrally, increases in northern atolls and declines in named southern atolls. [MDV-E-101]
- ESCAP's exposure model estimates 47% of Meemu's population and over 30% in Alifu Dhaalu, Haa Alifu, Haa Dhaalu and Kaafu exposed under its greater-than-one-metre sea-level-rise scenarios by 2060. [MDV-E-106]
- ESCAP models high-precipitation exposure for agricultural land in Kaafu and on Thoddo/Thoddoo and Kashidhoo, with low-elevation agricultural exposure in Gnaviyani, Seenu, Haa Dhaalu and Raa. [MDV-E-108]
- Source dated not stated reports: NDMA recorded 251 mixed-hazard incidents across 20 atolls in 2024 and 1,423 flood-affected households; Kaafu, Seenu and Laamu had the most reported rainwater floods. [MDV-E-109]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Tourism and fisheries account for nearly half of Maldives GDP and employment; reef and fishery climate impacts are evident and projected to worsen by mid-century. [MDV-E-001]
- The NDC identifies exposure of public utilities, health services, transport, and telecommunications and says climate action should address the needs of women, youth, and other vulnerable groups. [MDV-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-11-28 reports: CCA says migrant precarity and weak legal protection heighten climate-related displacement and livelihood risks. [MDV-E-102]
- The January 2021 CCA discusses gendered water/hygiene burdens and cites a conditional pathway from livelihood or food stress to exploitative coping, including transactional sex. [MDV-E-105]
- ESCAP describes inaccessible warning/preparedness information for people with disabilities and models substantial future rainfall exposure. [MDV-E-107]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The summary says nearly all shallow coral cover could be lost if global temperatures exceed 2°C and recommends island-specific planning that incorporates sea-level rise, flooding, and ocean heating. [MDV-E-003]
- The CEA recommends strengthening environmental agency capacity and empowering island and atoll councils to manage protected areas and participate in monitoring. [MDV-E-004]
- Source dated not stated reports: ECRFS and Maldives Meteorological Service discussed a localized farm-weather pilot for Addu Meedhoo, but still planned to assess feasibility and data needs. [MDV-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-11-28 reports: CCA flags local risk-data capacity, community participation and cross-sector integration gaps. [MDV-E-103]
- Source dated not stated reports: NDMA reports remote-island underreporting, limited demographic disaggregation, no systematic aid-effectiveness tracking and mostly reactive response without predefined island contingency plans. [MDV-E-110]
- Source dated not stated reports: ECRFS reported practical climate-smart gardening training for 88 students and school staff at Ghaazee School in August 2026, with continued technical support planned. [MDV-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-09-08 reports: UNDRR reports its technical support helped align the Maldives' national DRR Strategy 2024-2030 with adaptation planning and integrate hazard assessments across government agencies. [MDV-E-113]
