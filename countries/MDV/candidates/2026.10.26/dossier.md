# Maldives Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- The summary projects 0.5–0.9 m sea-level rise by 2100; 10-year floods could damage up to 3.3% of assets (US$0.7–1.1bn). [MDV-E-002]
- Tourism and fisheries account for nearly half of Maldives GDP and employment; reef and fishery climate impacts are evident and projected to worsen by mid-century. [MDV-E-001]
- The summary says nearly all shallow coral cover could be lost if global temperatures exceed 2°C and recommends island-specific planning that incorporates sea-level rise, flooding, and ocean heating. [MDV-E-003]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The summary projects 0.5–0.9 m sea-level rise by 2100; 10-year floods could damage up to 3.3% of assets (US$0.7–1.1bn). [MDV-E-002]
- The NDC describes the country's low-lying narrow islands as exposed to sea-level rise and increasingly unpredictable rainfall and extreme weather, with risks to infrastructure, ecosystems, food and water security, and livelihoods. [MDV-E-005]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Tourism and fisheries account for nearly half of Maldives GDP and employment; reef and fishery climate impacts are evident and projected to worsen by mid-century. [MDV-E-001]
- The NDC identifies exposure of public utilities, health services, transport, and telecommunications and says climate action should address the needs of women, youth, and other vulnerable groups. [MDV-E-006]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The summary says nearly all shallow coral cover could be lost if global temperatures exceed 2°C and recommends island-specific planning that incorporates sea-level rise, flooding, and ocean heating. [MDV-E-003]
- The CEA recommends strengthening environmental agency capacity and empowering island and atoll councils to manage protected areas and participate in monitoring. [MDV-E-004]
