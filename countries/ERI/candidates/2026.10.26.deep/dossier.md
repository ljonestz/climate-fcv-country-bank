## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC reports a 17-30% rainfall decline in 1900-2018, with different temperature trends on the plateau and coast. [ERI-E-001]
- NDC presents RCP2.6/RCP8.5 projections against 1986-2006, including 2030/2050 precipitation maps. [ERI-E-002]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: For solar-asset planning, GCA describes lowland and coastal rainfall below 100 mm with dry spells over 200 days, versus wetter highlands; drought hazard is very high across most Eritrea and dust/wind risk is elevated in Anseba. [ERI-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-03 reports: For 2030–2060, mean annual rainfall is projected to fall by up to 100 mm on the Eritrean Plateau, with slight increases in western lowlands and southern areas; wet-day changes are small under RCP4.5 and RCP8.5. [ERI-E-111]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Consultation ranks farmers, pastoralists, rural residents, fishers and urban poor among groups most vulnerable to climate impacts. [ERI-E-003]
- NDC identifies 23 land-degradation hotspots (9.8% of Eritrea) and reports crop-variety loss, pests and livestock deaths among drought concerns. [ERI-E-005]
- UNDP cites about 500,000 cultivated hectares, 93% rain-fed, and says low, erratic rainfall and land degradation expose rural communities to drought risks. [ERI-E-006]
- Source dated 2002 reports: Rapid borehole development in Gash-Barka changed water use and encouraged larger livestock populations; the study cautioned that resource pressure could threaten poor returnee and stayee livelihoods. [ERI-E-106]
- Source dated 2002 reports: Around Goluj, respondents linked scarcer fuelwood to population-related forest depletion; poor households lacking transport or money reduced use, while animal owners could access markets. [ERI-E-107]
- Source dated 2012-11 reports: UNDP’s case study reports erratic below-average rain in 2002–2004, especially in parts of Habero, caused livestock losses and impaired livelihoods; annual rain ranged from 190 mm in Habero to about 367 mm in Hamelmalo. [ERI-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- NDC reports three major droughts in the prior 30 years and water constraints from low rainfall, few permanent rivers and insufficient storage. [ERI-E-004]
- The proposal reports that seasonal Anseba River baseflow lasted only a few months and groundwater lay 2–3 m below the stream, constraining local water access. [ERI-E-007]
- Source dated 2002 reports: After the border war disrupted transhumance and Tekezze-Setit basin watering access, herds shifted to wells around Goluj and nearby return sites; the study reports emerging farmer–livestock-owner conflict. [ERI-E-105]
- Source dated 2012-11 reports: Anseba herders moved west in July–September and toward the eastern escarpment or Northern Red Sea in November–March; land-use pressure, degradation and settlement policies disrupted this practice. [ERI-E-109]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-01-28 reports: At five consulted project sites, dams, diversions and water-harvesting structures still supported irrigation, pastoralism and hygiene; siltation and maintenance problems limited sustainability. [ERI-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-01-28 reports: Ministry-led MIHAP replication sustained agricultural diversification and livestock production; Anseba authorities reported 58 of 640 participating households became small or medium commercial enterprises. [ERI-E-102]
- The ex-post evaluation observed three of six hydromet stations: all three generated data, but two had partial functionality. Data were not centrally automated and farm advice was uneven. [ERI-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-01-28 reports: The evaluation found women’s participation and leadership sustained after the project; women reported reduced water-fetching burdens, and children’s school attendance improved. [ERI-E-104]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Near Goluj, border-war restrictions narrowed seasonal livestock water access and concentrated local resource pressures; findings are historical and exceptional. [ERI-P-101; ERI-E-105, ERI-E-106]
  - Documented impact: War left Tekezze–Setit watering areas inaccessible and concentrated herds around Goluj and reintegration sites.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict-related loss of transhumance/watering access and concentrated use of wells and cropland.
  - Possible consequence: Narrower seasonal coping options and localized livestock–farmer tensions were reported.
