# South Sudan Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- The bank now establishes a robust direction of future warming and explicit precipitation uncertainty from South Sudan CMIP6 views, alongside observed severe-year flood exposure; it still lacks a complete extracted set of numeric hazard magnitudes across scenarios, horizons, extremes, and subnational areas. [SSD-E-003, SSD-E-020, SSD-E-021, SSD-E-027, SSD-P-001, SSD-P-003]
- Differentiated vulnerability is documented for selected flood-affected locations and for women and girls, but the bank does not support complete national or group-specific coverage. [SSD-E-004, SSD-E-006, SSD-E-009, SSD-P-004, SSD-P-005]
- Selected safety-net and community-governance programs demonstrate household, digital-delivery, local-planning, and infrastructure capacities, while third-party dependence, limited geographic reach, and the lack of independent evaluation prevent national generalization. [SSD-E-022, SSD-E-023, SSD-E-024, SSD-E-025, SSD-E-026, SSD-P-006]

Critical remaining gaps:
- CCKP establishes future warming direction and precipitation uncertainty, but the bank still lacks a complete extraction of numeric p10, p50, and p90 temperature, precipitation, variability, and extreme-event projections across scenarios, horizons, seasons, and subnational areas. [SSD-E-020, SSD-E-021]
- Adaptive capacity is demonstrated only for selected social-protection beneficiaries and community institutions; evidence remains insufficient for national systems, climate-specific capabilities, durability, and excluded locations or groups. [SSD-E-022, SSD-E-023, SSD-E-024, SSD-E-026]
- Official project monitoring reports reach, functionality, and beneficiary outcomes, but no independent evaluation establishes causal effectiveness, delivery failure across the portfolio, durability, or unintended effects. [SSD-E-018, SSD-E-025, SSD-E-026]
- Dedicated evidence is missing for fisheries, forests, the Sudd wetland system, disability-differentiated vulnerability, and several states. [SSD-E-004, SSD-E-009, SSD-E-014]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Use ERA5 as light climate context, retaining its model and precipitation-trend caveats. [SSD-E-001]
- Screen for changing flood persistence and dry-season water stress, not a single uniform hazard trend. [SSD-E-002]
- Screen agrifood investments for projected heat-related sorghum yield risk through 2050. [SSD-E-003]
- Map all-season access to facilities and aid routes, including the duration of flood disruption. [SSD-E-005]
- Treat violence, seasonal roads, service functionality, and secondary displacement as one access-risk screen. [SSD-E-007]
- Use current event tracking to size immediate health, shelter, and food needs without treating it as a displacement stock. [SSD-E-010]
- Treat future warming as a robust direction while retaining scenario and model-spread uncertainty. [SSD-E-020]
- Do not treat future rainfall direction or magnitude as settled without scenario- and location-specific extraction. [SSD-E-021]
- Screen access, services, and livelihoods for severe-year flood exposure that can affect a large share of national territory. [SSD-E-027]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Screen livelihood recovery and host-community capacity together because flood displacement can reverse hosting roles. [SSD-E-006]
- Integrate GBV, child-marriage, reproductive-health, and girls' education safeguards into climate response. [SSD-E-009]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Assess whether assistance strengthens viable livelihoods and reciprocal coping rather than replacing them. [SSD-E-004]
- Use INFORM to flag compound vulnerability and coping constraints, not to rank individual project sites. [SSD-E-008]
- Selected beneficiaries demonstrated asset and savings gains, but these cannot be generalized nationally or attributed specifically to climate adaptation. [SSD-E-022]
- Selected counties demonstrated functioning community institutions for planning and maintaining local resilience investments. [SSD-E-024]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Check whether projects close documented planning, coordination, capacity, budget, and early-warning gaps. [SSD-E-011]
- Align activities with NDC sector priorities while testing participation and implementation capacity locally. [SSD-E-012]
- Conflict-sensitive flood response combined high-ground preparation with community dialogue [SSD-E-018]
- A selected program demonstrated functioning payment and grievance systems, while wider government delivery capacity remains uncertain. [SSD-E-023]
- Selected infrastructure and flood defenses show reported functionality and reach, pending independent outcome verification. [SSD-E-025]
- Treat third-party delivery dependence and weak government maintenance capacity as material implementation constraints. [SSD-E-026]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Repeated flooding may increase localized host-displaced tension when displacement concentrates people on scarce high ground with limited services and livelihood resources. [SSD-P-001; SSD-E-002, SSD-E-006, SSD-E-010, SSD-E-013, SSD-E-018]
  - Documented impact: Flood displacement, prolonged stays on high ground, livelihood loss, and pressure on receiving communities
  - FCV mediator: Scarce dry land and services, repeated displacement, weak infrastructure, and strained host-community resources
  - Possible consequence: Localized disputes or tensions may become more likely where displaced and host communities must share limited land, water, services, and livelihood resources

  - Alternative explanations: ['Conflict, poverty, service deficits, and pre-existing land disputes can independently drive displacement and host-community tension.']
  - Uncertainty: Localized pressure is documented; tension is neither universal nor primarily attributable to flooding.
- Climate-related livelihood and mobility changes may raise farmer-herder tension when customary route agreements and dispute-resolution arrangements are weak. [SSD-P-002; SSD-E-002, SSD-E-004, SSD-E-014, SSD-E-017]
  - Documented impact: Altered pastoral mobility, livelihood disruption, and greater interaction between cattle keepers and farming communities
  - FCV mediator: Weakened customary route agreements, weapons availability, cattle-raiding dynamics, and uneven local dispute-resolution capacity
  - Possible consequence: Farmer-herder disputes and cattle-related violence may intensify where changed mobility is not coordinated through trusted agreements
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SSD-E-002'], 'impact': ['SSD-E-004', 'SSD-E-014', 'SSD-E-017'], 'mediator': ['SSD-E-014', 'SSD-E-017'], 'consequence': ['SSD-E-014', 'SSD-E-017']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Political mobilization, cattle-raiding economies, weapons availability, and unresolved land claims can drive violence without a climate shock.']
  - Uncertainty: Mobility can support adaptation; tension depends on weak coordination and cannot be generalized.
- Flooded infrastructure and insecurity can jointly restrict service and humanitarian access, with possible implications for exclusion and grievance. [SSD-P-003; SSD-E-005, SSD-E-007]
  - Documented impact: Damaged or impassable roads and facilities, interrupted public services, and reduced humanitarian reach
  - FCV mediator: Violence, insecurity, limited infrastructure redundancy, and uneven public and humanitarian presence
  - Possible consequence: Unequal or delayed access to health, food, education, water, and protection may deepen exclusion and grievances in already fragile locations
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SSD-E-005', 'SSD-E-007'], 'impact': ['SSD-E-005', 'SSD-E-007'], 'mediator': ['SSD-E-007'], 'consequence': ['SSD-E-007']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Chronic underinvestment, poor maintenance, administrative exclusion, and active conflict can restrict services even without flooding.']
  - Uncertainty: Joint access constraints are documented; grievance or violence effects are unmeasured.
- Repeated shocks may increase harmful coping and protection risks when conflict, poverty, displacement, and weak markets leave households few livelihood alternatives. [SSD-P-004; SSD-E-004, SSD-E-006, SSD-E-008, SSD-E-009, SSD-E-017]
  - Documented impact: Food insecurity, livelihood loss, reduced reciprocal support, and reliance on aid or environmentally damaging coping options
  - FCV mediator: Conflict-related market disruption, displacement, poverty, gender inequality, and limited livelihood alternatives
  - Possible consequence: Households may adopt harmful coping strategies or face heightened recruitment, exploitation, or protection risks when food and livelihood options contract
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SSD-E-004', 'SSD-E-006', 'SSD-E-009'], 'impact': ['SSD-E-004', 'SSD-E-006', 'SSD-E-008', 'SSD-E-009'], 'mediator': ['SSD-E-008', 'SSD-E-009', 'SSD-E-017'], 'consequence': ['SSD-E-009', 'SSD-E-017']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Macroeconomic crisis, armed conflict, displacement, market failure, and aid shortfalls may independently produce food insecurity and harmful coping.']
  - Uncertainty: Food and livelihood stress is documented; harmful coping and recruitment prevalence is unquantified.
- Climate-related displacement and service disruption may heighten gendered protection and health risks where conflict and inequality already restrict safety and services. [SSD-P-005; SSD-E-007, SSD-E-009]
  - Documented impact: Displacement, unsafe shelter, economic hardship, school closure, and reduced health and sexual and reproductive health access
  - FCV mediator: Gender inequality, conflict-related protection gaps, service disruption, and limited safe livelihood options
  - Possible consequence: Women and girls may face heightened gender-based violence, intimate-partner violence, exploitation, child marriage, and health risks
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['SSD-E-009'], 'impact': ['SSD-E-007', 'SSD-E-009'], 'mediator': ['SSD-E-009'], 'consequence': ['SSD-E-009']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Conflict, displacement, economic crisis, and entrenched gender inequality independently create severe protection and health risks.']
  - Uncertainty: Protection risks are underreported; climate, conflict, poverty, and inequality effects remain inseparable.
- Conflict and weak institutions can constrain adaptation capacity when instability, low trust, infrastructure deficits, and implementation gaps disrupt planning and delivery. [SSD-P-006; SSD-E-008, SSD-E-011, SSD-E-012, SSD-E-016, SSD-E-019]
  - Documented impact: High exposure and vulnerability create sustained demand for early warning, public investment, sector planning, and local response capacity
  - FCV mediator: Conflict, political instability, weak rule of law and institutions, limited infrastructure, low trust, and policy and budget implementation gaps
  - Possible consequence: Adaptation investments and warnings may be delayed, uneven, or less effective, leaving households and institutions more vulnerable to subsequent shocks

- Conflict legacies and contested land can restrict climate-related evacuation, return, or relocation where routes and destinations are unsafe or disputed. [SSD-P-007; SSD-E-015]
  - Documented impact: Flood-affected people may need to cross, temporarily occupy, or return to land outside their usual settlements
  - FCV mediator: Landmine contamination, armed-actor presence, land grabbing, contested tenure, and weak land governance
  - Possible consequence: Safe evacuation, return, and relocation options may narrow, while land disputes or protection risks may increase

  - Uncertainty: Examples are not nationally prevalent and include cooperation; local mine, land, and security arrangements determine outcomes.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Plan high-ground relocation with host-resource, land-use, food-security, and coexistence safeguards. [SSD-E-013]
- Support negotiated mobility and resource-sharing rules before changing water, pasture, or cropland access. [SSD-E-014]
- Assess mines, contested tenure, land-grabbing, and negotiated access before evacuation, return, or relocation. [SSD-E-015]
- Screen whether conflict and institutional weakness are constraining climate adaptation capacity. [SSD-E-016]
- Climate-security pathways can pass through livelihoods, mobility, recruitment, and elite exploitation [SSD-E-017]
- Localized forecasting and dialogue face language, finance, supply, and trust constraints [SSD-E-019]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National syntheses coexist with localized evidence from selected counties, payams, bomas, and sites; localized findings must not be generalized to all of South Sudan. [SSD-E-001, SSD-E-004, SSD-E-005, SSD-E-006, SSD-E-009, SSD-E-010]
- Flood-related evidence is concentrated in Unity State and selected locations, leaving substantial state-level geographic gaps. [SSD-E-004, SSD-E-010, SSD-E-013, SSD-P-001]
- New program-result evidence covers ten safety-net counties and twenty community-resilience counties; it improves subnational capacity evidence but must not be generalized to the remaining counties or routine national systems. [SSD-E-022, SSD-E-023, SSD-E-024, SSD-E-025, SSD-E-026]
- Agriculture, livestock, roads, health, education, water, and humanitarian access appear in the bank, but fisheries, forests, and the Sudd wetland system lack dedicated evidence records. [SSD-E-003, SSD-E-004, SSD-E-005, SSD-E-007, SSD-E-009, SSD-E-014, SSD-P-002, SSD-P-003]
- Official monitoring now documents selected safety-net, local-governance, infrastructure, and flood-protection results, including delivery-system and third-party implementation lessons; no independent evaluation establishes causal effectiveness, durability, or unintended effects. [SSD-E-018, SSD-E-019, SSD-E-022, SSD-E-023, SSD-E-024, SSD-E-025, SSD-E-026, SSD-P-006]
