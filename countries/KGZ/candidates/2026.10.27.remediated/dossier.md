## Climate pressures and exposure

- The World Bank summary reports more severe storms, landslides, heat waves, and droughts; inaction could cut GDP 2–3% by 2040 via agriculture and infrastructure damage. Scenario: Policy-inaction case; the summary does not identify its climate-model scenario. [KGZ-E-004]
- The summary estimates up to 24% of irrigation demand could go unmet by mid-century as temperatures and crop-water needs rise. Scenario: The official summary does not specify the climate-model scenario for this estimate. [KGZ-E-006]
- The Isfara supplies irrigation and city water to more than 40 villages in Batken district and downstream Isfara district in Tajikistan. [KGZ-E-101]
- In the 2019 six-village Isfara study, 72% perceived lower winter snowfall and 78% hotter summers; separate snow and upstream precipitation measurements were incomplete. [KGZ-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The 2021 NDC describes the Kyrgyz Republic as highly climate-vulnerable and reliant on climate-sensitive sectors; disasters, disease, infrastructure damage, and reduced water can threaten development, energy, and irrigation. [KGZ-E-001]
- The NDC says women and children in rural households carry much of the work of obtaining domestic water and fuel, while women face barriers to finance, training, technology, and climate decision-making. [KGZ-E-002]
- The summary says inaction could push 170,000 more people into poverty by 2040, especially in rural areas, and reduce jobs and incomes for the bottom 20%. Scenario: Policy-inaction case; the summary does not specify the climate-model scenario. [KGZ-E-005]
- The 2019 Isfara study records perceived irrigation shortages and lower yields associated with erosion, pests and disease; perceived falling river water contrasted with an increasing measured runoff trend. [KGZ-E-103]
- At February 2023 consultations, representatives of Batken, Osh and Jalal-Abad described water shortages, drought, forest fires, heat illness and limited finance; the release is not a completed risk assessment. [KGZ-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The Resilient Landscape Restoration Project aims to benefit over 50,000 rural people, including women and vulnerable groups, across Jalal-Abad, Osh, Issyk-Kul, and Naryn regions. [KGZ-E-007]
- The project plans to improve glacier, snow-cover, and mudflow monitoring and restore landscapes with local labor payments and livelihood training; implementation is planned through 2029. [KGZ-E-008]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The 2020 Isfara research paper describes contested water, pasture and land redistribution and unclear borders as historical conflict drivers; this does not isolate a climate effect or describe the present border settlement. [KGZ-E-104]
- The 2021 regional analysis describes restrictions on cross-border resource sharing and plans for reservoirs or canals without sufficient local consultation, warning of tension risks. [KGZ-E-106]
- International Alert's 2021 analysis describes poorly functioning Water Users Associations in Batken and Sughd as a constraint on improving distribution and cooperation. [KGZ-E-107]
- FAO's May 2024 feature reports canal sensors in Naryn and Batken pilot areas supporting water accounting and rational allocation, and claims irrigation disputes were eliminated. [KGZ-E-109]
- UNDP's February 2024 release describes adaptation-plan development as a next step after February 2023 provincial consultations; later plan status is unverified. [KGZ-E-112]

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- The NDC warns that inequitable access to natural and social resources amid climate pressures can lead to social conflict and calls for women's participation in resource allocation and conflict reduction. Scenario: The NDC cites water-availability decline over 2050-2100; the conflict-risk statement does not specify an emissions scenario. [KGZ-E-003]
- International Alert's 2021 analysis describes water disputes and agreement violations in the Batken-Sughd clashes, with climate, nationalism, politics and COVID-19 adding stress. [KGZ-E-105]
- FAO's May 2024 feature links stream drying in Ak-Bashat to climate change; one farmer describes switching from orchards to cattle and water depletion causing disputes. [KGZ-E-108]
- FAO's May 2024 feature describes community construction of a Kashka-Suu artificial glacier for seasonal water, with additional locally funded structures; benefits were not independently evaluated. [KGZ-E-110]
