Critical remaining gaps:
- The NDC 3.0 provides conditional climate-to-community-tension and displacement links but does not document measured violent conflict or a reverse conflict constraint on adaptation. []
- Recent primary subnational evidence is concentrated on Libreville and coastal localities; comparable municipal vulnerability data for inland provinces were not verified. []
- The 2025 NDC and NAP-II announcement describe plans and conditional risks; implementation and local outcomes remain unevaluated in the reviewed passages. []
- No measured climate-caused violence rate or verified insecurity-to-adaptation constraint was found. National protection and resource-tension narratives must not be assigned to specific coastal towns without evidence. []
- NAP-II consultation locations are planning coverage, not equal local exposure or verified implemented adaptation. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025 reports: The CCDR estimates that 41% of Libreville's built-up area lay in flood-exposed zones in 2021; unplanned settlement and mangrove loss add to urban and coastal risk. [GAB-E-101]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025 reports: The CCDR says poor urban households often occupy flood-prone, weakly planned areas and lack resources to adapt; Gabonese urban populations may face heat 7–8°C above rural areas. [GAB-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: The 2025 NDC identifies sea-level rise and erosion threats to Libreville and Port-Gentil, including port and oil infrastructure, and lists gradual relocation of exposed areas among planned measures. [GAB-E-104]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The Gabon CCDR describes how drought/disaster-related collection burdens can expose rural women and girls to protection risks through gendered work and constrained safe alternatives; this is a conditional mechanism, not an incidence estimate. [GAB-P-101; GAB-E-103]
  - Documented impact: Longer collection journeys and greater unpaid care demands for rural women and girls.
  - FCV mediator: Gendered allocation of domestic work, poverty and unequal access to assets/protection constrain safe alternatives.
  - Possible consequence: The CCDR describes heightened harassment/sexual-assault exposure and lost educational/economic opportunities; no estimated climate-attributable incidence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2025 reports: The CCDR says rural women and girls may walk farther for water and fuel during droughts and disasters, losing time and facing greater harassment and assault risks. [GAB-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: The NDC says growing climate-related pressure on farmland, forests and fishing areas may heighten tensions between communities, weaken social cohesion and make climate-linked displacement more harmful. [GAB-E-105]
