## Executive assessment

- Selected CAPA sites and Belize City neighborhoods add local vulnerability detail; relative indices and expert scores are not national rankings or annual-loss estimates. [BLZ-E-101, BLZ-E-102, BLZ-E-106, BLZ-E-107]
- Participation, tenure and municipal financing constraints affect adaptation. Human-wildlife interactions and an older water-conflict warning do not establish climate-driven human violence. [BLZ-E-103, BLZ-E-104, BLZ-E-109, BLZ-E-112, BLZ-E-114]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No countrywide causal estimate of climate impacts on conflict or violence is established by these sources. []
- Full subnational coverage and independently evaluated long-term response effectiveness remain gaps. []
- All three admitted sources are World Bank accounts; independent corroboration and direct climate-conflict evidence remain gaps. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []
- The bank contains no completed mediated climate-FCV pathway; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- No inspected source establishes that climate change caused interpersonal or political violence in Belize; IISD describes human-wildlife conflict and the older National Communication forecasts possible water conflicts, not observed conflict events. []
- The May 2012 National Communication projections are based on dated models and the reported Belize River saltwater intrusion is not evidence of current conditions. []
- The IISD rankings cover selected CAPA landscape communities and CORVI covers Belize City; neither provides a comprehensive district-by-district national ranking. []
- The 2024 First Biennial Transparency Report timed out, FAO's fisheries vulnerability assessment exceeded the browser content limit, and PDC subnational profiles were listed but not accessible as full text; none of these sources supports claims here. []
- The 2021 CCA reports resource-depletion and competition risks but does not attribute them to climate change; no source inspected established violence as a barrier to adaptation delivery. []
- Human-wildlife conflict is retained as livelihood sensitivity, not human social FCV. []
- No new complete climate-human-FCV chain is inferred from old water-conflict warnings or urban migrant settlement. []
- CAPA participation barriers are not assigned to particular Garifuna settlements without passage-level disaggregation. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Low-lying Belize City is exposed to storm-surge, river and rainfall flooding; experts rated extreme-weather impacts the assessment's highest risk. [BLZ-E-106]
- The assessment projects lower groundwater recharge in northern Belize, where rice, papaya and sugar use water intensively; central districts face similar pressure, but the water-rich south less. [BLZ-E-113]
- The communication edited in May 2012 reports earlier saltwater intrusion upstream on the Belize River near the Double Run intake, with treatment/quality implications; present conditions are unknown. [BLZ-E-115]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Flooding disrupts Belize's roads and access to urban centres; the 2019 drought damaged farming, livestock and fisheries livelihoods. [BLZ-E-001]
- May 2024 wildfires damaged Maya forests and farms in southern Belize, threatening food and cash-income sources. [BLZ-E-003]
- Belize's reef and mangroves support fisheries, tourism and coastal protection; bleaching and reduced fish catch affect coastal livelihoods. [BLZ-E-005]
- The April 2025 CAPA profile describes fishing, processing and tourism dependence in seven selected marine communities, with fishing's cultural importance in Hopkins and Seine Bight. [BLZ-E-101]
- Among the CAPA assessment's selected sites, Placencia/Sarteneja rank highest among seven marine communities and Hattieville/Mahogany Heights among 12 terrestrial communities; this is not a national ranking. [BLZ-E-102]
- In the Maya Forest Corridor, the profile links climate-related biodiversity loss and jaguar habitat destruction to more farm/community encounters and residents killing jaguars to protect livestock. [BLZ-E-103]
- Faber's Road Extension is a low-income flood-susceptible area; Krooman Lagoon and London Bridges show expansion threatening coastal ecosystems. [BLZ-E-107]
- The December 2024 CORVI assessment describes urban expansion—including tourism, residential development and some informal housing—clearing protective wetlands/mangroves and increasing Belize City flood vulnerability. [BLZ-E-108]
- The 2021 UN CCA identifies high poverty and exclusion in Toledo and discusses Maya customary land rights and consultation duties; these are dated social/tenure conditions, not a current legal assessment. [BLZ-E-112]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Marine adaptation combined ecosystem protection with alternative-livelihood training to reduce dependence on fishing. [BLZ-E-006]
- In Sarteneja, vocational training helped a fishing family pursue tourism-related work alongside seasonal fishing restrictions. [BLZ-E-007]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The project reported support for climate-smart agriculture and strengthened risk-information systems across public agencies. [BLZ-E-002]
- The April 2025 CAPA profile describes underrepresentation of women, youth and Indigenous Garifuna in adaptation/resource governance; local inputs are reportedly not fully considered. [BLZ-E-104]
- Belize City's funding dependence and central approval requirements can delay local projects; residents in flood-prone areas often lack electricity, sewerage and clean water. [BLZ-E-109]
- The report states that Belize City governments had installed flood-pumping stations and constructed canals to drain floodwaters. [BLZ-E-110]
- The 2021 UN CCA describes an IOM-supported capacity project concerning internal climate-related migration/displacement, not movement counts or evaluated results. [BLZ-E-111]
