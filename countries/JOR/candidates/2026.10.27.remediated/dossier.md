# Jordan Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Mafraq's Dahiyyat Al-Malik Abdullah study adds local water-network, zoning and displacement-status access barriers, alongside household coping. The small sample and primary role of over-abstraction limit generalization. [JOR-E-101, JOR-E-102, JOR-E-103, JOR-E-104, JOR-E-105, JOR-E-106]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2022 reports: At 1995–2014 baseline, desert areas had over 80 April–September hot days; SSP5-8.5 projects about 40% of summer days near-term and 50% mid-term above 35°C. Scenario: SSP5-8.5; six downscaled models; 2021–2040 and 2041–2060 versus 1995–2014. [JOR-E-111]
- Source dated 2022 reports: Under SSP5-8.5, modeled drought frequency generally rises across Jordan by mid-term; the report identifies Amman and areas south of the city as future flood-risk locations. Scenario: SSP5-8.5; six-model ensemble; 2041–2060 versus 1995–2014. [JOR-E-112]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The 2022 CCDR reports 97 m³ of water per person annually and projects climate change could reduce per-capita availability about 30% by 2040 while raising demand. Scenario: The inspected executive-summary passage does not specify a climate scenario. [JOR-E-113]

- Source dated 2026 reports: In Dahiyyat Al-Malik Abdullah, 2022 Amman–Zarqa abstraction was 162.4 MCM versus 87.5 MCM safe yield; the report identifies chronic hydrological drought. [JOR-E-101]
- Source dated 2026 reports: Surveyed households: 27% lacked network connection; summer supply fell and 63% reported inadequate pressure. [JOR-E-102]
- Source dated 2026 reports: 71% of Syrian versus 25% of Jordanian households had never applied; barriers included zones, fees and landlord support, while Syrians could not register meters individually. [JOR-E-103]
- Source dated 2026 reports: Unregulated areas cover 62% of the neighborhood; rural zoning prevents Yarmouk Water Company extending services to some households. [JOR-E-104]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: The CCDR says regional conflicts since 2011 led to an influx of 1.3 million Syrian refugees; population growth increased pressure on service delivery and scarce water and land. [JOR-E-114]
- Source dated 2024-02-11 reports: Conflict-driven Syrian refugee arrivals strained water systems in Irbid and Mafraq; remote infrastructure was aged. [JOR-E-115]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026 reports: GMM's climate-resilient master plan and Urban Observatory support planning; initiatives focus on flood mitigation. [JOR-E-105]
- Source dated 2023-10 reports: In 2023, climate-security policy integration remained inadequate due to awareness, technical-capacity and funding limits. [JOR-E-110]
- ICRC's summary of an external before/after evaluation reports pumping-station operational efficiency rising from 20% to 86% after intervention, then falling to 65% by 2022 amid maintenance, parts and staffing constraints. [JOR-E-116]
- Source dated 2024-02-11 reports: Supply rose from 32 to 107 litres per capita daily, then 85 in 2022; people below 20 litres fell from 26,000 to 6,000. [JOR-E-117]
- ICRC described a Hofa workshop/training centre planned for completion at end-2023 and operator training for four northern governorates. The report does not verify later building completion or long-term maintenance outcomes. [JOR-E-118]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Jordan's 2023 roadmap warns that climate-exacerbated water/livelihood pressures can interact with pre-existing grievances and strained allocation to weaken cohesion; this is a conditional national mechanism, not a measured local climate–violence effect. [JOR-P-101; JOR-E-107, JOR-E-108, JOR-E-109, JOR-E-110]
  - Documented impact: Possible further reductions in water/livelihood security and greater service/resource competition.
  - FCV mediator: Pre-existing grievances, unequal/strained service access, resource-management failures and contested allocation can shape how scarcity is experienced.
  - Possible consequence: The 2023 roadmap warns of disputes, social discontent and anti-refugee sentiment; no measured climate-attributable violence or local incident trend.
