# Malawi Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Named 2023/24 drought and flood examples distinguish meteorological conditions from household losses and later recovery constraints. [MWI-E-101, MWI-E-102, MWI-E-106]
- Economic stress, unequal control over resources and weak protection services form a plausible protection-risk mechanism, but reported cases and forecasts are not causal estimates. [MWI-E-103, MWI-E-104, MWI-E-105, MWI-E-108, MWI-P-101]
- Dzaleka/host payouts and district preparedness outputs are documented without assuming sustained resilience gains. [MWI-E-109, MWI-E-110, MWI-E-111, MWI-E-113]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- NDC reports floods, droughts and cyclones have doubled in frequency since the 1980s. [MWI-E-002]
- The NDC reports Cyclones Idai (2019), Ana (2022) and Freddy (2023) caused widespread infrastructure, crop and livestock damage and displaced over one million people. [MWI-E-003]
- Without climate-resilience investments, the CCDR projects GDP reductions of 3-9 percent by 2030, 6-20 percent by 2040 and 8-16 percent by 2050. Scenario: Without the climate-resilience investments described in the release [MWI-E-005]
- The CCDR summary says climate change could push another two million people into poverty over the following decade. Scenario: The inspected summary does not specify it. [MWI-E-006]
- Source dated 2025-04-01 reports: In the 2023/24 season, meteorological drought lasted five months in Machinga, Mangochi, Zomba and Chikwawa, and four months in Chitipa, Nsanje, Dedza, Phalombe, Chiradzulu and Thyolo; additional southern and central districts had dry spells through much of the season. [MWI-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-04-01 reports: For the 2023/24 season, DoDMA data in the report count 17,033 households affected by floods in Karonga and Nkhotakota; all recorded hazards affected 21,333 households. [MWI-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Agriculture is largely rain-fed; under 1% of land is irrigated and most farmers cultivate under one hectare. [MWI-E-001]
- Source dated 2024-09-04 reports: Drought lengthened women's water-collection journeys; women, girls and people with disabilities were excluded from key response-governance structures. [MWI-E-105]
- Source dated 2025-04-07 reports: The CCA reports 20,000+ people displaced by March 2024 floods in Karonga and Nkhotakota; about 400 households remained displaced in Nsanje, Chikwawa, Zomba and Nkhotakota; early camp closures prompted returns to unrepaired, flood-exposed homes. [MWI-E-106]
- UNICEF's January 2026 report says 11 districts exceeded 2025 severe-wasting projections, naming Neno, Nsanje and Chikwawa; these are service/planning indicators, not district prevalence estimates. [MWI-E-112]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC sets a 2030 target for agro-meteorological and early-warning services to reach 90 percent of farmers and fishers. [MWI-E-004]
- Source dated 2025-04-07 reports: After dry spells and food insecurity, mostly male members of drought-affected Southern Region households moved semi-voluntarily for casual work in Blantyre, Mzuzu, Lilongwe, Rumphi or Mzimba. [MWI-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated not stated reports: WFP and UNHCR reported that about 4,000 households affected by El Niño in Dzaleka refugee camp and its host community received a US$400,000 parametric-insurance payout in 2024. [MWI-E-109]
- Source dated not stated reports: In 2024, IOM and UNDP integrated climate risk into 23 district development and contingency plans; UNEP trained 136 district-planning members, while a local climate-risk mapping tool remained under development. [MWI-E-110]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNICEF reported training over 60 district disaster-risk-management staff in risk analysis, response planning, gender, GBV and protection from exploitation after an after-action review identified weak government capacity. [MWI-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-01-26 reports: UNICEF reported deep-water systems in drought-affected schools and communities in Nsanje and Neno reaching over 12,000 people, more than half women and girls; improved sanitation reached 36,569 people in Chikwawa and Nsanje. [MWI-E-113]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The 2024 assessment links livelihood stress and unequal resource access with protection risks, with a historical Machinga reporting example; causal attribution is unresolved. [MWI-P-101; MWI-E-103, MWI-E-104, MWI-E-105]
  - Documented impact: Household economic stress and harmful coping pressures affected children and women.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal control over relief/resources, weak community protection services and economic dependence.
  - Possible consequence: The assessment reports exploitation risks and rising reported child marriage; Machinga counts illustrate reporting, not prevalence or attribution.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2024-09-04 reports: The assessment links El Niño income stress to children joining guardians' casual work, school absenteeism and child labour. [MWI-E-103]
- The July 2024 UN assessment cites a rapid gender analysis linking deprivation to exploitation risk and weak local protection services; Machinga recorded 286 child marriages in January–May 2024 versus 162 a year earlier. [MWI-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-04-07 reports: The CCA warns climate-driven mobility may intensify scarce-land competition and migrant–host tensions; it also reports households moving for arable land and fishers seeking grounds along Lake Malawi. Scenario: Conditional pathway; no quantified scenario or timeline stated. [MWI-E-108]
