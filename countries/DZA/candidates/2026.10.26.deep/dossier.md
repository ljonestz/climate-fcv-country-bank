# Algeria Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Béchar's 2024 flood and Oran's coastal storm exposure provide named cases. Broader heat and drought evidence distinguishes coastal, Saharan and steppe settings without inventing wilaya-specific values. [DZA-E-101, DZA-E-102, DZA-E-103, DZA-E-104, DZA-E-106]
- Historical disaster-risk-finance gaps and intended Algiers studies describe coping and institutional constraints. Proposed fiscal reforms and statutory warning/planning requirements are not verified implementation outcomes. [DZA-E-107, DZA-E-109, DZA-E-110, DZA-E-111, DZA-E-112, DZA-E-113]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No existing DZA country baseline was present in the worktree. []
- The IMF climate-to-displacement pathway is projected risk framing, not measured migration, intergroup tension or conflict. []
- No country-specific evidence was found that conflict or insecurity has disrupted climate adaptation or service delivery; this is not evidence of absence. []
- The National Climate Plan PDF exceeded web extraction size limits and its Ministry download page timed out; no claims rely on search snippets. []
- IEA heat ranges cover unnamed groups of coastal and southern wilayas; the report does not provide named-wilaya values in the cited passage. []
- The 2002 World Bank annex documents intended studies and training but not implementation results. []
- Projected mobility pressure on the northern coast is not treated as conflict or violence. No full mediated FCV pathway is assembled from the retrieved evidence. []
- Statutory requirements are reported as written in the 2024 law, not as a legal compliance assessment or proof that local plans function. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-07-03 reports: The assessment reports September 2024 flooding displaced 280 families in Béchar and damaged roads and electricity infrastructure in southern and western Algeria. [DZA-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-07-03 reports: Across 1995–2014, 14 coastal wilayas recorded 20–39 days annually above 35°C, versus 112–161 days in seven southern Saharan wilayas. [DZA-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-07-03 reports: IEA projects lower precipitation in northern populated regions under both assessed scenarios, while the south could receive up to 71% more rainfall in the high-warming case. [DZA-E-103]
- Source dated 2023 reports: It reports nearly 300,000 people affected by 42 major floods in 1980–2020, affecting dense urban populations and rural farmers. [DZA-E-105]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The diagnostic says coastal storms particularly affect the Oran coastline, damaging port infrastructure, maritime transport and aquaculture farms. [DZA-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2023 reports: The diagnostic identifies the high cereal plains and pre-Saharan steppe pastures as drought-sensitive; it records a 1999–2002 drought and losses in 2013. [DZA-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-03-07 reports: IMF staff says lower farm productivity may harm jobs and incomes; water/food insecurity, disasters and harsher conditions may displace people and pressure northern-coast infrastructure and resources. [DZA-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2023 reports: In its 2023 snapshot, UNDP found inclusive insurance virtually absent; the State funded over 99% of disaster costs, local insurers covered under 1%, and no sovereign risk-transfer mechanism was used. [DZA-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-03-07 reports: IMF staff models that reducing the fossil-fuel price gap by one-third could raise average annual revenue by 7% of GDP over a decade, with space for targeted transfers and adaptation investment. [DZA-E-109]
- Source dated 2002-07-18 reports: Annex names Hamiz Oued, Oued El Harrach, Ouchaiah Oued, Kniss Oued and Telemly for a flood, landslide, seismic and pollution study; it proposes separate catchment-basin work for Bouzareah. [DZA-E-110]
- Source dated 2002-07-18 reports: After the 2001 Algiers flood, the Wilaya of Algiers and public agencies, administrations and residents provided emergency measures and temporary relief that partly mitigated immediate impacts. [DZA-E-111]
- Source dated 2002-07-18 reports: The project design proposed a meteorological monitoring and early-warning system in Algiers for possible national expansion and training for civil protection, water-resources and meteorology agencies. [DZA-E-112]
- Source dated 2024-03-06 reports: The law requires risk analysis at site, commune, wilaya, inter-wilaya and national scales, with warning levels at national, local and site levels, plus local training and prevention plans. [DZA-E-113]

## Bibliography and review decision

- International Energy Agency. National Climate Resilience Assessment for Algeria. 2025-07-03 (date basis: publication). https://www.iea.org/reports/national-climate-resilience-assessment-for-algeria [DZA-SRC-101] Methodology: IEA assessment combines climate observations, IPCC projections, INFORM hazard indicators and energy-system exposure analysis. Limitations: Most infrastructure examples concern energy assets; household vulnerability is not comprehensively mapped.
- United Nations Development Programme Insurance and Risk Finance Facility and UNDP Algeria. Inclusive insurance and risk financing in Algeria: Snapshot and way forward 2023. 2023 (date basis: document-version). https://data.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke476/files/2025-01/undp-irff-diagnostic-algeria.pdf [DZA-SRC-102] Methodology: The 23-page summary presents an inclusive-insurance and disaster-risk-finance country diagnostic and key hazard impacts. Limitations: Historical hazard and insurance data are dated, not a current local assessment.
- International Monetary Fund. Algeria: Selected Issues, Fiscal Reforms to Support Addressing Climate Change Challenges. 2024-03-07 (date basis: document-version). https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1dzaea2024002.pdf [DZA-SRC-103] Methodology: IMF staff assesses physical and fiscal climate risks and models policy options using the IMF-World Bank Climate Policy Assessment Tool. Limitations: Impact pathways and policy results are projections or model outputs; they do not establish observed displacement or violence.
- World Bank. Urban Natural Hazard Vulnerability Reduction Project: Technical Annex to the Memorandum of the President. 2002-07-18 (date basis: document-version). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/265221468741587343/pdf/multi0page.pdf [DZA-SRC-104] Methodology: Technical annex documents local vulnerability diagnoses, project components, institutional arrangements and intended emergency works. Limitations: This is a 2002 design-stage annex; proposed surveys, training and protections are not evidence of completed results.
- Official Gazette of the People's Democratic Republic of Algeria. Law No. 24-04 of 26 February 2024 on prevention, intervention and reduction of disaster risks. 2024-03-06 (date basis: publication). https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2024/F2024016.pdf [DZA-SRC-105] Methodology: Official Gazette publishes the French translation of Law 24-04 and its disaster-risk governance requirements. Limitations: Statutory mandates do not establish whether local plans, training or warning systems operate effectively.
