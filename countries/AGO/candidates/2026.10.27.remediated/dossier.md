- Automated machine-screening is not independent source verification or human approval. 16 of 17 evidence records and 1 of 1 pathways lack claim-level verification in this remediation; review the linked records before relying on them. - No pre-existing AGO country dossier was present under countries/ in this worktree; this is a new-country Phase 2 extraction. []
- The UN Secretary-General report explicitly relates the Namibe clash to dam access amid prolonged drought, but it does not establish causality independently or identify the communities. []
- No opened source connected conflict or insecurity to weaker climate adaptation, climate-service delivery or disaster response; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- The World Bank risk report PDF exceeded the reader size limit, the UNDRR full profile was unavailable beyond landing-page metadata, and the 2026 OHRLLS PDF returned HTTP 403; snippets and metadata were not used for claims. []
- The NDC uses older southern-province groupings for some historic statistics despite Angola's 2025 administrative reorganization; those aggregate figures are not mapped to new province boundaries. []
- FEWS NET's outlook is based on information through 30 June 2026 and its projected food-security outcomes are not observed results. []
- The August 2025 Namibe incident is a source-reported water-access clash under drought. The report does not identify communities, isolate climate causation or provide a current provincial violence trend. []
- Broad regional humanitarian-access constraints in the UN report are not assigned specifically to Angola's adaptation services. []
- Historical province labels and drought statistics are preserved without remapping them onto reorganized administrative boundaries. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: NDC links drainage and erosion deficits to flood and life risks in urban Luanda. [AGO-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: NDC reports over one million people affected in Huíla, Namibe and Cunene during 2015–16 drought, with $286m damage and about $437m losses. [AGO-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: NDC identifies low-income people, Indigenous communities, small producers and rural women as especially vulnerable. [AGO-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-09 reports: Drought may draw girls into distant water collection, increasing early-marriage and gender-based-violence risks. [AGO-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: UNICEF attributes drought affecting 5.6 million people to El Niño-era rainfall deficits; it reports 1.2 million water-scarce people and 50–80% of southern water points non-operational in 2022. [AGO-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: The report links crop losses to delayed, below-average rain and says inflation, currency weakness and fuel-subsidy removal further worsened food insecurity. [AGO-E-109]
- Source dated 2015-09-10 reports: The report cites OCHA estimates that 2013–14 drought affected 1.8 million people in Cunene, Namibe, parts of Benguela and Huíla; about 700,000 faced food-insecurity risk. [AGO-E-113]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: 2025/26 rains were delayed and dry, with below-average harvests and crop failures at Oncócua, Cunene. [AGO-E-114]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: FEWS NET projects 1.0–1.49 million people needing assistance in southern agropastoral areas in November 2026–January 2027, with IPC Phase 3-aligned outcomes. Scenario: FEWS NET's most-likely outlook through January 2027. [AGO-E-116]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Some isolated herders in Cunene, Namibe and Cuando Cubango with prior-drought herd losses and few alternatives are assessed as likely IPC Phase 3. [AGO-E-117]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: These wetlands and peatlands support rural water and livelihoods but face fire and land-use pressure. [AGO-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Projected dryness may delay southern water recharge and pasture, prompting earlier movement toward Namibia and reducing veterinary access. Scenario: FEWS NET's most-likely outlook assumes El Niño and below-average October 2026–January 2027 rainfall. [AGO-E-115]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-09 reports: NDC notes watershed and reservoir data gaps and insufficient technical capacity for long-term water projections. [AGO-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-12 reports: In drought-affected Huíla and Cunene, UNICEF reports measles vaccination for 11,587 at-risk children and maternal or child health services for 251,559 women and children. [AGO-E-110]
- Source dated 2015-09-10 reports: In August 2015, Angola's Civil Protection Commission and UNDP trained 43 national and provincial officials in Luanda on needs assessment and resilient recovery. [AGO-E-111]
- Source dated 2015-09-10 reports: After Benguela floods, a CNPC–UNDP exercise assessed recovery needs in Lobito, Catumbela and Cabrais, including housing, health, education, livelihoods and agriculture. [AGO-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The UN reports that contested dam access amid prolonged drought preceded a deadly intercommunal clash in Namibe in August 2025; it does not establish climate as the sole cause or a national violence trend. [AGO-P-101; AGO-E-101]
  - Documented impact: Pressure around access to a water dam.
  - FCV mediator: Contested dam access between two unnamed communities.
  - Possible consequence: The UN Secretary-General's report records violent clashes on 17 August 2025, a 12–20 fatality range and thousands displaced.

  - Uncertainty: One UN-reported historical episode. No named communities, independently identified climate effect, anthropogenic drought attribution or current violence map. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
