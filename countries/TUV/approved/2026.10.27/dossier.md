## Climate pressures and exposure

- Tuvalu's 2025 NAP, summarizing SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 modeling, projects that current 50-year coastal floods may recur every 1-5 years by 2060 in Funafuti, Nui, Nukufetau and Vaitupu. [TUV-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Low elevation, a narrow resource base and capacity constraints heighten Tuvalu's sensitivity to rising seas and cyclone impacts. [TUV-E-001]
- The preliminary 2022 Tuvalu risk profile identifies sea-level threats to territory, identity and maritime baselines; its assessment predates Falepili's 2024 entry into force. [TUV-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The retrospective describes scholarships in climate, coastal engineering and environmental fields as long-term local capacity investment. [TUV-E-004]
- Vaitupu is proposed for hybrid coastal protection and community stewardship; June 2026 approval initiates preparation rather than completed works. [TUV-E-005]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- UNDP reports Phase I closure in December 2025 and investment in local technical skills and coastal mapping. [TUV-E-002]
- TCAP works targeted Funafuti, Nanumea and Nanumaga to reduce wave damage to homes, schools and health assets. [TUV-E-003]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNCDF reports three community-grant cycles invested over $400,000 and benefited over 1,000 people, including drought-resilient storage in Nukulaelae, solar systems in Nukufetau and food-security units in Nanumea. [TUV-E-106]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNCDF reports climate-data and public-financial-management training in 2024-2025 reached 31 local officials, 42% women. [TUV-E-107]
- Falepili Treaty articles 2-3 recognize continuing Tuvaluan statehood despite sea-level rise, support remaining at home and establish a bilateral mobility pathway to live, study and work in Australia. [TUV-E-108]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Tuvalu's NAP links dry spells and insufficient storage with shared-water disputes; local frequency and distribution remain unknown. [TUV-P-101; TUV-E-102]
  - Documented impact: Inadequate household storage increases dependence on shared water distribution.
  - FCV mediator: Unequal access and competition at constrained water points.
  - Possible consequence: NAP reports water theft/disputes and community stress, without incidence or causal estimates.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Storage investment, power reliability, distribution rules and household resources also shape shortages and grievances.']
  - Uncertainty: Government NAP synthesis citing CIVRA 2024, not a new event inventory or proof of climate-caused armed violence. No island-specific dispute claim. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2025-08-15 reports: The 2025 NAP says climate- and economic-driven migration to Funafuti strains resources and traditional land access, weakening cohesion and raising conflict and civil-unrest risks. [TUV-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-08-15 reports: The NAP links long dry spells and inadequate storage to water theft and conflict at distribution points; it reports about 70% of the population lacks adequate storage. [TUV-E-102]
- Source dated 2022-11-10 reports: The preliminary profile says climate pressures can push outer-island residents toward Funafuti, where overcrowding may heighten tensions, while leavers risk loss of place. [TUV-E-104]
- Source dated 2022-11-10 reports: The preliminary profile warns that perceived unfair disaster or climate assistance could create grievances and recommends conflict-sensitive planning and aid monitoring. [TUV-E-105]
