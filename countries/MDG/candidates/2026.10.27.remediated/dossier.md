## Climate pressures and exposure

- The CCDR links Grand Sud drought and cyclone flooding with crop/livelihood losses, high prices and food insecurity; over two million faced acute hunger in the 2020-2022 crisis, also involving COVID price shocks. [MDG-E-001]
- NDC reports Grand Sud droughts affected over 1.3 million people in 2015-2022; in 2024 over 1.2 million faced acute food crisis. [MDG-E-004]
- The NDC estimates that 57 percent of schools have high cyclone exposure and 50 percent have high drought exposure. [MDG-E-005]
- The NDC reports 285,800 people were forcibly displaced in 2022 following sudden climate-related events, including tropical cyclones and floods. [MDG-E-006]
- Source dated 2025-10 reports: The October 2025 HCT note says 22 Grand Sud/Grand Sud-Est districts faced drought, cyclone and locust shocks since late 2024, damaging crops and livelihoods and leaving little recovery time. [MDG-E-107]
- UNICEF's June 2026 account reports Cyclone Gezani affected over 478,000 people on the east coast and damaged homes, schools and health facilities. It distinguishes predominantly wind-driven Gezani damage from Fytia's extensive flooding. [MDG-E-110]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The CCDR says Grand Sud climate action is intertwined with exclusion, fragility, isolation and poverty. [MDG-E-002]
- The CCDR reports early marriage, transactional sex and school dropout as Grand Sud drought-period concerns for women and girls. [MDG-E-003]
- Source dated 2025-06-02 reports: The feature describes Great South households reliant on farming and fishing facing repeated drought and water scarcity, followed by damaging floods, stronger southern winds, drifting sand and desertification that undermine crops and livelihoods. [MDG-E-105]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025-06-02 reports: By June 2025, the World Bank reported MIONJO served about 1.4 million people in 235 rural communes across Atsimo-Andrefana, Androy and Anosy; project partners had stabilized 1,500 hectares of dunes, reforested 330 hectares and established 24 nurseries. [MDG-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-10 reports: The HCT said about US$96 million in anticipated 2025 funding was suspended or redirected; 15 NGO field offices, mostly in high-need districts, closed, shrinking coverage. [MDG-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-06-19 reports: UNICEF says advance forecasts, pre-positioned supplies and renewed 2026 parametric insurance supported rapid response; reported outputs included restored safe water for 15,879 people and child-friendly or psychosocial support for over 2,000 people. [MDG-E-112]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Post-Gezani displacement-site crowding and limited services heightened child violence/exploitation risk; incidence changes were not measured. [MDG-P-101; MDG-E-110, MDG-E-111]
  - Documented impact: Housing and service damage displaced people into temporary sites.
  - FCV mediator: Crowding and limited services in displacement sites weakened children's protective environment.
  - Possible consequence: UNICEF reports heightened risks of violence and exploitation, not measured post-cyclone incident rates.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- A March 2023 World Bank article summarizing a 480-person panel reports that 47% of respondents perceived worsening violence against women at the 2021 drought peak. [MDG-E-101]
- The same Southern Madagascar panel summary reports respondents linked drought-period hunger and poverty with opportunistic cattle raiding and other violence. [MDG-E-102]
- Source dated 2023-03-03 reports: Migration intensified after other household coping options failed; women migrants faced heightened GBV and trafficking risks. [MDG-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-06-19 reports: The article says nearly 60,000 children had learning disrupted and 20,854 people took refuge in temporary sites after Gezani, where protection concerns intensified. [MDG-E-111]
