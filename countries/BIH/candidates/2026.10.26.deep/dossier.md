# Bosnia and Herzegovina Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Evidence distinguishes Sava floodplain exposure, projected Sava-tributary flows and named municipal exposure, vulnerability and coping-capacity patterns; composite indices are not event forecasts. [BIH-E-101, BIH-E-102, BIH-E-103, BIH-E-110, BIH-E-111, BIH-E-112, BIH-E-113, BIH-E-114, BIH-E-115]
- The 2014 recovery assessment documents flooding/landslides moving legacy mines and recovery work requiring survey and clearance. This is a conflict-legacy interaction, not climate causing the original conflict. [BIH-E-106, BIH-E-107, BIH-E-108, BIH-P-101]
- The 2024 rapid assessment identifies remote-area access/service gaps and anticipated protection risks. These and selected municipal capacities have explicit assessment and outcome limits. [BIH-E-109, BIH-E-117, BIH-E-118, BIH-E-119, BIH-E-120, BIH-P-102]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No inspected primary source establishes current armed or intercommunal violence disrupting adaptation or disaster-risk-reduction delivery. The clearest conflict interaction is legacy mine/UXO contamination affecting flood recovery; INFORM also documents war-related economic scarring and weaker disaster recovery. []
- The 2021 NAP is a dated institutional baseline; later legal and spatial-planning reforms were not verified. Its Sava flood model does not cover the Adriatic watershed. []
- The 2026 INFORM index excludes disaster-risk-reduction capability and humanitarian coping indicators because of insufficient data; its composite rankings are not event warnings. []
- The UNDRR municipal scorecards are selected local self-assessments from 2021–2022, and the 2024 MIRA is a purposive key-informant snapshot rather than a representative or independently verified post-disaster assessment. []
- The MIRA GBV statement is a conditional risk attributed to a separate UN Women rapid gender report; this collection did not independently inspect that separate report. []
- Jablanica is retained without the intake's erroneous Central Bosnia Canton tag. []
- Historical mine-recovery restrictions and 2024 anticipated GBV risks are not current incident or service-status reports. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2021-09 reports: The NAP reports 1961–2018 warming (annual 0.4–1.2°C; growing-season up to 1.4°C), rising heavy-rainfall share and variability; it names Trebinje, Zenica, Laktaši and Sanski Most among repeatedly affected towns. [BIH-E-101]
- Source dated 2021-09 reports: Record May 2014 rain plus snowmelt broke Sava embankments and flooded Central Posavina, Odžačka Posavina and Semberija; later heavy rain caused repeat flooding and landslides. [BIH-E-102]
- Source dated 2021-09 reports: Under RCP8.5, upper-quartile 100-year daily rainfall implies at least 23% higher end-century 100-year peak flows across modeled BiH Sava tributaries. [BIH-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: INFORM ranks Brod (7.5) and Bihać (7.3) highest for exposure; Neretva municipalities Konjic and Mostar face flood/landslide risks, and about 12,000 people in Konjic live in landslide-prone areas. [BIH-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2014-06-30 reports: After the May 2014 floods, 81 municipalities suffered impacts; about 90,000 people were displaced and more than 40,000 sheltered or stayed with relatives. [BIH-E-105]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: The report records a 2024 Jablanica landslide that buried buildings and killed 19 people, although its municipal hazard-exposure score was moderate. [BIH-E-113]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Trnovo (RS) and Foča score 7.4 for vulnerability with unemployment above 20%; the report links job scarcity partly to war-damaged economies and says benefit dependence hampers post-disaster recovery. [BIH-E-114]
- Source dated 2024-11-21 reports: The rapid assessment says road and bridge damage restricted movement from remote areas and slowed recovery; in Jablanica it counted 483 affected people and 188 evacuees across interviewed flood-hit locations. [BIH-E-117]
- Source dated 2024-11-21 reports: The assessment calls for mobile maternal, reproductive and psychological care for women and girls in remote areas; it names social-care staffing shortages in Jablanica, Konjic and Kiseljak. [BIH-E-119]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2022-11 reports: Bihać, Bijeljina and Gradačac established local DRR platforms, adopted aligned strategies and risk assessments, and reported engineering measures and shock-responsive social-protection plans. [BIH-E-109]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: Busovača's scorecard names floods as its main hazard; it reports no training for poorest groups, recent business continuity plans in only 20–40% of firms, and warning reach of about half the population. [BIH-E-110]
- Source dated 2022-11 reports: Jajce reports an outdated risk assessment, no training for poorest groups, continuity plans in under 20% of firms and warning reach below half the population. [BIH-E-111]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Bosansko Grahovo, Livno and Kupres score poorly on coping capacity; Bosansko Grahovo's road density is under one-seventh the national average, constraining evacuation and aid access. [BIH-E-115]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2021-09 reports: In 2021, the NAP found climate law and spatial planning insufficient; environmental powers lay with the entities and Brčko, and existing plans had not integrated climate change. [BIH-E-104]
- Source dated 2014-06-30 reports: The RNA reports that Civil Protection and BHMAC response located and destroyed more than 10,000 mines, UXO and weapons, with no recorded casualties. [BIH-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-11-21 reports: Responders reported aid across six affected municipalities; civil protection, governments, UN/NGOs, businesses and community groups coordinated food, water, medicine, cash and repair kits. [BIH-E-120]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Floods mobilized conflict-era mines/UXO, making recovery access contingent on professional survey and clearance in affected areas. [BIH-P-101; BIH-E-106, BIH-E-107, BIH-E-108]
  - Documented impact: Water and slope movements displaced legacy mines/UXO into previously safe or suspected areas.
  - FCV mediator: Conflict-era contamination and uncertain marking/survey status made reconstruction and farm access unsafe until professional survey and clearance.
  - Possible consequence: Potential accidents and restrictions on essential-service reconstruction and agricultural recovery; later delay duration is not established.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Direct flood damage and general reconstruction capacity also affect recovery.', 'Potential contamination estimates require professional verification.']
  - Uncertainty: Historical source-stated interaction and planned safety restrictions, not a current contamination map, clearance instruction or causal estimate of recovery delay.
- The 2024 flood assessment anticipated GBV risks through livelihood loss, crowding and disrupted protection services; no incidence increase was verified. [BIH-P-102; BIH-E-118, BIH-E-119]
  - Documented impact: Affected households faced displacement, overcrowding and psychological distress.
  - FCV mediator: Disrupted protection services and constrained survivor reporting can weaken safeguards.
  - Possible consequence: The rapid assessment anticipated possible increased GBV risk, not measured violence incidence.

  - Uncertainty: Historical anticipated risk, not verified harm, a current forecast or a nationally representative local assessment.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2014-06-30 reports: Floods and landslides shifted mines/UXO from marked or suspected areas; 51 of 3,000 reported landslides were in suspected hazardous areas, and 105 km² was flagged as potentially contaminated. [BIH-E-106]
- Source dated 2014-06-30 reports: Residents in Posavina and Brčko reported mines/UXO near homes; repair and reconstruction in unsafe areas were restricted pending survey and clearance, leaving farmers and workers at risk. [BIH-E-107]
- Source dated 2024-11-21 reports: The report warns GBV could rise after flood-related livelihood loss, overcrowding and distress; disrupted services may also hinder survivor reporting. [BIH-E-118]
