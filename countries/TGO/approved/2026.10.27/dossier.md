## Climate pressures and exposure

- Communication reports retreat along 40 km of sandy coast at an average 10 m yearly, aggravated by storms and local sediment dynamics. [TGO-E-002]
- Under RCP6.0, sea level is projected to rise 11.35 cm by 2025 and 62 cm by 2100, worsening coastal erosion and livelihood risks. Scenario: RCP6.0. [TGO-E-003]
- Source dated 2026-08-28 reports: FAO reports that early-season dryness and erratic rain delayed planting and required replanting in parts of Kara and Savanes; early-August satellite data showed below-average vegetation pockets in northern Kara. [TGO-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-04-14 reports: UNICEF reports a bad 2024 rainy season flooded Maritime and Grand Lomé, affecting 42,000 people; its regional overview says floods damaged crops and infrastructure and displaced communities. [TGO-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Agriculture vulnerability is high nationwide and very high in Savanes; under RCP6.0, Kara also reaches very high by 2050. Scenario: RCP6.0; projection horizon 2050. [TGO-E-001]
- NDC reports 2020-2021 Oti and Mono floods caused deaths and damaged roads, markets, health centers, homes, schools, power and telecoms. [TGO-E-006]
- NDC estimates 6,902 hectares of food crops flooded in 2020 and production losses near 9,000 tonnes; dry spells also reduced yields. [TGO-E-007]
- FAO's August 2026 brief places about 45% of the nationally estimated June–August CH Phase 3 population in Savanes. It separately reports insecurity-related livelihood/trade disruption and about 13,400 IDPs as of June. [TGO-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-08-28 reports: FAO reports late-June 2026 heavy rain flooded Greater Lomé and parts of Maritime and Plateaux, affecting about 26,600 people and damaging housing and infrastructure. [TGO-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-04-14 reports: By October 2024, UNICEF recorded 49,607 displaced people nationally, including 39,436 refugees and 10,171 IDPs; it reports displaced people in 71 Savanes localities had limited access to water, sanitation, health care, education and protection services. [TGO-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-11 reports: The Ministry–UNICEF climate-landscape summary says prolonged drought, recurring floods, coastal erosion and rising temperatures threaten children’s rights and access to safe water, food, education, health care and protection. [TGO-E-108]
