# Venezuela Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Model-based regional and Indigenous vulnerability findings are low-confidence because the government report was accessible only through an unverified user-uploaded mirror. [VEN-E-101, VEN-E-102, VEN-E-103]
- IACHR and UNICEF provide named flood, evacuation and child-service findings. Exact caseloads, duration and group identities remain bounded by each source. [VEN-E-108, VEN-E-109, VEN-E-112, VEN-E-113, VEN-E-116]
- Community water and radio-referral activities add response capacities, while electoral and border-conflict constraints are separate dated context. No climate-to-armed-violence chain is inferred. [VEN-E-111, VEN-E-114, VEN-E-115, VEN-E-117, VEN-E-118, VEN-E-119]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Automated machine-screening is not independent source verification or human approval. 14 of 14 evidence records and 0 of 0 pathways lack claim-level verification in this remediation; review the linked records before relying on them. [VEN-E-101, VEN-E-102, VEN-E-103, VEN-E-108, VEN-E-109, VEN-E-111, VEN-E-112, VEN-E-113, VEN-E-114, VEN-E-115, VEN-E-116, VEN-E-117, VEN-E-118, VEN-E-119]
- The reviewed evidence gives climate-linked displacement, service and livelihood pathways but no primary passage establishing climate hazards as a cause of armed conflict, mining violence or political unrest. []
- Searches found no source stating that insecurity specifically obstructed climate adaptation or early warning; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of no effect. []
- MINEC's full report text was accessible only through a user-uploaded Scribd mirror; official-host verification and reuse terms remain unresolved. []
- The 2025 IACHR climate chapter synthesizes attributed reporting rather than providing an independent climate-attribution analysis; UNICEF response figures are agency-reported. []
- Venezuela's 2024 climate transparency submission was not analyzed because the UNFCCC PDF exceeded the browser tool's accessible size; IOM and IFRC records tested were blocked or unsupported by the browser reader. []
- The 2025 MINEC assessment was read only on a user-uploaded Scribd mirror. Official-host authenticity, exact model assumptions and reuse terms remain unresolved; its retained model claims are low-confidence draft context requiring human source review. []
- The model's territorial extent is not endorsed as a sovereignty determination. Disputed-territory tags were removed; geographic administrative claims require official verification. []
- No verified climate-caused armed violence or complete reverse-FCV pathway is retained. Mining violence and border/electoral insecurity are separate context. []
- UNICEF describes combined flooding/electoral access constraints, not identified causal effects or verified interruption of an adaptation intervention. []
- Flood-related evacuations and temporary movement have uncertain denominators and duration; Warao concerns and Amazonas evacuation counts must not be conflated. An undated general projection was excluded. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- Official-host Venezuela climate reports were located, but full documents exceeded the web reader size limit and the direct NDC download returned HTML. No official-original authentication of the user-uploaded mirror is claimed. VEN-E-101 to VEN-E-103 remain low-confidence provisional mirror claims. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- A user-uploaded mirror attributed to MINEC's 2025 vulnerability assessment presents model-dependent warming and rainfall changes for 2021–2080 under SSP2-4.5/SSP5-8.5. Official-host authenticity and scenario details remain pending; this is low-confidence draft model context, not an observed national outcome. Scenario: HadGEM3-GC31-LL/MIROC6; SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5; 2021–2080. [VEN-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-12-17 reports: Tropical-wave rains affected Mérida, Táchira, Trujillo and Barinas; over 8,000 families were affected and rural isolation forced temporary internal relocation. [VEN-E-108]
- The IACHR describes Warao concerns about potential displacement from Orinoco river rise and separately reports authorities evacuating more than 200 Amazonas families in July 2025. It does not establish that the evacuated families were Warao. [VEN-E-109]
- Source dated 2025-02-06 reports: Fifty-one tropical waves affected 47 municipalities in 17 states; over 75,200 people, including 28,400 children, were affected. [VEN-E-112]
- Source dated 2025-08-01 reports: Early rains affected 31 municipalities across ten states, damaging homes, schools, health facilities and services; over 37,000 people, including 13,600 children, were affected. [VEN-E-116]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- An unverified user-uploaded mirror attributed to MINEC (2025) reports: High-vulnerability agricultural lands include Monagas, Miranda, Aragua, Carabobo, Cojedes, Portuguesa, Lara, Barinas, Táchira and Mérida. Scenario: HadGEM3-GC31-LL; sector model, 2021–2040. [VEN-E-102]
- An unverified user-uploaded mirror attributed to MINEC (2025) reports: Projected rainfall and heat anomalies affect Indigenous communities in Gran Sabana, Gran Guajira, Perijá, eastern Amazonas and Orinoco Delta. Scenario: HadGEM3-GC31-LL, SSP2-4.5; 2021–2040. [VEN-E-103]
- The IACHR, attributing allegations to OHCHR, describes illegal mining and violent clashes involving Ye'kwana territories in Amazonas. This is separate FCV context, not evidence of climate-caused mining violence. [VEN-E-111]
- Source dated 2025-02-06 reports: Floods affected Montes, Valdez and Mejía in Sucre and Catatumbo, Colón and Rosario de Perijá in Zulia, including over 24,000 children. [VEN-E-113]
- Source dated 2025-02-06 reports: Post-election civil unrest disrupted children’s access to essential services and increased protection risks; logistical and administrative barriers delayed lifesaving imports. [VEN-E-115]
- UNICEF's mid-2025 report describes seasonal flooding and the electoral context jointly limiting primary-care access in border and hard-to-reach areas, with women and young children particularly affected. It does not isolate each contribution or establish that violence interrupted a climate-adaptation project. [VEN-E-117]
- UNICEF's mid-2025 report describes separate border fighting, displacement and child-protection risks in Táchira and Zulia. This is dated FCV context, not climate-caused displacement or evidence that flooding caused armed confrontation. [VEN-E-119]
