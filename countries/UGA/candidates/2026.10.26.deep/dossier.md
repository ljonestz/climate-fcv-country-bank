## Climate pressures and exposure

- The CCDR reports heavy rainfall, floods and droughts affected about 200,000 Ugandans per year on average over the prior two decades. [UGA-E-001]
- Climate-related internal migration could reach 12 million by 2050, mainly in the Lake Victoria Basin. [UGA-E-002]
- Source dated 2026 reports: The plan reports over 80% of populations in Katakwi, Otuke, Kitgum, Nwoya, Sembabule and Kalungu exposed to floods at least one metre deep. [UGA-E-101]
- Source dated 2026 reports: The plan reports over 80% population exposure to landslides in Bulambuli, Kapchorwa, Bukwo, Kween, Kisoro and Rubanda; over 60% of homes are exposed in five of these districts. [UGA-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The CCDR identifies northern, northeastern and north-central households as highly exposed to weather shocks with inadequate coping strategies. [UGA-E-004]
- The NDC risk assessment rates crop-and-livestock vulnerability at 4.07 and risk at 4.18; forestry vulnerability is 3.25 and risk 4.25. [UGA-E-005]
- Source dated 2026 reports: In Karamoja, perennial surface water is scarce and valley dams cannot meet livestock needs; basic drinking-water access is 38%, compared with 97% in Kampala. [UGA-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: KCCA identifies recurring floods in low-lying informal Bwaise, Katwe and Namuwongo; Nakawa, Makindye and Kawempe score highest on its division vulnerability index. [UGA-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC sets out 48 priority adaptation actions and 82 indicators with targets for 2025 and 2030 across 13 sectors. [UGA-E-006]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: KCCA reports flood-resistant housing and drainage among more consistently adopted household measures in Kawempe, Lubaga and Nakawa; stormwater retention adoption was low, especially in Central and Nakawa. [UGA-E-107]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- CCDR reports a government pilot saved 51 percent in emergency-response costs and raised adaptive capacity by 37 percent. [UGA-E-003]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: In KCCA's 400-person household survey, 30% knew of climate policies and more than half considered them ineffective. [UGA-E-108]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- KCCA describes Kawempe water stress interacting with queues and unequal access to fuel social tension; no violence rate is established. [UGA-P-101; UGA-E-106]
  - Documented impact: Crop and water-access pressures increase reliance on communal points and costly alternatives.
  - FCV mediator: Long queues, inadequate storage and unequal household purchasing power.
  - Possible consequence: The report states queues fuel social tension, without measuring violence incidence.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Urban development, groundwater use, storage and service reliability also drive shortages.']
  - Uncertainty: Source-stated qualitative mechanism citing older material, not a causal study or current incident census.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2026 reports: The plan projects internal climate mobility may concentrate in southwestern Uganda by 2050, pressuring host-area water and WASH systems. [UGA-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-06 reports: KCCA links repeated 10-day dry spells in Kawempe to crop failures and unsafe-water reliance; long queues at communal water points are said to fuel social tension. [UGA-E-106]
- Source dated 2025 reports: For 2018-2023, the Karamoja Cluster study says crisis policies restricted transhumant pastoral mobility, leaving communities more exposed to climate impacts. [UGA-E-109]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The abstract reports vegetation associated with lower conflict broadly, but cluster conflict peaked during scarcity following periods of resource abundance. [UGA-E-110]

## Bibliography and review decision

Uganda Country Climate and Development Report: Executive Summary. 2025-07 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062825014535652/pdf/P501644-2aa2615c-c6f0-43d6-bd37-0bee781668ae.pdf [UGA-SRC-001] Methodology: CCDR executive summary reports six climate scenarios, two economic futures and modeled impacts with and without additional climate action. Limitations: Summary-level modeled results; migration estimate is not tied to a specific scenario in the inspected passage.
- Republic of Uganda, Ministry of Water and Environment. Updated Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC). 2022-09-12 (date basis: submission). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/NDC/2022-09/Updated%20NDC%20_Uganda_2022%20Final.pdf [UGA-SRC-002] Methodology: NDC update uses national risk and vulnerability assessments, the Third National Communication and climate/emissions projections. Limitations: Assessment ratings and national projections are summarized; rainfall projections carry wide uncertainty.

WASH National Adaptation Plan for Uganda (2026-2030). 2026 (date basis: document-version). https://www.mwe.go.ug/public/index.php/publications/294/download [UGA-SRC-101] Methodology: Plan combines a WASH vulnerability assessment, hazard mapping and district service indicators. Limitations: Inputs include OPM's 2019 atlas; the plan is not an impact evaluation.
- Republic of Uganda, Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA). Conducting and Developing the Climate Change and Vulnerability Assessment (CCVA) Framework Report for Kampala Capital City Authority. 2025-06 (date basis: document-version). https://www.kcca.go.ug/media/docs/KCCA_CCVA%20Framework%20Report%2025.08.2025_Final_v2.pdf [UGA-SRC-102] Methodology: GIS and climate/social datasets, 400 household surveys, 20 focus groups and 30 interviews used purposive stratified sampling. - Rebecca Navarro, Lamis Saleh and Evelyne Owino; International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction; abstract hosted by PreventionWeb. Pastoral conflict on the greener grass?
