# Bulgaria Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Historical drought and heat vary across Dobrudzha, the Danube corridor and Struma/Mesta valleys. Asparuhovo provides one dated example of unequal housing losses, not a national household risk ranking. [BGR-E-101, BGR-E-102, BGR-E-104, BGR-E-105, BGR-E-106]
- Recent government analysis identifies named-town network losses, peripheral-settlement service constraints and irrigation limitations. Climate pressure is one contributor alongside infrastructure and governance. [BGR-E-109, BGR-E-110, BGR-E-111, BGR-E-112]
- Commission findings identify risk-information, implementation and coordination gaps; program allocations and municipal commitments are capacities, not proven outcomes. [BGR-E-113, BGR-E-114, BGR-E-115]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-12-31 reports: Struma valley near Kresna Gorge is hottest: some years had 6–8 days above 40°C; 1961–2020 records show 40–55 hot days/year there versus 2–3 on the northern Black Sea coast. [BGR-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-12-31 reports: BTR projects lower precipitation in southern and southeastern Bulgaria under SSP5-8.5 in the far future, potentially compounding hotter conditions. Scenario: SSP5-8.5, far future as labeled in the report. [BGR-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-12-03 reports: NSI records 38 2024 disaster declarations, mainly for wildfires, floods, drought and hail; declarations for drinking-water shortage due to drought increased from 2023. [BGR-E-107]
- Source dated 2025-12-03 reports: 2024 declared disasters partly or fully affected 47 municipalities in 19 administrative regions; those municipalities had 1,308,715 residents, 20.3% of Bulgaria's population. [BGR-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024-12-31 reports: 1981–2010 drought mapping identifies the Danube corridor and central north to the Pre-Balkans; Northeast Bulgaria/Dobrudzha is most affected, with pockets around Blagoevgrad, Petrich–Sandanski and Mesta valley. [BGR-E-101]
- Source dated 2021-03 reports: For the June 2014 torrential-rain flash flood in Asparuhovo (Varna), the WB box reports 122 evacuated families, Roma ownership of 60% of destroyed houses, and 14 deaths, most among Roma. [BGR-E-105]
- Source dated 2021-03 reports: The report links unequal flood losses in Asparuhovo to informal Roma housing that failed building-code/zoning standards and to inadequate municipal prevention; it says no clear data show how many Roma live in flood-prone areas. [BGR-E-106]
- The March 2026 MOEW summary reports high water-network loss indicators for Shumen, Pernik, Targovishte, Haskovo, Yambol and Pazardzhik. These service-system losses are not attributed to climate change; underlying operator periods vary. [BGR-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-03 reports: MOEW says small and peripheral settlements are particularly vulnerable to restrictions, low pressure and interruptions; only 1,869 of 5,302 meter zones have continuous flow/pressure measurement with data archiving. [BGR-E-110]
- Source dated 2026-03 reports: In 2024, Irrigation Systems supplied 342,614 decares against 5.376 million decares of design capacity (about 6.4%); MOEW says long-term decline limits agricultural production and increases drought vulnerability. [BGR-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-06-03 reports: The 2021–2027 Environment Operational Programme provides EUR 225.64 million in climate-adaptation support; the Commission says planning-to-investment links and use of climate, hydrological and early-warning information need strengthening. [BGR-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-03 reports: MOEW says climate change amplifies, but does not fully explain, water stress; underinvestment, fragmented responsibility, weak expertise and data gaps also contribute. It recommends monitoring, modernization, river/flood planning and local water retention. [BGR-E-112]
- Source dated 2026-06-03 reports: The Commission says adaptation barriers include implementation capacity, data availability and cross-level coordination; the national risk assessment is outdated and comparable regional/local risk information is lacking for prioritization. [BGR-E-113]
- Source dated 2026-06-03 reports: Only around 24% of Bulgaria's population lives in Covenant of Mayors coverage; 10 of 49 cities made adaptation commitments and 44% of signatories submitted SECAPs on time. Sofia has an adaptation strategy; Burgas a development vision. [BGR-E-115]
