## Climate pressures and exposure

- The report projects the population share adversely affected by rainfall to rise to 16% by 2050 under a moderate-emissions scenario and 44% under a high-emissions scenario. Scenario: Moderate- and high-emissions scenarios; the passage does not specify scenario labels. [LBR-E-001]
- Liberia’s NDC describes its 580-km coastline as exposed to sea-level rise, erosion, and storm surges, especially near Monrovia and Buchanan; it reports that 60% of the population lives within 50 km of the coast. [LBR-E-004]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The CCDR reports that over 60% of Liberia’s urban population lives in informal settlements and that wetland and floodplain development in Greater Monrovia leaves dwellings waterlogged and flooded during the rainy season. [LBR-E-002]
- Source dated 2020 reports: Duala Market in New Kru Town has fragmented, blocked storm drains with no coherent outflow; local discussions report common peak-rainy-season flooding. Wetland-adjacent toilets also pose groundwater-contamination risks. [LBR-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-01-09 reports: FAO reports 2025 dry spells after near- or below-average rainfall in May–August, followed by localized flood-related crop losses in Grand Bassa, Margibi, Montserrado and Sinoe counties. [LBR-E-103]
- Source dated 2026-05-29 reports: EPA remarks identify farmers in Bong and Lofa, coastal fishers in Grand Cape Mount and Montserrado, and communities in Maryland, Margibi and Nimba as facing rising temperatures, erratic rainfall, coastal erosion and environmental degradation. [LBR-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC sets 2035 targets for green-grey infrastructure along 60% of the highly vulnerable coastline, coastal-flood early warning and scenario modeling, and flood-resilient water and sanitation in at least 40% of vulnerable urban slums and informal settlements. Scenario: Government adaptation targets for 2035, not outcome projections. [LBR-E-005]
- FAO's January 2026 brief describes a 2024–2030 agenda targeting 50,000 hectares of lowland irrigated rice in Lofa, Nimba and Bong; this is a target, not completed irrigation. [LBR-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-05-29 reports: The 2025–2030 SARTLA project aims to support climate-smart agriculture, sustainable fisheries, ecosystem restoration, coastal and land management, climate finance, and livelihoods across seven counties, with emphasis on women and youth. [LBR-E-110]
- Source dated 2026-03-12 reports: An EPA-implemented, UNDP-supervised and GCF-funded climate-resilience project targets vulnerable West Point, exposed to sea-level rise and intense storms; the ESIA page says the plan includes environmental and social safeguards. [LBR-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The report says Liberia lacks adequate baseline data on floods, windstorms, heat waves, and droughts; only Greater Monrovia had a detailed flood assessment in the cited discussion. [LBR-E-003]
- Source dated 2020 reports: The review says years of conflict left Greater Monrovia's political and administrative authority centrally controlled, limiting fiscal allocation and local governments' ability to provide adequate services. [LBR-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-01-09 reports: FAO reports that in 2025 the government provided farmers cash for land preparation, inputs, mechanization and equipment; the brief says this support enabled area expansion and improved yields. [LBR-E-105]
