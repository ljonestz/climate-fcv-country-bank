# Ukraine Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Ukraine faces increasing heat, drought, seasonal water imbalance, flood, and wildfire pressures, but much of the physical baseline predates the full-scale invasion and does not reflect war-altered land, population, monitoring, and infrastructure systems. [UKR-E-001, UKR-E-002, UKR-E-003, UKR-P-001, UKR-P-004]
- The principal current Climate-FCV interaction runs from conflict to climate vulnerability: war damage, displacement, contamination, mines, and access constraints weaken energy, water, agriculture, ecosystems, services, and future adaptation. [UKR-E-005, UKR-E-006, UKR-E-007, UKR-E-009, UKR-E-010, UKR-E-011, UKR-E-013, UKR-P-002, UKR-P-003, UKR-P-005]
- Municipal planning, emergency coping, climate-informed reconstruction options, and area-based recovery provide capacity, while independent evaluation shows environmental, inclusion, staffing, finance, and monitoring trade-offs. [UKR-E-004, UKR-E-008, UKR-E-014, UKR-E-015, UKR-E-016, UKR-P-006]

Critical remaining gaps:
- Ukraine lacks an integrated, current, high-resolution climate baseline that reflects war-altered land, population, infrastructure, hydrology, monitoring coverage, and occupied-territory uncertainty. [UKR-E-001, UKR-E-002, UKR-E-003, UKR-E-005, UKR-P-001, UKR-P-003]
- Independent response evaluation is localized to Mykolaiv; climate-integrated reconstruction, municipal adaptation, displacement integration, ecosystem recovery, and unintended distributional effects remain largely unevaluated. [UKR-E-013, UKR-E-014, UKR-E-015, UKR-E-016, UKR-P-005, UKR-P-006]
- The fcv-to-climate reverse pathway is well supported qualitatively through infrastructure, access, displacement, and environmental damage, but its additional effect on future climate losses and adaptation performance is not quantified. [UKR-E-005, UKR-E-007, UKR-E-009, UKR-E-011, UKR-E-013, UKR-P-002, UKR-P-003, UKR-P-005]
- Territorially comparable evidence disaggregated by gender, age, disability, displacement status, livelihood, and occupied or frontline location remains incomplete. [UKR-E-007, UKR-E-008, UKR-E-013, UKR-E-014, UKR-P-002, UKR-P-005]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Observed and projected warming raises Ukraine's heat baseline. [UKR-E-001]
- Heat, drought, flood, wildfire, and seasonal pressures vary geographically. [UKR-E-002]
- Warming can substantially expand rainfed-cropland water exposure. [UKR-E-003]
- War destruction has altered the systems and assets underlying climate exposure and recovery. [UKR-E-005]
- Kakhovka caused simultaneous inundation and desiccation as a conflict-related infrastructure disaster. [UKR-E-010]
- Displacement and uneven municipal capacity create differentiated local vulnerability. [UKR-E-013]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- War-related irrigation loss sharply reduced production in affected systems. [UKR-E-006]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Emergency coping reduced but did not equalize outage impacts. [UKR-E-008]
- Hostilities rapidly disrupted water systems and reduced coping capacity. [UKR-E-009]
- Municipal planning and climate-informed reconstruction provide intended adaptive capacity. [UKR-E-016]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Data, staffing, coordination, appraisal, and monitoring constrain adaptation delivery. [UKR-E-004]
- Independent evaluation found recovery gains and environmental, inclusion, and transaction-cost trade-offs. [UKR-E-014]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heat and drought may deepen agricultural losses where war has damaged irrigation, assets, access, logistics, and adaptive capacity. [UKR-P-001; UKR-E-001, UKR-E-002, UKR-E-003, UKR-E-005, UKR-E-006, UKR-E-011]
  - Documented impact: Higher rainfed-cropland exposure, yield variability, livestock stress, and irrigation demand
  - FCV mediator: War-damaged assets, mines, land-access restrictions, input and logistics disruption, displacement, and limited adaptation finance
  - Possible consequence: Climate pressures may deepen rural livelihood and food-system losses in war-affected areas, but warfare and markets remain independent primary drivers

  - Alternative explanations: ['Military damage, occupation, mines, input prices, trade disruption, farm policy, and labour shortages can reduce production without a climate shock.']
  - Uncertainty: Climate exposure and war damage are separately documented, but their joint contribution to observed production is not estimated.
- War-damaged energy systems can convert severe weather into cascading and unequal heating, water, health, and education failures. [UKR-P-002; UKR-E-001, UKR-E-002, UKR-E-005, UKR-E-007, UKR-E-008]
  - Documented impact: Heating, power, water, health, and school disruption during energy attacks and cold exposure
  - FCV mediator: Targeted infrastructure destruction, centralized-system dependence, repair constraints, poverty, disability, and high-rise building design
  - Possible consequence: Weather exposure produces larger and more unequal health and service losses when warfare disables interconnected infrastructure

  - Uncertainty: The observed cold-war interaction is direct, but cold is not attributed to climate change and future heat interactions are inferential.
- War damage to water and irrigation systems can magnify drought and flood vulnerability by reducing flexibility, access, monitoring, and ecosystem functions. [UKR-P-003; UKR-E-002, UKR-E-006, UKR-E-009, UKR-E-010, UKR-E-011, UKR-E-016]
  - Documented impact: Higher agricultural, drinking-water, ecosystem, and irrigation demand under damaged and altered hydrological systems
  - FCV mediator: Destroyed water infrastructure, Kakhovka reservoir loss, power dependence, contamination, mines, occupation, and restricted monitoring
  - Possible consequence: Conflict damage can reduce water-system flexibility and magnify future drought or flood losses across southern and eastern Ukraine

- Climate fire weather and warfare can compound wildfire risk through added ignition, explosive contamination, restricted access, and weakened suppression. [UKR-P-004; UKR-E-002, UKR-E-011, UKR-E-012]
  - Documented impact: Higher background ignition and spread conditions, burned landscapes, air pollution, and ecosystem damage
  - FCV mediator: Military ignition sources, unexploded ordnance, access restrictions, degraded monitoring, and constrained fire suppression
  - Possible consequence: Wildfires may become more frequent, larger, or harder to control where climate fire weather and hostilities overlap

  - Documented impact: Unequal housing, health, water, education, social-protection, and emergency-service demand
  - FCV mediator: War displacement, damaged housing, population and revenue change, uneven staffing, rural capacity gaps, and incomplete local integration
  - Possible consequence: Climate losses and recovery may become territorially unequal where frontline, host, return, rural, and smaller municipalities have different resources and needs

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Attacks converted severe cold exposure into multisystem service failure. [UKR-E-007]
- Conflict degradation erodes the foundations of climate resilience. [UKR-E-011]
- Climate fire weather and conflict constraints can compound wildfire risk. [UKR-E-012]
- Area-based recovery created selected local resilience and coordination capacity. [UKR-E-015]

## Geographic and livelihood-system differentiation

- National assessments are complemented by evidence from southern and eastern agricultural and water systems, the Lower Dnipro, combat-affected forests, government-controlled cities, frontline and host municipalities, and Mykolaiv; these findings have different territorial and access coverage. [UKR-E-003, UKR-E-006, UKR-E-007, UKR-E-010, UKR-E-012, UKR-E-013, UKR-E-014, UKR-P-001, UKR-P-002, UKR-P-003, UKR-P-004, UKR-P-005]
- Occupied, frontline, recently retaken, return, host, rural, and smaller-municipality conditions are not comparably observed, and national totals systematically carry access and classification gaps. [UKR-E-005, UKR-E-009, UKR-E-013, UKR-P-005]
- Agriculture, water, energy, housing, municipal services, ecosystems, displacement, forestry, health, and education are represented; private-sector adaptation, coastal risk, transport resilience, and national social-protection performance remain thin. [UKR-E-003, UKR-E-005, UKR-E-007, UKR-E-009, UKR-E-011, UKR-E-013, UKR-P-001, UKR-P-002, UKR-P-003, UKR-P-005]
