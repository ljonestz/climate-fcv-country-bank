# Ukraine Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Mykolaiv's historical water-system damage and proposed Southern Bug supply illustrate why recovery choices need scenario-specific water planning; future modeled shares include environmental flows. [UKR-E-101, UKR-E-102, UKR-E-103]
- Canal monitoring adds combined water-quality pressures, while municipal energy support and attack-related delays concern service resilience, not demonstrated climate-adaptation outcomes. [UKR-E-104, UKR-E-105, UKR-E-106, UKR-E-107, UKR-E-108, UKR-E-109]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- World Bank analysis reports rapid observed warming since the 1980s and projects substantially higher end-century temperatures under RCP8.5 than RCP4.5. Scenario: RCP4.5 and RCP8.5 through 2081-2100 [UKR-E-001]
- Official assessments identify heat, drought, river and urban flooding, wildfire, and divergent seasonal water balance, with greater drying risk in parts of southern and central Ukraine. Scenario: Multiple RCP pathways [UKR-E-002]
- World Bank modelling projects large expansion of rainfed cropland exposed to green-water scarcity at 1.5 and 3 degrees Celsius warming, especially in east-central areas. Scenario: 1.5 and 3 degrees Celsius warming levels [UKR-E-003]
- RDNA5 estimates national-scale war damage, losses, and recovery needs across housing, transport, energy, agriculture, water, municipal services, and ecosystems through December 2025. [UKR-E-005]
- The Kakhovka dam breach caused downstream inundation and upstream reservoir desiccation affecting settlements, wetlands, forests, agriculture, water supply, and irrigation. [UKR-E-010]
- War displacement has redistributed population, service demand, housing needs, and municipal revenue, while pre-war staffing capacity already varied between urban, settlement, and rural municipalities. [UKR-E-013]
- For Mykolaiv's proposed Southern Bug alternative, the study models water-supply plus minimum environmental-flow requirements reaching 40–79% of flow in May–October 2051–2080 under SSP5-8.5. Scenario: SSP5-8.5; far-future 2051–2080, as specified by the authors. [UKR-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- RDNA5 reports extensive irrigation damage and a sharp production decline on formerly irrigated land affected by loss of irrigation service, concentrated in southern and eastern systems. [UKR-E-006]
- Source dated 2024-10-02 reports: By 2024, remote-area wells yielded 5,000–10,000 m³/day against pre-war demand of at least 80,000; reported quality exceeded drinking-water limits and water was trucked daily. [UKR-E-102]
- Source dated 2026-04-25 reports: The study says the 2023 Kakhovka destruction increased the Dnipro–Donbas canal’s importance as a water and food-security route for Ukraine’s steppe zone. [UKR-E-104]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Backup generators, water distribution, and Points of Invincibility provided emergency coping during outages, but mobility limits and dependence on lifts or pumped water left unequal access. [UKR-E-008]
- Early-war research identified widespread realized or potential impacts on water resources and infrastructure, including complete water-system failure in multiple settlements. [UKR-E-009]
- JRC records substantial municipal climate-planning participation, while UNEP recommends that Kakhovka reconstruction alternatives use monitoring, nature-based options, and future climate and water projections rather than automatic restoration of the prior design. [UKR-E-016]
- Source dated 2026-04-22 reports: UNDP reports energy equipment support that restored or maintained services for more than 6.6 million people; it also says attacks delayed commissioning of several units. [UKR-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-01-04 reports: The ministry reported that more than 570 MW of new public-sector distributed-generation capacity had been commissioned by January 2026; it says modular boilers and co-generation can operate near users when large facilities are damaged or de-energized. [UKR-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Ukraine's adaptation communication identifies data gaps, limited capacity at all administrative levels, reactive planning, weak monitoring, and insufficient cost-benefit evidence as adaptation barriers. [UKR-E-004]
- The Mykolaiv Recovered evaluation found local service, coordination, and project-management gains alongside reduced green ambition, inconsistent inclusion, administrative burdens, and limited follow-up. [UKR-E-014]
- Source dated 2024-10-02 reports: Military operations destroyed Mykolaiv’s Dnipro intake on 12 April 2022, cutting drinking water to the city’s roughly 470,000 residents. [UKR-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-04-22 reports: UNDP reports 30 municipal ESCO solar contracts across 22 cities, totalling 5.1 MW and intended to serve 541,000 people; it says these installations eased pressure on damaged grids and helped maintain services during attacks and outages. [UKR-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-01-04 reports: The ministry says UAH 665 million was allocated in 2025 to frontline oblasts for fuel supporting municipal and critical-infrastructure operation. [UKR-E-109]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Heat and drought may deepen agricultural losses where war has damaged irrigation, assets, access, logistics, and adaptive capacity. [UKR-P-001; UKR-E-001, UKR-E-002, UKR-E-003, UKR-E-005, UKR-E-006, UKR-E-011]

  - FCV mediator: War-damaged assets, mines, land-access restrictions, input and logistics disruption, displacement, and limited adaptation finance
  - Possible consequence: Climate pressures may deepen rural livelihood and food-system losses in war-affected areas, but warfare and markets remain independent primary drivers

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- OHCHR documents attacks on energy infrastructure coinciding with severe winter cold and disrupting electricity, heating, water, health services, and schooling in government-controlled areas. [UKR-E-007]
- RDNA5 and JRC document mines and unexploded ordnance, industrial contamination, burned landscapes, soil degradation, forest loss and ecosystem damage in conflict-affected areas; these harms plausibly constrain safe land access and ecological and productive functions relevant to future adaptation. [UKR-E-011]
- Hotter and drier fire weather can raise background wildfire risk while warfare adds ignition, explosive contamination, and restrictions on monitoring and suppression. [UKR-E-012]
- The Mykolaiv evaluation documents locally anchored recovery offices, coordination, community initiatives, and institutional capacity that can support inclusive recovery, while sustainability depends on local staffing and finance. [UKR-E-015]
- Source dated 2026-04-25 reports: From 2015–2024, up to one-quarter of sampled canal water during low-flow periods was classed as only limited-suitability irrigation water; the authors attribute salinity changes to low flows, evaporation and combined climate and infrastructure pressures. [UKR-E-105]

## Bibliography and review decision

- World Bank Group. Ukraine: Building Climate Resilience in Agriculture and Forestry. 2022-01 (date basis: publication). https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/893671643276478711/pdf/Ukraine-Building-Climate-Resilience-in-Agriculture-and-Forestry.pdf [UKR-SRC-001] Methodology: Climate-model ensemble across approximately 7,400 grid points with sector and distributional modelling. Limitations: Prepared before the full-scale invasion, uses older RCPs, and has no war-adjusted exposure baseline.
- Government of Ukraine and United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. Ukraine's Climate Change Adaptation Communication to UNFCCC. 2024-05-27 (date basis: document-version). https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-05/Ukraine%201st%20Adaptation%20Communication.pdf [UKR-SRC-002] Methodology: Official synthesis of climate risks, policy arrangements, barriers, needs, and sector priorities. Limitations: Mostly repackages pre-invasion assessments and has limited post-2022 quantitative risk updating.
- Government of Ukraine, World Bank Group, European Commission and United Nations. Ukraine Fifth Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment: February 2022-December 2025. 2026-02-23 (date basis: publication). https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-04/ukraine-fifth_rapid_damage_and_needs_assessment.pdf.pdf [UKR-SRC-003] Methodology: Joint administrative, engineering, remote-sensing, partner, and modelling assessment using sector damage, loss, and needs methods. Limitations: Occupied and frontline data are incomplete, methods evolve, and needs are not outcomes or firm finance commitments.
- European Commission Joint Research Centre. Status of Environment and Climate in Ukraine: Assessing the Impact of War and Implications for Reconstruction. 2025 (date basis: publication). https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC141480/JRC141480_01.pdf [UKR-SRC-004] Methodology: Multi-source environmental synthesis using remote sensing, administrative data, mapped analysis, and policy review. Limitations: Coverage is weakest in occupied and combat areas and attribution among climate, warfare, and land management is often limited. Reuse terms: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.
- Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights. Attacks against Ukraine's Energy Infrastructure and Update on the Human Rights Situation in Ukraine, 1 December 2025-31 May 2026. 2026-06-29 (date basis: publication). https://ukraine.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/2026-06-29%20PR44.pdf [UKR-SRC-005] Methodology: Field monitoring, 180 visits, 1,926 interviews overall, and specialized energy-impact visits and interviews. Limitations: Many impacts were modelled or preliminary; the breach is a conflict-related infrastructure disaster, not a climate event.
