## Differentiated vulnerability

- The summary says unplanned urbanization heightens city climate risk; more frequent, intense floods could cause losses and damage critical infrastructure. Scenario: The summary describes outcomes without preventive measures. [LAO-E-001]
- Climate change is increasing water-resource pressure, and hydropower is likely to be significantly affected. Scenario: Scenario not specified in inspected passage. [LAO-E-002]
- The CCDR summary says climate change could harm rice cultivation and livestock, disproportionately affecting poor people and increasing farmers’ need for adaptation support. Scenario: Scenario not specified in inspected passage. [LAO-E-003]
- The NDC reports 2018 floods affected more than 600,000 people nationwide, with damage and losses estimated at US$371 million, or 2% of GDP. [LAO-E-005]
- The NDC says tropical storms and depressions in 2019 affected more than 764,000 people across two central and four southern provinces, disrupting livelihoods and damaging property. [LAO-E-006]
- The NDC expects warming across the Mekong region but says rainfall changes vary by location; altered temperature and precipitation may worsen flood conditions and affect crops, hydropower, roads, water supply, and food security. Scenario: Scenario not specified in inspected passage. [LAO-E-007]
- In five Savannakhet districts, the evaluation describes recurring flood damage to homes, farms and infrastructure, plus drought-related food insecurity linked to forest degradation and reduced fisheries; Luang Prabang City faces flash floods and landslides. [LAO-E-101]
- UNFPA's October 2024 flood response reports urgent needs for GBV-service information among affected northern communities; this documents protection-service needs, not observed climate-caused violence. [LAO-E-109]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The CCDR recommends road maintenance and expanded paving in high flood-risk provinces, prioritizing National Road 13 in Champassak. [LAO-E-004]
- The report presents Village Disaster Management Committees, loudspeakers and mobile-operator SMS partnerships as channels for actionable last-mile warnings. [LAO-E-106]
- The project reports 3,500 households received livestock-evacuation toolkits and silage-making guidance; district and provincial officials received training-of-trainers for livestock guidelines and warning dissemination. [LAO-E-108]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The evaluation says the absence of effective early-warning systems in the project regions compounds local flood and drought vulnerability. [LAO-E-102]
- The December 2024 midterm evaluation reports 16 solar-pumped wells and 9,632 beneficiaries, plus 200 trained officials, but flags inconsistent beneficiary denominators and limited outcome tracking. [LAO-E-103]
- Some visited residents linked land use to flood and drought risks but poorly understood planned measures; the MTR also flagged weak beneficiary tracking and watershed needs beyond the grant. [LAO-E-104]
- UNDRR identifies high staff turnover, few skilled forecasters and absent legal tools to mobilize private resources as constraints on Lao PDR's multi-hazard warning system. [LAO-E-105]
- The flood protocol was activated twice in September 2024, reaching 15 at-risk communities in Champhone, Songkhone and Xonnabouly and 4,000 households with early warnings and watertight drums. [LAO-E-107]
- UNFPA reports distribution of 200 dignity kits in affected areas and a further 466 kits handed to the Lao Women's Union for Luang Prabang, Xayaburi and Luang Namtha provinces. [LAO-E-110]
