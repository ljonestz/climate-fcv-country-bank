## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-07 reports: In 1980–2017, events were most frequent in Limón, Puntarenas and Alajuela; 72% were heavy rain, storms or hail. [CRI-E-101]
- Source dated 2024-06-28 reports: UNDRR reports heavy rain and drought in Costa Rica in 2023 and lists floods, hurricanes, storms, drought and water scarcity as climate-exacerbated hazards. [CRI-E-106]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Chorotega droughts primarily affect agriculture; extreme rain mainly impacts roads, farms and homes. [CRI-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Huetar Caribe extreme rainfall mainly affects roads, farms, rivers and streams; cited risks also include farm and fishing yields. [CRI-E-103]
- Source dated 2025-07 reports: Listed vulnerable groups include rural/coastal women in informal work and Indigenous communities lacking resources, water, electricity or internet. [CRI-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-06-28 reports: After October 2017 Storm Nate, about 20,000 Acosta residents were cut off for days without water, electricity, food or medicine. [CRI-E-107]
- Source dated 2024-06-28 reports: A 2023 assessment found about 60% of critical roads and bridges vulnerable to floods. [CRI-E-110]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2024-06-28 reports: Acosta residents organized food/medicine delivery and recovery planning; over 400 participated, helping inspire 32 municipalities to join an SDG network. [CRI-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-06-28 reports: Over 15,000 volunteer water managers serve nearly 30% of residents; support reinforced pipes, hydrants, weather stations and watershed warnings. [CRI-E-109]
- The undated MINAE project registry describes intended family-farming and food-security adaptation with Bribri and Cabécar organizations associated with Talamanca and Valle de la Estrella. Named territories are not assumed to be the same administrative unit; delivery and outcomes are unverified. [CRI-E-114]
- Source dated not stated reports: A Huetar Norte program lists 36-month adaptation support on 60 farms for soil/water conservation, crop diversity and water harvesting. [CRI-E-116]
- Source dated not stated reports: A Paquera-Tambor project proposes coastal-ecosystem protection and climate-adaptation capacity-building with fisher associations. [CRI-E-117]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The NAP progress report's June 2026 summary lists 67 of 232 products complete, 66 progressing, 43 unstarted or cancelled and 56 without progress information. It elsewhere reports information obtained on 179 products; these coverage descriptions are not reconciled and are not outcome measures. [CRI-E-111]
- Source dated 2026 reports: Of 144 products conditioned on external finance, 44 were complete, 37 progressing, 32 canceled or unstarted and 31 unknown; some needed funds did not materialize. [CRI-E-112]
- Source dated 2026 reports: A planned automated groundwater network for Central and Huetar Norte was to track water levels, temperature and conductivity, but its progress was unreported. [CRI-E-113]
