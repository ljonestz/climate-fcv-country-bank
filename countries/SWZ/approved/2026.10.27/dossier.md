## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC reports annual surface temperature increased by about 0.14-0.19 degrees C per decade during 1979-2020, alongside fewer rainy days and longer dry spells. [SWZ-E-001]
- The profile reports that the 2015/16 drought reduced crop production by about 30 percent, lowered Hawane Dam levels, and left some rural boreholes dry. [SWZ-E-002]
- The NDC labels its high-emissions pathway RCP8.5/SSP8.5 (SSP shorthand ambiguous); it projects up to 1.9 degrees C warming by the 2050s and 3-4 times more Highveld days above 33 degrees C by 2050. Scenario: RCP8.5/SSP8.5, as printed; SSP shorthand is ambiguous [SWZ-E-005]
- Source dated 2024-09-10 reports: FAO agricultural-stress data plotted by IMF show agricultural land in all four provinces under recurring stress from 2020 onward; the figure shows monthly provincial maxima. [SWZ-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The profile says women, children and other vulnerable groups were disproportionately affected by the 2015/16 drought. [SWZ-E-003]
- Source dated 2021 reports: The 2021 risk profile classifies 60% of residents as medium/high climate-vulnerability; poverty underlies risk, with hotspots in Shiselweni and Lubombo and moderate vulnerability in Hhohho and Manzini. [SWZ-E-101]
- Source dated 2021 reports: Citing Chuang et al. (2017), the communication reports increasing malaria attributed to climate-associated flooding, especially in Lubombo and Hhohho and in populated lowlands of Manzini and Shiselweni. [SWZ-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-09-10 reports: The IMF report says adverse weather reduced 2023 sugarcane output by 6%; it also describes 80% of farming as rain-fed and drought-vulnerable. [SWZ-E-106]
- Source dated 2024-09-10 reports: The IMF notes Eswatini ranks worst among SACU countries on population displaced, needing immediate aid or injured by drought, storms or floods. [SWZ-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-08-18 reports: The 2025 survey covered Lunkhuntfu and Phafeni (Nkilongo), Mabantaneni and Ntuthwakazi (Lubulini), and Mamisa and Luhlanyeni (Sithobela) in semi-arid Lubombo. [SWZ-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-08-18 reports: Among surveyed Lubombo households, 96.2% said climate change harmed food production and water access, 96.1% reported harder food availability, and 83.8% worsening water scarcity. [SWZ-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-08-18 reports: The surveyed Lubombo smallholders were 59.8% women; 62.6% reported unemployment and 66.8% depended primarily on agriculture for livelihoods. [SWZ-E-110]
- Source dated 2026-08-25 reports: FAO estimated the completed 2026 maize harvest at about 70,000 tonnes, below average and 6% lower year-on-year after heavy rain caused waterlogging and curbed yields. [SWZ-E-113]
- Source dated 2026-08-25 reports: FAO relays an IPC projection that 286,000 people (23%) face IPC 3+ in Oct 2026-Mar 2027; El Nino-linked low rain and high temperatures may reduce farm labour demand and incomes. Scenario: FAO GIEWS outlook dated 25 August 2026; El Nino-linked below-average rainfall and above-normal temperatures expected in the 2026/27 cropping season. [SWZ-E-114]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC sets a target to keep annual agricultural yield reductions from climate shocks below 15 percent by 2035. [SWZ-E-006]
- Source dated 2021 reports: In 2021, the Climate-Smart Market-Oriented Agriculture Project supported irrigation at Mavulandlela/Ntfonjeni (Hhohho) and Intamakuphila/Ngwempisi (Manzini), emphasizing jobs for women and youth. [SWZ-E-103]
- The 2021 communication reports over 10,000 irrigated hectares and a further 5,000-hectare Lower Usuthu phase then under development. [SWZ-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-08-18 reports: Households most often reported crop diversification (80.1%), changed planting dates (73.9%) and drought-tolerant crops (68.6%); small-scale irrigation was adopted by 19.5%. [SWZ-E-111]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The profile rates drought assessment and monitoring capacity as medium, while describing early-warning capacity as medium and noting gaps in comprehensive risk assessment and implementation coordination. [SWZ-E-004]
- Source dated 2026-08-18 reports: Respondents cited finance (83.0%), credit (73.9%), information (68.4%), extension services (54.4%) and insecure land tenure (44.1%) as adaptation barriers. [SWZ-E-112]
