# Namibia Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Named northern and central water-stressed areas face differentiated livelihood, nutrition and service-access constraints, with most local indicators dating to 2015/16–2022. [NAM-E-101, NAM-E-102, NAM-E-103, NAM-E-104, NAM-E-105, NAM-E-106, NAM-E-108]
- Pastoral mobility is an established coping strategy. A localized IFRC assessment associates drought-related movement with resource competition and unspecified conflict; human–wildlife conflict is a separate finding. [NAM-E-109, NAM-E-110, NAM-E-111, NAM-P-101]
- Finance and insurance access, local response authority, early-action delays and distribution safeguards constrain capacity. Reported historical interventions and 2024/25 response reach do not establish sustained recovery. [NAM-E-112, NAM-E-113, NAM-E-114, NAM-E-115, NAM-E-116, NAM-E-117, NAM-E-118, NAM-E-119, NAM-E-120]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2023-01 reports: In 2021/22, delayed rains and prolonged dry spells left crop areas with below-average harvests, including northern Kunene and Omusati and parts of Oshana and Otjozondjupa. [NAM-E-104]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Review reports 1.4 million assessed people in IPC Phase 3+ during the 2024/25 drought, associated with low rainfall, crop failure, livestock losses and higher food prices. [NAM-E-116]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2021-07 reports: The diagnostic identifies the central area, Walvis Bay–Swakopmund coast and Cuvelai as highly water-stressed; northern regions have the largest shares lacking safe water and sanitation. [NAM-E-101]
- Source dated 2021-07 reports: The report links recurring drought and volatile rainfall with poverty risks for rural households reliant on agriculture; water and sanitation shortfalls are associated with child stunting. [NAM-E-102]
- Source dated 2021-07 reports: The report’s 2015/16 profile places poverty above the national average in Kavango East, Kunene, Omaheke and Zambezi; poor groups included children, older people and female-headed households. [NAM-E-103]
- Source dated 2023-01 reports: NAMVAC reported ongoing drought effects in Kunene, Erongo and Omusati; Kunene had eight consecutive drought years, with livestock deaths, lost income and depleted grazing and water. [NAM-E-105]
- Source dated 2023-01 reports: In 2022, households consuming only zero to two food groups were most prevalent in Kunene (42%), Kavango East (39.7%), Oshikoto (30.4%) and Kavango West (29.7%). [NAM-E-106]
- Source dated 2022 reports: IFRC names Kunene, Erongo, Omusati and Omaheke as drought-prone; communal crop/livestock households were heavily affected. [NAM-E-108]
- The 2022 IFRC report describes human–wildlife conflict over water and grazing during the 2018/19 drought. This is a livelihood and protection interaction, not evidence of interpersonal or armed conflict. [NAM-E-109]
- Source dated 2022 reports: Women spent longer fetching water; children sometimes left school as pastoral households moved, and many such households were female-headed. [NAM-E-111]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Smallholders had limited credit; missing collateral linked to communal-land titling, low productivity and recurrent climate shocks constrained investment, while AgriBank outreach to communal farmers was limited. [NAM-E-113]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2022 reports: Red Cross actions included Kunene borehole repairs, solar pumps, WASH outreach and cash aid in 2019–21. [NAM-E-112]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2023 reports: The 2023 diagnostic found agricultural insurance largely limited to commercial farmers; proposed or tested drought-index products included options for communal farmers in Kavango East, Kavango West and Zambezi. [NAM-E-114]
- Source dated 2023 reports: The National Disaster Fund was active but its broad mandate was assessed as inefficient for disaster finance; a separate National Drought Fund remained a proposal in a pending policy. [NAM-E-115]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Response reached about 385,000 households across all 14 regions, but warnings did not trigger timely action and aid often arrived after livelihoods eroded. [NAM-E-117]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Regional committees were often not functional or empowered; centralized funds and authority constrained local action, while contingency plans were weak or not operationalized. [NAM-E-118]
- The June 2026 after-action review reports that water trucking maintained basic access for households and livestock and reduced unsafe coping, especially for women, while finding the response under-scaled for long-term resilience. This is a retrospective review finding, not a quantified causal evaluation. [NAM-E-119]
- Source dated 2026-06 reports: Some targeting included female-headed households and pregnant women, but review found no comprehensive gender analysis and identified exclusion and safety risks at distribution sites. [NAM-E-120]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- The 2022 IFRC assessment associates drought-related pastoral movement with resource competition and unspecified conflict, not an armed-conflict forecast. [NAM-P-101; NAM-E-110]
  - Documented impact: Pastoral households move seasonally in search of grazing and water, including between Namibia and Angola.
  - FCV mediator: Concentration of users and increased pressure on scarce grazing and water resources.
  - Possible consequence: IFRC associates this movement with resource competition and conflict, without specifying conflict type, incidents or scale.
  - Link evidence: {'pressure': ['NAM-E-110'], 'impact': ['NAM-E-110'], 'mediator': ['NAM-E-110'], 'consequence': ['NAM-E-110']}
  - Alternative explanations: ['Land access, service provision, pre-existing resource arrangements and livelihood conditions also shape competition; mobility is an established risk-management practice.']
  - Uncertainty: Localized qualitative assessment. Conflict is unspecified and must not be read as armed violence, ethnic hostility or a general risk posed by migrants. Not independently reverified in the 27 October 2026 remediation; check the cited passage before relying on this claim.
