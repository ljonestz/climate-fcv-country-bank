## Climate pressures and exposure

- The NDC gives preliminary forecasts of 0.2–0.4°C average annual warming by 2030 and 5% less rainfall by 2050. [TJK-E-002]
- Source dated 2026-07 reports: UNICEF rates 16 districts Very High and eight High for drought exposure; 918,108 children (26% nationally) face at least moderate drought. Temurmalik, Khuroson and Dangara are listed at 94%, 81% and 70% of their child populations, respectively. [TJK-E-101]
- UNICEF's district narrative identifies Rogun and Nurobod as especially exposed to landslide-related child vulnerability. [TJK-E-102]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The government describes Tajikistan as a mountainous, natural-resource-dependent country highly vulnerable to climate variability and extreme events, with institutional and community climate-risk capacity still requiring strengthening. [TJK-E-001]
- The NDC identifies energy, water, agriculture, and transport as highly susceptible; drought, lower rain-fed yields, crop failures, and livestock losses threaten dehkan farms. [TJK-E-003]
- The CCDR summary projects climate-related infrastructure, livestock, and agricultural damages could reduce real GDP by 5–6% by 2050. [TJK-E-005]
- The release states that the Vakhsh River Basin generates 90% of Tajikistan’s electricity and uses it to illustrate the country’s climate-development challenge. [TJK-E-006]
- Source dated 2026-07 reports: UNICEF identifies high child-service vulnerability: Nurobod and Kuhistoni Mastchoh score 10.0 for WASH vulnerability, while Vakhsh, Qubodiyon and Tojikobod face education-service delivery challenges with scores of 8.1-8.7. [TJK-E-103]
- RUSI's 2025 targeted interviews and scoping synthesis describe rural farm-income and livelihood pressures, including in Khatlon, interacting with poverty, unemployment and migration decisions. [TJK-E-107]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- The NDC recognizes women agricultural workers’ climate vulnerability and plans gender-sensitive indicators, awareness, and women’s participation in climate planning. [TJK-E-004]
- The CCDR recommends stronger social assistance for vulnerable households affected by climatic and economic shocks, alongside labor-market support for green-transition skills. [TJK-E-007]
- World Bank reports that RESILAND Tajikistan addresses land degradation and soil erosion affecting rural livelihoods, involving over 16,000 residents in Sughd, Khatlon, and GBAO; 613 community groups and 250 business plans are reported. [TJK-E-008]
- Source dated 2025-11-28 reports: RUSI reports that the STREAM programme in the Khojibakirgan watershed reportedly shows promise: women help identify water-management gaps and shape adaptation responses. [TJK-E-109]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2022-04 reports: The source says neither state maintained disputed water infrastructure; repair attempts provoked cross-border backlash, while weak Water User Associations constrained local authorities in Sughd and Batken. [TJK-E-105]
- Source dated 2024-04-05 reports: The World Bank reports 17 bridges rehabilitated in GBAO, 27.5 km of embankments in Khatlon, and 20 road sections renovated, efforts benefiting 676,000 people. [TJK-E-110]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Sughd–Batken disputes and repair backlash constrain irrigation governance under variable water availability. [TJK-P-101; TJK-E-104, TJK-E-105]
  - Documented impact: Disputed cross-border irrigation infrastructure lacks maintenance and reliable allocation.
  - FCV mediator: Unresolved jurisdiction, cross-border distrust and backlash against repairs constrain infrastructure governance.
  - Possible consequence: Water-management capacity can remain weak under climate variability; no effect on a named adaptation project is estimated.

  - Alternative explanations: ['Infrastructure degradation, water losses, agricultural demand and contested jurisdiction are important independently of climate trends.']
  - Uncertainty: Source-stated institutional mechanism, not isolated climate causation or an audit of current 2026 border conditions.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- International Alert's April 2022 article describes water-allocation disputes between Sughd and Batken preceding April 2021 clashes, with agreement breaches eroding trust. It presents climate as a stressor while emphasizing poor management and infrastructure. [TJK-E-104]
- Source dated 2022-04 reports: International Alert and ARGO convened regional climate-conflict dialogue with experts, government, local authorities and civil society, and formed a civil-society preparedness council. [TJK-E-106]
- RUSI's 2025 qualitative scoping report proposes that isolation, discrimination and economic insecurity abroad may expose some migrants to recruiter exploitation. [TJK-E-108]
- Source dated 2024-04-05 reports: The Bank says its preventative resilience approach focused on GBAO and Khatlon, described as vulnerable to natural hazards and FCV, and included social cohesion, stronger local institutions, youth-inclusive services and livelihood support. [TJK-E-111]
- The Bank's April 2024 feature reports 53 community projects completed and 17 youth centres refurbished, alongside youth training/livelihood support. Female beneficiaries' participation in planning, decisions or monitoring reportedly rose from 0% to 56%. [TJK-E-112]
