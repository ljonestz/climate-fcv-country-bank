## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2013-09 reports: The assessment placed most coastal homes, tourist lodging and utility lines within 30 metres of Melekeok’s high-water mark, vulnerable to sea rise, waves and typhoons. Scenario: The 2013 assessment considers sea-level rise, storm surge and waves through 2100. [PLW-E-101]
- Source dated 2021 reports: The report describes 2012–13 typhoon-driven debris, saltwater inundation affecting Kayangel gardens and homes, and flooding again in April 2021 at the low-lying traditional-village sites. [PLW-E-110]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Office page projects annual widespread coral bleaching by 2040 and stronger typhoon intensity; it says rising sea level will worsen high-tide flooding, storm surge and erosion. Scenario: The official webpage gives these projections but does not identify an emissions scenario. [PLW-E-116]
- Source dated 2020-02-06 reports: UNDP reports Typhoon Haiyan caused severe multi-sector infrastructure damage in Palau in 2013, with an estimated 454 homes destroyed or damaged. [PLW-E-117]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2013-09 reports: It identified the southern-coast wastewater plant as a low-lying mangrove-edge asset at risk of inundation, damage and sewage release. Scenario: Projected sea-level rise and storm surge. [PLW-E-102]
- Source dated 2013-09 reports: Ngardok Nature Reserve covers one-third of Melekeok and supplies its only drinking water; prolonged drought is identified as its greatest natural-system threat, alongside flood and erosion risks. [PLW-E-103]
- The 2025 water policy identifies the Ngerikiil catchment in Airai as the primary supply area for Airai and Koror. Its population-coverage figures vary between passages, so the bank retains qualitative dependence rather than a precise current coverage share. [PLW-E-105]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The policy says households on remote atolls including Kayangel, Sonsorol and Hatohobei mostly rely on rainwater catchments rather than piped treated water. [PLW-E-106]
- Source dated 2025 reports: While Koror–Airai water quality is described as good, small rural Babeldaob plants often have high turbidity after heavy rain and sometimes detect E. coli or coliforms. [PLW-E-107]
- Source dated 2021 reports: At Iporu on Tobi, surveyors found erosion had exposed archaeological remains; low-lying Tobi cultural sites (3–4 metres and below) were considered at risk of inundation. [PLW-E-109]
- Source dated 2021 reports: At the Melekeok coast, four stone monoliths had been undercut by erosion and were resting on the beach; the report recommends restoring and reinforcing the site and keeping a drainage culvert clear. [PLW-E-111]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2023-11-03 reports: The local authority adopted a 2021 coastal-fisheries plan applying traditional knowledge to reef-fish and invertebrate recovery and sustainable harvesting for the Koror community. [PLW-E-114]
- Source dated 2020-02-06 reports: UNDP says the 2016 El Niño drought caused water shortages across 80% of Palau and sanitation risks; NEMO noted the country’s remoteness delays resources and that assessment training can improve response planning. [PLW-E-118]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2013-09 reports: Proposed outputs included resilient wastewater, watershed and water-supply systems plus a state adaptation plan; no project adaptation strategies had yet been implemented. [PLW-E-104]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The policy records that the 2016 drought brought water shortages, poor sanitation, crop failures and malnutrition risk; it also says utility financial shortfalls prevent needed quality improvements in smaller systems. [PLW-E-108]
- Source dated 2023-11-03 reports: The Rock Islands Southern Lagoon resilience strategy was co-designed over two years by the local management authority and community with NGO and scientific partners, drawing on reef-condition data and community dependence on reef resources. [PLW-E-112]
- Source dated 2023-11-03 reports: The strategy account says earlier plans lacked implementation funds and integration; its first phase includes cultural-site preservation, sustainable fisheries, conservation finance and adaptive-management capacity, with a resilience officer placed in Koror State Government. [PLW-E-113]
- Source dated not stated reports: The Office of Climate Change says the PCCP 2025 covers nine sectors and five pillars and is accompanied by a ten-year action plan developed through a two-year process involving 150-plus people. [PLW-E-115]
- Source dated 2020-02-06 reports: A February 2020 UNDP–SPC–NEMO partnership planned five-day post-disaster assessment and recovery training for 30 officials and stakeholders, 40% women, across sectors including gender, water, health and housing. [PLW-E-119]
