# Brazil Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Amazon river-dependent settlements face transport and service isolation during low-water periods; 2024 Amazonas estimates are dated snapshots, not current caseloads. [BRA-E-104, BRA-E-105, BRA-E-106, BRA-E-108]
- Insecurity can compound drought-related access constraints. Flood shelter protection failures are a separate documented pathway, not an explanation for armed conflict. [BRA-E-111, BRA-E-112, BRA-E-115, BRA-P-101, BRA-P-102]
- Urban poverty, differentiated Indigenous and quilombola impacts, adaptive social protection gaps and flood-defense weaknesses shape vulnerability. Reported response activities are not evaluated impacts. [BRA-E-101, BRA-E-102, BRA-E-103, BRA-E-109, BRA-E-113, BRA-E-114]

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024-07-12 reports: A drought-isolation screen identified 1,850 non-Indigenous localities, 1,495 in Amazonas; 755 of 1,359 screened Indigenous villages were also there. [BRA-E-104]
- Source dated 2024-07-12 reports: Across 90 river gauges, 2005, 2010 and 2016 had low-water periods about 36.8 days above the 2000-2021 median. [BRA-E-105]
- A September 2024 UNICEF update relays 745,000 drought-affected people in Amazonas, including 330,000 people, of whom 115,000 were children, in 2,200 Indigenous and riverside communities isolated or at risk of isolation. These are dated and distinct exposure estimates. [BRA-E-108]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2023 reports: The World Bank reports reduced water availability and longer droughts; urban-poor residents, especially in informal settlements, are particularly vulnerable to extreme-weather losses. [BRA-E-101]
- Source dated 2023 reports: Climate shocks could place 800,000-3 million more people in extreme poverty by 2030, depending on Brazil's development pathway. Scenario: The executive summary gives a range across development pathways but does not specify an SSP or RCP scenario. [BRA-E-102]
- Source dated 2024-07-12 reports: Low rivers disrupt navigation and isolate rural communities, restricting access to food, fuel, medicine, health care and education. [BRA-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-01-28 reports: ACAPS says river isolation heightens exposure to trafficking, forced labour and abuse; women bear added burdens when drought reduces crops or livestock. [BRA-E-112]
- Source dated 2025-03-31 reports: In Rio Grande do Sul, 88% of quilombola communities were flood-affected; 84 Indigenous communities and about 16,691 people also faced disrupted services and subsistence. [BRA-E-113]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2023 reports: The World Bank says Brazil's adaptive social protection system has scalability and timeliness constraints; it recommends climate-risk data in the social registry. [BRA-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-07-12 reports: One media account reported that low-water navigation constraints hindered environmental enforcement against deforestation, hunting and illegal logging. [BRA-E-107]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Flood displacement into inadequately protected shelters was associated with reported sexual and gender-based violence in the 2024 response. [BRA-P-101; BRA-E-115]
  - Documented impact: Displacement into emergency shelters.
  - FCV mediator: Inadequate shelter divisions, privacy and security, as described by the rights mission.
  - Possible consequence: Reports of sexual and gender-based violence in shelters, including Porto Alegre; not a measured population-wide increase.

- ACAPS describes insecurity compounding drought-related isolation and humanitarian access constraints, not drought causing armed conflict. [BRA-P-102; BRA-E-106, BRA-E-111, BRA-E-112]

  - FCV mediator: Armed-group resource exploitation, recruitment and forced displacement compound access restrictions in drought-affected areas.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- In September 2024, UNICEF warned that persistent drought and fires could increase child separation, violence and exploitation risks, including through family movement. This is a conditional warning from that crisis, not a 2026 forecast or incident count. Scenario: Conditional on the crisis persisting; the flash update does not quantify likelihood or resulting incidents. [BRA-E-110]
- Source dated 2025-01-28 reports: ACAPS says armed groups exploiting resources, recruiting communities or forcing displacement aggravate drought-affected access, especially in Amazonas. [BRA-E-111]
- The rights mission on the 2024 Rio Grande do Sul floods describes inadequate divisions, privacy and security in emergency shelters and records reports of sexual and gender-based violence, including in Porto Alegre. It recommends specific protection protocols; these reports are not shelter-wide prevalence estimates. [BRA-E-115]

## Bibliography and review decision

- World Bank Group. Limitations: Only the 14-page executive summary was used; projections do not establish local incidence.
- Communications Earth & Environment; authors from Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais and partner research institutions. Severe droughts reduce river navigability and isolate communities in the Brazilian Amazon. 2024-07-12 (date basis: publication). https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01530-4.pdf [BRA-SRC-102] Methodology: Maps settlement proximity to rivers and roads, codes reported drought impacts and analyzes 90 river-gauge time series. Limitations: Media-reported impacts are incomplete; distance screening indicates isolation risk, not observed isolation for each settlement.
- UNICEF Latin America and Caribbean Regional Office. Climate-related Crisis in the Amazon Region: Flash Update No. 1, Reporting Period August-September 2024. 2024-09-30 (date basis: publication). https://www.unicef.org/media/163156/file/LACR-Flash-Update-Climate-Related-Crisis-in-the-Amazon-Region-30-September-2024.pdf [BRA-SRC-103] Methodology: Flash update combines state, Indigenous-organization and humanitarian coordination data with UNICEF response monitoring. Limitations: Dated situation snapshot; some population and service figures are relayed from third parties, not a prevalence study.
- ACAPS. Impact of drought in the Brazilian Amazon and 2025 outlook. 2025-01-28 (date basis: publication). https://www.acaps.org/fileadmin/Data_Product/Main_media/20250128_ACAPS_BRAZIL_-_Impact_of_Drought_in_the_Brazilian_Amazon_and_2025_Outlook_.pdf [BRA-SRC-104] Methodology: Seven-page thematic report based on review of public secondary sources. Limitations: Remote-area needs and access data are explicitly scarce; armed-group information is synthesized from cited sources.
- Inter-American Commission on Human Rights, Special Rapporteurship on Economic, Social, Cultural and Environmental Rights (REDESCA). Impacts of the Floods in Rio Grande do Sul: Observations and Recommendations for the Guarantee of Economic, Social, Cultural, and Environmental Rights. 2025-03-31 (date basis: publication). https://www.oas.org/en/iachr/reports/pdfs/2025/informe_redesca_brasil_en.pdf [BRA-SRC-105] Methodology: December 2024 mission, meetings with authorities, civil society, scientists and affected communities, plus document review. Limitations: State-specific mission; reported figures draw partly on administrative and secondary sources, not a representative survey.
