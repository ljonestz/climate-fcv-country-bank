Critical remaining gaps:
- No full CCDR PDF was inspected; the 2025 World Bank publisher summary does not disclose projection assumptions. []
- The NDC’s differentiated vulnerability statements were not independently triangulated with subnational data. []
- Draft collection pending human source review and approval. IDA eligibility is not an FCV classification. []
- National and selected local examples do not establish district-level coverage or causal attribution. []
- The bank contains no completed mediated climate-FCV pathway; this is an evidence gap, not evidence of absence. []
- No inspected source explicitly links climate hazards to armed conflict or violence in Cabo Verde. The CCDR links drought with rural-to-urban migration, and IFRC reports flood displacement and heightened protection risks, without establishing conflict causation. []
- The CCDR's municipality food-insecurity categories use island-level poverty data; modeled hazard estimates use different return periods and cited data years. []
- IFRC flood counts are operation-period estimates; response monitoring was incomplete, and the WMO roadmap was still awaiting validation in its December 2025 update. []
- UNDP's project summary does not name every municipality or independently quantify the adaptation outcomes of its completed project. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- The World Bank summary says sea-level rise threatens coastlines on which much of Cabo Verde’s tourism depends. [CPV-E-001]
- The summary identifies warming oceans and overfishing as threats to fisheries, which accounted for 72% of exports in 2021. [CPV-E-003]
- Without action, the CCDR summary projects GDP could be up to 3.6% smaller in 2050 and tourism revenue up to 10% lower. [CPV-E-004]
- The NDC reports about 0.14°C warming per decade (roughly 0.7°C over 50 years); rainfall is highly variable and has no statistically significant long-term national trend. [CPV-E-005]
- Source dated 2025-01-15 reports: Estimated: 16% exposed to extreme drought, 7% to extreme floods; poor households face 1.4x drought exposure. Drought affects all islands (lower in São Vicente/Sal); 90% of flood exposure is in Santiago/Santo Antão. [CPV-E-101]
- Source dated 2025-01-15 reports: RCP 8.5 projections raise coastal-flood exposure from 29% of coastal residents now to about 32% by 2050; Sal's exposed urban coast could rise from 33% to over 38%. [CPV-E-103]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- The summary identifies drought and saline intrusion as threats to agriculture, income and food security in rural communities of this water-scarce country. [CPV-E-002]
- The NDC says rural and peri-urban women in agriculture, water management and care face higher workloads and reduced income during prolonged drought and resource scarcity, while remaining underrepresented in climate governance. [CPV-E-006]
- The NDC identifies youth risks linked to limited jobs, education and decision participation, and children’s exposure to climate-related health risks, food insecurity and schooling disruption. [CPV-E-007]
- Source dated 2025-01-15 reports: The CCDR identifies 143,000 people (28%) in poverty/food-insecurity hotspots; six municipalities have acute food insecurity of at least 15%, five on Santiago and one on Santo Antão. [CPV-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-01-15 reports: The CCDR identifies drought and tourism as rural-to-urban migration drivers, especially toward Sal and Boa Vista. [CPV-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-10-22 reports: The IFRC update reports at least 12 deaths, five missing, over 27,500 affected and 1,500 displaced in São Vicente after floods and landslides, with damage to homes, roads, health services, markets and water systems. [CPV-E-105]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP's completed-project page describes pilots across three municipalities and five basins, including groundwater intake and drip irrigation in Ribeira Seca and planned reforestation/soil conservation at Planalto Leste on Santo Antão. [CPV-E-109]
- Source dated 2017-10-06 reports: For the 2017–2018 drought and poor agricultural year, the government announced an 880,000-contos program for livestock rescue, water-scarcity management and family employment, aimed at rural farm productivity, income and food-security losses. [CPV-E-110]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- The World Bank reports that hazard and climate-risk assessments were incorporated into municipal spatial-planning tools and that a Ministry of Finance risk service and National Emergency Fund were operationalized. [CPV-E-008]
- Source dated 2025-10-22 reports: By September 2025, the Red Cross had provided household items to 238 households and food to 271 in named São Vicente neighborhoods, and conducted three protection and inclusion trainings for 79 volunteers. [CPV-E-106]
- Source dated 2025-10-22 reports: The Red Cross reports ferrying about 150 m³ of drinking water daily from Santo Antão to São Vicente after flooding damaged the local distribution system. [CPV-E-107]
- WMO's December 2025 update reports an Early Warnings for All launch and draft five-year roadmap; validation and implementation were then expected during 2026. [CPV-E-108]
