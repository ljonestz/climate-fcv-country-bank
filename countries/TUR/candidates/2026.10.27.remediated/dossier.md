## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024 reports: The strategy rates urban heavy-precipitation risk at its highest level in Mersin, Adana, Hatay, Osmaniye, Samsun, Ordu, Kayseri, Kahramanmaraş, Şırnak, Batman and Diyarbakır. [TUR-E-101]
- Source dated 2024 reports: The water-resources assessment identifies high current drought risk in Konya, Isparta, Aksaray, Niğde, Nevşehir, the eastern Mediterranean and all Southeastern Anatolia. [TUR-E-103]
- Source dated 2024-05-30 reports: The World Bank reports that 2021 wildfires in Türkiye’s southern and western regions caused deaths, evacuated hundreds of residents and tourists, and affected tourism, livestock, beekeeping, timber and other sectors. [TUR-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024 reports: The strategy highlights Antalya, Mersin, Istanbul, Bursa and Izmir for urban heatwave risk, citing sprawl, impermeable surfaces, constrained streams and limited green space. [TUR-E-102]
- Source dated 2022-06 reports: The CCDR identifies Şanlıurfa and Mardin as wildfire and socioeconomic-vulnerability hotspots; their annual land-burned shares averaged 6.6% and 5.4% in 2011–2021, and about one-fifth of Türkiye’s poor lived in high wildfire-risk areas. [TUR-E-104]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP reports 99% of Syrians under temporary protection in host communities and over 2.2 million in Istanbul and major southeastern cities, where service, livelihood and cohesion pressures were reported. [TUR-E-107]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP cites work-permit barriers, informal low-paid jobs and heightened intergroup tensions where employment formalization is needed. [TUR-E-108]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2022-06 reports: The CCDR says poor households are often more vulnerable because they have less ability to prepare for, cope with and recover from extreme events. [TUR-E-106]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2024-06-27 reports: At June 2024 approval, the project planned flood-control works and nature-based solutions across seven named basins, with over 150,000 people targeted for direct benefit. [TUR-E-110]
- Source dated 2024-06-27 reports: The approval plan included real-time agricultural drought monitoring in the Ceyhan Basin and monitoring data for 10,000 farmers to support water-stress decisions. [TUR-E-111]
- Source dated 2024-05-30 reports: At its 2024 launch, a $400 million project targeted wildfire management and forest resilience in 14 highest-risk provinces, home to about 20 million people, many dependent on forestland for livelihoods. [TUR-E-113]
- Source dated 2025-10-03 reports: At project close in October 2025, the programme reported 2025–2030 local adaptation plans and sectoral vulnerability/risk analyses for Samsun, Sakarya, Muğla and Konya, plus urban climate-finance strategies. [TUR-E-114]
- Source dated 2025-10-03 reports: The programme reported 29 community-adaptation grant projects and 77 beneficiaries, completed capacity-building activities and developed 11 training modules. [TUR-E-115]
