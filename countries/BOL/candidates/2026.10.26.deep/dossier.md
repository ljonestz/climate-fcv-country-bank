# Bolivia Climate-FCV Evidence Dossier

## Executive assessment

- Highland examples distinguish Oruro/Potosí Indigenous farming, ayllu governance and irrigation maintenance; urban water evidence concerns historical La Paz/El Alto droughts. [BOL-E-101, BOL-E-102, BOL-E-103, BOL-E-104, BOL-E-109, BOL-E-110]
- Wildfire evidence identifies named lowland Indigenous territories and San Ignacio de Velasco water constraints. Ayoreo protection risks are conditional and require non-contact safeguards. [BOL-E-105, BOL-E-106, BOL-E-107, BOL-P-101]
- Camiri/Kaami planning and WFP Oruro evaluation summaries provide capacities and limits; participation, spending allocations and short-term consumption protection do not establish lasting resilience or peace effects. [BOL-E-112, BOL-E-113, BOL-E-114, BOL-E-115, BOL-E-116]

Critical remaining gaps:
- No reviewed primary source establishes armed conflict or organized violence disrupting climate-adaptation delivery in Bolivia; documented social conflict and land disputes are localized and not evidence of climate-caused violence. []
- REDESCA's 2024 wildfire mission is not representative and does not establish climate-change attribution for ignition; its Ayoreo forced-contact pathway is a conditional risk, not an observed violence event. []
- The full WFP evaluation PDF required JavaScript bot verification, and the FAO 2025 Oruro–Potosí anticipatory-action PDF exceeded the reader's size limit; claims rely only on accessible official page text or other opened reports. []
- The UNDP 2021 Oruro resilience report PDF returned 403; no claim relies on its body. The World Bank EVAs findings are based on project evaluations and 2023 site visits, with limited replication and municipal-plan continuity. []
- The Ayoreo pathway is a conditional protection risk grounded in REDESCA, not a report of unwanted contact or observed violence; prevention must respect non-contact. []
- A generic drought-crisis/social-conflict illustration is not treated as a demonstrated urban conflict pathway. []
- Draft extension: human source review and approval remain pending. Lending eligibility is not an FCV classification. []

## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2025-05-30 reports: Over 2.5 million hectares burned in lowland Indigenous territories; Chiquitanía fires consumed 92% of forest in Guarayo, Monte Verde and Pantanal territories. [BOL-E-105]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2026-02 reports: In southeastern Oruro and northern Potosí, models project drier dry seasons and wetter wet seasons with more floods and droughts; Quechua and Aymara highland farmers rely on rainfed or small-irrigation systems. [BOL-E-101]
- Source dated 2026-02 reports: The report attributes the 2023 highland drought to climate-related heat and El Niño: water flows fell one-third, and crop losses affected nearly two million farmers across seven departments including Oruro and Potosí. [BOL-E-102]
- Source dated 2025-05-30 reports: San Ignacio de Velasco Chiquitano communities faced fire-worsened water scarcity, reduced flows from private dams, agrochemical contamination and reportedly unenforced drought rules. [BOL-E-106]
- Source dated 2026-07 reports: In 2016–2017, rainfall deficits reached 36% in La Paz–El Alto; 256,000 people in 100 La Paz neighborhoods and seven El Alto districts faced water rationing. [BOL-E-109]
- Source dated 2026-04-17 reports: WFP's Oruro intervention addressed rural poverty and water scarcity affecting climate-vulnerable smallholder subsistence producers and Indigenous communities through cash transfers and water-related productive assets. [BOL-E-115]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2026-02 reports: By 2023, visited irrigation systems remained operational under local maintenance; participating farmers continued selling crops during drought while neighboring rainfed communities struggled. [BOL-E-103]
- Source dated not stated reports: Camiri allocated a budget to connect Kaami's plan with municipal planning; workshops engaged 16 of 19 Chaco Indigenous communities and produced a co-designed 3D map for the municipal planning process. [BOL-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2026-02 reports: Only two of six municipalities ratified project land-use plans, and turnover weakened their use; ayllu bylaws remained active for land management and resolving disputes with neighboring communities. [BOL-E-104]
- Source dated 2025-05-30 reports: Fire-season reports described pressure, invasion and disputes over Indigenous lands in Santa Cruz. [BOL-E-108]
- Source dated 2026-07 reports: The 2022–2023 drought was meteorologically longer and more intense than 2016–2017, yet EPSAS reported no service restrictions or cuts after its drought protocol and system measures. [BOL-E-110]
- The IDB's 2026 note reports that Bolivia lacked an implemented national drought plan in the period reviewed, while a 2023-2024 response allocated over BOB 187 million across 156 municipalities. This is dated institutional and allocation information, not verified current delivery. [BOL-E-112]
- Source dated not stated reports: UNDP identifies underrepresentation of Indigenous views in Lifesystems plans and no established pathway to integrate hyperlocal priorities into municipal PTDIs and national adaptation planning. [BOL-E-113]
- Source dated 2026-04-17 reports: WFP's synopsis says cash transfers protected consumption after climate shocks, while their contribution to longer-term resilience remained unclear; it describes Urus water-access results as significant but needing better tailoring. [BOL-E-116]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- Wildfire habitat loss could force unwanted contact and heighten violence risk for isolated Ayoreo; REDESCA does not report that this occurred. [BOL-P-101; BOL-E-107]
  - Documented impact: Fire threatens habitat used by Ayoreo people in voluntary isolation.
  - FCV mediator: Habitat destruction could force unwanted contact where territorial and non-contact safeguards are inadequate.
  - Possible consequence: Conditional exposure to violence and disease and threats to physical/cultural survival; no contact or violence event is asserted.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- REDESCA's 2025 report says 2024 fires threatened Ayoreo people in voluntary isolation in the Chaco. It warns habitat destruction could force unwanted outside contact, exposing people to disease and violence; the extent of damage to uncontacted Ayoreo territory was unknown. [BOL-E-107]
- Source dated 2026-07 reports: The IDB note presents an illustrative pathway from drought emergency management to social and political conflict. [BOL-E-111]
