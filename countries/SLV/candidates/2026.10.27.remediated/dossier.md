## Climate pressures and exposure

- Source dated 2024 reports: The assessment places 73% of districts in high-drought zones, concentrated in high-poverty Usulután, Ahuachapán, Cabañas and La Unión departments. [SLV-E-103]
- Source dated 2018-02 reports: The assessment ranks San Salvador, Soyapango, Ilopango and Cuscatancingo as most vulnerable to heavy-rain flooding and landslides; San Salvador recorded 209 floods and 79 landslides during 1960–2015. [SLV-E-112]

## Differentiated vulnerability

- Source dated 2024 reports: Dry Corridor drought followed by intense rain disrupts planting and harvests; the assessment reports agricultural labor-income losses for 13% of rural households and particular exposure among subsistence farmers. [SLV-E-102]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The 2025 NDC says 88.7% of national territory faces disaster risk; Cabañas, Morazán and Ahuachapán have high vulnerability combining socioeconomic factors, weak basic-service access and environmental stress. [SLV-E-105]
- Source dated 2018-02 reports: During three years of rainfall anomalies, maize and sorghum yields fell 43% and bean yields 29% in La Unión and Morazán; over 90% of producers used maize-bean-sorghum systems. [SLV-E-113]

## Coping and adaptive capacity

- Source dated 2019-11 reports: The study reports that gang dynamics required WFP to negotiate program access and beneficiaries to obtain permission to participate; field visits included San Miguel, Uluazapa, Jiquilisco, Ozatlán, Usulután, Suchitoto, Agua Caliente and San Salvador. [SLV-E-110]
- Source dated 2018-02 reports: Municipalities with more land in staple grains tend to have higher climate vulnerability, while adaptive capacity is very low across the studied Eastern Dry Corridor municipalities. [SLV-E-114]

## Institutions and delivery systems

- Source dated 2025 reports: The NDC sets a 2030–2035 objective to strengthen adaptation, food security and community resilience to reduce environmental and climate-related migration, and proposes a data system on such movements. [SLV-E-107]
- Source dated 2025 reports: The NDC describes an interagency mobility mechanism coordinated by Foreign Affairs and MARN, with agriculture, migration, housing and territorial-government bodies, intended to connect mobility, environment and climate agendas. [SLV-E-108]

## Climate-FCV pathways

- In historical Dry Corridor programme settings, drought-related harvest loss overlapped with low cohesion and gang restrictions that constrained coping and programme access; climate causation of gang violence is not established. [SLV-P-101; SLV-E-109, SLV-E-110, SLV-E-111]
  - Documented impact: Smallholder harvest and livelihood losses in the programme context examined in 2019.
  - FCV mediator: Low cohesion, gang-related social control and negotiated programme access can narrow collective coping and participation.
  - Possible consequence: Combined livelihood stress and access restrictions may undermine resilience; no causal claim that drought created gangs or a quantified increase in violence.

## Resilience and peace-supporting capacities

- Source dated 2024 reports: The assessment says floods and extended droughts affecting food security could exacerbate conflict and migration. [SLV-E-101]
- Source dated 2019-11 reports: The 2019 case study describes Dry Corridor rural communities as vulnerable to climate impacts and gang-related conflict; during a severe drought, small-scale farmers lost most maize harvests while community cohesion was low. [SLV-E-109]
- Source dated 2019-11 reports: The case study reports that WFP resilience activities brought landless agricultural day laborers and smallholders into communication with landowners and local government, with observed contributions to social cohesion and women's empowerment. [SLV-E-111]
